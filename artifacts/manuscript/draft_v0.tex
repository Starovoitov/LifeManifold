\title{Asymmetric Surrogate Value in {MAP}-{E}lites: Attachment-Point Decomposition under {LLM} Variation}

\author{Artem Starovoitov\corref{cor1}}
\ead{artem.starovoitov@proton.me}

\address{Independent Engineer, Moscow, Russia}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author}

\begin{abstract}
Classical surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithms ask where a surrogate should intervene in the propose$\rightarrow$evaluate loop (individual- vs.\ generation-based evolution control). LLM-guided MAP-Elites raises a structurally related question: should a shared surrogate attach \emph{before} generation (prompt-side advice) or \emph{after} generation (evaluation gating)? We isolate those intervention points with checkpoint, archive, budget, and emitters held fixed. After-generation attachment improves evaluation-count efficiency on our testbed (${\sim}{+}3.7$~pp coverage per simulation at mid-budget; $33.5\%$ fewer sims at fixed iterations). Matched before-generation scalars stay within $\pm 2$~pp of stub constants on \texttt{qwen-turbo} ($n{=}10$; TOST). The LLM is one concrete emitter; CMA-ME, secondary domains, and multi-provider rows bound the same methodological contrast. The contribution is surrogate \emph{model management} in MAP-Elites.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\textbf{One scientific question.} Classical SAEA model management asks where a surrogate should intervene before an expensive evaluation~\cite{jin2005fitness}. LLM-guided MAP-Elites introduces a new emitter architecture, but the same methodological question reappears: should a shared surrogate attach \emph{before} generation (prompt-side advice) or \emph{after} generation (evaluation gating)? We hold checkpoint, archive, budget, and emitters fixed and treat that choice as an \emph{intervention-point} (attachment-point) decomposition. After-generation intervention improves evaluation-count efficiency on our testbed; matched before-generation intervention stays within $\pm 2$~pp. The LLM is one concrete variation operator; CMA-ME, secondary domains, and provider replications bound the answer.

Quality-diversity (QD) maintains archives of solutions that are both high-performing and behaviourally diverse. MAP-Elites assigns each candidate to a behavioural niche and keeps the best elite per cell~\cite{mouret2015illuminating}. Every candidate still needs a black-box evaluation. When that roll-out is costly, evaluation count dominates wall time, and surrogate-assisted illumination---a QD instance of classical SAEA model management~\cite{jin2005fitness}---becomes attractive~\cite{gaier2018sail}.

The scientific object is that EC model-management question, not an LLM product. An LLM-guided stack still couples emitters, a simulator, and an archive. A surrogate is a natural place to inject feedback into that loop. Pipelines usually change several of those pieces at once; we change only the surrogate intervention point.

Previous work often compared complete \emph{pipelines}. We compare \textbf{intervention points} (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}). Payoffs are measured on a controlled black-box evaluator (\S\ref{sec:substrate}). Labels \textbf{H1}--\textbf{H5} name contrasts that probe one methodological model (Table~\ref{tab:role-exp}): before-generation intervention, after-generation intervention, and their interaction under an LLM emitter.

\subsection{Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Intervention-point decomposition (methodology).} We formulate surrogate attachment as an intervention-point decomposition for LLM-guided quality-diversity: a structurally related instance of the classical evolution-control question---where a surrogate should intervene relative to expensive evaluation~\cite{jin2005fitness}---once an LLM emitter is present (\S\ref{sec:related}). We do not claim a full generalization of the individual-/generation-based SAEA taxonomy. After-generation attachment is individual-based pre-selection; before-generation attachment is a soft prompt-side channel with no direct SAIL analogue.
  \item \textbf{After-generation intervention works on the evaluation axis (H2).} With a shared checkpoint and matched \texttt{uniform\_frontier} genetic emitters, a threshold gate improves coverage \emph{per simulation} by ${\sim}3.7$~pp at mid-budget and skips $33.5\%$ of sims at fixed iterations (Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition}; \S\ref{sec:h2}). On this cheap evaluator the same gate finishes $-0.83$~pp at fixed iterations and is slower in wall time ($\sim$20 vs.\ $\sim$7~min).
  \item \textbf{Before-generation intervention is not the historical ``hints'' story (H1); boundary checks.} At matched policy, prompt-side scalars stay within $\pm 2$~pp of stub constants on \texttt{qwen-turbo} ($n{=}10$; TOST; \S\ref{sec:decomposition}). The locked $+15$~pp stub$\rightarrow$hints PASS is mostly archive policy (${\approx}{+}14.8$~pp). Continuous-relaxation CMA-ME, dungeon/maze transfer, and LLM$+$filter stacking under gray-zone repair (H3; confirmatory F-RQ3-gray NI pass, no coverage win; \S\ref{sec:h3}) bound the same intervention model (appendix~\S\ref{sec:h3-appendix}).
\end{enumerate}

Every experiment in this paper exists to answer one question: which surrogate intervention point is causally responsible for gains in this MAP-Elites loop? Provider replications, CMA-ME, dungeon/maze, and offline threshold/replay diagnostics are kinds of evidence for that answer---not parallel research lines. Section~\ref{sec:method} defines the loop; \S\ref{sec:protocol} the design; \S\ref{sec:results} the evidence; \S\ref{sec:discussion} the reading. Scope and threats: \S\ref{sec:limitations}.

\section{Background and related work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Quality-diversity and MAP-Elites}

Quality-diversity (QD) algorithms seek collections of high-performing yet behaviourally diverse solutions rather than a single optimum~\cite{pugh2016quality,cully2018quality,chatzilygeroudis2021qd}. MAP-Elites maintains an archive partitioned by user-defined behavioural characteristics (BC) and retains the best elite per niche~\cite{mouret2015illuminating}. Heterogeneous emitter sets can improve illumination efficiency relative to a single variation operator~\cite{cully2020multiemitter}; our confirmatory stack mixes random, genetic, and LLM slots in one batch.

Continuous variation operators remain important on fixed-length genomes. Covariance Matrix Adaptation MAP-Elites (CMA-ME) couples CMA-ES adaptation with MAP-Elites archiving and often dominates random or isotropic mutation on continuous control and design tasks~\cite{fontaine2020cmame}. CMA-MAE extends the idea with MAP-annealing and separate result archives, trading coverage for per-niche quality~\cite{fontaine2023cmamae,zhao2024cmamae}. We implement both through \texttt{ribs} v0.11.0~\cite{pyribs2020} with an adapter that evaluates candidates on the same black-box simulator as the LLM stack (\S\ref{sec:method}). Our pyribs baselines use homogeneous CMA-ES emitter pools rather than mixed pyribs ``cocktails,'' so they represent strong but not maximal pyribs configurations.

\subsection{Surrogate-assisted illumination}

Expensive simulators motivate surrogate models that predict outcomes from static design features~\cite{jin2005fitness,jones1998ego}.
Classical surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithms (SAEAs) treat that prediction problem as \emph{model management}: when, and how strongly, to trust the approximate fitness instead of the true evaluator~\cite{jin2005fitness}.
Two recurring evolution-control patterns are \emph{individual-based} control (pre-select or filter candidates before expensive evaluation) and \emph{generation-based} control (evaluate only some generations fully, or alternate approximate and exact generations).
We do not claim a full generalization of that taxonomy. We ask whether a structurally related \emph{intervention-point} dilemma reappears once an LLM emitter is present: before-generation (soft prompt-side advice) vs.\ after-generation (hard evaluation gating) attachment of one shared surrogate.
The after-generation surrogate is individual-based pre-selection: a threshold gate decides whether a proposed genome spends a simulator call.
The before-generation surrogate is a softer use of the same model: scalars enter the LLM prompt and try to shape variation, but never skip \texttt{evaluate\_candidate}. Soft advice is easy for a constrained LLM emitter to ignore; hard individual-based gating reallocates evaluations (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}--\ref{sec:h2}).

Surrogate-Assisted Illumination (SAIL) is the canonical QD answer: fit a Gaussian process over the behaviour--fitness landscape, run MAP-Elites on an acquisition surface (classically UCB-style), and spend real evaluations only on a selected subset~\cite{gaier2018sail,gaier2017sail,gaier2017aiaa}.
Deep Surrogate Assisted MAP-Elites (DSA-ME) replaces the GP with neural predictors trained online in an inner loop and validates candidates in an outer game-engine loop~\cite{grillotti2022dsame}.
Related Bayesian QD lines couple acquisition to descriptor and fitness models~\cite{kent2025boqd}.
Discount Model Search continues the surrogate-QD line without LLMs, replacing histogram discounts with a learned continuous discount model for high-dimensional measure spaces~\cite{tjanaka2026dms}.
Across this QD family the surrogate is a \emph{search accelerator}: it can change which candidates receive ground truth, and often \emph{is} the surface being illuminated rather than a side channel into an otherwise unchanged MAP-Elites loop.
SAIL-style acquisition is thus a strong form of individual-based evolution control specialized to behaviour--fitness GPs; our after-generation threshold gate is SAIL-\emph{kin} in that controlling sense, while the before-generation prompt channel has no direct SAIL analogue.

Reported evaluation savings are strongest in computational design.
On two-dimensional airfoil tasks SAIL needs orders-of-magnitude fewer flow simulations than MAP-Elites while filling behaviour bins~\cite{gaier2018sail}; with the budget CMA-ES requires to optimize one bin, SAIL can match that quality in every bin~\cite{gaier2017sail}.
Three-dimensional aerodynamics and high-dimensional discrete deckbuilding show the same qualitative pattern~\cite{gaier2017aiaa,grillotti2022dsame}.
Evidence for stochastic simulators is thinner: these studies assume expensive but largely deterministic evaluators or batched outer validation, not single-rollout archive updates under seeded simulator noise.
Our confirmatory tier holds the simulator budget fixed (32{,}500 evaluations per seed) and asks, for LLM-guided QD, \emph{where} the same surrogate should attach under full-simulation archive updates---not whether a new SAIL-style illuminator brand wins on expensive CFD.

\textbf{Positioning vs.\ SAIL / DSA-ME.}
Our stack differs from those lineages on four points that matter for the before/after framework.
(i)~\emph{Model:} a multi-task MLP ensemble with MC-dropout over genome features (optional LightGBM path), not a monolithic GP fitness surface; offline MLP-vs-GP/UCB checks prefer the production MLP under the runtime compose definition (appendix~\S\ref{sec:surrogate-ablation-appendix}).
(ii)~\emph{Attach points:} the before-generation surrogate \textbf{never skips} \texttt{evaluate\_candidate}---it only supplies scalars to the LLM prompt---while the after-generation surrogate optionally gates with a production \texttt{threshold\_gate} ($\texttt{min\_fit}{=}0.45$, $\texttt{max\_u}{=}1.0$), not SAIL's GP-UCB illuminator.
(iii)~\emph{Truth vs.\ guidance:} prompt fitness is recomposed from predicted components with the same closed form as the simulator (Eq.~\eqref{eq:fitness}), but archive insertion always uses simulated metrics; DSA-ME's online inner/outer split is closer to the after-generation surrogate than to the before-generation surrogate, yet still does not inject surrogate text into an LLM emitter.
(iv)~\emph{Disclosure:} compose-gate dual-definition diagnosis for blocked H3 is in appendix~\S\ref{sec:h3-appendix}---transparency, not a main-plot branch.
We therefore treat SAIL/DSA-ME as related surrogate-QD lineages rather than drop-in baselines; pyribs CMA-ME/CMA-MAE (\S\ref{sec:pyribs}) are the quantitative QD baselines for this protocol.

\subsection{LLMs in evolutionary and QD search}

LLMs have been used as variation operators, prompt optimizers, and evolutionary engines~\cite{wu2025ecllm,wang2025llmea,vanstein2024llamea,guo2024evoprompt,liu2024llmeo,lange2024llmes}. Program-search lines pair a frozen LLM with an external evaluator to evolve code or rewards (FunSearch; Eureka)~\cite{romera2024funsearch,ma2024eureka}; Promptbreeder evolves task and mutation prompts self-referentially~\cite{fernando2024promptbreeder}; OpenELM surveys open-ended LLM evolution~\cite{lehman2023openelm}. Closest to our QD setting, LLMatic couples LLM code generation with CVT-MAP-Elites for neural architecture search~\cite{nasir2024llmatic}, and in-context QD generators sample diverse solutions from LLM priors~\cite{lim2024llmqd}. Behaviour-space analyses of LLaMEA further compare dual-prompt mutation strategies on anytime search dynamics~\cite{vanstein2025behaviour}. Concurrent LLM+MAP-Elites applications include D{\'e}j{\`a}Q's learnability-guided curation of RL-with-verifiable-rewards datasets~\cite{ropke2026dejaq} and medical decision-pipeline evolution without a surrogate/gating layer~\cite{sviridov2026medical}---both use LLM variation in a QD loop, but neither treat before- vs.\ after-generation surrogates as an experimental factor. Procedural-content pipelines apply evolutionary search and LLMs to level generators and structured genotypes~\cite{togelius2010sbpcg,earle2022nca}; emitters must respect simulators that validate and repair invalid outputs (as in our dungeon domain, \S\ref{sec:results}). Open-ended CA worlds have likewise been illuminated with QD archives~\cite{faldor2024lenia}.

A complementary line treats the \emph{prompt itself} as the search space: Diverse Prompts applies MAP-Elites over prompt phenotypes for BigBench Lite tasks~\cite{santos2025diverseprompts}, evolutionary sampling explores instruction diversity more broadly~\cite{saletta2024promptspace}, and parameter-efficient neuroevolution steers frozen LLMs by evolving prompt embeddings rather than using the LLM as a genotype emitter~\cite{guo2026qdllm}. Those settings illuminate prompts (or embeddings) for a fixed downstream LLM call, whereas we mutate \texttt{WorldSpec} genomes and inject simulator-derived \emph{surrogate scalars} into the mutation prompt. A different surrogate use keeps the LLM on fitness estimation: LLM-assisted evolutionary algorithms (LAEA) and related LLM-driven SAEAs prompt a model to classify or regress candidate quality~\cite{hao2024laea,xie2025llmsae}. We instead pair a genome-aligned ML regressor (trained on simulator logs) with an LLM \emph{emitter} that edits structured genotypes---the LLM is \emph{not} the surrogate.

\subsection{Seeded simulators and uncertain QD}

Prior LLM+QD systems often evaluate on deterministic or batched offline tasks. Our confirmatory evaluations are \emph{deterministically seeded} from the genotype (canonical SHA-256), so re-evaluating the same candidate reproduces the same fitness and BC even when the underlying simulator rules are stochastic. Elites are therefore not lucky-seed artifacts in the UQD sense.

Uncertain QD (UQD) formalizes settings where fitness and BC are distributions and archive policies re-sample to limit lucky outliers~\cite{flageat2023uq,flageat2025extractqd,flageat2025tradeoff}. We use a single seeded pass per insertion and paired experiment seeds with Holm on terminal coverage and mean elite fitness (\S\ref{sec:protocol}). We separate \emph{archive truth} (always simulated) from \emph{emitter guidance} (before- and/or after-generation surrogates). Target selection is orthogonal to those two surrogate roles.

\label{sec:method}

Everything below implements one contrast: before- vs.\ after-generation attachment of a shared surrogate in a MAP-Elites propose$\rightarrow$evaluate loop (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}). The LLM is an optional emitter; the evaluator is a measurement substrate only (\S\ref{sec:substrate}). Archive fitness always comes from the simulator---the surrogate never replaces ground truth for insertion.

\subsection{Attachment points and contrast labels}
\label{sec:terminology}

A MAP-Elites iteration is select $\rightarrow$ \textbf{generate} $\rightarrow$ (optionally) gate $\rightarrow$ \textbf{evaluate} $\rightarrow$ archive update. As in Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Before-generation surrogate} --- predictions enter the emitter and try to change \emph{what} is proposed (never skips simulation).
  \item \textbf{After-generation surrogate} --- predictions gate the candidate and change \emph{whether} it spends ground truth (SAIL-\emph{kin} hard gate).
\end{itemize}

We use those two names throughout. Synonyms appear only here: before-generation $\equiv$ prompt hints / soft intervention; after-generation $\equiv$ acquisition / candidate filtering / hard intervention; attachment point $\equiv$ intervention point / surrogate attachment policy. Table~\ref{tab:role-exp} maps intervention points to reader labels \textbf{H1}--\textbf{H5}. Prompt placeholders and thresholds are mechanisms \emph{inside} a role. Surrogate-informed parent selection is out of scope; archive \texttt{target\_selection} remains ordinary MAP-Elites policy.

\subsection{MAP-Elites illuminator loop}

Each run executes \texttt{iterations} $\times$ \texttt{batch\_size} candidate slots in fixed order. Production schedulers use $650 \times 50 = 32{,}500$ simulator calls per seed. For slot~$k$ the loop:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item selects a target niche (behaviour-cell centre) according to \texttt{target\_selection};
  \item calls the slot emitter (\texttt{random}, \texttt{genetic}, or \texttt{llm}) to propose a \texttt{WorldSpec};
  \item optionally applies surrogate acquisition (\S\ref{sec:acquisition});
  \item runs \texttt{evaluate\_candidate} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{run\_world} (always for accepted slots in MVP hint mode);
  \item inserts into the archive if fitness strictly improves the cell incumbent;
  \item appends training data to the surrogate buffer when \texttt{surrogate.enabled}.
\end{enumerate}
LLM slots log \texttt{llm\_fallback} when parsing fails and a genetic repair path is used. Parallel evaluation uses a process pool sized to the batch; LLM emit uses up to four concurrent HTTP workers per iteration. Outputs: \texttt{map\_elites\_archive.jsonl}, optional \texttt{archive\_trace.jsonl}, and \texttt{nightly\_run\_summary.json}.

\textbf{Target selection.} \texttt{min\_fitness\_frontier} picks the occupied cell with lowest elite fitness (bundled \texttt{stub}). \texttt{uniform\_frontier} samples uniformly among occupied cells (\texttt{hints}, filter arms, matched H1/H2). These are archive policies, not surrogate roles: they do not use the surrogate to select parents. Scientific H1 and H2 hold this policy fixed (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}, \S\ref{sec:h2}). The historical stub$\rightarrow$hints gap does not. Surrogate-assisted parent selection is out of scope.

\subsection{Surrogate model}
\label{sec:surrogate-method}

The surrogate maps a canonical \texttt{WorldSpec} to predicted MAP-Elites outcomes without a simulator call (Figure~\ref{fig:surrogate}). Feature schema~2.1 encodes 24 genome-aligned dimensions (discrete rule bits and continuous scalars; \S\ref{sec:substrate}). A multi-head regressor (\textbf{Strategy~A}) predicts seven component targets---stability, diversity, oscillation, two topology proxies, final density, and early-extinction probability---trained from JSONL buffer rows appended after each real evaluation when the surrogate is enabled.

Production checkpoint \texttt{nightly\_v3\_mc\_d005.pkl} is a four-member MLP ensemble with MC-dropout uncertainty ($N=35{,}114$ training rows; hold-out at runtime-aligned compose gate~$0.95$: $R^2_{\text{fitness}}=0.94$ [95\% CI $0.94{,}0.95$], MAE$_{\text{fitness}}=0.029$, NMAE$=0.112$; appendix~\S\ref{sec:checkpoint-appendix}). Runtime fitness is recomposed via \texttt{compute\_fitness\_from\_prediction} using Eq.~\eqref{eq:fitness} (same weights as the simulator). \texttt{get\_surrogate()} falls back to constant \texttt{StubSurrogate} values if the checkpoint is missing, dimension-mismatched, or fails the \texttt{hints\_ok} gate ($R^2 \ge 0.30$, MAE $< 0.085$).

\textbf{Before-generation surrogate (H1).} Before each LLM call, \texttt{SurrogateFacade.predict(parent)} runs on the \emph{parent} elite's spec. Placeholders \texttt{\{surrogate\_mean\}} and \texttt{\{surrogate\_uncertainty\}} in the user prompt receive \texttt{prediction.fitness} and calibrated ensemble spread. When \texttt{surrogate.enabled=false}, fixed YAML stubs (\texttt{stub\_mean}, \texttt{stub\_uncertainty}) are substituted without calling \texttt{predict}. One LLM HTTP request per LLM slot regardless of surrogate mode.

\subsection{LLM emitter}

The \texttt{LlmEmitter} sends the parent \texttt{WorldSpec} JSON and niche centre to a chat-completions API; the model returns a JSON patch applied via \texttt{world\_spec\_from\_llm}. Stack~v2 uses temperature~0.2, \texttt{max\_tokens=350}, and \texttt{llm\_parallel\_workers=4}. Primary LLM runs use Alibaba \texttt{qwen-turbo} (DashScope); multi-provider replications swap provider YAML while keeping prompts and schedulers fixed (\S\ref{sec:results}). Model IDs are window-pinned in run summaries (\texttt{llm\_spec\_hash}); floating endpoint aliases are disclosed per provider.

Accepted LLM elites typically differ from parents by compact named-field rule/scalar edits rather than bit noise (edit-anatomy case study, appendix~\S\ref{sec:edit-anatomy}).

\subsection{After-generation surrogate (H2)}
\label{sec:acquisition}

Optional \texttt{acquisition.mode=filter} evaluates \texttt{surrogate.predict} \emph{before} \texttt{evaluate\_candidate}. A \texttt{threshold\_gate} skips simulation when predicted fitness falls below \texttt{min\_predicted\_fitness=0.45} (primary grid) subject to uncertainty calibration. Skipped slots are logged to \texttt{surrogate\_archive.jsonl} but do not update the MAP-Elites archive. \texttt{shadow} mode logs counterfactual skips while still evaluating. We use an MLP ensemble with a fitness threshold---not SAIL-style GP+UCB---as the production after-generation mechanism; offline MLP-vs-GP/UCB comparisons are appendix~\S\ref{sec:surrogate-ablation-appendix}. Primary H2 evidence is the matched genetic filter contrast (\S\ref{sec:h2}); LLM$+$filter stacking (H3) is exploratory (\S\ref{sec:h3}).

\subsection{pyribs CMA-ME / CMA-MAE baselines}
\label{sec:pyribs}

Gradient QD baselines run through \texttt{run\_pyribs\_baseline.py} (\texttt{ribs==0.11.0}), not as native \texttt{EmitterKind} types. The adapter maps the same 21-D genetic genome to \texttt{WorldSpec}, evaluates with the same simulator, and writes comparable JSONL archives. Each algorithm uses five homogeneous \texttt{EvolutionStrategyEmitter} instances (batch~50 $\Rightarrow$ ask size~250), $\sigma_0=0.2$, and 32{,}500 evaluations. CMA-ME uses learning rate~1.0 with 2-improvement ranking; CMA-MAE uses learning rate~0.01 with a separate result archive. This is a strong but not maximal pyribs configuration (no mixed ImprovementEmitter cocktail). Encoding asymmetries are disclosed below.

\textbf{Encoding (continuous relaxation).}
CMA proposes continuous $\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{21}$; \texttt{decode\_genome} then \emph{rints} the 18 rule bits and clips the three scalars. Genetic / LLM emitters edit discrete rule tables directly. The comparison is therefore not bit-identical: CMA searches a continuous relaxation where covariance adaptation can exploit structure that bit-flip genetics never sees. We treat pyribs arms as an upper-bound-style ceiling for continuous-friendly representations. Three supplementary controls (\S\ref{sec:h4}): Bernoulli-decode CMA-ME, native bit-flip, and pbCMA (latent Gaussian + bit threshold). Bernoulli-decode and pbCMA both lead hints by $\sim$10~pp; native discrete by $+5.2$~pp. pbCMA closes most of the native$\rightarrow$Bernoulli gap but still trails \texttt{rint} ($-3.4$~pp).

\textbf{Warm-start (symmetric).} All confirmatory primary-grid arms---\texttt{vanilla}, genetic ME variants, \texttt{stub}/\texttt{hints}/\texttt{filter}, and pyribs CMA-ME/MAE---load the \emph{same} nightly grid baseline JSONL ($\sim$2{,}614 elites) via \texttt{--load-archive} before the 32{,}500-evaluation budget. Terminal coverage is reported after warm-start merge/collapse. The pyribs contrast is matched on initialization as well as evaluation count; asymmetries arise from emitter pipelines and continuous-relaxation encoding, not from unequal archive seeds.

\subsection{Measurement substrate}
\label{sec:substrate}

Primary contrasts use a controlled black-box evaluator---not a domain contribution. Each candidate is a structured \texttt{WorldSpec}: birth/survival neighbour-count rule tables (18 bits) plus three continuous scalars (\texttt{noise}, \texttt{resource\_regen}, \texttt{predation}). The simulator runs a seeded $50\times50$ toroidal cellular-automaton world for $T_{\mathrm{sim}}{=}200$ steps (early extinction $\Rightarrow$ fitness~0). Behaviour characteristics are stability and diversity in $[0,1]$, so the primary archive is a $50\times50$ niche grid over that plane; insertion fitness is a fixed weighted recompose of diversity, survival, oscillation, and topology proxies (Eq.~\eqref{eq:fitness} in appendix~\S\ref{sec:substrate-appendix}). Genetic emitters flip bits / nudge scalars; LLM emitters edit the same named fields as JSON. The evaluator is cheap relative to CFD-style SAIL domains, so H2 claims evaluation-indexed efficiency and reports wall time separately (\S\ref{sec:cost}). Structured named fields make both soft before-generation and hard after-generation interventions testable---the reason for a synthetic CA primary testbed rather than a pure $\mathbb{R}^d$ literature toy as the sole domain. Table~\ref{tab:domain} lists the knobs; example elites and fitness algebra remain in the appendix. Coverage is occupied-cell percentage after warm-start merge; mean best fitness averages elites over filled cells; QD-score (Eq.~\eqref{eq:qd}) is descriptive only. CVT geometry is appendix sensitivity~\cite{vassiliades2018cvt}. Complementary literature Sphere H2 transfer (threshold gate only; no LLM) is Table~\ref{tab:sphere-h2}.

\section{Experimental protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

Experiments follow \emph{internally frozen} Q1 protocols v2/v3
(primary grid evaluator; freeze date 2026-07-12) and v4 (dungeon factorial;
freeze date 2026-07-17). We use ``confirmatory'' for families locked in
those documents. This submission does \emph{not} claim an external
OSF/Zenodo pre-registration timestamp (decision locked 2026-07-22: keep
in-repo freeze dates). Post-freeze protocol edits are dated in an amendment
log (Table~\ref{tab:amendments}; full logs in the repository protocol files)
and do not silently rewrite locked Holm families. Descriptive tiers and pilot
arms are not pooled into Holm tests.

\subsection{Design principles}

\textbf{Paired seeds.} All primary-grid contrasts use the same integer seeds $0,\ldots,9$ across conditions within a tier. The dungeon and maze factorials use the same seed indices with independent domain RNG.

\textbf{Primary archive and warm-start.} Confirmatory primary-grid endpoints use the \textbf{50$\times$50 grid}. Every primary-grid arm in the batch driver warm-starts from the same baseline JSONL before the matched evaluation budget (\S\ref{sec:pyribs}).

\textbf{Multiple comparisons.} Each confirmatory contrast set uses Holm step-down on one-sided Wilcoxon tests at $\alpha=0.05$ (within set). A set passes only if every test in that set rejects (and local effect-size gates where the frozen protocol requires them). Matched H1 TOST and other pilots sit outside the primary-grid confirmatory sets; amended \textbf{F-RQ3-gray} ($m{=}3$; \S\ref{sec:h3}) is a separate confirmatory family on \texttt{filter\_gray\_zone}, not rehabilitation of locked production \texttt{filter}. Paper-wide Holm over the pooled primary-grid $p$-vector agrees on every claim-driving reject/non-reject (Table~\ref{tab:paper-holm}; details in appendix~\S\ref{sec:stats-appendix}).

\textbf{Power and non-rejection.} Formal paired-$t$ MDE / TOST power is reported for matched-policy H1 ($|\Delta\mathrm{cov}|\le 2$~pp; Table~\ref{tab:decomposition}). Elsewhere, a Holm non-reject is failure to reject. Retrospective MDEs from observed paired SDs appear in appendix~\S\ref{sec:stats-appendix} and do not change locked pass/fail rules. H4's CMA-ME miss is a large reverse effect; locked production H3 remains blocked ($d{=}0.528$), with confirmatory F-RQ3-gray NI pass on \texttt{filter\_gray\_zone} (\S\ref{sec:h3}).

\textbf{Bundled stub vs.\ hints.} The locked \texttt{stub} vs.\ \texttt{hints} contrast differs in surrogate use and in \texttt{target\_selection} (\S\ref{sec:method}). Scientific H1 holds target selection fixed (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}).

\subsection{Endpoints and statistical reporting}
\label{sec:stats}

\textbf{Primary endpoints:} terminal coverage (\%) and mean best elite fitness on the collapsed archive. Confirmatory inference uses paired seeds ($n{=}10$ unless noted), one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, and Holm within each pre-specified contrast set (\S\ref{sec:protocol}). Tables may also report paired probability of superiority $\mathrm{PPS}$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:pps}; appendix~\S\ref{sec:stats-appendix})---seed-wise favorable-pair rate (ties as half), distinct from independent-sample $A_{12}$~\cite{vargha2000new}. Matched-policy H1 uses a post-hoc TOST at $|\Delta\mathrm{cov}|\le 2$~pp (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}). Pilot arms report mean $\Delta$, sign counts, and bootstrap CIs. Surrogate hold-out $R^2$/MAE/NMAE are validity checks, not endpoints (\S\ref{sec:checkpoint}).

\subsection{Multi-LLM replication}

The primary-grid stack uses \texttt{qwen-turbo} on seeds 0--9. We additionally replicate on DeepSeek V4 Pro and OpenAI \texttt{gpt-4o-mini} on seeds 0--9 with identical prompts and schedulers. \textbf{No cross-model pooling.} Each provider is a separate experiment: API decoding is stochastic, tokenizers and context packing differ, and absolute coverage levels are not exchangeable (Table~\ref{tab:g1}). We therefore never average $\Delta$ across LLMs, never run a single Holm correction over pooled provider replicates, and never treat ``mean over models'' as a confirmatory endpoint. Holm is applied \emph{within each provider} at $n{=}10$; models are not merged.

\subsection{Dungeon factorial}

The cross-domain dungeon factorial uses a 16$\times$16 tile dungeon, separate surrogate calibration (\texttt{checkpoint\_benchmark\_v2.pkl}), and five arms at \textbf{5{,}000 proposals} per seed ($n=10$). Quality gates require LLM fallback $\le 5\%$ and filter skip 25--45\%; all passed before seeds 5--9. \textbf{Confirmatory} dungeon endpoints are AUC at matched real evaluations on this 5k matrix. A \textbf{supplementary} CPU pair (\texttt{genetic} vs.\ \texttt{genetic\_filter} only, 32{,}500 proposals, $n=10$) checks H2 at the primary-grid proposal budget; LLM arms at 32{,}500 proposals are deferred to a journal extension (protocol amendment before runs). Protocol v4 §6 notes that coverage-threshold timing metrics were not pre-specified before the first pilot and are descriptive only.

\subsection{Maze factorial (journal track; $n{=}10$)}

A third-domain maze factorial mirrors the dungeon five-arm design at the primary-grid proposal budget (\textbf{32{,}500} proposals/arm; 900-cell behaviour grid; separate maze surrogate and filter calibration $\tau{=}0.685$). Endpoints match the dungeon H5 design: coverage / QD-score AUC at matched real evaluations (Holm $m{=}8$). All five arms complete on seeds $0$--$9$ ($n{=}10$). Quality gates pass (pooled LLM fallback $0.009\%$; genetic\_filter skip $27.3 \pm 0.6\%$).

\subsection{After-generation surrogate (H2)}
\label{sec:h2}

H2 isolates after-generation attachment on matched no-LLM arms: \texttt{genetic\_me\_uniform} vs.\ \texttt{genetic\_me\_filter} ($n=10$; Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition}).

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig06_acquisition_anytime.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{Memory figure (eval-efficiency).} Coverage vs.\ simulator evaluations for \texttt{genetic\_me\_uniform} vs.\ \texttt{genetic\_me\_filter} (median + IQR, $n=10$).}
  \label{fig:acquisition}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Evaluation-indexed} coverage is the primary H2 axis (Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition}). At equal evaluation budgets, filter leads by $+3.65$~pp at 20{,}000 evaluations (10/10 seeds) and reaches 50\%/55\% coverage with 28\%/29\% fewer evaluations. At fixed 650 iterations, filter uses 33.5\% fewer simulator calls, finishes $-0.83$~pp below uniform ($58.21 \pm 1.03\%$ vs.\ $59.04 \pm 0.76\%$), and is slower in wall time ($\sim$20 vs.\ $\sim$7~min). The gain is sample efficiency under a matched proposal schedule.

\textbf{Why MLP can cost more than skipped sims.} Acquisition runs \texttt{predict\_batch} on every proposal, including those later skipped, using a four-member MLP ensemble with MC-dropout ($p{=}0.05$) before the cheap simulator roll-out. Simulator evaluations are process-pooled; surrogate inference is a per-candidate Python path. When $t_{\mathrm{sim}}$ is milliseconds, that path dominates wall. Skipping 33.5\% of sims therefore removes little wall while adding full-batch MLP work.

\textbf{Wall-time break-even (descriptive).} Write wall cost per proposal batch as $(1-s)\,t_{\mathrm{sim}}+t_{\mathrm{MLP}}$ with skip rate $s$. Filter wins on wall only if $t_{\mathrm{MLP}}<s\,t_{\mathrm{sim}}$. On the primary grid ($s{\approx}0.335$), matching the observed wall ratio ($\sim$20 vs.\ $\sim$7~min) implies $t_{\mathrm{MLP}}/t_{\mathrm{sim}}\approx 2.2$, so the simulator would need to be roughly $\mathbf{7\times}$ slower before a 33.5\% skip saves wall clock.

The same inequality, applied to maze micro-timings ($t_{\mathrm{BFS}}{\approx}0.34$~ms, $t_{\mathrm{MLP}}{\approx}0.95$~ms) and writing $t_{\mathrm{sim}}=t_{\mathrm{BFS}}+\delta$ for an injected per-eval delay $\delta$, yields a maze-specific break-even
\[
\delta^{\star}=\frac{t_{\mathrm{MLP}}-s\,t_{\mathrm{BFS}}}{s}.
\]
Under the observed skip rate of this wall study ($s{=}36.5\%\pm1.7\%$ at $2{,}500$ proposals; higher than the $27.3\%$ full-budget maze CPU operating point because early-trajectory proposals are easier for the gate to reject), $\delta^{\star}{\approx}2.3$~ms (${\approx}7.7\times$ bare BFS). A supplementary injected-delay run at $\delta{=}10$~ms ($n{=}10$; same maze domain and checkpoint; fitness labels unchanged) confirms the predicted wall win: genetic $28.3\pm 1.1$~s vs.\ genetic\_filter $22.1\pm 3.6$~s (mean $\Delta{=}{+}6.3$~s; filter faster on $9/10$ seeds; Wilcoxon one-sided $p{=}0.002$). This validates the break-even prediction under an artificially thickened evaluator within the same domain family, rather than demonstrating the effect on a naturally expensive simulator; the package is descriptive and does not amend maze H5 Holm.

\textbf{Literature Sphere transfer (supplementary).} The same after-generation rule---threshold gate plus empty-bin force-eval---on Fontaine linear-projection Sphere ($D{=}20$; MAP-Elites + isotropic Gaussian; no LLM) reproduces the evaluation-indexed pattern (Table~\ref{tab:sphere-h2}): $+7.88$~pp coverage at matched evaluations (10/10) with $\sim$38\% skips and near-parity terminal coverage at fixed proposals. Sphere validates the H2 \emph{mechanism} on a recognized domain; it is not a SAIL CFD replicate and does not test H1.

\subsection{Why the after-generation surrogate wins (mechanism)}
\label{sec:why-filter}

The H2 gain is not a black box. Logged \texttt{surrogate\_archive.jsonl} rows for \texttt{genetic\_me\_filter} ($n{=}10$; $325{,}000$ proposal slots) show what the gate discards (Table~\ref{tab:skip-mech}). Live filter recomposes fitness at extinction gate~$0.95$. All skips cite \texttt{below\_fitness\_threshold} at $\tau{=}0.45$ (skip rate $33.5\%$). Skipped proposals have mean predicted fitness $0.21$ vs.\ $0.61$ for evaluated ones. $81\%$ of skips have predicted early-extinction probability $>0.8$ vs.\ $<0.1\%$ of evaluated proposals. Skipped diversity is low (mean $0.24$); evaluated diversity is high (mean $0.74$). Uncertainty barely separates the classes (medians both $\approx 0.16$). The operative signal is predicted fitness / extinction risk. Random emitters contribute disproportionately to skips ($49\%$ of random slots vs.\ $23\%$ of genetic). Among evaluated proposals, only $12.7\%$ realize true fitness $<0.45$ (pred--true MAE $0.066$).

The after-generation surrogate hard-constrains simulator spend. The before-generation surrogate only soft-advises an LLM under a JSON grammar; H1's flat matched contrast fits advice that is easy to ignore.

\textbf{Threshold sensitivity.} A sweep (Table~\ref{tab:h2-tau-sweep}) varies $\tau\in\{0.35,0.45,0.55\}$ ($n{=}10$ each). Skip rate scales monotonically ($24\%$ / $34\%$ / $51\%$). At $\tau{=}0.45$ filter leads 10/10 seeds at 15k and 20k evaluations. At $\tau{=}0.35$ mid-budget gain is smaller ($+2.5$~pp @15k, 9/10). At $\tau{=}0.55$ it is larger at 15k ($+5.4$~pp, 10/10) but terminal coverage falls further ($56.4\%$, paired $\Delta{=}{-}2.6$~pp). Aggressive gates trade terminal coverage for faster mid-budget fill.

\subsection{H3 --- LLM$+$filter interaction (exploratory)}
\label{sec:h3}

After H1 and H2, H3 asks whether adding the same after-generation gate to the LLM$+$hint stack helps further. On the \textbf{locked production filter}, skip-agreement fails ($d{=}0.528 > 0.05$ at $\tau{=}0.45$); we report those levels only (Table~\ref{tab:h3-prod-levels}).

\textbf{Compose-gate diagnosis.} Hard fitness recompose zeros a candidate when predicted $p_{\mathrm{ext}}\ge$ an extinction gate. Historical combat logs used live gate~$0.95$ while the locked skip-agreement check compared gates $0.5$ vs.\ $0.95$; $74.6\%$ of proposals sit in the gray band $p_{\mathrm{ext}}\in[0.5,0.95)$ (appendix~\S\ref{sec:h3-appendix}). Hold-out validity is aligned to gate~$0.95$ (appendix~\S\ref{sec:checkpoint-appendix}).

\textbf{Gray-zone repair ($n{=}10$, confirmatory F-RQ3-gray).} We ran \texttt{filter\_gray\_zone} (\texttt{force\_eval\_extinction\_gray\_zone}) vs.\ frozen \texttt{q1-full/hints} on seeds~0--9 in tier \texttt{q1-v3-h3-gray-zone} (duplicate of an exploratory pilot on \texttt{q1-v3-h3-gray-zone-pilot}). Skip rate hits the pre-flight band ($11.9 \pm 0.2\%$; target $8$--$18\%$). Paired vs.\ hints: mean $\Delta\text{cov} = -0.60 \pm 1.06$~pp (2/10 wins)---better than locked production filter ($-1.14$~pp; 1/10) but not a coverage win (pilot mean $-0.52$~pp; 3/10). Under amended F-RQ3-gray margins (confirmatory Holm $m{=}3$; Table~\ref{tab:h3-gray-holm}): eval count $\downarrow$ (10/10 seeds; Holm reject), coverage NI ($\Delta > -3$~pp; 10/10 per-seed; Holm reject), fitness NI ($\Delta_{\mathrm{rel}} > -5\%$; Holm reject). \textbf{Interpretation:} the repair validates live mechanism and satisfies NI \emph{margin} tests on a pre-registered duplicate tier, but does not establish a positive LLM$+$filter stack; it does not rehabilitate historical production \texttt{filter} at 33.5\% skip.

Primary after-generation evidence stays H2 (\S\ref{sec:h2}). Dungeon/maze show the same pattern (filter ports; LLM$+$filter does not; \S\ref{sec:h5}).

\subsection{H4 --- Continuous-relaxation pyribs reference (CMA-ME / CMA-MAE)}
\label{sec:h4}

Pyribs baselines (five homogeneous CMA-ES emitters, $n=10$) provide a continuous-relaxation reference under the same warm-start and evaluation budget as other primary-grid arms. CMA searches $\mathbb{R}^{21}$ with \texttt{rint} decode (\S\ref{sec:pyribs}), so this is an upper-bound-style reference for continuous-friendly representations. Mean levels: cma\_me $73.37 \pm 3.65$\%, fitness $0.576 \pm 0.028$; cma\_mae $60.56 \pm 2.16$\%, fitness $0.489 \pm 0.007$; hints $60.41 \pm 0.82$\%, fitness $0.539 \pm 0.008$.

\textbf{Encoding ablation} (seeds~0--4, descriptive): alternate rule-bit \emph{decode} and cold-start CMA-ME leave the continuous-relaxation gap intact. These runs vary how continuous proposals map to rule bits; they do \emph{not} test discrete search in the same space as genetic/LLM emitters. \texttt{threshold} ($\geq 0.5$ bits) is \emph{bit-identical} to frozen \texttt{rint} on every seed; \texttt{bernoulli} decode is $-0.46$~pp mean ($72.18\%$); empty-archive \texttt{cold} is $-3.47$~pp ($69.17\%$)---still $\geq +8.8$~pp above hints on every arm mean.

\textbf{Bernoulli-decode CMA-ME} ($n{=}10$, descriptive). Same warm-start and 32{,}500-evaluation budget; CMA proposes in $\mathbb{R}^{21}$ but rule bits are Bernoulli-sampled at decode (discrete genotypes at evaluation, continuous covariance adaptation unchanged). Mean coverage $71.25 \pm 2.88$\%, fitness $0.578 \pm 0.020$. Paired vs.\ \texttt{hints}: $+10.84 \pm 2.94$~pp (10/10 seeds); vs.\ \texttt{genetic\_me\_uniform}: $+12.22 \pm 2.56$~pp (10/10); vs.\ \texttt{rint} CMA-ME: $-2.12 \pm 4.93$~pp (3/10).

\textbf{Native discrete-search CMA-ME} ($n{=}10$, descriptive). Custom bit-flip emitter on rule bits + Gaussian on floats, discrete $\{0,1\}$ genotype stored in archive, $\sigma$ adapted by a 1/5 success rule (MVP). Mean coverage $65.57 \pm 3.40$\%, fitness $0.542 \pm 0.028$. Paired vs.\ \texttt{hints}: $+5.16 \pm 3.67$~pp (10/10); vs.\ \texttt{genetic\_me\_uniform}: $+6.54 \pm 3.76$~pp (10/10); vs.\ Bernoulli: $-5.68 \pm 5.90$~pp (1/10); vs.\ \texttt{rint}: $-7.80 \pm 5.07$~pp (0/10).

\textbf{pbCMA CMA-ME} ($n{=}10$, descriptive). Latent $(\mu/\mu_w,\lambda)$-CMA on $\mathbb{R}^{21}$ with bit threshold at $0.5$, margin correction, and CMA updates from \emph{latent} ranked parents (discrete $\{0,1\}$ genotype stored in archive). Mean coverage $69.95 \pm 5.42$\%, fitness $0.548 \pm 0.034$. Paired vs.\ \texttt{hints}: $+9.54 \pm 5.25$~pp (10/10); vs.\ \texttt{genetic\_me\_uniform}: $+10.92 \pm 5.80$~pp (10/10); vs.\ native bit-flip: $+4.38 \pm 5.93$~pp (8/10); vs.\ Bernoulli: $-1.30 \pm 6.54$~pp (4/10); vs.\ \texttt{rint}: $-3.42 \pm 7.12$~pp (2/10). Latent covariance adaptation therefore recovers most of the MVP bit-flip deficit vs.\ Bernoulli and nearly half the \texttt{rint} gap, but continuous-relaxation \texttt{rint} still leads on eight seeds---consistent with a modest residual encoding advantage, not ``any CMA-ME pipeline wins.''

Paired hints $-$ cma\_me: $\Delta\text{cov} = -12.96 \pm 3.46$~pp (0/10 positive), $\Delta\text{fit} = -0.077 \pm 0.029$ (0/10); CMA-ME dominates coverage on every seed (largest gap at seed~4: $-20.0$~pp). Paired hints $-$ cma\_mae: $\Delta\text{cov} = -0.15 \pm 2.25$~pp (5/10), $\Delta\text{fit} = +0.010 \pm 0.008$ (10/10) --- near parity on coverage, with a small mean-fitness edge for hints.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Synthesis}

This paper recasts a classical evolution-control question---where a surrogate should intervene relative to expensive evaluation---as an intervention-point decomposition for LLM-guided MAP-Elites. Hard after-generation intervention (H2) improves coverage \emph{per simulation} (Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition}); soft before-generation intervention (H1) stays within $\pm 2$~pp on \texttt{qwen-turbo}. A literature Sphere transfer validates the \emph{H2 mechanism} on a recognized continuous QD domain (Table~\ref{tab:sphere-h2}); it does \emph{not} generalize the flat H1 result, which remains testbed- and model-specific. CMA-ME, multi-provider, and dungeon/maze rows are further evidence kinds for that methodological answer. Magnitudes are testbed-specific (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).

\subsection{Mechanistic reading}
\label{sec:why-discussion}

Results report \emph{what} moved (Fig.~\ref{fig:acquisition}); the skip logs and matched contrasts suggest a candidate \emph{why}.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Budget reallocation.}
 Skip logs (\S\ref{sec:why-filter}) show discarded proposals at low predicted fitness (mean $0.21$ vs.\ $0.61$), high extinction risk ($81\%$ with $p>0.8$), and low diversity. Uncertainty barely separates skip from evaluate. At matched evaluations that discarded mass frees slots ($+3.65$~pp @ 20k); at fixed iterations the same gate finishes $-0.83$~pp.
  \item \textbf{Shared checkpoint, asymmetric use.}
 Offline hold-out at gate~$0.95$ gives $R^2_{\mathrm{fitness}}{=}0.94$ with NMAE$=0.112$ (appendix~\S\ref{sec:checkpoint-appendix})---worse absolute error than the historical gate-$0.5$ NMAE$=0.052$. Live skip logs still separate skip vs.\ evaluate by predicted fitness / extinction (Table~\ref{tab:skip-mech}); false-accept mass among evaluated proposals is $12.7\%$. The same predictor feeds H1 and yields a flat matched contrast, so attach role matters more than headline $R^2$.
  \item \textbf{Soft vs.\ hard channel.}
 Before-generation advises; after-generation constrains spend. Matched H1 stays within $\pm 2$~pp; enriched pilots stay within $|\Delta\mathrm{cov}|<2$~pp. Prompt-side scalars are easy to ignore on this stack.
  \item \textbf{Warm-start context.}
 Confirmatory arms warm-start from the same $\sim$2{,}614-elite baseline. H2 mid-budget gains arise inside an already seeded archive. Whether before-generation advice would matter more from a cold start remains open (\S\ref{sec:bootstrap-hyp}).
  \item \textbf{Threshold as a design knob.}
 Primary H2 uses $\tau{=}0.45$; the sweep at $\{0.35,0.45,0.55\}$ shows mid-budget gains scale with skip rate while aggressive $\tau$ hurts terminal coverage (Table~\ref{tab:h2-tau-sweep}).
  \item \textbf{Prompt numeracy in this stack.}
 Before-generation scalars do not move coverage here. Prompt design, grammar, model choice, and mid-band saturation remain plausible modulators.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Working interpretation.} The same checkpoint yields asymmetric payoffs by attach role. After-generation reallocates simulations by filtering predicted low-fitness / high-extinction / low-diversity proposals. Before-generation leaves those predictions as soft advice and stays flat. The $\tau$ sweep is consistent with a mid-budget effect that is not locked to one gate setting. Magnitudes are testbed-specific.

\subsection{Boundary conditions}

Several contrasts sharpen what the positive H2 reading covers. Matched H1 is flat on \texttt{qwen-turbo} (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}). Multi-provider runs reproduce the bundled $\Delta$ direction. Relative to matched genetic ME, LLM+\texttt{hints} adds only $\approx{+}1.4$~pp at roughly $10\times$ wall time.

Under continuous-relaxation encoding, CMA-ME leads hints by $\approx 13$~pp; Bernoulli-decode by $\sim$11~pp; pbCMA by $\sim$9.5~pp; native bit-flip by $\sim$5~pp. pbCMA closes most of the native$\rightarrow$Bernoulli gap but still trails \texttt{rint} (2/10 seeds). CMA-MAE is near parity on coverage. Stacking the after-generation gate under LLM does not improve fixed-iteration coverage on locked production \texttt{filter} (about $-1$~pp); gray-zone repair narrows but does not reverse the gap (confirmatory mean $-0.60$~pp; pilot $-0.52$~pp; \S\ref{sec:h3}). On dungeon and maze, genetic MAP-Elites leads all LLM arms while after-generation sample-efficiency still ports. On maze the genetic terminal gap is inflated by a 900-cell ceiling; compare matched-eval AUC.

\subsection{Implications for LLM emitters}
\label{sec:when-llm}

If the decision criterion is archive coverage, wall time, or API dollars on this evaluator, an LLM emitter is mostly not the metrics winner: continuous-relaxation CMA-ME leads LLM+\texttt{hints} by $\sim$13~pp (H4); native bit-flip by $\sim$5~pp; matched-policy genetic ME is within $\approx{+}1.4$~pp of hints at roughly $10\times$ lower wall and \$0 API (\S\ref{sec:cost}, Figure~\ref{fig:ladder}). Those facts bound the attach-point story.

The scientific object remains surrogate \emph{intervention} inside loops that already use an LLM as a variation operator (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}). H2's primary arm drops the LLM (\texttt{genetic\_me\_filter}) so after-generation attachment can be isolated on the evaluation axis. Among discrete emitters at matched policy, genetic$+$after-generation wins on that axis (\S\ref{sec:h2}).

A remaining niche for LLM emitters is \emph{structured, human-readable variation} (named-field edits vs.\ anonymous continuous coordinates). A computational edit-anatomy case study on accepted elites in \texttt{q1-full/hints} ($n{=}10$ seeds; resolvable parent in the same archive) makes that claim measurable without a user study (appendix~\S\ref{sec:substrate-appendix}, Figure~\ref{fig:edit-anatomy}): mean rule Hamming is $1.61\pm0.15$ for LLM vs.\ $2.86\pm0.08$ for genetic elites (paired Wilcoxon one-sided $p{=}9.8\times10^{-4}$); $72.7\%$ of LLM rule diffs flip $\le 2$ bits vs.\ $46.8\%$ genetic; mean scalar L1 is about half ($0.050$ vs.\ $0.112$). This is descriptive compactness of named-field edits---not human preference and not a QD win. Dungeon/maze reverse the LLM vs.\ genetic coverage ranking. Practical reading: use an LLM when editable structured genotypes are a product requirement; otherwise prefer genetic$+$after-generation (or a CMA-style baseline) for archive metrics, and attach any shared surrogate primarily after generation if an LLM is retained.

\subsection{Hypothesis: stage-dependent integration}
\label{sec:bootstrap-hyp}

The fixed-slot decomposition motivates an \emph{untested} scheduling hypothesis---a consequence of the where-question, not a co-equal claim of this paper:

\begin{quote}
\textit{The before-generation surrogate is most useful during exploration / archive bootstrapping; the after-generation surrogate remains beneficial across the optimization trajectory on the evaluation axis.}
\end{quote}

Evidence consistent with that reading (not a test of it):

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{After-generation across the trajectory.}
 On H2 anytime curves (Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition}), \texttt{genetic\_me\_filter} leads \texttt{genetic\_me\_uniform} from early through mid budgets (about $+1.4$~pp @ 5k evaluations; $+3.0$ @ 10k; peak $\sim$+$3.7$ @ 15k--20k; 10/10 seeds), while skip rates stay high in early/mid/late iteration terciles ($\sim$37\% / 33\% / 31\%). That pattern suggests a mid-trajectory sample-efficiency advantage rather than a cold-start-only effect; by the full 32{,}500-evaluation endpoint the filter is slightly behind on coverage ($-0.8$~pp).
  \item \textbf{Before-generation early.}
 Terminal matched-policy H1 is flat within $\pm 2$~pp ($n{=}10$), which is compatible with either little prompt-side value overall or value concentrated in a phase we did not instrument. Soft-vs-hard channel asymmetry (\S\ref{sec:why-discussion}) and the usual MAP-Elites bootstrap/refine split make an early-only reading plausible. Frozen LLM arms lack \texttt{archive\_trace.jsonl}, so an early lift---if present---is not yet localized. Terminal elite histograms show low-fitness mass in filled niches, but as static snapshots they cannot date niche discovery.
  \item \textbf{Emitter scheduling.}
 Terminal mid-band parity (genetic ME $\approx$ LLM+\texttt{hints}) also invites LLM-for-bootstrap / genetics-for-refinement schedules; equally, genetics may simply catch up by iteration~650, or LLM value may be BC-region-specific.
\end{itemize}

Stage-dependent integration is a hypothesis motivated by the observed decomposition, not a second research question of this paper. Our fixed attach-role contrasts ask \emph{where} the surrogate should attach; they naturally suggest a future question: \emph{when} should each attach role be active? Follow-ons include anytime first-fill curves for hints vs.\ stub\_uniform, filter on/off by phase, and LLM for the first $K$ iterations then genetics.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\subsubsection{Threats to validity}

Primary evidence uses a cheap evaluator where MLP inference dominates wall time, so H2's evaluation-indexed gain is not a wall-clock win on the unmodified primary or maze BFS evaluators (\S\ref{sec:cost}). Wall-clock transfer above the predicted break-even is now empirically validated on maze via injected delay (\S\ref{sec:h2}); transfer to a naturally expensive simulator remains untested. The pyribs coverage contrast searches a continuous relaxation of a mixed discrete/continuous genome; decode ablations still propose in $\mathbb{R}^{21}$ before rounding, and the CMA comparison is a pipeline reference rather than a factorial emitter decomposition (\S\ref{sec:h4}). Matched evaluation budget is not matched dollar cost.

H3 is \textbf{exploratory on locked production filter} and \textbf{confirmatory on gray-zone repair}: the locked production filter fails compose-gate skip agreement ($d{=}0.528$), but offline replay localizes the mechanism and documents corrected hold-out validity after aligning the extinction gate to production~$0.95$ (NMAE $0.052\!\rightarrow\!0.112$; appendix~\S\ref{sec:checkpoint-appendix}). Confirmatory \texttt{filter\_gray\_zone} ($n{=}10$; tier \texttt{q1-v3-h3-gray-zone}) validates the repair mechanism (skip $\sim$11.9\%) and passes F-RQ3-gray NI margins (Table~\ref{tab:h3-gray-holm}), but mean coverage remains $-0.60$~pp vs.\ hints (2/10 wins)---not a stack win and not rehabilitation of production \texttt{filter}. An exploratory pilot on the same policy replicated directionally ($-0.52$~pp; 3/10 wins). Live skip logs always used gate~$0.95$ (Table~\ref{tab:skip-mech}).

LLM endpoints are window-pinned with \texttt{llm\_spec\_hash}, but floating aliases limit bit-exact replay; genetic ME and pyribs CMA remain locally re-runnable. Holm is within locked contrast sets; paper-wide Holm agrees on claim-driving rows (Table~\ref{tab:paper-holm}). Only matched H1 carries a formal TOST/MDE. Other non-rejects are fail-to-reject, not equivalence claims: dungeon and maze hints$-$stub and LLM$+$filter are small or wrong-direction; H4's CMA-ME miss is a large reverse effect. Maze five-arm Holm $m{=}8$ is complete at $n{=}10$ but remains \textbf{partial} (filter AUC Holm-rejects; family does not pass).

\subsubsection{Generalizability}

Primary contrasts use a controlled structured-genotype evaluator (\S\ref{sec:substrate}) chosen so that both soft prompt-side and hard after-generation interventions are definable. Dungeon and maze are further discrete PCG transfer domains; magnitudes ($+3.7$~pp, $33.5\%$ skips) remain testbed-specific. We do not claim a universal ranking on expensive continuous genomes or CFD-style SAIL domains.

To check whether the \emph{after-generation} mechanism depends on the custom CA, a supplementary Fontaine linear-projection Sphere run applies the same threshold-gate rule (with empty-bin force-eval) on anonymous $\mathbb{R}^{20}$ genotypes ($n{=}10$; 32{,}500 proposals): filter skips $37.9\%\pm 2.7\%$ of proposals and leads by $+7.88$~pp coverage at matched evaluations (10/10; Wilcoxon one-sided $p{=}9.8\times10^{-4}$; Table~\ref{tab:sphere-h2}). Terminal coverage at fixed proposals stays near parity ($+0.18$~pp). \textbf{What Sphere validates:} H2-style evaluation gating on a recognized continuous QD benchmark. \textbf{What it does not:} H1 (no named fields for prompt scalars), LLM emitters, or CFD-scale absolute numbers---H1 remains testbed- and model-specific (primary CA / \texttt{qwen-turbo}). That package is descriptive / supplementary, not a confirmatory Holm family. Repository CMA Sphere/Rastrigin tiers remain pyribs-runner sanity checks. The maze injected-delay wall study (\S\ref{sec:h2}) is a separate controlled validation of the break-even formula, not further Sphere evidence.

Dungeon and maze transfer filter AUC at calibrated skip-band $\tau$, not bundled LLM QD lift; maze genetic arms also hit a 900-cell ceiling. Secondary-domain $\tau$ was set by shadow replay, not by repeating the primary online sweep (Table~\ref{tab:h2-tau-sweep}). Multi-provider rows replicate the bundled $\Delta$ direction at $n{=}10$; absolute coverage still differs by API (matched H1 remains \texttt{qwen-turbo}).

\subsubsection{Scope of this paper}

Hypotheses and analysis rules are locked in dated repository protocol files (primary grid: 2026-07-12; dungeon: 2026-07-17; Table~\ref{tab:amendments}), not a public OSF/Zenodo pre-registration. This draft reports frozen primary-grid sets, locked dungeon/maze H5 (partial), supplementary Sphere H2 (Table~\ref{tab:sphere-h2}), cost-scaled maze wall H2 ($n{=}10$), supplementary CPU H2 pairs (Table~\ref{tab:h2-cpu-full}), confirmatory gray-zone F-RQ3-gray ($n{=}10$; \S\ref{sec:h3}), and matched H1 multi-provider ($n{=}10$; Table~\ref{tab:h1-matched-providers}). Still open: full-budget LLM dungeon, dungeon/maze $\tau$ ablations, and DSA-ME-style deckbuilding H1+H2 (Future work).
\subsection{Future work}

What remains open is less about new LLMs or domains than about mechanism timing (\S\ref{sec:why-discussion}): (1)~\textbf{phase analysis}---extend anytime curves to genetic ME and mid-trajectory H2 vs.\ hints; (2)~\textbf{prompt micro-ablation} (drop uncertainty / predicted fitness / archive stats) to refine the soft-channel reading of H1. H2 threshold sensitivity at $\tau\in\{0.35,0.45,0.55\}$ ($n{=}10$) is reported in Table~\ref{tab:h2-tau-sweep}; a wider $\tau$ grid remains optional.

The fixed attach-role contrasts (Table~\ref{tab:role-exp}) leave open a follow-on question: when should before- vs.\ after-generation be active, and when to call LLM vs.\ genetic emitters (\S\ref{sec:bootstrap-hyp})? That is future work, not a claim of this paper. Planned journal extensions: (i)~a recognized LLM+QD domain (DSA-ME-style deckbuilding~\cite{grillotti2022dsame}) to test whether the flat H1 result and positive H2 result replicate under a structured discrete genome with an established after-generation gate baseline; (ii)~full-budget LLM dungeon and dungeon/maze $\tau$ ablations; (iii)~optional expert preference pilot on the same parent$\rightarrow$child pairs used in the edit-anatomy case study (\S\ref{sec:edit-anatomy}). An external OSF/Zenodo timestamp remains optional.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Previous work often compared QD pipelines; we compared surrogate intervention points, extending the classical evolution-control perspective to an LLM-guided MAP-Elites instance. After-generation intervention (H2) yields $\sim$3.7~pp mid-budget coverage per simulation and 33.5\% fewer sims at fixed iterations; at the same schedule it finishes $-0.83$~pp and is slower in wall time on this evaluator. Matched before-generation intervention (H1) stays within $\pm 2$~pp on \texttt{qwen-turbo} (TOST). Genetic ME sits within about $1.4$~pp of LLM+\texttt{hints}; continuous-relaxation CMA-ME leads by $\sim$13~pp (\S\ref{sec:h4}). Dungeon H5 is partial (Table~\ref{tab:families}): genetic\_filter$-$genetic AUC passes Holm; hints$-$stub and LLM$+$filter do not. We release an internally frozen stack (tag~\texttt{\SubmissionGitTag} / commit~\texttt{\SubmissionGitShort}). Scope: \S\ref{sec:limitations}.

\section{Statistical details}
\label{sec:stats-appendix}

Main-text reporting uses paired Wilcoxon tests and within-set Holm (\S\ref{sec:stats}). This appendix records effect-size conventions and the paper-wide Holm check.

\paragraph{Multiple-comparison policy.}
Primary decisions use Holm within each pre-specified confirmatory contrast set ($\alpha{=}0.05$). Sets are protocol-locked before analysis (bundled stack; H4; H5 dungeon AUC; per-provider replications; F-RQ3-gray on \texttt{filter\_gray\_zone}); matched H1 TOST sits outside primary-grid confirmatory sets. As a robustness check, Table~\ref{tab:paper-holm} applies one paper-wide Holm step-down to the pooled primary-grid raw $p$-vector ($m{=}20$): \textbf{11/20} reject, and every within-set reject/non-reject that drives a pass/fail claim is unchanged. Primary outcomes remain Table~\ref{tab:families}.

\paragraph{Paired probability of superiority ($\mathrm{PPS}$).}
Classic Vargha--Delaney $A_{12}$ is defined for independent samples~\cite{vargha2000new}. On paired seeds we report the paired probability of superiority
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{PPS} \;=\; \frac{\#\{\Delta_i > 0\} + 0.5\,\#\{\Delta_i = 0\}}{n},
  \label{eq:pps}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_i$ is treatment $-$ control on seed $i$ (for ``less,'' count $\Delta_i < 0$). This is the paired common-language / proportion-of-favorable-pairs reading of stochastic superiority~\cite{mcgraw1992cl,kerby2014simple}---not $A_{12}$ and not a propensity score. $\mathrm{PPS}{=}0.5$ is chance under no systematic paired advantage, and (with ties handled as half) $\mathrm{PPS}$ coincides with the Sign column rate when there are no ties. We use $\mathrm{PPS}$ only as a descriptive companion next to Wilcoxon tests. We do \emph{not} import independent-sample $A_{12}$ magnitude cutoffs (e.g.\ 0.56/0.64/0.71), and $\mathrm{PPS}$ is \emph{not} a confirmatory gate (Holm / protocol $d_z$ decide pass/fail). Tables that show $\mathrm{PPS}$ state this in the caption.

\paragraph{H1 TOST and MDE; retrospective MDEs elsewhere.}
Matched-policy H1 uses a post-hoc TOST equivalence check at $|\Delta\mathrm{cov}|\le 2$~pp (outside confirmatory Holm sets). Approximate minimum detectable effect for a paired $t$-test at $n$ seeds, two-sided $\alpha{=}0.05$, 80\% power is
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{MDE} \;=\; \bigl(t_{1-\alpha/2,\,n-1} + t_{0.80,\,n-1}\bigr)\,\frac{s_{\Delta}}{\sqrt{n}},
  \label{eq:mde}
\end{equation}
with $s_{\Delta}$ the observed paired-difference SD (descriptive when used retrospectively). For H1, $\mathrm{MDE}{\approx}1.2$~pp at $n{=}10$; accept/reject and CIs appear in \S\ref{sec:decomposition}. Elsewhere we quote only the resulting MDE numbers under the same convention (e.g.\ dungeon hints$-$stub coverage AUC ${\approx}1.2$~pp at $n{=}10$; maze genetic\_filter coverage AUC ${\approx}2.1$~pp at $n{=}10$ before Holm $m{=}8$)---not a second formula statement. These bound what $n$ can detect; they do not re-open locked Holm families or convert non-rejects into equivalence claims.

\paragraph{Other descriptive metrics.}
QD-score (Eq.~\eqref{eq:qd}), evaluation count, wall-clock time, LLM fallback rate, anytime curves, and bootstrap 95\% CIs on median $\Delta$ are reported for interpretation. QD-score and eval-to-threshold curves are \emph{not} used for confirmatory rejection. Hold-out surrogate CIs use $B{=}10{,}000$ row resamples; cross-gate comparisons prefer NMAE when the fitness target distribution changes (appendix~\S\ref{sec:checkpoint-appendix}).

\section{Shared-checkpoint validity (hold-out algebra)}
\label{sec:checkpoint-appendix}
\label{sec:checkpoint}

Main text cites only the runtime-aligned headline ($R^2{=}0.94$, NMAE$=0.112$). This appendix records why $R^2$ and MAE can move in opposite directions when the compose gate changes.

Offline training on the nightly buffer ($N=35{,}114$ rows; schema~2.1, 24-D features; hold-out $n{=}7{,}023$) yields hold-out metrics for checkpoint \texttt{nightly\_v3\_mc\_d005.pkl}. Protocol amendment 2026-07-28 aligns the offline compose gate with runtime YAML ($0.95$): $R^2_{\text{fitness}}=0.94$ (bootstrap 95\% CI $[0.94, 0.95]$), MAE$_{\text{fitness}}=0.029$ ($[0.027, 0.030]$), NMAE$=\mathrm{MAE}/\mathrm{std}(y)=0.112$ ($[0.107, 0.117]$), MAE$_{\text{stability}}=0.040$, passing \texttt{hints\_ok}.

At gate~$0.5$, $96.2\%$ of hold-out labels are exactly zero (std$(y){=}0.133$); at gate~$0.95$, only $29.0\%$ are zero and the continuous mass spreads (std$(y){=}0.255$, $\times 1.92$). Because $R^2{=}1{-}\mathrm{SS}_{\mathrm{res}}/\mathrm{SS}_{\mathrm{tot}}$, larger target variance inflates $R^2$ even as absolute MAE grows ($\times 4.12$). Scale-normalized error worsens (NMAE $0.052\!\rightarrow\!0.112$). Therefore $R^2{=}0.94$ is \emph{not} a model-quality upgrade over the historical $R^2{=}0.76$ headline---it is the correct metric for the runtime target (artifact: \texttt{holdout\_compose\_gate\_alignment.json}). The misalignment affected only the offline validity metric; live filter logs always used gate~$0.95$. Hold-out metrics do not replace simulator endpoints for H1/H2/H3.

\section{MLP vs.\ GP/UCB ablation (offline)}
\label{sec:surrogate-ablation-appendix}

Production H2 uses an MLP ensemble with MC-dropout and a fitness threshold ($\texttt{min\_fit}{=}0.45$, $\texttt{max\_u}{=}1.0$), not SAIL-style GP+UCB. On the nightly buffer hold-out at runtime-aligned compose gate~$0.95$, the production MLP achieves $R^2{=}0.94$ (NMAE$=0.112$) vs.\ $0.89$ (NMAE$=0.222$) for a scikit-learn GP fitness regressor trained on 5{,}000 rows under the \emph{same} label definition, and is ${\sim}37\times$ faster at a 32{,}500-proposal batch. An earlier M1 snapshot at misaligned gate~$0.5$ reported MLP $R^2{=}0.76$ vs.\ GP ${\approx}0.22$; that gap is not reusable as a model-quality argument because ${\approx}96\%$ of gate-$0.5$ labels are zero (\S\ref{sec:checkpoint-appendix}).

Offline acquisition replay: replacing the MLP with the GP under the \emph{same} threshold gate worsens the false-skip proxy ($0.7\%\!\rightarrow\!3.2\%$); a UCB skip rule on GP scores ($\mu{+}\beta\sigma < \texttt{min\_fit}$, $\beta\in\{0.15,0.5,1.0\}$) stays near $99\%$ skip. On logged Q1 filter proposals ($n{=}325{,}000$), replaying UCB on the \emph{same} MLP $\mu/\sigma$ only softens the gate (skip $33.5\%\!\rightarrow\!28.5\%/25.7\%/21.2\%$) and never adds skips. With $\texttt{max\_u}{=}1.0$, uncertainty is not load-bearing for the production threshold gate. GP+UCB is therefore a related SAIL lineage, not a competitive drop-in (artifact: \texttt{artifacts/surrogate/gp\_ucb\_ablation.json}).

\section{Primary evaluator details}
\label{sec:substrate-appendix}

Main-text \S\ref{sec:substrate} sketches genome, simulator, and behaviour axes. This appendix records fitness composition algebra and a computational edit-anatomy case study (LLM vs.\ genetic parent$\rightarrow$child diffs)---not domain claims.

\paragraph{Genome and evaluation.}
Emitters search a 21-D encoding (18 birth/survival rule bits + noise / resource\_regen / predation). Each evaluation runs a fixed $50\times50$, $T_{\mathrm{sim}}{=}200$ toroidal grid; a canonical SHA-256 seed of the genome makes fitness/BC a deterministic function of the genotype. Early extinction yields archive fitness~0.

\paragraph{Behaviour and fitness.}
Archive axes are stability and diversity in $[0,1]$. Authoritative insertion fitness recomposes diversity, survival, oscillation, and topology proxies. All fitness components below are dimensionless scores already scaled to $[0,1]$ before weighting (topology indices TI/TH are likewise clipped into $[0,1]$ before averaging):
\begin{equation}
  E_{\mathrm{ext}} = \mathrm{clip}(1 - \rho_{\mathrm{final}}, 0, 1), \quad
  T_{\mathrm{topo}} = \mathrm{clip}\!\left(\tfrac{\text{TI} + \text{TH}}{2}, 0, 1\right)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:fitness}
  \text{fitness} = \mathrm{clip}\bigl(
    0.45\,D + 0.25\,(1 - E_{\mathrm{ext}}) + 0.20\,A + 0.10\,T_{\mathrm{topo}}
  ,\; 0,\; 1\bigr)
\end{equation}
where $D\in[0,1]$ is clipped diversity, $A\in[0,1]$ is \texttt{oscillation\_score}, and $T_{\mathrm{topo}}\in[0,1]$ is the topology score (distinct from the simulator horizon $T_{\mathrm{sim}}{=}200$ above). The outer $\mathrm{clip}(\cdot,0,1)$ is defensive. The surrogate recomposes the same formula from predicted components (\S\ref{sec:surrogate-method}). QD-score is
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{QD} \;=\; \sum_{c \in \mathrm{filled}} f_c \;=\; |\mathrm{filled}| \cdot \bar f.
  \label{eq:qd}
\end{equation}

\section{CVT archive sensitivity}
\label{sec:cvt-sensitivity}

The scientific object of this paper is the attachment-point split (H1 / H2) on the confirmatory $50\times50$ grid (with H3 only as a descriptive LLM-stack check), not archive geometry. A CVT sensitivity run (\texttt{q1-cvt}, $n{=}10$) only checks that the \emph{bundled} stub/hints/filter directions are not grid-specific (Table~\ref{tab:cvt}): H1-s concordance 10/10; H3-s evaluation reduction $\sim$31\% on CVT vs.\ 33.5\% on grid. Absolute $\Delta$coverage is larger on CVT mainly because the stub baseline is lower ($\sim$37\% vs.\ $\sim$45\%). These runs are descriptive and outside confirmatory Holm contrast sets; they do not alter the H1 flat matched contrast or H2 eval-axis claims.
