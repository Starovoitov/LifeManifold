\title{Two Roles for Surrogates in {LLM}-Guided {MAP}-{E}lites: Disentangling Prompt Hints from Acquisition Filtering}

\author{Artem Starovoitov\corref{cor1}}
\ead{artem.starovoitov@proton.me}
\ead[url]{https://orcid.org/0009-0005-3170-4865}
\address{Independent Engineer, Moscow, Russia}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author}

\begin{abstract}
In LLM-guided quality-diversity pipelines, a surrogate can enter the propose$\rightarrow$evaluate loop at two structurally distinct times: \emph{before generation} (semantic integration: shape what the LLM proposes) and \emph{after generation} (evaluation integration: shape which proposals spend simulator budget). We do not invent those slots---they follow from the loop---but we \emph{make them explicit} and \emph{measure} their relative payoff under one shared checkpoint (Figure~1). \textbf{Takeaway:} empirical evidence suggests the algorithmic after-generation gate (Role~2: matched genetic filter anytime curves, Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition} in Results) is the main positive payoff, while matched-policy before-generation prompt guidance (RQ1) shows no large lift and stacking both under LLM (RQ3) is not a simple sum. Evidence comes from a controlled LifeManifold testbed (cheap CA; $50\times50$ archive; internally frozen protocol): a locked bundled contrast that mixed slots with target selection looked supportive ($\approx{+}15$ percentage points, pp), but at matched policy semantic integration is \emph{descriptively flat} ($+0.26$~pp on $n{=}10$ paired seeds; exploratory Wilcoxon $p{=}0.41$; not a confirmatory null) while filtering without an LLM improves the evaluation axis ($\sim$3.7~pp at 15k--20k evaluations; 33.5\% fewer simulations; Role~2 filter AUC also passes on a second dungeon domain at 5k proposals, with supplementary CPU confirmation at 32.5k). Combining both slots under LLM is not a simple sum; we do \emph{not} claim cross-domain LLM QD lift. Magnitudes are testbed-specific; the before/after taxonomy is meant to travel even when the magnitudes do not.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Quality-diversity (QD) algorithms maintain archives of solutions that are strong \emph{and} diverse in behaviour space. MAP-Elites and its descendants assign each candidate to a behavioural niche and keep the best elite per cell, enabling illumination of a simulator rather than a single optimum~\cite{mouret2015illuminating}. In practice, every candidate still requires a black-box evaluation---a full simulator roll-out for each proposed world, controller, or design. When that roll-out is costly (CFD, game engines, robotics), evaluation count dominates wall time; surrogate-assisted illumination is motivated for that regime~\cite{gaier2018sail}. Our confirmatory benchmark is a \emph{cheap} CA, so we treat evaluation-indexed efficiency as the primary filter claim and report wall time separately (\S\ref{sec:results}).

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used as variation operators inside quality-diversity loops: they read structured genotypes or prompts and emit semantically meaningful mutations. An \textbf{LLM-guided QD pipeline} therefore couples (i)~an LLM (or hybrid) emitter that proposes candidates, (ii)~a black-box evaluator that buys ground truth, and (iii)~an archive that keeps elites. The LLM does \emph{not} observe simulator outcomes unless the loop explicitly feeds feedback into the prompt; surrogates are a natural way to supply that feedback---either as prompt text or as a gate on expensive evaluations. Surrogate-assisted illumination (SAIL) historically emphasized the latter~\cite{gaier2018sail}; prompt-facing uses of predictors are common in LLM search but rarely compared to skip-eval gating inside one architecture.

\textbf{Positioning.} The object of this paper is the design of LLM-guided QD pipelines: when should surrogate information enter the propose$\rightarrow$evaluate loop? LifeManifold is the controlled testbed in which we measure payoffs---not the scientific subject. The before/after split itself is not a new invention; the contribution is to \emph{name the slots, hold the checkpoint fixed, and compare payoffs}.

\subsection{Formalizing before/after attach times (not inventing them)}

\begin{quote}
\textit{Surrogate information can be injected before generation or after generation; those times are not interchangeable---and that claim needs a payoff test, not a new brand name.}
\end{quote}

A MAP-Elites (or any QD) iteration is a short causal chain: select parents $\rightarrow$ \textbf{generate} a candidate $\rightarrow$ decide whether to \textbf{evaluate} it on the simulator $\rightarrow$ update the archive. Surrogate knowledge can intervene at essentially two moments on that chain (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}):

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Before generation} --- \emph{semantic integration}: predictions enter the emitter (here, LLM prompt hints) and try to change \emph{what} is proposed.
  \item \textbf{After generation} --- \emph{evaluation integration}: predictions gate the candidate (here, acquisition filtering) and change \emph{whether} it spends ground truth.
\end{itemize}

Those slots are forced by propose$\rightarrow$evaluate---Role~2 is SAIL-\emph{kin} as evaluation gating; the before-generation (RQ1) path is prompt-facing numerology that never skips simulation. We do \emph{not} claim to invent either mechanism. What is missing from prior work, and what this paper supplies, is a \textbf{matched within-architecture comparison}: same checkpoint, same archive/budget/emitters, before vs.\ after vs.\ both. Heuristics (threshold gates, prompt templates, uncertainty calibration) are implementations \emph{inside} a slot. Surrogate-informed parent selection would be a third, earlier slot; we do not implement it. Archive \texttt{target\_selection} remains ordinary MAP-Elites policy (and a confound in the bundled stub vs.\ hints contrast).

What an empirical study can test is therefore not the existence of the slots---that is architectural---but their \textbf{relative payoff}: does before-generation advice move coverage, and does after-generation gating improve sample efficiency? In our testbed, the comparison suggests after $\gg$ before on the evaluation axis (Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition} vs.\ \S\ref{sec:decomposition}): matched-policy RQ1 is \emph{descriptively flat} at $n{=}10$ (exploratory; not a confirmatory Holm family), while Role~2 improves sample efficiency. The memory figure for that Role~2 claim is the matched \texttt{genetic\_me\_uniform} vs.\ \texttt{genetic\_me\_filter} anytime plot (Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition}); we defer it until the Role~2 results section, after the RQ1 semantic control. A normative slogan for LLM$+$QD design follows as a hypothesis, not a theorem: integrate surrogate knowledge differently when the need is semantic direction vs.\ evaluation efficiency. Stage-dependent scheduling of the two slots remains a conjecture (\S\ref{sec:bootstrap-hyp}).

\begin{enumerate}

\section{Background and related work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Quality-diversity and MAP-Elites}

Quality-diversity (QD) algorithms seek collections of high-performing yet behaviourally diverse solutions rather than a single optimum~\cite{pugh2016quality,cully2018quality,chatzilygeroudis2021qd}. MAP-Elites maintains an archive partitioned by user-defined behavioural characteristics (BC) and retains the best elite per niche~\cite{mouret2015illuminating}. Heterogeneous emitter sets can improve illumination efficiency relative to a single variation operator~\cite{cully2020multiemitter}; our LifeManifold stack mixes random, genetic, and LLM slots in one batch. Confirmatory archives are a fixed $50\times50$ grid; CVT geometry is appendix sensitivity only~\cite{vassiliades2018cvt}.

Continuous variation operators remain important on fixed-length genomes. Covariance Matrix Adaptation MAP-Elites (CMA-ME) couples CMA-ES adaptation with MAP-Elites archiving and often dominates random or isotropic mutation on continuous control and design tasks~\cite{fontaine2020cmame}. CMA-MAE extends the idea with MAP-annealing and separate result archives, trading coverage for per-niche quality~\cite{fontaine2023cmamae,zhao2024cmamae}. We implement both through \texttt{ribs} v0.11.0~\cite{pyribs2020} with an adapter that evaluates candidates in the LifeManifold simulator (\S\ref{sec:method}). Our pyribs baselines use homogeneous CMA-ES emitter pools rather than mixed pyribs ``cocktails,'' so they represent strong but not maximal pyribs configurations.

\subsection{Surrogate-assisted illumination}

Expensive simulators motivate surrogate models that predict outcomes from static design features~\cite{jin2005fitness,jones1998ego}.
Surrogate-Assisted Illumination (SAIL) is the canonical QD answer: fit a Gaussian process over the behaviour--fitness landscape, run MAP-Elites on an acquisition surface (classically UCB-style), and spend real evaluations only on a selected subset~\cite{gaier2018sail,gaier2017sail,gaier2017aiaa}.
Deep Surrogate Assisted MAP-Elites (DSA-ME) replaces the GP with neural predictors trained online in an inner loop and validates candidates in an outer game-engine loop~\cite{grillotti2022dsame}.
Related Bayesian QD lines couple acquisition to descriptor and fitness models~\cite{kent2025boqd}.
Discount Model Search continues the surrogate-QD line without LLMs, replacing histogram discounts with a learned continuous discount model for high-dimensional measure spaces~\cite{tjanaka2026dms}.
Across this family the surrogate is a \emph{search accelerator}: it can change which candidates receive ground truth, and often \emph{is} the surface being illuminated rather than a side channel into an otherwise unchanged MAP-Elites loop.

Reported evaluation savings are strongest in computational design.
On two-dimensional airfoil tasks SAIL needs orders-of-magnitude fewer flow simulations than MAP-Elites while filling behaviour bins~\cite{gaier2018sail}; with the budget CMA-ES requires to optimize one bin, SAIL can match that quality in every bin~\cite{gaier2017sail}.
Three-dimensional aerodynamics and high-dimensional discrete deckbuilding show the same qualitative pattern~\cite{gaier2017aiaa,grillotti2022dsame}.
Evidence for stochastic simulators is thinner: these studies assume expensive but largely deterministic evaluators or batched outer validation, not single-rollout archive updates under seeded CA noise.
Our confirmatory tier holds the simulator budget fixed (32{,}500 evaluations per seed) and asks, for LLM-guided QD, \emph{where} the same surrogate should attach under full-simulation archive updates---not whether a new SAIL-style illuminator brand wins on expensive CFD.

\textbf{Positioning vs.\ SAIL / DSA-ME.}
Our stack differs from those lineages on four points that matter for the before/after framework.
(i)~\emph{Model:} a multi-task MLP ensemble with MC-dropout over genome features (optional LightGBM path), not a monolithic GP fitness surface; an offline hold-out ablation prefers the production MLP over a sklearn GP on composed fitness ($R^2{\approx}0.76$ vs.\ ${\approx}0.22$) and rejects GP+UCB as a drop-in acquisition upgrade (\S\ref{sec:acquisition}).
(ii)~\emph{Attach points:} in the before-generation (hint) path the surrogate \textbf{never skips} \texttt{evaluate\_candidate}---it only supplies scalars to the LLM prompt---while the after-generation path optionally gates acquisition with a production \texttt{threshold\_gate} ($\texttt{min\_fit}{=}0.45$, $\texttt{max\_u}{=}1.0$), not SAIL's GP-UCB illuminator.
(iii)~\emph{Truth vs.\ guidance:} prompt fitness is recomposed from predicted components with the same closed form as the simulator (\S\ref{eq:fitness}), but archive insertion always uses simulated metrics; DSA-ME's online inner/outer split is closer to Role~2 gating than to RQ1 prompt hints, yet still does not inject surrogate text into an LLM emitter.
(iv)~\emph{Disclosure:} we report an explicit compose-gate mismatch (D1) between offline and online extinction thresholds that blocks confirmatory LLM+filter non-inferiority---honesty that SAIL-style papers rarely need when fitness definitions stay matched.
We therefore treat SAIL/DSA-ME as related surrogate-QD lineages rather than drop-in baselines; pyribs CMA-ME/CMA-MAE (\S\ref{sec:pyribs}) are the quantitative QD baselines for this protocol.

\subsection{LLMs in evolutionary and QD search}

LLMs have been used as variation operators, prompt optimizers, and evolutionary engines~\cite{wu2025ecllm,wang2025llmea,vanstein2024llamea,guo2024evoprompt,liu2024llmeo,lange2024llmes}. Program-search lines pair a frozen LLM with an external evaluator to evolve code or rewards (FunSearch; Eureka)~\cite{romera2024funsearch,ma2024eureka}; Promptbreeder evolves task and mutation prompts self-referentially~\cite{fernando2024promptbreeder}; OpenELM surveys open-ended LLM evolution~\cite{lehman2023openelm}. Closest to our QD setting, LLMatic couples LLM code generation with CVT-MAP-Elites for neural architecture search~\cite{nasir2024llmatic}, and in-context QD generators sample diverse solutions from LLM priors~\cite{lim2024llmqd}. Behaviour-space analyses of LLaMEA further compare dual-prompt mutation strategies on anytime search dynamics~\cite{vanstein2025behaviour}. Concurrent LLM+MAP-Elites applications include D{\'e}j{\`a}Q's learnability-guided curation of RL-with-verifiable-rewards datasets~\cite{ropke2026dejaq} and medical decision-pipeline evolution without a surrogate/gating layer~\cite{sviridov2026medical}---both use LLM variation in a QD loop, but neither treat before- vs.\ after-generation surrogate attach time as an experimental factor. Procedural-content pipelines apply evolutionary search and LLMs to level generators and structured genotypes~\cite{togelius2010sbpcg,earle2022nca}; emitters must respect simulators that validate and repair invalid outputs (as in our dungeon domain, \S\ref{sec:results}). Open-ended CA worlds have likewise been illuminated with QD archives~\cite{faldor2024lenia}.

A complementary line treats the \emph{prompt itself} as the search space: Diverse Prompts applies MAP-Elites over prompt phenotypes for BigBench Lite tasks~\cite{santos2025diverseprompts}, evolutionary sampling explores instruction diversity more broadly~\cite{saletta2024promptspace}, and parameter-efficient neuroevolution steers frozen LLMs by evolving prompt embeddings rather than using the LLM as a genotype emitter~\cite{guo2026qdllm}. Those settings illuminate prompts (or embeddings) for a fixed downstream LLM call, whereas we mutate \texttt{WorldSpec} genomes and inject simulator-derived \emph{surrogate scalars} into the mutation prompt. A different surrogate use keeps the LLM on fitness estimation: LLM-assisted evolutionary algorithms (LAEA) and related LLM-driven SAEAs prompt a model to classify or regress candidate quality~\cite{hao2024laea,xie2025llmsae}. We instead pair a genome-aligned ML regressor (trained on simulator logs) with an LLM \emph{emitter} that edits structured genotypes---the LLM is \emph{not} the surrogate.

\subsection{Seeded simulators and uncertain QD}

Prior LLM+QD systems often evaluate on deterministic or batched offline tasks. LifeManifold's CA \emph{rules} are stochastic (Bernoulli initialization, per-cell noise flips), but Q1 evaluation is \emph{deterministically seeded}: a canonical SHA-256 hash of the \texttt{WorldSpec} sets the RNG, so re-evaluating the same genome reproduces the same fitness and BC. Elites are therefore not lucky-seed artifacts in the UQD sense---there is no independent evaluation noise across repeats of one genotype.

Uncertain QD (UQD) formalizes the complementary setting where fitness and BC are distributions and archive policies re-sample to limit lucky outliers~\cite{flageat2023uq,flageat2025extractqd,flageat2025tradeoff}. We do \emph{not} claim that confirmatory LifeManifold results stress-test UQD: every inserted elite uses a single seeded pass, and tests use paired experiment seeds with Holm correction on terminal coverage and mean elite fitness (\S\ref{sec:protocol}). We separate \emph{archive truth} (always simulated) from \emph{emitter guidance} (surrogate prompt hints and/or acquisition filtering). Archive target-selection policy is orthogonal to those two surrogate mechanisms. UQD methods remain relevant if future work disables canonical seeding or budgets multiple independent rollouts per niche.

\noindent\textit{Key difference from SAIL:} we do not replace MAP-Elites search with a GP acquisition illuminator; we ask \emph{where} a fixed MLP checkpoint attaches in an LLM-guided propose$\rightarrow$evaluate loop, and we gate with a fitness threshold rather than UCB over a GP surface (\S\ref{sec:acquisition}). \section{Testbed: LifeManifold domain and evaluation}

The scientific object is LLM-guided QD with before/after surrogate slots (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}). LifeManifold is the primary \textbf{testbed}: a two-layer cellular automaton used as a black-box evaluator for MAP-Elites. Each candidate is a \texttt{WorldSpec}---static rules and scalars---evaluated by a full simulation; the archive keeps at most one elite per behavioural niche. The CA is computationally \emph{cheap} relative to CFD-style SAIL domains, so we treat evaluation-indexed efficiency as the primary filter claim and report wall time separately---not as evidence that skip-eval always saves wall clock. This section defines the search space, simulator, archive geometry, and fitness used in confirmatory experiments (\S\ref{sec:results}). Figure~\ref{fig:elites} shows example occupied niches.

\subsection{WorldSpec and seeded CA evaluation}

A \texttt{WorldSpec} describes one world: Moore-neighbour birth/survival tables (\texttt{birth}, \texttt{survival}, each over neighbour counts 0--8), and three continuous scalars---\texttt{noise} $\in [0, 0.2]$, \texttt{resource\_regen} $\in [0, 0.5]$, \texttt{predation} $\in [0, 1]$---that parameterize stochastic resource dynamics and crowding death. Emitters search a 21-dimensional genetic encoding (nine birth bits, nine survival bits, three scalars); values are clipped to these bounds before simulation.

The field is $N \times N$ with toroidal Moore neighbours. Two layers evolve: \emph{life} (alive/dead) and \emph{food}. Each run initializes life with Bernoulli($0.2$) occupancy and food with Bernoulli(\texttt{resource\_regen}) per cell, then applies birth/survival, food regeneration, predation under crowding, and per-cell noise flips with probability \texttt{noise}. All Q1 grid runs use $N=50$ and $T=200$ steps (CLI \texttt{--grid 50 --steps 200}).

\textbf{Canonical seeding (deterministic eval).} Before evaluation, the illuminator assigns a \emph{canonical seed}: an integer derived from SHA-256 of the JSON \texttt{WorldSpec} with the runtime \texttt{seed} field removed. Identical genomes therefore yield identical RNG streams, metrics, and archive fitness on every retry. The update rules are stochastic in distributional form, but Q1 evaluation is a deterministic function of the genome---not an independent noisy draw per call.

\subsection{Behavioural coordinates and archive fitness}

Each simulation returns a 12-dimensional \texttt{WorldMetrics} vector. MAP-Elites uses two clipped behavioural characteristics (BC) as archive axes:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Stability}---temporal steadiness of mean live density (high when density fluctuates little over the run).
  \item \textbf{Diversity}---spatial pattern variety on the final grid (fraction of unique local signatures among 128 sampled patches).
\end{itemize}
Both lie in $[0,1]$. Bins are uniform partitions of $[0,1]^2$; a candidate's measured $(\text{stability}, \text{diversity})$ determines its cell, independent of the emitter's target niche.

\textbf{Early extinction.} If mean live density reaches zero before step~200, the run is marked \texttt{early\_extinct} and archive fitness is~0.

\textbf{Archive fitness} (authoritative for MAP-Elites insertion; distinct from generator scalar \texttt{mo\_eoc\_indicator}) combines diversity, survival, oscillation, and topology proxies:
\begin{equation}
  E_{\mathrm{ext}} = \mathrm{clip}(1 - \rho_{\mathrm{final}}, 0, 1), \quad
  T = \mathrm{clip}\!\left(\tfrac{\text{TI} + \text{TH}}{2}, 0, 1\right)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:fitness}
  \text{fitness} = \mathrm{clip}\bigl(
    0.45\,D + 0.25\,(1 - E_{\mathrm{ext}}) + 0.20\,\mathrm{clip}(A,0,1) + 0.10\,T
  ,\; 0,\; 1\bigr)
\end{equation}
where $D$ is clipped diversity, $A$ is \texttt{oscillation\_score}, and TI/TH are topology interface and window-heterogeneity indices from the final state. Weights follow \texttt{worldspace/illuminators/evaluation.py}; the surrogate recomposes the same formula from predicted components at runtime (\S\ref{sec:surrogate-method}).

\subsection{Grid archive and reported metrics}

The primary archive is a \textbf{50$\times$50} grid ($2{,}500$ niches). An insert replaces the incumbent in a cell only if the new candidate has strictly higher \emph{simulated} fitness. \textbf{Coverage} is the percentage of occupied cells after warm-start merge of the run JSONL (\texttt{load\_and\_collapse\_jsonl}). \textbf{Mean best fitness} $\bar f$ averages elite fitness over filled cells only. \textbf{QD-score} is the canonical sum of elite fitness over filled cells,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{QD} \;=\; \sum_{c \in \mathrm{filled}} f_c \;=\; |\mathrm{filled}| \cdot \bar f,
  \label{eq:qd}
\end{equation}
which holds exactly when $\bar f$ is the mean over the same filled set (not over empty cells). QD-score is reported descriptively only. Table~\ref{tab:domain} summarizes fixed domain knobs and the production emitter mix (20 random + 20 genetic + 10 LLM slots per batch of 50).

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

We implement MAP-Elites in \texttt{worldspace/illuminators/} with heterogeneous emitters, an optional surrogate for LLM hints and acquisition filtering, and an external pyribs baseline for gradient QD comparison. The surrogate never replaces simulation for archive fitness (\S\ref{sec:results}).

\subsection{MAP-Elites illuminator loop}

Each run executes \texttt{iterations} $\times$ \texttt{batch\_size} candidate slots in fixed order. Production schedulers use $650 \times 50 = 32{,}500$ simulator calls per seed. For slot~$k$ the loop:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item selects a target niche (behaviour-cell centre) according to \texttt{target\_selection};
  \item calls the slot emitter (\texttt{random}, \texttt{genetic}, or \texttt{llm}) to propose a \texttt{WorldSpec};
  \item optionally applies surrogate acquisition (\S\ref{sec:acquisition});
  \item runs \texttt{evaluate\_candidate} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{run\_world} (always for accepted slots in MVP hint mode);
  \item inserts into the archive if fitness strictly improves the cell incumbent;
  \item appends training data to the surrogate buffer when \texttt{surrogate.enabled}.
\end{enumerate}
LLM slots log \texttt{llm\_fallback} when parsing fails and a genetic repair path is used. Parallel evaluation uses a process pool sized to the batch; LLM emit uses up to four concurrent HTTP workers per iteration. Outputs: \texttt{map\_elites\_archive.jsonl}, optional \texttt{archive\_trace.jsonl}, and \texttt{nightly\_run\_summary.json}.

\textbf{Target selection (not a surrogate role).} \texttt{min\_fitness\_frontier} picks the occupied cell with lowest elite fitness (used by the bundled \texttt{stub} arm). \texttt{uniform\_frontier} samples uniformly among occupied cells (used by \texttt{hints}, filter arms, and matched RQ1/Role~2 controls). These are archive policies over occupied niches; they do \emph{not} use the surrogate to select parents. The policy confound is explicit in the bundled stub vs.\ hints contrast; scientific RQ1 removes it (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}). Surrogate-assisted parent/niche selection exists in the QD literature but is out of scope here.

\subsection{Surrogate model}
\label{sec:surrogate-method}

The surrogate maps a canonical \texttt{WorldSpec} to predicted MAP-Elites outcomes without running the CA (Figure~\ref{fig:surrogate}). Feature schema~2.1 encodes 24 genome-aligned dimensions (rule bits and scalars from \S3). A multi-head regressor (\textbf{Strategy~A}) predicts seven component targets---stability, diversity, oscillation, two topology proxies, final density, and early-extinction probability---trained from JSONL buffer rows appended after each real evaluation when the surrogate is enabled.

Production checkpoint \texttt{nightly\_v3\_mc\_d005.pkl} is a four-member MLP ensemble with MC-dropout uncertainty ($N=35{,}114$ training rows; hold-out $R^2_{\text{fitness}}=0.76$, MAE$_{\text{fitness}}=0.007$). Runtime fitness is recomposed via \texttt{compute\_fitness\_from\_prediction} using Eq.~\eqref{eq:fitness} (same weights as the simulator). \texttt{get\_surrogate()} falls back to constant \texttt{StubSurrogate} values if the checkpoint is missing, dimension-mismatched, or fails the \texttt{hints\_ok} gate ($R^2 \ge 0.30$, MAE $< 0.085$).

\textbf{Before generation (RQ1) --- prompt hints.} Before each LLM call, \texttt{SurrogateFacade.predict(parent)} runs on the \emph{parent} elite's spec. Placeholders \texttt{\{surrogate\_mean\}} and \texttt{\{surrogate\_uncertainty\}} in the user prompt receive \texttt{prediction.fitness} and calibrated ensemble spread. When \texttt{surrogate.enabled=false}, fixed YAML stubs (\texttt{stub\_mean}, \texttt{stub\_uncertainty}) are substituted without calling \texttt{predict}. One LLM HTTP request per LLM slot regardless of surrogate mode.

\subsection{LLM emitter}

The \texttt{LlmEmitter} sends the parent \texttt{WorldSpec} JSON and niche centre to a chat-completions API; the model returns a JSON patch applied via \texttt{world\_spec\_from\_llm}. Stack~v2 uses temperature~0.2, \texttt{max\_tokens=350}, and \texttt{llm\_parallel\_workers=4}. Primary CA LLM runs use Alibaba \texttt{qwen-turbo} (DashScope); multi-provider replications swap provider YAML while keeping prompts and schedulers fixed (\S\ref{sec:results}). Model IDs are window-pinned in run summaries (\texttt{llm\_spec\_hash}); floating endpoint aliases are disclosed per provider.

\textbf{What LLM mutations change (qualitative).} Archive JSONL stores \texttt{parent\_id} and \texttt{generated\_by}, so accepted LLM elites can be compared to their parents. On \texttt{hints} seed~4, of 779 LLM elites with a resolvable parent and nonzero genome diff, typical edits are structured rule and scalar tweaks rather than random bit noise---for example: (i)~add birth rule~6 and replace survival~8 by~7 while nudging \texttt{noise}/\texttt{resource\_regen}/\texttt{predation}; (ii)~shift birth $\{0,1,3\}\!\to\!\{0,1,4\}$ and survival $\{0,1,2\}\!\to\!\{0,1,3\}$ with larger scalar moves, jumping stability $0.09\!\to\!0.37$; (iii)~add birth~7 on a high-fitness parent with a small diversity retreat; (iv)~extend survival with neighbour count~7 and raise \texttt{resource\_regen}. These examples support calling LLM variation \emph{semantic} (rule-table edits + coordinated scalars) without claiming that such edits improve QD once target selection is matched.

\subsection{Role~2 --- surrogate acquisition filter}
\label{sec:acquisition}

Optional \texttt{acquisition.mode=filter} evaluates \texttt{surrogate.predict} \emph{before} \texttt{evaluate\_candidate}. A \texttt{threshold\_gate} skips simulation when predicted fitness falls below \texttt{min\_predicted\_fitness=0.45} (LifeManifold) subject to uncertainty calibration. Skipped slots are logged to \texttt{surrogate\_archive.jsonl} but do not update the MAP-Elites archive. \texttt{shadow} mode logs counterfactual skips while still evaluating.

\textbf{Why MLP + threshold gate, not SAIL-style GP+UCB.}
We use an MLP ensemble with MC-dropout uncertainty and a fitness threshold gate ($\texttt{min\_fit}{=}0.45$, $\texttt{max\_u}{=}1.0$), rather than a SAIL-style GP+UCB acquisition.
On the nightly buffer hold-out, the production MLP achieves $R^2{\approx}0.76$ vs.\ ${\approx}0.22$ for a scikit-learn GP fitness regressor trained on 5{,}000 rows, and is ${\sim}12\times$ faster at a 32{,}500-proposal batch.
Offline acquisition replay shows that replacing the MLP with the GP under the \emph{same} threshold gate worsens the false-skip proxy ($0.7\%\!\rightarrow\!3.2\%$); a UCB skip rule on GP scores ($\mu{+}\beta\sigma < \texttt{min\_fit}$, $\beta\in\{0.15,0.5,1.0\}$) stays near $99\%$ skip and does not improve false-skip.
On logged Q1 filter proposals ($n{=}325{,}000$), replaying UCB on the \emph{same} MLP $\mu/\sigma$ only softens the gate (skip $33.5\%\!\rightarrow\!28.5\%/25.7\%/21.2\%$) and never adds skips---still without claiming an online QD win for UCB.
With $\texttt{max\_u}{=}1.0$, uncertainty is not load-bearing for the production threshold gate.
We therefore treat GP+UCB as a related SAIL lineage, not a competitive drop-in for this stack (artifact: \texttt{artifacts/surrogate/gp\_ucb\_ablation.json}).

The locked compose-gate check for LLM+filter non-inferiority remains \textbf{blocked}: live proposal replay at threshold~0.45 yields divergent skip rate $d=0.528$ (required $\le 0.05$). Scientific RQ3 is therefore descriptive; Role~2 reports matched \texttt{genetic\_me\_uniform} vs.\ \texttt{genetic\_me\_filter} as the primary filter evidence (\S\ref{sec:role2}).

\subsection{pyribs CMA-ME / CMA-MAE baselines}
\label{sec:pyribs}

Gradient QD baselines run through \texttt{run\_pyribs\_baseline.py} (\texttt{ribs==0.11.0}), \emph{not} as native \texttt{EmitterKind} types. The adapter maps the same 21-D genetic genome to \texttt{WorldSpec}, evaluates with the LifeManifold simulator, and writes comparable JSONL archives. Each algorithm uses \textbf{five homogeneous} \texttt{EvolutionStrategyEmitter} instances (batch~50 each $\Rightarrow$ ask size~250 per generation), initial step size $\sigma_0=0.2$, and 32{,}500 total evaluations. CMA-ME uses learning rate~1.0 with 2-improvement ranking and filter selection; CMA-MAE uses learning rate~0.01, improvement ranking, and a separate result archive. This is a strong but not maximal pyribs configuration (no mixed ImprovementEmitter / RandomDirectionEmitter cocktail). We treat pyribs comparisons as cross-pipeline references alongside the in-repo heterogeneous MAP-Elites stack---same honesty bar as the coverage-ceiling caveat: encoding and initialization are disclosed below, not hidden advantages.

\textbf{Encoding (continuous relaxation; apples-to-oranges caveat).}
CMA's \texttt{EvolutionStrategyEmitter} proposes continuous $\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{21}$; \texttt{decode\_genome} then \emph{rints} the 18 rule bits and clips the three scalars before simulation. Genetic / LLM emitters, by contrast, edit discrete rule tables (and named scalars) directly. The comparison is therefore \emph{not} bit-identical variation: CMA searches a continuous relaxation of a mixed discrete/continuous genotype, where covariance adaptation can exploit local structure that bit-flip genetics never sees. We treat the pyribs arms as an \textbf{upper-bound style ceiling for continuous-friendly representations} of this genome---not as a fair discrete-vs-discrete duel. A discrete CMA-ES / categorical / Bernoulli emitter would be a fairer head-to-head against genetic and LLM rule-table edits; we did not run one because pyribs' stock path is continuous ES, and a narrower gap would not overturn the ceiling reading (CMA-ME still leads the mid-band LLM/genetic cluster under the shared warm-start and evaluation budget; \S\ref{sec:results}). Attack directions cut both ways: (i)~rint may blunt CMA's local model (understate a fully continuous ceiling), or (ii)~the $[0,1]$ relaxation before rounding may overstate CMA relative to bit flips. We keep this encoding because it is the shared LifeManifold interface (\texttt{encode\_world}/\texttt{decode\_genome}) used by genetic ME and the pyribs adapter, so fitness/BC definitions stay matched. A post-hoc decode/cold-start ablation (\texttt{q1-cma-encoding-ablation}) leaves the ceiling intact (\S\ref{sec:results}). Analytic sphere/rastrigin checks validate the pyribs runner without bit-rounding.

\textbf{Warm-start (symmetric).} All confirmatory CA arms---\texttt{vanilla}, genetic ME variants, \texttt{stub}/\texttt{hints}/\texttt{filter}, and pyribs CMA-ME/MAE---load the \emph{same} nightly grid baseline JSONL ($\sim$2{,}614 elites) via \texttt{--load-archive} before the 32{,}500-evaluation budget. Terminal coverage is reported after warm-start merge/collapse. The CMA ceiling contrast is therefore matched on initialization as well as evaluation count; asymmetries in the coverage-ceiling contrast arise from emitter pipelines and encoding, not from unequal archive seeds.

\section{Experimental protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

Experiments follow \emph{internally frozen} Q1 protocols v2/v3
(LifeManifold CA; freeze date 2026-07-12) and v4 (dungeon factorial;
freeze date 2026-07-17). We use ``confirmatory'' for families locked in
those documents. This submission does \emph{not} claim an external
OSF/Zenodo pre-registration timestamp (decision locked 2026-07-22: keep
in-repo freeze dates). Descriptive tiers and pilot arms are not pooled
into Holm tests.

\subsection{Design principles}

\textbf{Paired seeds.} All primary CA contrasts use the same integer seeds $0,\ldots,9$ across conditions within a tier. The dungeon factorial uses the same seed indices with independent domain RNG.

\textbf{Primary archive and warm-start.} Confirmatory CA endpoints use the \textbf{50$\times$50 grid}. Every CA arm in the batch driver warm-starts from the same baseline JSONL before the matched evaluation budget (\S\ref{sec:pyribs}).

\textbf{Multiple comparisons.} Each confirmatory family applies Holm step-down on one-sided Wilcoxon tests at $\alpha=0.05$. A family \textbf{passes} only if every test in that family rejects and local effect-size gates are met (paired $d_z \ge 0.5$ on both primary metrics where specified in the frozen protocol). Families are \emph{not} merged across the bundled-stack, coverage-ceiling, and dungeon AUC families (Table~\ref{tab:families}).

\textbf{Bundled stub vs.\ hints confound (declared).} The locked \texttt{stub} vs.\ \texttt{hints} contrast differs in surrogate use \emph{and} in \texttt{target\_selection} (\S\ref{sec:method}). Scientific RQ1 holds target selection fixed (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}).

\subsection{Endpoints and statistical reporting}
\label{sec:stats}

\textbf{Primary (confirmatory):} terminal \texttt{coverage\_pct} and \texttt{mean\_best\_fitness} on the collapsed archive.

\textbf{Confirmatory vs.\ exploratory tests.} One-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank $p$-values and Holm correction are \emph{inferential} only for confirmatory families in Table~\ref{tab:families}. For exploratory / pilot arms (early multi-provider pilots, hint-channel pilots, analytic runner checks, anytime thresholds), we report mean/median paired $\Delta$, sign counts, and bootstrap CIs; any Wilcoxon $p$ shown there is \emph{exploratory} (not a confirmatory claim) and is labeled as such. We never call a $p$-value ``descriptive.''

\textbf{Paired probability of superiority.} Classic Vargha--Delaney $A_{12}$ is defined for independent samples. On paired seeds we instead report the paired probability of superiority
\begin{equation}
  PS \;=\; \frac{\#\{\Delta_i > 0\} + 0.5\,\#\{\Delta_i = 0\}}{n},
  \label{eq:ps}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_i$ is treatment $-$ control on seed $i$ (for ``less,'' count $\Delta_i < 0$). Tables use the column label $PS$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:ps}), not independent-sample $A_{12}$. $PS=0.5$ is the null; we treat $\sim$0.56/0.64/0.71 as small/medium/large only as rough guides~\cite{vargha2000new}.

\textbf{Other descriptive metrics:} QD-score (Eq.~\eqref{eq:qd}), simulator evaluation count, wall-clock time, LLM fallback rate, anytime curves at matched evaluation budgets where traces exist, paired Cohen's $d_z$, and bootstrap 95\% CIs on median $\Delta$. QD-score and eval-to-threshold curves are \emph{not} used for confirmatory rejection.

\textbf{Scientific RQ2 (surrogate validity):} offline hold-out $R^2$ and MAE on recomposed fitness from checkpoint summaries; supports but does not replace simulator endpoints.

\subsection{Multi-LLM replication}

The primary CA stack uses \texttt{qwen-turbo} on seeds 0--9 (\texttt{q1-full}). We additionally replicate on DeepSeek V4 Pro and OpenAI \texttt{gpt-4o-mini} on seeds 0--9 with identical prompts and schedulers. \textbf{No cross-model pooling.} Each provider is a separate experiment: API decoding is stochastic, tokenizers and context packing differ, and absolute coverage levels are not exchangeable (Table~\ref{tab:g1}). We therefore never average $\Delta$ across LLMs, never run a single Holm family over pooled provider replicates, and never treat ``mean over models'' as a confirmatory endpoint. Holm is applied \emph{within each provider} at $n{=}10$; models are not merged.

\subsection{Dungeon factorial}

The cross-domain dungeon factorial (\texttt{q1-v4-dungeon-rerun}) uses a 16$\times$16 tile dungeon, separate surrogate calibration (\texttt{checkpoint\_benchmark\_v2.pkl}), and five arms at \textbf{5{,}000 proposals} per seed ($n=10$). Quality gates require LLM fallback $\le 5\%$ and filter skip 25--45\%; all passed before seeds 5--9. \textbf{Confirmatory} dungeon endpoints are AUC at matched real evaluations on this 5k matrix. A \textbf{supplementary} CPU pair (\texttt{q1-v4-dungeon-full-cpu}: \texttt{genetic} vs.\ \texttt{genetic\_filter} only, 32{,}500 proposals, $n=10$) checks Role~2 at CA-matched proposal budget; LLM arms at 32{,}500 proposals are deferred to a journal extension (protocol amendment before runs). Protocol v4 §6 notes that coverage-threshold timing metrics were not pre-specified before the first pilot and are descriptive only.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

All confirmatory tests use paired seeds ($n=10$ unless noted), one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on $\Delta = \text{hints} - \text{control}$, and Holm step-down within each confirmatory family ($\alpha=0.05$; \S\ref{sec:stats}). Primary endpoints are archive coverage (\%) and mean best elite fitness on the collapsed 50$\times$50 grid. Effect-size columns report paired $PS$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:ps}). Results follow RQ1 (semantic) $\rightarrow$ Role~2 (algorithmic; headline) $\rightarrow$ RQ3 (interaction).

\subsection{RQ2 --- Surrogate validity}
\label{sec:rq2}

Offline training on the nightly buffer ($N=35{,}114$ rows; schema~2.1, 24-D features) yields hold-out metrics for checkpoint \texttt{nightly\_v3\_mc\_d005.pkl}: $R^2_{\text{fitness}}=0.76$, MAE$_{\text{fitness}}=0.007$, MAE$_{\text{stability}}=0.040$, passing both the \texttt{hints\_ok} and production quality gates. Scientific RQ2 is therefore supported at the offline gate: the same checkpoint is accurate enough to feed RQ1 prompts and Role~2 acquisition. Hold-out metrics do not replace simulator endpoints for RQ1/RQ3/Role~2.

\subsection{Bundled stack contrast (motivation; not scientific RQ1)}

We first report the locked contrast that \emph{motivated} the study---not scientific RQ1. \subsection{Non-LLM MAP-Elites baselines (RQ0 + B1b)}

\textbf{RQ0 (bundled floor PASS).} Pure random MAP-Elites (\texttt{vanilla}, 50$\times$ random emitters) reaches $42.36 \pm 0.29$\% coverage and fitness $0.373 \pm 0.005$. This floor contrast mixes emitter mix and target selection with the LLM stack; it does not isolate hint text.

Descriptive stub $-$ vanilla: $+2.96$~pp coverage --- LLM-stub with genetic slots already beats pure random under \texttt{min\_fitness\_frontier}.

\textbf{B1b genetic MAP-Elites.} \texttt{genetic\_me} (20R+30G, default target selection, no LLM) achieves $45.93 \pm 1.50$\% coverage---near historical \texttt{stub} ($45.32\%$). Matched-policy \texttt{genetic\_me\_uniform} (same emitters, \texttt{uniform\_frontier}) jumps to $59.04 \pm 0.76$\%---the $46\%\!\rightarrow\!59\%$ step is \emph{target selection}, not Role~2 filtering. \subsection{Role~2 --- Algorithmic filtering without semantic guidance (headline positive)}
\label{sec:role2}

Role~2 is the \textbf{headline empirical result}: matched no-LLM acquisition \texttt{genetic\_me\_uniform} vs.\ \texttt{genetic\_me\_filter} (\texttt{uniform\_frontier}, threshold gate, $n=10$; Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition}). This isolates the algorithmic after-generation slot without LLM or prompt-hint confounds. Do \emph{not} read genetic\_me $46\%$ $\rightarrow$ genetic\_me\_filter $58\%$ as a filter effect---that conflates policy with acquisition; Role~2 holds policy fixed at \texttt{uniform\_frontier}.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig06_acquisition_anytime.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{Memory figure (main positive).} Same surrogate, evaluation integration only: coverage vs.\ simulator evaluations for \texttt{genetic\_me\_uniform} vs.\ \texttt{genetic\_me\_filter} (median + IQR, $n=10$). This is the gain semantic prompt hints do \emph{not} deliver at matched policy (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}).}
  \label{fig:acquisition}
\end{figure}

We treat \textbf{evaluation-indexed} coverage as the primary Role~2 axis (Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition}). At equal evaluation budgets, filter leads by $+3.65$~pp at 20{,}000 evaluations (10/10 seeds) and reaches 50\%/55\% coverage with 28\%/29\% fewer evaluations. At fixed 650 iterations (matched proposal slots, not matched sims), filter uses 33.5\% fewer simulator calls but finishes $-0.83$~pp below uniform ($58.21\%$ vs.\ $59.04\%$ coverage) and is \emph{slower} in wall time ($\sim$20 vs.\ $\sim$7~min). Together with matched-policy RQ1 at $n{=}10$ (\emph{descriptively flat}; exploratory), this is empirical evidence suggesting Role~2 $\gg$ RQ1 on the evaluation axis---not a confirmatory cross-slot Holm test.

\textbf{Why MLP can cost more than skipped CA sims.} This is not mysterious invoice noise: acquisition still runs \texttt{predict\_batch} on \emph{every} proposal in the batch (including those later skipped), using a four-member MLP ensemble with MC-dropout uncertainty ($p{=}0.05$) in Python before the cheap CA roll-out. Simulator evaluations are process-pooled; surrogate inference is a per-candidate Python path whose wall share dominates when $t_{\mathrm{sim}}$ is milliseconds. Skipping 33.5\% of sims therefore removes little wall while adding full-batch MLP work---an engineering trade-off on this domain, not evidence that skip-eval cannot help on expensive simulators.

\textbf{Wall-time break-even (descriptive).} Write wall cost per proposal batch as $(1-s)\,t_{\mathrm{sim}}+t_{\mathrm{MLP}}$ with skip rate $s\approx 0.335$ vs.\ $t_{\mathrm{sim}}$ for full eval. Filter wins on wall only if $t_{\mathrm{MLP}}<s\,t_{\mathrm{sim}}$. Matching the observed wall ratio ($\sim$20 vs.\ $\sim$7~min) implies $t_{\mathrm{MLP}}/t_{\mathrm{sim}}\approx 2.2$ on this CA, so the simulator would need to be roughly $\mathbf{7\times}$ slower than today before a 33.5\% skip saves wall clock---an extrapolation to expensive domains, not a measured LifeManifold win.

\subsection{Why evaluation integration wins (mechanism)}
\label{sec:why-filter}

The Role~2 gain is not a black box. Logged \texttt{surrogate\_archive.jsonl} rows for \texttt{genetic\_me\_filter} ($n{=}10$; $325{,}000$ proposal slots) show \emph{what} the gate discards (Table~\ref{tab:skip-mech}). All skips cite \texttt{below\_fitness\_threshold} at $\tau{=}0.45$ (skip rate $33.5\%$). Skipped proposals have mean predicted fitness $0.21$ vs.\ $0.61$ for evaluated ones; $81\%$ of skips have predicted early-extinction probability $>0.8$ vs.\ $<0.1\%$ of evaluated proposals; skipped predicted diversity is low (mean $0.24$; $70\%$ with diversity $<0.3$) while evaluated proposals sit in high-diversity bands (mean $0.74$). Uncertainty barely separates the two classes (medians both $\approx 0.16$), so the operative signal is predicted fitness / extinction risk, not calibrated uncertainty. Random emitters contribute disproportionately to skips ($49\%$ of random slots skipped vs.\ $23\%$ of genetic). Among evaluated proposals, only $12.7\%$ realize true fitness $<0.45$ (pred--true MAE $0.066$), consistent with a gate that removes likely dead/low-diversity mass and reallocates the fixed evaluation budget toward niches that can still fill.

This is a mechanistic reading of \emph{evaluation} integration: the surrogate hard-constrains simulator spend. Semantic integration, by contrast, only soft-advises an LLM that already mutates under a JSON grammar; RQ1's descriptively flat matched contrast and no-large-signal enriched pilots are consistent with advice that is easy to ignore. We do \emph{not} claim uncertainty-aware acquisition or threshold optimality---$\tau{=}0.45$ is a single locked knob (sensitivity deferred).

\subsection{RQ3 --- Interaction: filtering on top of the LLM stack}
\label{sec:rq3}

After RQ1 (semantic) and Role~2 (algorithmic), scientific RQ3 asks the interaction question: given the LLM$+$hint stack, does adding the same after-generation gate help further? On the bundled LLM tier, \texttt{filter} vs.\ \texttt{hints} is $-1.14$~pp coverage at fixed iterations ($59.27\%$ vs.\ $60.41\%$)---LLM+filter reaches the mid-band but does not beat full-eval hints at terminal coverage. Stacking both slots is therefore \emph{not} a simple sum of RQ1$+$Role~2. The locked compose-gate check for filter non-inferiority remains \textbf{blocked} ($d=0.528 > 0.05$ required), so RQ3 is reported descriptively. The dungeon factorial gives partial transfer evidence for filter AUC under Holm (\S\ref{sec:results}).

\subsection{Coverage ceiling --- pyribs CMA-ME / CMA-MAE}
\label{sec:ceiling}

Pyribs baselines (\texttt{q1-v3-pyribs}, five homogeneous CMA-ES emitters, $n=10$) set an upper reference under the same warm-start and evaluation budget as other CA arms. The continuous-relaxation encoding (\S\ref{sec:pyribs}) means this is an upper-bound-style ceiling for continuous-friendly representations---not a discrete bit-flip duel against genetic/LLM emitters. Mean levels: cma\_me $73.37 \pm 3.65$\%, fitness $0.576 \pm 0.028$; cma\_mae $60.56 \pm 2.16$\%, fitness $0.489 \pm 0.007$; hints $60.41 \pm 0.82$\%, fitness $0.539 \pm 0.008$.

\textbf{Encoding ablation} (\texttt{q1-cma-encoding-ablation}, seeds~0--4, descriptive): alternate rule-bit decode and cold-start CMA-ME leave the ceiling intact. \texttt{threshold} ($\geq 0.5$ bits) is \emph{bit-identical} to frozen \texttt{rint} on every seed; \texttt{bernoulli} decode is $-0.46$~pp mean ($72.18\%$); empty-archive \texttt{cold} is $-3.47$~pp ($69.17\%$)---still $\geq +8.8$~pp above hints on every arm mean.

Paired hints $-$ cma\_me: $\Delta\text{cov} = -12.96 \pm 3.46$~pp (0/10 positive), $\Delta\text{fit} = -0.077 \pm 0.029$ (0/10); CMA-ME dominates coverage on every seed (largest gap at seed~4: $-20.0$~pp). Paired hints $-$ cma\_mae: $\Delta\text{cov} = -0.15 \pm 2.25$~pp (5/10), $\Delta\text{fit} = +0.010 \pm 0.008$ (10/10) --- near parity on coverage, with a small mean-fitness edge for hints.

\subsection{Cost and wall time (descriptive)}
\label{sec:cost}

Matched \emph{evaluation} budget (32{,}500 sims) is \emph{not} matched dollar or wall cost (Table~\ref{tab:cost}). Mixed LLM arms issue 6{,}500 chat completions per seed ($\sim$90\% of wall is HTTP emit time). Using published international DashScope \texttt{qwen-turbo} list rates ($\approx$\$0.05/M input, \$0.20/M output tokens; mid-2026) and a conservative $\sim$1.5k~in~/~0.25k~out tokens per emit, API spend is on the order of \textbf{$\sim\$1$--2 per seed} for \texttt{hints}/\texttt{stub}/\texttt{filter} ($\sim\$10$--20 for $n{=}10$); genetic and pyribs arms have \$0 API. Exact invoices vary with prompt length and region; we report call counts as the primary opaque budget and dollars as an order-of-magnitude guide.

On this cheap CA, acquisition filtering \emph{increases} wall time despite fewer simulations (break-even in \S\ref{sec:results}). Dungeon LLM arms require $\sim$51--57~min at 5k proposals vs.\ $<$1~min for genetic ME.

\textbf{Matched-dollar reading.} At near-equal coverage, \texttt{genetic\_me\_uniform} ($59.0\%$, $\sim$6~min, \$0 API) is Pareto-superior to \texttt{hints} ($60.4\%$, $\sim$94~min, $\sim$6.5k LLM calls / $\sim\$1$--2) on wall and money for a $\approx{+}1.4$~pp coverage gap. CMA-ME remains the coverage ceiling at moderate wall and \$0 API. Matched-eval bundled-stack and coverage-ceiling contrasts should not be read as matched-cost competitiveness of the LLM stack.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What works}

\textbf{0. Taxonomy vs.\ payoff.} For LLM-guided QD pipelines, the before/after-generation split is structural (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline})---we \emph{formalize} it for comparison, we do not invent it. We also do \emph{not} claim a new surrogate model, a new SAIL-style illuminator, or a universal filter recipe: the contribution is the matched payoff test showing that the two slots are not interchangeable under one shared checkpoint. Our testbed's \emph{main} measured payoff is Role~2 (algorithmic after-generation; Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition}), with RQ1 as the semantic control and RQ3 as the interaction check. Empirical comparison suggests after $\gg$ before: matched-policy RQ1 is \emph{descriptively flat} at $n{=}10$ (exploratory Wilcoxon; not a confirmatory Holm family), while enriched/weak pilots remain underpowered ($n{\le}3$). Magnitudes need not transfer unchanged to every LLM$+$QD stack; the taxonomy (before vs.\ after) is meant to travel even when the magnitudes do not.

\textbf{0b. Not SAIL rebranded (novelty scope).}
A fair reviewer objection is that Role~2 is ``just SAIL gating on a new stack.'' We accept the kinship and reject the collapse. SAIL/DSA-ME treat the surrogate primarily as a \emph{search surface} / evaluation accelerator inside QD~\cite{gaier2018sail,grillotti2022dsame}; our Role~2 gate is deliberately in that family (after-generation spend control), and we do not sell threshold filtering as a new acquisition theory. What those lineages do \emph{not} settle---and what this paper isolates---is the \emph{before}-generation slot that appears once an LLM is the variation operator: the same checkpoint can enter as prompt text that never skips \texttt{evaluate\_candidate} (RQ1), as a hard gate (Role~2), or as both (RQ3). The empirical claim is therefore not ``gating helps'' in the abstract, but that under one shared MLP checkpoint the two attach modes have asymmetric payoff, and that stacking them under LLM is not additive. That is a within-architecture comparison forced by propose$\rightarrow$evaluate LLM$+$QD structure, not a general theory of surrogates and not a packaging of Role~2 alone as a conceptual revolution.

\textbf{1. Narrative: RQ1 $\rightarrow$ Role~2 $\rightarrow$ RQ3.} Semantic (before) is descriptively flat at matched policy (\texttt{stub\_uniform}, $n{=}10$; not a confirmatory null); one-seed enriched pilots show no large signal only; algorithmic filtering without LLM (Role~2) is the positive sample-efficiency result; stacking both under LLM (RQ3) is not additive. Target selection alone reproduces most of the historical stub$\rightarrow$hints jump (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}, \S\ref{sec:role2}).

\textbf{2. Bundled stack as regression check only.} The bundled stub/hints and hints/vanilla contrasts and multi-provider direction checks remain useful engineering smoke tests---not evidence that surrogate scalars in the prompt cause QD gains.

\subsection{Why filtering wins (and why hints do not)}
\label{sec:why-discussion}

Results report \emph{what} moved (Fig.~\ref{fig:acquisition}); here we adjudicate the natural \emph{why} candidates against available evidence. Not every candidate is settled---we mark which are supported, which are ruled out, and which remain open.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Hard budget reallocation (supported; primary).}
 The filter does not invent better proposals; it refuses to spend simulator budget on predicted junk. Skip logs (\S\ref{sec:why-filter}): skipped mean pred-fitness $0.21$ vs.\ $0.61$ evaluated; $81\%$ of skips have extinction $p>0.8$; skipped diversity is low. Uncertainty is flat across skip/eval, so the operative signal is predicted fitness / extinction / diversity, not calibrated UCB. At matched evaluations that discarded mass frees slots for niches that can still fill ($+3.65$~pp @ 20k); at fixed iterations the same gate finishes $-0.83$~pp---consistent with reallocation, not a free coverage gift.
  \item \textbf{Surrogate accurate enough to make the gate trustworthy (supported; enabling, not sufficient).}
 Offline $R^2_{\mathrm{fitness}}{=}0.76$ (RQ2) and among evaluated proposals only $12.7\%$ realize true fitness $<0.45$ (MAE $0.066$) show the checkpoint is good enough for a hard threshold not to be noise. Accuracy alone does not explain the Role~2 win: the same checkpoint feeds RQ1 prompts and yields a descriptively flat matched semantic contrast. The difference is \emph{how} the prediction is used (gate vs.\ advice), not whether a usable predictor exists.
  \item \textbf{Soft channel vs.\ hard channel (supported; explains the RQ1/Role~2 asymmetry).}
 Prompt hints only soft-advise an LLM that already mutates under a JSON grammar; the acquisition gate hard-constrains spend. Matched-policy RQ1 at $n{=}10$ is descriptively flat, and enriched/weak-model pilots ($n{\le}3$) stay within $|\Delta\mathrm{cov}|<2$~pp---consistent with advice that is easy to ignore, not with a useless surrogate. We do \emph{not} claim a process study of LLM attention to numbers; the behavioural evidence is that changing prompt content does not move the archive once policy is matched.
  \item \textbf{``Empty archive'' as the reason (ruled out for Role~2 as stated).}
 Confirmatory CA arms warm-start from the same $\sim$2{,}614-elite baseline; Role~2 is not a cold-start miracle on a vacant map. Filter still helps mid-budget on the evaluation axis, which is better read as discarding dead proposals while many niches remain empty \emph{inside} an already seeded archive---not as ``filtering works only because nothing is filled yet.'' Whether LLM emitters would help more in a true cold start is a separate bootstrap hypothesis (\S\ref{sec:bootstrap-hyp}), untested here.
  \item \textbf{Particular threshold $\tau{=}0.45$ (open).}
 All skips cite \texttt{below\_fitness\_threshold} at one locked $\tau$. We have no sweep; Role~2 could shrink or vanish at other thresholds. Treat $\tau$ as an implementation knob inside the after-generation slot, not as the scientific claim.
  \item \textbf{LLM ``cannot use numbers'' as a universal claim (overreach; not endorsed).}
 The data support a weaker statement: \emph{in this stack}, in-prompt scalars do not buy coverage at matched policy. That may reflect prompt design, grammar constraints, model choice, or saturation of the mid-band---not a general theorem that LLMs ignore floats.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Compressed causal story.} Same checkpoint $\rightarrow$ two attach modes. After-generation gating wins on the evaluation axis because it hard-filters predicted low-fitness / high-extinction / low-diversity proposals and reallocates sims; before-generation prompting does not, because the same numbers enter only as ignorable advice. Threshold optimality and LLM process explanations remain open.

\subsection{What does not}

In-prompt hint content does not carry the bundled effect once \texttt{uniform\_frontier} is fixed (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}): \texttt{stub\_uniform} $\approx$ \texttt{hints} on seeds~0--9 ($\Delta\text{cov}{=}{+}0.26$~pp; exploratory Wilcoxon $p{=}0.41$; \emph{descriptively flat}, not a confirmatory null); enriched and weak-model pilots stay within $|\Delta\text{cov}|<2$~pp (\emph{no large pilot signal}, $n{\le}3$---not a confirmatory null). Multi-provider runs replicate the \emph{bundled} $\Delta$, not a matched hint-content contrast across APIs. Relative to matched-policy genetic ME, the full LLM+\texttt{hints} stack adds only $\approx{+}1.4$~pp at roughly $10\times$ wall time.

Gradient-based pyribs CMA-ME remains the coverage ceiling (family FAIL; $\Delta\text{cov} \approx -13$~pp vs.\ hints). CMA-MAE is near parity on coverage; the sole ceiling-family reject is a small fitness edge vs.\ CMA-MAE ($\Delta\text{fit}{=}+0.010$), which we do not over-interpret. Under LLM, the acquisition filter does not beat full-eval \texttt{hints} at fixed iterations (about $-1$~pp), and formal RQ3 stays blocked by compose-gate divergence. On the dungeon domain, genetic MAP-Elites dominates all LLM arms by $\sim$19~pp coverage: the bundled LLM+surrogate QD lift does \emph{not} transfer, even though filter sample-efficiency does.

\subsection{When to use LLM emitters---and when not}
\label{sec:when-llm}

A natural reviewer question follows from Role~2: \emph{why keep the LLM at all, if an MLP acquisition filter on genetic emitters already improves sample efficiency?} Our answer separates objectives---and refuses to smuggle a coverage claim for the LLM.

\textbf{Why the LLM is in the paper at all.} The scientific object is \emph{LLM-guided} QD pipelines: systems that already use an LLM as a variation operator and must decide where a surrogate attaches (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}). Role~2's headline arm deliberately drops the LLM (\texttt{genetic\_me\_filter}) so that evaluation integration can be measured without semantic confounding; that is a \emph{disentanglement} result, not evidence that LLM emitters are obsolete. If the design goal is archive coverage / sample-efficiency under a fixed-length genome and a CPU budget, our ranking already prefers CMA-ME or genetic$+$filter (\S\ref{sec:cost})---and the paper says so. The LLM remains in the narrative because RQ1/RQ3 require an LLM emitter to test the before-generation slot and the interaction, and because practitioners who choose LLMs for structured genotypes still need an attach-time answer.

\textbf{Not the LLM's job in this paper.} On evaluation-indexed coverage, CMA-ME is the ceiling and \texttt{genetic\_me\_filter} already carries the surrogate sample-efficiency gain without API cost (\S\ref{sec:role2}, Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition}). Matched-policy prompt hints do not buy coverage (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}). Modest Role~2 magnitudes ($\sim$3.7~pp mid-budget; $33.5\%$ fewer sims at fixed iterations) are \emph{evidence that attach time matters}, not a claim that filtering beats continuous ES on absolute coverage. For pure numerical illumination under a CPU/matched-dollar budget, our testbed ranking therefore prefers CMA-ME ($\sim$73\% coverage, $\sim$22~min/seed, \$0) or matched-policy genetic ME / Role~2 ($\sim$58--59\%, minutes, \$0) over LLM+\texttt{hints} ($\sim$60\%, $\sim$94~min, $\sim$6.5k API calls for $+\!1$--$2$~pp; \S\ref{sec:cost}). Figure~\ref{fig:heatmaps} shows where CMA wins: low-diversity and mid-archive niches the LLM stack leaves empty. Relative to SAIL, Role~2 is the SAIL-like \emph{evaluation} use of a surrogate; relative to LAEA, we refuse to make the LLM the fitness model.

\textbf{The LLM's niche.} The emitter is for \emph{structured, human-readable variation} that continuous CMA or bit-flip genetics do not expose as first-class objects. LifeManifold genomes are birth/survival rule tables plus named scalars; LLM mutations edit those tables as coherent rule changes (add birth~6, replace survival~8 by~7, coordinated \texttt{resource\_regen}/\texttt{predation} nudges) rather than as anonymous continuous coordinates rounded at decode (\S\ref{sec:method}). Figure~\ref{fig:elites} shows the resulting worlds as inspectable CA patterns; the parent$\rightarrow$child diffs logged for accepted LLM elites are the qualitative evidence that variation is semantic. We do \emph{not} claim a human preference study that LLM elites are ``more interesting'' than CMA elites, nor that semantic edits improve QD metrics once policy is matched---the dungeon factorial even reverses the LLM vs.\ genetic ranking on coverage. The claim is narrower: if the product need is interpretable rule-space exploration (PCG, design briefs, editable genotypes), an LLM emitter is the right \emph{variation} tool; the surrogate should then attach primarily after generation (Role~2), not as prompt numerology (RQ1).

\textbf{Design slogan.} Use CMA/genetic$+$filter when the objective is archive metrics per evaluation or dollar; use an LLM emitter when the objective is structured semantic proposals---and still prefer Role~2 over RQ1 for the surrogate. Do not read Role~2's no-LLM headline as ``delete the LLM from every QD stack''; read it as ``if you already use an LLM emitter, do not expect prompt numerology to carry the surrogate gain.''

\subsection{Hypothesis: stage-dependent integration (not tested)}
\label{sec:bootstrap-hyp}

The fixed-slot decomposition motivates a scheduling hypothesis that this paper does \emph{not} test:

\begin{quote}
\textit{Semantic guidance (before generation) is most useful during exploration / archive bootstrapping; evaluation filtering (after generation) remains beneficial across the optimization trajectory on the evaluation axis.}
\end{quote}

We did \textbf{not} directly test stage-dependent activation of either slot. What we observed still motivates \textbf{adaptive integration} (and, separately, adaptive emitter scheduling):

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Filtering across the trajectory (partially motivated).}
 On Role~2 anytime curves (Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition}), \texttt{genetic\_me\_filter} leads \texttt{genetic\_me\_uniform} from early through mid budgets (about $+1.4$~pp @ 5k evaluations; $+3.0$ @ 10k; peak $\sim$+$3.7$ @ 15k--20k; 10/10 seeds), while skip rates stay high in early/mid/late iteration terciles ($\sim$37\% / 33\% / 31\%). The advantage is therefore not a cold-start-only artifact. It is also \emph{not} ``win at every terminal readout'': by the full 32{,}500-evaluation / 650-iteration endpoint the filter is slightly behind on coverage ($-0.8$~pp). So the honest reading is mid-trajectory sample-efficiency, not unconditional dominance throughout.
  \item \textbf{Semantic guidance early (motivated weakly; unmeasured).}
 Terminal matched-policy RQ1 is descriptively flat ($n{=}10$), which is compatible with either ``hints never help'' or ``hints help only in a phase we did not instrument.'' Soft-vs-hard channel asymmetry (\S\ref{sec:why-discussion}) and the usual MAP-Elites bootstrap/refine split make the early-only reading plausible. It is \emph{not} evidenced: frozen LLM arms lack \texttt{archive\_trace.jsonl}, so we cannot locate an early semantic lift even if one existed. Terminal elite metric distributions (dashboard histograms over collapsed archives) show substantial low-fitness mass still in filled niches, but they are \emph{static} snapshots---they do not date when a niche was discovered and cannot confirm phase-dependent prompt value.
  \item \textbf{Emitter scheduling (sibling conjecture).}
 Terminal mid-band parity (genetic ME $\approx$ LLM+\texttt{hints}) also invites LLM-for-bootstrap / genetics-for-refinement schedules. Equally plausible: genetics simply catch up by iteration~650, or LLM value is BC-region-specific.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Bottom line.} We treat stage-dependent integration as a hypothesis motivated by the observed decomposition, not as a result. Follow-ons: anytime first-fill curves for hints vs.\ stub\_uniform; filter on/off by phase; LLM for the first $K$ iterations then genetics. That is the bridge from this paper's ``where to inject the surrogate'' question to ``when.''

\subsection{Limitations}

\textbf{Testbed scope.} Primary families use LifeManifold; a dungeon factorial is a second LLM-guided QD domain. Magnitudes (e.g.\ $+3.7$~pp, $33.5\%$ skips) are testbed-specific; the before/after framework targets LLM-guided QD pipelines generally. We do not claim wall-time wins from skip-eval on cheap evaluators (break-even $\sim$7$\times$; \S\ref{sec:cost}). The dungeon factorial transfers filter AUC but not bundled LLM QD lift. Sphere/rastrigin only validate the pyribs runner.

\textbf{Bundled confirmatory contrast.} Historical \texttt{stub} uses \texttt{min\_fitness\_frontier}; \texttt{hints}/\texttt{filter} use \texttt{uniform\_frontier}. Matched sensitivity (\texttt{stub\_uniform} vs.\ \texttt{hints}, seeds 0--9) shrinks the gap to $+0.26$~pp mean coverage (exploratory Wilcoxon $p{=}0.41$). The confirmatory bundled contrast remains a valid \emph{stack} test; it is not an isolated test of surrogate scalars in the prompt. Hint-content and weak-model pilots ($n{\le}3$) are exploratory only.

\textbf{Protocol freezing.} Hypotheses and analysis rules are locked in dated repository protocol files (CA: 2026-07-12; dungeon: 2026-07-17). This submission uses that \emph{internally frozen} wording only---no OSF/Zenodo deposit---and does not claim a public pre-registration.

\textbf{arXiv v1 scope (preprint vs.\ journal extension).} The preprint reports all frozen CA confirmatory families (v2/v3) unchanged and dungeon confirmatory family F-B4 on the \emph{5k five-arm matrix} (\texttt{q1-v4-dungeon-rerun}, $n{=}10$; \textbf{PARTIAL}). Supplementary dungeon evidence adds only the CPU Role~2 pair at CA-matched proposal budget (\texttt{q1-v4-dungeon-full-cpu}; Table~\ref{tab:b4-fullcpu})---\emph{not} a redefinition of F-B4 endpoints. Full-budget LLM dungeon arms (\texttt{llm\_stub}, \texttt{llm\_hints}, \texttt{llm\_hints\_filter} @ 32{,}500 proposals), a \texttt{random} MAP-Elites baseline @ 32.5k, and one dungeon ablation family are \emph{explicitly deferred} to a post--arXiv journal revision under a dated protocol amendment (\texttt{EXPERIMENT\_PROTOCOL\_Q1\_v4.md} \S11 draft; amendment before any new runs). We do not treat deferred compute as missing evidence for the preprint's scoped claims: primary CA payoffs and B4 Role~2 portability are already measured; the extension tests whether weak LLM contrasts strengthen at full proposal budget and adds baselines reviewers may request.

\textbf{Multi-provider and dungeon scope.} Extra LLM providers complete the confirmatory per-provider bundled contrast at $n{=}10$ (Holm \emph{within each provider}---never a pooled cross-LLM family). Absolute coverage under \texttt{gpt-4o-mini} lags stronger models even when $\Delta$ direction is stable; that level shift is itself a reason not to average providers. Dungeon timing-to-threshold metrics were not locked before the first feasibility pilot and are exploratory; confirmatory dungeon endpoints are AUC at matched evaluation budget under the v4 protocol on the 5k five-arm matrix. Supplementary CPU Role~2 runs at 32{,}500 proposals (\texttt{q1-v4-dungeon-full-cpu}) are reported descriptively and in Table~\ref{tab:b4-fullcpu}; full-budget LLM dungeon factorial and additional baselines are deferred to a post--arXiv journal extension with a dated protocol amendment.

\textbf{Acquisition ablation scope.} The GP+UCB comparison above is offline (hold-out regression + acquisition replay) plus a counterfactual on logged MLP predictions; it does \emph{not} re-run the confirmatory filter matrix under GP or UCB, and does not claim online QD superiority for either policy.

\textbf{Pipelines and instrumentation.} The CMA ceiling contrast compares complete QD pipelines (homogeneous five-emitter CMA pools vs.\ heterogeneous MAP-Elites+LLM), not a factorial decomposition of emitters. Homogeneous pyribs pools may understate a mixed-emitter ceiling; the coverage-ceiling family already fails against CMA-ME alone. A post-hoc decode/cold-start ablation (\texttt{q1-cma-encoding-ablation}, seeds~0--4) shows the hints$\ll$CMA gap survives \texttt{threshold}, \texttt{bernoulli}, and cold-start variants ($\geq +8.8$~pp vs.\ hints on the weakest arm); \texttt{threshold} matched frozen \texttt{rint} exactly on all five seeds. Warm-start is symmetric when enabled; cold-start costs $\sim$3.5~pp mean coverage but does not erase the ceiling. Matched evaluation budget is not matched dollar cost (\S\ref{sec:cost}). Selective re-runs (\texttt{q1-anytime-ladder}, seeds~0--4) supply \texttt{archive\_trace.jsonl} for vanilla / hints / cma\_me (Figure~\ref{fig:anytime-ladder}); Fig.~\ref{fig:acquisition} remains the matched Role~2 pair only. Surrogate features exclude grid size and step count (documented). Floating model aliases and API rate limits constrain scheduling; we window-pin endpoints and log \texttt{llm\_spec\_hash}.

\textbf{No discrete CMA-ES baseline (why it does not change conclusions).} pyribs' stock CMA path is continuous ES with \texttt{rint}-decoded rule bits (\S\ref{sec:pyribs}); we did not run categorical / Bernoulli / discrete-CMA emitters that would be a fairer duel against genetic and LLM rule-table edits. That omission cannot overturn the paper's primary claims. (i)~The ceiling is already an \emph{upper-bound-style} continuous-friendly reference: a discrete CMA that closed part of the gap would only shrink the apples-to-oranges advantage, not flip Role~2 vs.\ RQ1 or the mid-band LLM$\approx$genetic reading. (ii)~Even under continuous ES, CMA-ME leads hints by $\sim$13~pp and the mid-band by $\gtrsim$8~pp after decode/cold-start ablations---so ``CMA is a strong coverage reference on this genome'' survives a fairer discrete head-to-head in either direction (narrower gap or still-large gap). (iii)~Before/after attach-time payoffs are within-architecture contrasts on the heterogeneous MAP-Elites stack; they do not depend on beating CMA. A discrete CMA baseline remains useful future work for QD specialists, not a missing pillar of the present argument.

\subsection{Future work}

Explanatory gaps that still matter more than new LLMs or domains (\S\ref{sec:why-discussion}): (1)~\textbf{phase analysis}---extend anytime curves to genetic ME and mid-trajectory Role~2 vs.\ hints; (2)~\textbf{threshold sensitivity} on $\tau\in\{0.3,0.5,0.7,0.9\}$ so Role~2 is not a single magic number; (3)~\textbf{prompt micro-ablation} (drop uncertainty / predicted fitness / archive stats) to tighten the soft-channel account of RQ1's descriptively flat matched contrast. Matched-dollar reading already appears in \S\ref{sec:cost}; a full coverage-vs-\$ Pareto curve is optional polish.

We investigated \emph{fixed} surrogate integration points (Table~\ref{tab:role-exp}). An important next step is \textbf{adaptive integration}: when to activate semantic vs.\ evaluation integration, and when to call LLM vs.\ genetic emitters (\S\ref{sec:bootstrap-hyp}). \textbf{Deferred compute (journal extension):} full-budget LLM dungeon factorial, \texttt{random} baseline @ 32.5k, and one dungeon ablation family (dated protocol amendment before runs); threshold sweep on $\tau$ if cheap. An external OSF/Zenodo protocol timestamp remains optional for a later revision, not required for this submission's internally frozen claim.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\textbf{Taxonomy for LLM-guided QD (formalize, do not invent).} Surrogate information can enter the propose$\rightarrow$evaluate loop before generation or after generation; remember Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}. We treat that split as an explicit comparison scaffold---not a newly discovered phenomenon and not a new regressor. We do not propose a replacement for SAIL, DSA-ME, or GP-UCB; the primary claim is a within-architecture comparison of \emph{where} a shared checkpoint attaches. Role~2 is SAIL-\emph{kin} as a mechanism; the non-redundant piece is the matched RQ1 vs.\ Role~2 vs.\ RQ3 contrast under one checkpoint once an LLM is the emitter. \textbf{Payoff in our testbed.} Empirical evidence suggests \textbf{Role~2} (algorithmic after-generation filtering) is the headline positive result---remember Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition}---while matched-policy RQ1 (\texttt{stub\_uniform}, $n{=}10$) is only \emph{descriptively flat} and one-seed enriched/weak hint pilots show \emph{no large signal} (underpowered; not a confirmatory null). Do not read the bundled $+15$~pp stack contrast as prompt-content causation. RQ3 shows that stacking both slots under LLM is not a simple sum.

Those claims differ in kind: the before/after split is architectural for LLM$+$QD pipelines; the Role~2 payoff is empirical and testbed-scoped. Genetic ME already sits within about $1.4$~pp of the full LLM+scalar stack; CMA-ME remains a coverage ceiling under a continuous-relaxation encoding (\S\ref{sec:pyribs}). On a second domain, the dungeon AUC family is \textbf{PARTIAL}: Role~2 filter AUC (\texttt{genetic\_filter}$-$\texttt{genetic}) passes Holm, but hints$-$stub and LLM+filter interaction do not---filter engineering ports; bundled LLM QD lift does not. We release an internally frozen pipeline so others can reuse the \emph{framework} on their own LLM-guided QD stacks and re-measure payoffs---not so that they must reuse our CA magnitudes.
