\title{Where the Gain Actually Comes From: Auditing Surrogate Attach Points in {LLM}-Guided {MAP}-{E}lites}

\author{Artem Starovoitov\corref{cor1}}
\ead{artem.starovoitov@proton.me}
\ead[url]{https://orcid.org/0009-0005-3170-4865}
\address{Independent Engineer, Moscow, Russia}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author}

\begin{abstract}
An LLM emitter is never swapped into a quality-diversity (QD) loop in isolation---it arrives with a prompt, a parent-selection policy, a warm-start archive, and a budget convention---so a measured gain may not belong to the channel it is attributed to. We audit one such comparison end to end on a controlled structured-genotype MAP-Elites testbed with paired seeds. Our bundled contrast shows a large gap in favour of surrogate ``hints'' ($+15.09 \pm 2.42$~pp terminal coverage; $10/10$ seeds; Holm), but changing target selection alone recovers ${\approx}{+}14.8$~pp of it: the headline gap is mostly archive policy, not prompt content. After matching policy, the residual before-generation effect is $+0.26$~pp on primary \texttt{qwen-turbo} ($n{=}10$; paired-$t$ TOST at $\pm 2$~pp, exploratory---\S\ref{sec:limitations}; not provider-universal---DeepSeek mean-TOST fails). We then price the hard after-generation attach point on a non-LLM genetic stack: threshold gating buys ${\sim}{+}3.7$~pp evaluation-indexed coverage at mid-budget with $33.5\%$ fewer simulations, but costs $-0.83$~pp terminal coverage and wall time on a cheap evaluator. That trade has an explicit condition---gating pays on wall only when $t_{\mathrm{MLP}}<s\,t_{\mathrm{sim}}$ at skip rate $s$, requiring a simulator ${\sim}7\times$ slower than ours here---which we state as a portable break-even rule and sanity-check with an injected delay. The contributions are therefore a decision rule for where to attach a surrogate at a given evaluation cost, and an audit checklist (matched policy, distribution-matched prompt controls, compose-gate and error-denominator checks) that makes single-run LLM$+$QD comparisons testable. The two case studies are matched \emph{within} stack, not a single attach-point $2\times2$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Classical surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithms ask where a surrogate should intervene before an expensive evaluation~\cite{jin2005fitness}. Quality-diversity (QD) search with MAP-Elites makes the same budget pressure concrete: each candidate still needs a black-box roll-out before it can enter a behavioural archive~\cite{mouret2015illuminating,gaier2018sail}. LLM-guided MAP-Elites adds a soft channel that classical SAEAs did not have---prompt-side advice---so a natural model-management question is soft before-generation vs.\ hard after-generation use of one shared checkpoint. We situate both mechanisms in Jin's evolution-control vocabulary (Table~\ref{tab:jin-map}): H2 is individual-based pre-selection; H1 is a soft non-skipping use of the same model; generation-based control is not tested.

The practical difficulty is attribution. An LLM emitter is never swapped in isolation: it arrives with a prompt, a parent-selection policy, a warm-start archive, and a budget convention, and a gap measured across that bundle can be produced by any of them. We do not audit other authors' designs here, but our own bundled contrast shows how large the mis-attribution can be---most of a $15$~pp gap that reads as ``hints beat stub'' is recovered by changing target selection alone (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}). We therefore treat the question as an \emph{audit} problem---first attribute a measured gap to its actual source, then price each attach point against evaluation cost---rather than as a search for a new emitter. Related LLM$+$QD work varies emitters or prompts but does not treat before- vs.\ after-generation surrogate attachment as a factor (\S\ref{sec:related}).

We answer with \emph{two matched case studies} that share a checkpoint and warm-start archive but \emph{do not} form a single factorial attach-point experiment (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}; confound table: \S\ref{sec:cross-stack}). H1 holds the LLM mixed-emitter stack fixed and varies only prompt scalars vs.\ stub constants (terminal coverage). H2 holds a non-LLM genetic ME stack fixed and varies only threshold gating (evaluation-indexed coverage). Payoffs use a controlled structured-genotype evaluator (\S\ref{sec:substrate}). Labels \textbf{H1}--\textbf{H5}: Table~\ref{tab:h-status}. Headline numbers: Contributions.

\begin{quote}
\noindent\textbf{Genre.} This is an audit paper, not a new-algorithm paper: we do not propose a better LLM emitter or a coverage SOTA. We show how to attribute measured QD gains to surrogate attach points under matched controls, and state a portable break-even rule for when hard gating pays on wall time.
\end{quote}

\textbf{What a practitioner can decide after reading.} (i)~Before attributing a QD gain to an LLM emitter, re-run the control under the \emph{same} target-selection policy: on this testbed that single change accounts for ${\approx}98\%$ of the bundled gap (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}). (ii)~Attach the surrogate after generation (hard gate) when the evaluator is expensive relative to inference---quantitatively, when $t_{\mathrm{MLP}}<s\,t_{\mathrm{sim}}$ at the achieved skip rate $s$ (\S\ref{sec:h2-breakeven}); below that ratio the gate saves simulations but loses wall time. (iii)~Use an LLM emitter when editable, human-readable genotypes are the deliverable rather than archive metrics, since on coverage and wall it is dominated here by genetic ME and CMA-ME (\S\ref{sec:when-llm}).

\subsection{Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{An archive-policy confound, quantified.} Our bundled \texttt{stub}$\rightarrow$\texttt{hints} contrast yields a large gap in favour of the surrogate prompt ($+15.09 \pm 2.42$~pp coverage, $+0.047 \pm 0.010$ fitness; $10/10$; Holm $m{=}2$), yet changing target selection alone recovers ${\approx}{+}14.8$~pp of it (\texttt{stub\_uniform} $-$ \texttt{stub}; \S\ref{sec:decomposition}). We report the decomposition rather than the gap, and give the matched re-run recipe that produced it.
  \item \textbf{A portable break-even rule for the hard attach point.} Gating changes what gets simulated, so its wall-clock value is conditional: with per-proposal cost $(1-s)\,t_{\mathrm{sim}}+t_{\mathrm{MLP}}$, filtering pays only if $t_{\mathrm{MLP}}<s\,t_{\mathrm{sim}}$ (\S\ref{sec:h2-breakeven}). On the primary grid $t_{\mathrm{MLP}}/t_{\mathrm{sim}}\approx 2.2$, so the simulator must be ${\sim}7\times$ slower before a $33.5\%$ skip repays inference; maze micro-timings give $\delta^{\star}{\approx}2.3$~ms, sanity-checked by an injected-delay run. This is the transferable design rule of the paper, not an artifact of our testbed.
  \item \textbf{Bounded residuals for both attach points.} After matching policy, the soft channel leaves $+0.26$~pp on primary \texttt{qwen-turbo} (paired-$t$ TOST at $\pm 2$~pp, $n{=}10$; \texttt{gpt-4o-mini} likewise; DeepSeek mean-TOST does \emph{not}, $p_{\mathrm{TOST}}{\approx}0.20$---Table~\ref{tab:h1-matched-providers}; status \S\ref{sec:limitations}), with a mechanistic reading: the prompt already carries the parent's \emph{true} fitness, so the added scalars restate known information (\S\ref{sec:h1-impl-threat}). The hard channel gives ${\sim}{+}3.7$~pp mid-budget coverage per simulation and $33.5\%$ fewer sims, but $-0.83$~pp terminal and slower wall here (${\sim}20$ vs.\ ${\sim}7$~min).
  \item \textbf{Reusable audit checks and their failure modes.} Matched-policy re-runs; distribution-matched prompt controls---stub constants are \emph{not} one, and a descriptive $n{=}10$ shuffled placebo sits ${\sim}1.7$~pp above frozen hints late in search (\S\ref{sec:h1-impl-threat}); skip anatomy that says which proposals a gate removes (Table~\ref{tab:skip-mech}); absolute-$\tau$ oracle replay and ranking controls that separate ranking quality from threshold placement; and two measurement bugs we found and corrected in our own pipeline---an offline/runtime compose-gate mismatch and a shifting NMAE denominator (\S\ref{sec:checkpoint}, \S\ref{sec:h3-appendix}). Each is cheap and applies to any surrogate-assisted QD stack.
  \item \textbf{Scope, stated as a bound.} The two case studies are matched \emph{within} stack, not one attach-point $2\times2$; cross-stack factors (emitters, slot mix, reporting axis, parent vs.\ child surrogate inputs) are disclosed in \S\ref{sec:cross-stack} rather than claimed away. Further bounds (Table~\ref{tab:h-status}): LLM$+$filter stacking, CMA-ME (${\sim}13$~pp lead), maze H5 (non-LLM filter AUC yes; LLM arms no).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background and related work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Quality-diversity and MAP-Elites}

Quality-diversity (QD) algorithms seek collections of high-performing yet behaviourally diverse solutions rather than a single optimum~\cite{pugh2016quality,cully2018quality,chatzilygeroudis2021qd}. MAP-Elites maintains an archive partitioned by user-defined behavioural characteristics (BC) and retains the best elite per niche~\cite{mouret2015illuminating}. Heterogeneous emitter sets can improve illumination efficiency relative to a single variation operator~\cite{cully2020multiemitter}; our confirmatory stack mixes random, genetic, and LLM slots in one batch.

CMA-ME / CMA-MAE are strong continuous QD baselines~\cite{fontaine2020cmame,fontaine2023cmamae,zhao2024cmamae}; we run them via \texttt{ribs} v0.11.0~\cite{pyribs2020} on the same simulator as the LLM stack (\S\ref{sec:pyribs}), with homogeneous CMA-ES pools (strong but not maximal pyribs cocktails).

\subsection{Surrogate-assisted illumination}

Expensive simulators motivate surrogate models that predict outcomes from static design features~\cite{jin2005fitness,jones1998ego}.
Classical surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithms (SAEAs) treat that prediction problem as \emph{model management}: when, and how strongly, to trust the approximate fitness instead of the true evaluator~\cite{jin2005fitness}.
Two recurring evolution-control patterns are \emph{individual-based} control (pre-select or filter candidates before expensive evaluation) and \emph{generation-based} control (evaluate only some generations fully, or alternate approximate and exact generations)~\cite{jin2005fitness}.
Table~\ref{tab:jin-map} places our attach points in that taxonomy once; later sections reuse these names rather than re-arguing the mapping.

Surrogate-Assisted Illumination (SAIL) fits a Gaussian process over the behaviour--fitness landscape, runs MAP-Elites on an acquisition surface, and spends real evaluations on a selected subset~\cite{gaier2018sail,gaier2017sail,gaier2017aiaa}.
Deep Surrogate Assisted MAP-Elites (DSA-ME) replaces the GP with neural predictors trained online and validated in an outer game-engine loop~\cite{grillotti2022dsame}.
Related Bayesian QD and discount-model lines continue that accelerator tradition~\cite{kent2025boqd,tjanaka2026dms}.
Those systems often illuminate a surrogate surface; our confirmatory design instead keeps a fixed MAP-Elites propose$\rightarrow$evaluate loop and asks \emph{where} a shared MLP attaches under full-simulation archive updates (32{,}500 evaluations per seed). Reported SAIL savings on airfoils and deckbuilding motivate the evaluation-count axis~\cite{gaier2018sail,grillotti2022dsame}; pyribs CMA-ME/CMA-MAE are our quantitative QD baselines (\S\ref{sec:pyribs}).

\subsection{LLMs in evolutionary and QD search}

LLMs appear as variation operators and evolutionary engines~\cite{wu2025ecllm,wang2025llmea,vanstein2024llamea,guo2024evoprompt,liu2024llmeo,lange2024llmes,fernando2024promptbreeder,lehman2023openelm}, including program search (FunSearch; Eureka)~\cite{romera2024funsearch,ma2024eureka} and QD emitters (LLMatic; in-context QD; LLaMEA behaviour analyses)~\cite{nasir2024llmatic,lim2024llmqd,vanstein2025behaviour}. Concurrent LLM+MAP-Elites applications~\cite{ropke2026dejaq,sviridov2026medical}, PCG pipelines~\cite{togelius2010sbpcg,earle2022nca}, and open-ended CA illumination~\cite{faldor2024lenia} use LLM or QD variation, but not before- vs.\ after-generation surrogate attachment as a factor. Prompt-as-search-space lines~\cite{santos2025diverseprompts,saletta2024promptspace,guo2026qdllm} and LAEA-style LLM fitness models~\cite{hao2024laea,xie2025llmsae} are adjacent; we instead mutate \texttt{WorldSpec} genomes with an LLM emitter and score them with a genome-aligned MLP.

\subsection{Seeded simulators and uncertain QD}

Confirmatory evaluations are deterministically seeded from the genotype (canonical SHA-256), so elites are not lucky-seed artifacts in the UQD sense~\cite{flageat2023uq,flageat2025extractqd}. We use one seeded pass per insertion, paired seeds, and Holm on terminal coverage / mean elite fitness (\S\ref{sec:protocol}). Archive truth is always simulated; surrogate advice is separate. \subsection{Attachment points and contrast labels}
\label{sec:terminology}

A MAP-Elites iteration is select $\rightarrow$ \textbf{generate} $\rightarrow$ (optionally) gate $\rightarrow$ \textbf{evaluate} $\rightarrow$ archive update. As in Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Before-generation surrogate} --- predictions enter the emitter and try to change \emph{what} is proposed (never skips simulation).
  \item \textbf{After-generation surrogate} --- predictions gate the candidate and change \emph{whether} it spends ground truth (SAIL-\emph{kin} hard gate).
\end{itemize}

We use those two names throughout. Synonyms appear only here: before-generation $\equiv$ prompt hints / soft intervention; after-generation $\equiv$ acquisition / candidate filtering / hard intervention; attachment point $\equiv$ intervention point / surrogate attachment policy. Table~\ref{tab:h-status} summarizes H1--H5 outcomes in plain language; later prose states the finding rather than repeating a status enum. Prompt placeholders and thresholds are mechanisms \emph{inside} a role. Surrogate-informed parent selection is out of scope; archive \texttt{target\_selection} remains ordinary MAP-Elites policy.

\subsection{MAP-Elites illuminator loop}

Each run executes \texttt{iterations} $\times$ \texttt{batch\_size} candidate slots in fixed order. Production schedulers use $650 \times 50 = 32{,}500$ simulator calls per seed. For slot~$k$ the loop:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item selects a target niche (behaviour-cell centre) according to \texttt{target\_selection};
  \item calls the slot emitter (\texttt{random}, \texttt{genetic}, or \texttt{llm}) to propose a \texttt{WorldSpec};
  \item optionally applies surrogate acquisition (\S\ref{sec:acquisition});
  \item runs \texttt{evaluate\_candidate} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{run\_world} (always for accepted slots in MVP hint mode);
  \item inserts into the archive if fitness strictly improves the cell incumbent;
  \item appends training data to the surrogate buffer when \texttt{surrogate.enabled}.
\end{enumerate}
LLM slots log \texttt{llm\_fallback} when parsing fails and a genetic repair path is used. Parallel evaluation uses a process pool sized to the batch. Outputs: \texttt{map\_elites\_archive.jsonl}, optional \texttt{archive\_trace.jsonl}, and \texttt{nightly\_run\_summary.json}.

\textbf{Target selection.} \texttt{min\_fitness\_frontier} picks the occupied cell with lowest elite fitness (bundled \texttt{stub}). \texttt{uniform\_frontier} samples uniformly among occupied cells (\texttt{hints}, filter arms, matched H1/H2). These are archive policies, not surrogate roles: they do not use the surrogate to select parents. Scientific H1 and H2 hold this policy fixed (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}, \S\ref{sec:h2}). The historical stub$\rightarrow$hints gap does not. Surrogate-assisted parent selection is out of scope.

\subsection{Surrogate model}
\label{sec:surrogate-method}

The surrogate maps a canonical \texttt{WorldSpec} to predicted MAP-Elites outcomes without a simulator call (Figure~\ref{fig:surrogate}). Feature schema~2.1 encodes 24 genome-aligned dimensions (discrete rule bits and continuous scalars; \S\ref{sec:substrate}). A multi-head regressor (\textbf{Strategy~A}) predicts seven component targets---stability, diversity, oscillation, two topology proxies, final density, and early-extinction probability---trained from JSONL buffer rows appended after each real evaluation when the surrogate is enabled.

Production checkpoint \texttt{nightly\_v3\_mc\_d005.pkl} is a four-member MLP ensemble with MC-dropout uncertainty ($N=35{,}114$ training rows; hold-out at runtime-aligned compose gate~$0.95$: NMAE$=0.112$ [95\% CI $0.107{,}0.117$], MAE$_{\text{fitness}}=0.029$, and $R^2_{\text{fitness}}=0.94$ [95\% CI $0.94{,}0.95$]---the last inflated by target std$\times 1.92$ vs.\ gate~$0.5$, not a model upgrade; appendix~\S\ref{sec:checkpoint-appendix}). Runtime fitness is recomposed via \texttt{compute\_fitness\_from\_prediction} using Eq.~\eqref{eq:fitness} (same weights as the simulator). \texttt{get\_surrogate()} falls back to constant \texttt{StubSurrogate} values if the checkpoint is missing, dimension-mismatched, or fails the \texttt{hints\_ok} gate ($R^2 \ge 0.30$, MAE $< 0.085$).

\textbf{Why this regressor family.} Both case studies pin \emph{one} shared checkpoint so soft vs.\ hard mechanisms are not confounded by swapping models---but they still differ in emitter stack and reporting axis (\S\ref{sec:cross-stack}). An MLP ensemble fits that shared-model contract: multi-head Strategy~A targets, MC-dropout/ensemble uncertainty, and fast batch inference over 32{,}500-proposal schedules. SAIL-style GPs are the natural lineage check, not a required production drop-in: under the same runtime compose labels the MLP leads a scikit-learn GP on hold-out fitness (NMAE $0.112$ vs.\ $0.222$; $R^2{=}0.94$ vs.\ $0.89$ on that same high-variance target) and is ${\sim}37\times$ faster at a full-budget proposal batch, while GP+UCB threshold replays do not beat the production gate (appendix~\S\ref{sec:surrogate-ablation-appendix}). Gradient-boosted trees (e.g.\ XGBoost) are a plausible tabular alternative; the repository retains an optional LightGBM path, but confirmatory H1/H2 pin the MLP checkpoint. We do not claim MLP is uniquely optimal---only that a competent shared regressor was frozen so the soft/hard asymmetry is not confounded with regressor-family shopping.

\textbf{Before-generation surrogate (H1) --- what is predicted and what is inserted.}
For each of the $10/50$ LLM slots per batch, the emitter first selects the archive elite in the target niche (or a random parent if empty). Confirmatory H1 feeds that \emph{parent} \texttt{WorldSpec} to \texttt{SurrogateFacade.predict(parent)}---not a child candidate, and not a counterfactual edit. The user prompt already serializes the parent elite as JSON including its \emph{true} simulated \texttt{fitness}, BC measures, and \texttt{world\_spec}, plus few-shot neighbour elites that also carry true fitness (template: \texttt{prompts/map\_elites\_llm\_emitter\_user.txt}; full text: appendix~\S\ref{sec:h1-prompt-appendix}). The only H1 treatment fields are two additional scalars on one line:
\begin{quote}
\small\texttt{Surrogate predicts fitness} $\approx$ \texttt{\{surrogate\_mean\}, uncertainty = \{surrogate\_uncertainty\}}
\end{quote}
Under \texttt{hints}, those placeholders receive live \texttt{prediction.fitness} and calibrated ensemble uncertainty on the parent genotype. Under \texttt{stub\_uniform}, they are fixed YAML constants (\texttt{stub\_mean}$=0.5$, \texttt{stub\_uncertainty}$=1.0$) with \texttt{surrogate.enabled=false} (no \texttt{predict} call). One LLM HTTP request per LLM slot either way. Because the true parent fitness is already in the JSON block, the live scalars are largely a \emph{noisy re-statement of already-available parent information}, not a directional improvement signal about an unseen child. A null terminal result is therefore unsurprising and does not speak to before-generation guidance in general (\S\ref{sec:h1-impl-threat}).

\textbf{Why this weak actuator (and what it is not).} Confirmatory H1 tests the weakest non-skipping soft channel that still differs from stub constants under a shared checkpoint. It is \emph{not} a test of: (i)~exact archive parent statistics as the sole advice channel (true fitness is already present for both arms); (ii)~a confirmatory shuffled/permutation placebo (a descriptive $n{=}10$ shuffle is \S\ref{sec:h1-impl-threat}); (iii)~evidence that the LLM uses the numbers; (iv)~child-level advice, local counterfactual directions, or candidate rewriting. Stronger before-generation actuators remain out of confirmatory scope (Future work). Surrogate-informed parent selection is likewise out of scope.

\subsection{LLM emitter}

The \texttt{LlmEmitter} sends the parent \texttt{WorldSpec} JSON and niche centre to a chat API; the model returns a JSON patch applied via \texttt{world\_spec\_from\_llm}. Primary LLM runs use \texttt{qwen-turbo}; multi-provider replications swap endpoints while keeping prompts and schedulers fixed (\S\ref{sec:results}). Endpoint pins and decoding hyperparameters sit in appendix Table~\ref{tab:pins-full} / repository YAMLs---reproducibility detail, not a scientific factor.

Accepted LLM elites typically differ from parents by compact named-field rule/scalar edits rather than bit noise (edit-anatomy case study, appendix~\S\ref{sec:edit-anatomy}).

\subsection{After-generation surrogate (H2)}
\label{sec:acquisition}

Optional \texttt{acquisition.mode=filter} evaluates \texttt{surrogate.predict} \emph{before} \texttt{evaluate\_candidate}. A \texttt{threshold\_gate} skips simulation when predicted fitness falls below \texttt{min\_predicted\_fitness=0.45} (primary grid) subject to uncertainty calibration. Skipped slots are logged to \texttt{surrogate\_archive.jsonl} but do not update the MAP-Elites archive. \texttt{shadow} mode logs counterfactual skips while still evaluating. Production after-generation uses that same MLP checkpoint with a fitness threshold---not SAIL-style GP+UCB (\S\ref{sec:surrogate-method}; appendix~\S\ref{sec:surrogate-ablation-appendix}). Primary H2 evidence is the matched genetic filter contrast (\S\ref{sec:h2}); LLM$+$filter stacking is not confirmatory on production filter and not superior after gray-zone repair (Table~\ref{tab:h-status}; \S\ref{sec:h3}).

\subsection{pyribs CMA-ME / CMA-MAE baselines}
\label{sec:pyribs}

Gradient QD baselines run through \texttt{run\_pyribs\_baseline.py} (\texttt{ribs==0.11.0}), not as native \texttt{EmitterKind} types. The adapter maps the same 21-D genetic genome to \texttt{WorldSpec}, evaluates with the same simulator, and writes comparable JSONL archives. Each algorithm uses five homogeneous \texttt{EvolutionStrategyEmitter} instances (batch~50 $\Rightarrow$ ask size~250), $\sigma_0=0.2$, and 32{,}500 evaluations. CMA-ME uses learning rate~1.0 with 2-improvement ranking; CMA-MAE uses learning rate~0.01 with a separate result archive. This is a strong but not maximal pyribs configuration (no mixed ImprovementEmitter cocktail). Encoding asymmetries are disclosed below.

\textbf{Encoding (continuous relaxation).}
CMA proposes continuous $\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{21}$; \texttt{decode\_genome} then \emph{rints} the 18 rule bits and clips the three scalars. Genetic / LLM emitters edit discrete rule tables directly. The comparison is therefore not bit-identical: CMA searches a continuous relaxation where covariance adaptation can exploit structure that bit-flip genetics never sees. We treat pyribs arms as an upper-bound-style ceiling for continuous-friendly representations. Decode / discrete-search controls (Bernoulli, native bit-flip, pbCMA) are appendix~\S\ref{sec:encoding-appendix}.

\textbf{Warm-start (symmetric).} All confirmatory primary-grid arms---\texttt{vanilla}, genetic ME variants, \texttt{stub}/\texttt{hints}/\texttt{filter}, and pyribs CMA-ME/MAE---load the \emph{same} nightly grid baseline JSONL ($\sim$2{,}614 elites) via \texttt{--load-archive} before the 32{,}500-evaluation budget. Terminal coverage is reported after warm-start merge/collapse. The pyribs contrast is matched on initialization as well as evaluation count; asymmetries arise from emitter pipelines and continuous-relaxation encoding, not from unequal archive seeds.

\subsection{Measurement substrate}
\label{sec:substrate}

Primary contrasts use a controlled black-box evaluator---not a domain contribution. Each candidate is a structured \texttt{WorldSpec}: birth/survival neighbour-count rule tables (18 bits) plus three continuous scalars (\texttt{noise}, \texttt{resource\_regen}, \texttt{predation}). The simulator runs a seeded $50\times50$ toroidal cellular-automaton world for $T_{\mathrm{sim}}{=}200$ steps (early extinction $\Rightarrow$ fitness~0). Behaviour characteristics are stability and diversity in $[0,1]$, so the primary archive is a $50\times50$ niche grid over that plane; insertion fitness is a fixed weighted recompose of diversity, survival, oscillation, and topology proxies (Eq.~\eqref{eq:fitness} in appendix~\S\ref{sec:substrate-appendix}). Genetic emitters flip bits / nudge scalars; LLM emitters edit the same named fields as JSON. The evaluator is cheap relative to CFD-style SAIL domains, so H2 claims evaluation-indexed efficiency and reports wall time separately (\S\ref{sec:cost}). Structured named fields make both soft before-generation and hard after-generation interventions testable---the reason for a synthetic CA primary testbed rather than a pure $\mathbb{R}^d$ literature toy as the sole domain. Table~\ref{tab:domain} lists the knobs; example elites and fitness algebra remain in the appendix. Coverage is occupied-cell percentage after warm-start merge; mean best fitness averages elites over filled cells; QD-score (Eq.~\eqref{eq:qd}) is descriptive only. CVT geometry is appendix sensitivity~\cite{vassiliades2018cvt}. Complementary literature Sphere H2 transfer (threshold gate only; no LLM) is appendix~\S\ref{sec:sphere-h2-appendix}.

\section{Experimental protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

Confirmatory contrast sets were prospectively specified \emph{per family} in dated internal protocol files relative to that family's analysis window (primary-grid H1/H2; maze H5; appendix dungeon)---not as an externally timestamped OSF/AsPredicted pre-registration. H4 (pyribs ceiling) was listed as a confirmatory Holm family in the v3 freeze but is reported here as a \emph{descriptive ceiling check} (appendix~\S\ref{sec:amendments-appendix}). Later rows add packages, repair paths, or reporting notes; they do not silently rewrite already-frozen claim-driving Holm families (Table~\ref{tab:timeline}). Descriptive and pilot arms are not pooled into confirmatory Holm tests.

\subsection{Design principles}

\textbf{Paired seeds.} All primary-grid contrasts use the same integer seeds $0,\ldots,9$ across conditions within a tier. The maze factorial (and appendix dungeon) use the same seed indices with independent domain RNG.

\textbf{Primary archive and warm-start.} Confirmatory primary-grid endpoints use the \textbf{50$\times$50 grid} (frozen before readout). CVT is appendix sensitivity only (\S\ref{sec:cvt-sensitivity})---coverage depends on tessellation, so CVT does not re-lock Holm/TOST. Every primary-grid arm in the batch driver warm-starts from the same baseline JSONL before the matched evaluation budget (\S\ref{sec:pyribs}).

\textbf{Multiple comparisons.} Each confirmatory contrast set uses Holm step-down on one-sided Wilcoxon tests at $\alpha=0.05$ (within set). A set passes only if every test in that set rejects (and local effect-size gates where the frozen protocol requires them). Matched H1 TOST sits outside primary-grid confirmatory Holm sets; H3-gray is the separate NI family ($m{=}3$; Table~\ref{tab:h-status}). A paper-wide Holm check over the pooled primary-grid $p$-vector agrees on every claim-driving reject/non-reject (appendix~\S\ref{sec:stats-appendix}).

\textbf{Power and non-rejection.} Matched H1 reports a retrospective sensitivity MDE for a two-sided paired-$t$ \emph{difference} test (H$_0{:}\,\mathrm{mean}\,\Delta{=}0$), not TOST power (Table~\ref{tab:decomposition}; appendix~\S\ref{sec:stats-appendix}). Elsewhere, a Holm non-reject is failure to reject, not equivalence. Outcomes for H3--H4 (bounds / descriptive ceiling) are Table~\ref{tab:h-status}.

\textbf{Bundled stub vs.\ hints.} The locked \texttt{stub} vs.\ \texttt{hints} contrast differs in surrogate use and in \texttt{target\_selection} (\S\ref{sec:method}). Scientific H1 holds target selection fixed (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}).

\subsection{Endpoints and statistical reporting}
\label{sec:stats}

\textbf{Primary endpoints:} terminal coverage (\%) and mean elite fitness on the collapsed archive (occupied-bin average; logged column \texttt{mean\_best\_fitness}---a historical name, not archive-max). Confirmatory inference uses paired seeds ($n{=}10$ unless noted), one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, and Holm within each pre-specified contrast set (\S\ref{sec:protocol}).

\textbf{Paired effect-size companion ($\mathrm{PPS}$).} Result tables may show a column labelled $\mathrm{PPS}$: this is Kerby's \emph{paired proportion of favorable pairs}~\cite{kerby2014simple} (ties as half), not Vargha--Delaney $A_{12}$~\cite{vargha2000new} and not a propensity score. Definition, relation to the Sign column, and why independent-sample $A_{12}$ cutoffs do not transfer: Eq.~\eqref{eq:pps} and appendix~\S\ref{sec:stats-appendix}. $\mathrm{PPS}$ is descriptive only; Holm / protocol $d_z$ decide confirmatory outcomes.

Matched-policy H1 uses a paired-$t$ TOST at $|\Delta\mathrm{cov}|\le 2$~pp (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}; status \S\ref{sec:limitations}). Pilot arms report mean $\Delta$, sign counts, and bootstrap CIs. Surrogate hold-out NMAE/MAE/$R^2$ are validity checks, not endpoints; cross-gate algebra and bootstrap unit: \S\ref{sec:checkpoint}.

\subsection{Maze factorial (main-text H5; $n{=}10$)}

Main-text cross-domain H5 is a maze factorial at the primary-grid proposal budget (\textbf{32{,}500} proposals/arm; 900-cell behaviour grid; five arms; separate maze surrogate and filter calibration $\tau{=}0.685$). \subsection{H1 --- Before-generation surrogate}
\label{sec:decomposition}

\textbf{H1 answer (scoped).} On primary \texttt{qwen-turbo} at matched \texttt{uniform\_frontier}, a paired-$t$ TOST at $\pm 2$~pp supports \emph{mean terminal coverage} equivalence when swapping the tested parent-level scalar fields for stub constants (Table~\ref{tab:decomposition}; inferential status: \S\ref{sec:limitations}). That is the claim---not ``hints do not matter,'' not a general before-generation null, and not model-independent transfer (Table~\ref{tab:h1-matched-providers}; \S\ref{sec:h1-impl-threat}).

On \texttt{qwen-turbo} ($n{=}10$): mean coverage $60.15 \pm 0.73\%$ vs.\ $60.41 \pm 0.82\%$ (mean $\Delta = +0.26$~pp; SD~$1.19$~pp; ${\approx}6$--$7$ filled cells on the $50\times50$ grid). The \emph{primary estimand} is this paired mean $\Delta$cov; the TOST decides on that mean ($p_{\mathrm{TOST}}{=}6.4\times10^{-4}$; 90\% CI $[-0.43,+0.95]\subset[-2,2]$). Bootstrap 95\% CI on the same mean: $[-0.43,+0.98]$~pp. A retrospective sensitivity MDE ${\approx}1.2$~pp (Eq.~\eqref{eq:mde}) is for detecting a non-zero paired mean difference---\emph{not} TOST power, and \emph{not} the reason for the margin. A two-sided Wilcoxon $p{=}0.41$ is a descriptive companion only. The TOST does not establish equivalence of fitness, QD-score, anytime/convergence speed, wall/API cost, or archive structure; those companions are Table~\ref{tab:h1-qd-cost} (descriptive). Changing target selection alone recovers ${\approx}{+}14.8$~pp of the historical $+15$~pp gap (\texttt{stub\_uniform} $-$ \texttt{stub}).

\paragraph{Why $\pm 2$~pp (substantive).}
On a $2500$-cell archive, $1$~pp $=25$ cells, so $\pm 2$~pp $=\pm 50$ cells (${\approx}2\%$ of the grid). We treat that band as a \emph{practical indifference} threshold for this soft-channel case study---not as a noise-derived cutoff---because: (i)~relative to the same-stack interventions the paper already treats as large (archive-policy confound ${\approx}{+}14.8$~pp; CMA-ME ceiling ${\sim}13$~pp; H2 mid-budget eval-axis gain ${\sim}3.7$~pp), $\pm 50$ cells is an order of magnitude below those scientifically emphasized gaps; (ii)~relative to intervention cost, the LLM soft channel already costs roughly $10\times$ wall vs.\ matched genetic ME for only ${\approx}{+}1.4$~pp terminal tip (\S\ref{sec:cost}), so a parent-scalar swap whose mean terminal effect stays inside ${\sim}2\%$ of the niche grid is below the scale at which one would prefer that prompt complexity over cheaper archive-policy or genetic changes. The same $|\Delta\mathrm{cov}|{<}2$~pp number appears in pilot stopping gates (Table~\ref{tab:hint-pilots}). Inferential status of the band: \S\ref{sec:limitations}. Alternate bands $\pm\{1,1.5,2,3\}$~pp (same estimand): appendix Table~\ref{tab:h1-tost-margin}.

On \texttt{gpt-4o-mini} ($n{=}10$), matched mean $\Delta$cov $+0.12 \pm 1.02$~pp also accepts the same paired-$t$ $\pm 2$~pp band. DeepSeek V4 Pro ($n{=}10$) is noisier ($+1.02 \pm 3.51$~pp): paired-$t$ 90\% CI $[-1.01,3.06]$ exits $+2$~pp ($p_{\mathrm{TOST}}{=}0.20$), so mean-equivalence is \emph{not} established (Table~\ref{tab:h1-matched-providers}; seed-influence note there). A bootstrap-median TOST accepts only as a different estimand and is not co-claimed. Enriched / weak-model resource screens ($n{\le}3$; Table~\ref{tab:hint-pilots}) add \emph{no} confirmatory weight. Accepted-elite LLM-candidate proxies: Table~\ref{tab:h1-llm-quality}; prompt I/O: \S\ref{sec:h1-impl-threat}.

\textbf{Claim scope.} At a matched proposal budget the filter arm issues more MAP-Elites iterations per real simulation because skipped slots free schedule capacity. \subsubsection{Cost-benefit break-even}
\label{sec:h2-breakeven}

H2's supported claim is evaluation-count efficiency: fewer simulations for a given mid-budget coverage. That matters when the evaluator is expensive. It is \emph{not} a guarantee of wall-clock speedup on cheap testbeds.

Acquisition runs \texttt{predict\_batch} on every proposal (including skips) with a four-member MLP ensemble and MC-dropout before the roll-out. When $t_{\mathrm{sim}}$ is milliseconds, that path dominates wall, so skipping 33.5\% of sims can still increase wall time (here $\sim$20 vs.\ $\sim$7~min). Write wall cost per proposal as $(1-s)\,t_{\mathrm{sim}}+t_{\mathrm{MLP}}$ with skip rate $s$. Filter wins on wall only if $t_{\mathrm{MLP}}<s\,t_{\mathrm{sim}}$. On the primary grid ($s{\approx}0.335$) the observed wall ratio implies $t_{\mathrm{MLP}}/t_{\mathrm{sim}}\approx 2.2$, so the simulator needs to be roughly $\mathbf{7\times}$ slower before a 33.5\% skip saves wall clock.

Maze micro-timings ($t_{\mathrm{BFS}}{\approx}0.34$~ms, $t_{\mathrm{MLP}}{\approx}0.95$~ms) with $t_{\mathrm{sim}}=t_{\mathrm{BFS}}+\delta$ give
\[
\delta^{\star}=\frac{t_{\mathrm{MLP}}-s\,t_{\mathrm{BFS}}}{s}\approx 2.3~\mathrm{ms}
\]
at the wall-study skip rate ($s{=}36.5\%\pm1.7\%$ @ $2{,}500$ proposals). An injected-delay run at $\delta{=}10$~ms ($n{=}10$) is only a \emph{sanity check} of that inequality (genetic $28.3\pm 1.1$~s vs.\ filter $22.1\pm 3.6$~s; filter faster on $9/10$; $p{=}0.002$)---not a naturally expensive domain. Descriptive; does not amend maze H5 Holm. Sphere transfer of the gate rule: appendix~\S\ref{sec:sphere-h2-appendix}.

\subsection{Why the after-generation surrogate wins (mechanism)}
\label{sec:why-filter}

The H2 gain is not a black box. Logged \texttt{surrogate\_archive.jsonl} rows for \texttt{genetic\_me\_filter} ($n{=}10$; $325{,}000$ proposal slots) show what the gate discards (Table~\ref{tab:skip-mech}). Live filter recomposes fitness at extinction gate~$0.95$. All skips cite \texttt{below\_fitness\_threshold} at absolute $\tau{=}0.45$ (skip rate $33.5\%$)---not a niche-relative / incumbent comparison. Skipped proposals have mean predicted fitness $0.21$ vs.\ $0.61$ for evaluated ones. $81\%$ of skips have predicted early-extinction probability $>0.8$ (so most of the skip mass is extinction-class waste under the absolute threshold). Skipped diversity is low (mean $0.24$); evaluated diversity is high (mean $0.74$). Uncertainty barely separates the classes (medians both $\approx 0.16$). Random emitters skip at $49.0\%$ vs.\ $23.1\%$ genetic; skip rate falls slightly across search phases (early $37.3\%$, mid $32.3\%$, late $30.8\%$). Among \emph{evaluated} proposals, insert/improve rate is $8.1\%$ and $12.7\%$ realize true fitness $<0.45$ (pred--true MAE $0.066$). Shadow runs (\texttt{genetic\_me\_shadow}; Table~\ref{tab:h2-ranking-controls}) quantify would-skip error: false-skip $7.3\%$ and missed-elite $2.6\%$ among would-skip slots (all proposals still evaluated). Offline hold-out NMAE/$R^2$ motivate the regressor family but do \emph{not} validate the live gate (\S\ref{sec:h2-impl-threat}; appendix~\S\ref{sec:surrogate-ablation-appendix}). Script: \texttt{scripts/analyze\_h2\_gate\_diagnostics.py}.

\textbf{Threshold sensitivity.} A sweep (Table~\ref{tab:h2-tau-sweep}) varies $\tau\in\{0.35,0.45,0.55\}$ ($n{=}10$ each). Skip rate scales monotonically ($24\%$ / $34\%$ / $51\%$). At $\tau{=}0.45$ filter leads 10/10 seeds at 15k and 20k evaluations. At $\tau{=}0.35$ mid-budget gain is smaller ($+2.5$~pp @15k, 9/10). At $\tau{=}0.55$ it is larger at 15k ($+5.4$~pp, 10/10) but terminal coverage falls further ($56.4\%$, paired $\Delta{=}{-}2.6$~pp). Aggressive gates trade terminal coverage for faster mid-budget fill.

\subsubsection{H2 threats and missing controls}
\label{sec:h2-impl-threat}

\textbf{What H2 supports.} On the non-LLM genetic stack, absolute threshold gating reallocates a fixed proposal budget toward higher predicted-fitness children and raises mid-budget coverage \emph{per real simulation} (Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition}; $\Delta$AUC@$20\mathrm{k}{=}+0.025$, 10/10). That is simulator efficiency under skips---the quantity that matters when evaluations dominate cost.

\textbf{What it does not isolate (partially closed).} The same skip rate also lets the filtered arm process more proposals / MAP-Elites iterations before the proposal budget ends. Tier \texttt{q1-h2-ranking-controls} ($n{=}10$; Table~\ref{tab:h2-ranking-controls}) now supplies: (i)~rate-matched \texttt{random\_skip} at $33.5\%$; (ii)~\texttt{shadow} with all proposals evaluated (false-skip / missed-elite rates); (iii)~\texttt{filter\_eval\_matched} extended to ${\approx}32.5\mathrm{k}$ real sims. At matched ${\sim}21.6\mathrm{k}$ real evaluations, live filter beats random skip on terminal coverage ($+2.85$~pp, 10/10), cov@20k ($+2.85$~pp, 10/10), and AUC@20k ($+2.06$~pp, 10/10)---ranking contributes beyond skip volume. At matched real-eval count, filter\_eval\_matched \emph{exceeds} uniform terminal coverage ($+4.23$~pp, 10/10), so the fixed-proposal terminal deficit ($-0.83$~pp) is a budget-truncation artifact, not intrinsic gate harm. Offline absolute-$\tau$ oracle replay on the shadow stream (Table~\ref{tab:h2-oracle-replay}) leaves only $+0.73$~pp cov@19k headroom vs.\ live would-skip on the \emph{same} candidates---the MLP gate is near perfect absolute-$\tau$ ranking here; niche-relative (incumbent) gating remains open. Dual axes are reported (eval-indexed primary; fixed-proposal terminal). Maze H5 uses pre-specified matched-eval AUC; primary-grid 20{,}000 / 50\% / 55\% cuts remain descriptive.

\subsection{H3 --- LLM$+$filter stacking (boundary; appendix detail)}
\label{sec:h3}

Stacking the after-generation gate on the LLM$+$hint arm is \emph{not} a simple sum of H1$+$H2 (Table~\ref{tab:h-status}). Production \texttt{filter} is not confirmatory because the locked compose-gate skip-agreement fails ($d{=}0.528$): hard fitness recompose zeros a candidate when predicted extinction probability exceeds a gate, and offline diagnosis shows live filtering used extinction gate~$0.95$ while an earlier hold-out path used~$0.5$, so skip/accept decisions disagree on a large gray band of proposals ($p_{\mathrm{ext}}\in[0.5,0.95)$; appendix~\S\ref{sec:h3-appendix}). Those production levels are therefore descriptive only (about $-1$~pp vs.\ hints). A gray-zone repair clears non-inferiority margins at mean $\Delta$cov $-0.60$~pp but does not establish superiority (Holm $m{=}3$). Full levels, $\tau\times$emitter diagnosis, and NI table: appendix~\S\ref{sec:h3-appendix}. Primary after-generation evidence remains H2 (\S\ref{sec:h5} for maze).

\subsection{H4 --- Continuous-relaxation pyribs ceiling (descriptive)}
\label{sec:h4}

Pyribs baselines (five homogeneous CMA-ES emitters, $n=10$) provide a continuous-relaxation \emph{ceiling check} under the same warm-start and evaluation budget as other primary-grid arms---parallel in role to H3 production stacking: a bound, not a confirmatory Holm family. CMA searches $\mathbb{R}^{21}$ with \texttt{rint} decode (\S\ref{sec:pyribs}), so the comparison is an upper-bound-style reference for continuous-friendly representations, not a fair discrete-genotype contest. \section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Synthesis}

Canonical magnitudes and outcome board: Contributions and Table~\ref{tab:h-status}.

The load-bearing finding is attributional, not algorithmic. A bundled comparison of the kind commonly used to credit LLM emitters shows $+15.09$~pp coverage in favour of surrogate hints, and ${\approx}{+}14.8$~pp of that survives when the LLM prompt is held fixed and only target selection changes (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}). The effect is real; its attributed cause was not. That is the reading we would carry to another stack: a QD emitter comparison is uninterpretable until parent/target selection, warm-start, and budget accounting are matched, and the matched re-run is cheap relative to the original experiment.

Given that correction, the two attach points behave differently and for legible reasons. Within the non-LLM H2 stack, hard after-generation control moves evaluation-indexed coverage (Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition}) by removing proposals with low predicted fitness and high extinction risk (Table~\ref{tab:skip-mech}), and its wall-clock value is governed by an explicit inequality rather than by the coverage number alone (\S\ref{sec:h2-breakeven}). Within the LLM H1 stack, the residual soft effect after policy matching stays inside the $\pm 2$~pp TOST band on primary \texttt{qwen}---scoped mean-equivalence, not ``hints do not matter'' (\S\ref{sec:h1-impl-threat}, \S\ref{sec:limitations})---and the prompt already contains the parent's true fitness, so a small residual is what the interface predicts. Stacking / CMA / maze are bounds. Not interchangeable attach-role cells (\S\ref{sec:cross-stack}). Scope: \S\ref{sec:limitations}.

\subsection{Mechanistic reading}
\label{sec:why-discussion}

Skip logs explain the H2 eval-axis gain (\S\ref{sec:why-filter}; Table~\ref{tab:skip-mech}): discarded proposals have low predicted fitness / high extinction / low diversity; uncertainty does not separate classes. Warm-start ($\sim$2{,}614 elites) means mid-budget H2 gains arise in an already seeded archive; cold-start before-generation value remains open (\S\ref{sec:bootstrap-hyp}). Threshold $\tau$ trades mid-budget fill against terminal coverage (Table~\ref{tab:h2-tau-sweep}). In Jin's terms (Table~\ref{tab:jin-map}): individual-based gating reallocates sims; soft advice as tested does not; generation-based control untested. Whether the LLM \emph{used} the H1 scalars is a separate question (\S\ref{sec:h1-impl-threat}).

\subsection{Scope of the H1 mean-equivalence result}
\label{sec:h1-impl-threat}

\textbf{Admissible conclusion.} On primary \texttt{qwen-turbo}, the observed mean terminal-coverage gap is small ($+0.26$~pp), and the paired-$t$ TOST at $\pm 2$~pp accepts mean equivalence under matched \texttt{uniform\_frontier} (Table~\ref{tab:decomposition}; status \S\ref{sec:limitations}). The same mean-TOST accepts on \texttt{gpt-4o-mini}; DeepSeek mean-TOST rejects (Table~\ref{tab:h1-matched-providers} and its influence note). Do not pool providers into ``all are flat.'' Scope: mean terminal coverage only---not ``hints do not matter,'' not fitness/QD/anytime/cost equivalence, and not a model-independent before-generation null.

\textbf{What ``matched policy'' isolates (and what it does not).}
Matched H1 holds fixed: emitter mix ($20$ random / $20$ genetic / $10$ LLM), \texttt{uniform\_frontier} parent/target selection, system$+$user prompt \emph{templates}, JSON parse / genetic repair / coercion paths, warm-start archive, and the $32{,}500$-evaluation budget with no after-generation skips. Logged transport is identical on primary \texttt{qwen-turbo}: $6{,}500$ LLM emit attempts/seed and $0$ fallbacks on both arms (Table~\ref{tab:h1-qd-cost}; appendix~\S\ref{sec:h1-prompt-appendix}). The intended treatment is only the filled surrogate line (live parent MLP scalars vs.\ YAML constants $0.5$/$1.0$). That is \emph{not} a full semantic isolation of the hint channel: (i)~stub constants are not a shuffled / mismatched / length-matched placebo drawn from the live scalar distribution, so context length and number formatting are only approximately matched; (ii)~arms were run as separate batch tiers, not time-interleaved; (iii)~there is no manipulation check that emitted candidates \emph{follow} the surrogate line---accepted-elite proxies (Table~\ref{tab:h1-llm-quality}) are near-null and descriptive, and rejected genotypes were never logged. A terminal null therefore cannot separate ``informative hints do not help search'' from ``the model does not use the hint channel.''

\paragraph{Shuffled placebo ($n{=}10$; descriptive).}
Tier \texttt{q1-h1-placebo-pilot} permutes intact live MLP $(\mathrm{fitness},\mathrm{uncertainty})$ pairs across the ${\approx}10$ LLM slots after \texttt{prepare\_emit}, preserving the batch scalar multiset while breaking parent alignment (protocol \texttt{artifacts/Q1\_H1\_PLACEBO\_LOCK.md}). Offline vs.\ frozen mixed-stack \texttt{hints}/\texttt{stub\_uniform}: terminal coverage $62.17\pm1.70\%$ (placebo) vs.\ $60.52\pm0.72\%$ / $60.21\pm0.81\%$; paired placebo$-$hints $+1.65\pm1.73$~pp (8/10; Wilcoxon $p{\approx}0.020$), but cov@20k only $+0.42$~pp---a late-terminal gap, not a mid-budget lift. A $\pm 2$~pp TOST of placebo$\equiv$hints rejects (90\% CI $[+0.64,+2.65]$). This does \emph{not} reopen matched H1 (hints$\equiv$stub still holds) and is \emph{not} a confirmatory soft-channel win: the direction (shuffle above aligned live) is the wrong sign for ``correct numbers help,'' and the package is cross-tier (placebo later, $2$ LLM workers vs.\ frozen hints at $4$). A matched interleaved re-run (\texttt{q1-h1-placebo-interleaved}; protocol \texttt{Q1\_H1\_PLACEBO\_INTERLEAVED.md}; both arms, $2$ workers) stops at seed~0: placebo$-$hints $-0.92$~pp terminal / $-1.44$~pp @20k (both $<2$~pp bar), with interleaved hints matching frozen mixed-2x2 seed~0 ($+0.04$~pp)---so the historical $+1.65$~pp late gap does \emph{not} reproduce under matched calendar/workers and should be read as a caveated cross-tier threat, not a stable shuffle effect. The narrower audit lesson (prefer distribution-matched placebos over YAML $0.5$/$1.0$ alone) remains. Artifacts: \texttt{q1-h1-placebo-pilot/ANALYSIS.md}; \texttt{q1-h1-placebo-interleaved/ANALYSIS.md}.

\textbf{Why a near-null is still expected under the confirmatory interface.} The confirmatory user prompt already includes the parent elite's \emph{true} simulated fitness inside \texttt{current\_elite\_json}, plus neighbour elites with true fitness (appendix~\S\ref{sec:h1-prompt-appendix}). Live H1 only appends a surrogate estimate of fitness/uncertainty on that same parent genotype---a noisy re-statement of information the archive already holds---not a predicted child improvement or a rewrite instruction. Advice touches only ${\approx}10/50$ batch slots. Matched hints vs.\ stub remaining near-null is therefore unsurprising; the historical shuffled-placebo gap suggested that \emph{which} live scalars sit in which slot might move the late archive, but the matched interleaved seed~0 re-run did not reproduce that lift---so that threat stays caveated rather than a stable soft claim. Terminal coverage after a large warm-start (${\sim}2{,}614$ elites) can hide early differences; frozen confirmatory H1 lacks cold-start curves, but the mixed-stack $2\times2$ supplies eval-indexed readouts (cov@20k hints$-$stub $+0.71$~pp; Table~\ref{tab:mixed-2x2}). Resource-screening pilots at $n{\le}3$ (Table~\ref{tab:hint-pilots}) are not sequential inference and do not add evidential weight.

\textbf{What package~A adds (and still does not).}
Accepted-elite LLM quality proxies are now reported for \texttt{hints} vs.\ \texttt{stub\_uniform} (Table~\ref{tab:h1-llm-quality}): insert rate ${\approx}12\%$ vs.\ $11\%$, cell-improve rate ${\approx}5.6\%$ vs.\ $5.1\%$, realized child$-$parent $\Delta$fit ${\approx}{+}0.01$ both arms, compact edits (Hamming ${\approx}1.6$--$1.9$). These are descriptive, not Holm. Rejected LLM genotypes were never logged per-seed on frozen arms, so all-evaluated fitness and fail anatomy remain unavailable there; new runs append \texttt{proposal\_log.jsonl} (accepts$+$rejects; \texttt{artifacts/PROPOSAL\_LOG.md}). Offline MLP predict on a subsample of \emph{accepted} children systematically under-shoots true fitness (selection bias toward high realized fitness; not a logged emit-time score). \paragraph{Path~A child rewrite ($n{=}10$; descriptive).}
Tier \texttt{q1-h1-child-rewrite-pilot} adds a conditional second LLM pass after \texttt{predict(draft)} when predicted child fitness is below parent true fitness (protocol \texttt{artifacts/Q1\_H1\_CHILD\_REWRITE.md}). Paired vs.\ single-pass \texttt{hints}: terminal $+0.35\pm1.46$~pp (6/10; Wilcoxon $p{\approx}0.49$), cov@20k $+0.16\pm1.37$~pp, $\Delta$fit ${\approx}0$; a $\pm 2$~pp TOST of rewrite$\equiv$hints accepts (90\% CI $[-0.50,+1.20]$). Rewrite fired on ${\sim}39\%$ of draft slots (${\sim}2545$ extra calls/seed) at ${\sim}{+}35\%$ wall. Reading: this stronger soft actuator did \emph{not} move coverage beyond parent-scalar hints; cost without archive gain. The package is still a bundle (second pass $+$ instruction $+$ child scalars $+$ budget)---no rewrite-with-stub arm---so a null does not isolate ``surrogate on child is useless,'' only that the tested Path~A stack failed the soft-pilot bar. Offline soft-channel diagnostics (no new API; compose gate~$0.95$; artifact \texttt{SOFT\_CHANNEL\_OFFLINE.md}): on live evaluated LLM proposals, child-distribution NMAE is ${\approx}0.63$ (\texttt{llm}) / ${\approx}0.39$ (\texttt{llm\_rewrite}) vs.\ hold-out~$0.112$---noisier than parent-hold-out validity, not pure noise; rewrite edits are mostly null (${\approx}80\%$ identical draft returned; mean rule Hamming ${\approx}0.15$) and uncorrelated with $(\mathrm{parent\_true}-\mathrm{child\_pred})$ / uncertainty ($|r|{\lesssim}0.03$); parent-hint residual vs.\ parent$\to$child Hamming is weak ($r{\approx}0.07$). Artifact: \texttt{artifacts/experiments/q1-h1-child-rewrite-pilot/ANALYSIS.md}. Still open: cold-start H1; rewrite-stub control if Path~A is revisited (\S\ref{sec:limitations}, Future work). Interleaved placebo seed~0 stop: above.

\paragraph{Contribution framing.}
H1 is a within-stack case study of one weak, possibly redundant soft interface under one checkpoint---not a general before-generation impossibility result, and not an isolated soft-vs-hard attach-point theorem. Mixed-stack $2\times2$ anytime is complete (Table~\ref{tab:mixed-2x2}).

\subsection{Boundary conditions}

Bounds already in Table~\ref{tab:h-status} / Results: H4 is a descriptive continuous-relaxation ceiling (${\sim}13$~pp below CMA-ME; \S\ref{sec:h4}); H3 stacking not superior (\S\ref{sec:h3}); maze H5 ports only non-LLM filter AUC (\S\ref{sec:h5}); LLM+\texttt{hints} adds $\approx{+}1.4$~pp vs.\ matched genetic ME at roughly $10\times$ wall (\S\ref{sec:cost}).

\subsection{Implications for LLM emitters}
\label{sec:when-llm}

The LLM is one emitter in the H1 stack, not the product under test, and not the stack on which H2 was isolated (Table~\ref{tab:jin-map}; \S\ref{sec:cross-stack}). On coverage/wall here it is mostly not the metrics winner (CMA-ME; genetic ME; Figure~\ref{fig:ladder}). Niche: structured, human-readable edits (appendix~\S\ref{sec:edit-anatomy}).

\textbf{Decision rule (as tested here).} Let $t_{\mathrm{sim}}$ be evaluator cost, $t_{\mathrm{MLP}}$ surrogate inference cost per proposal, and $s$ the achieved skip rate.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Attribution first.} Before adopting any emitter on the strength of a reported gap, re-run the control under matched parent/target selection, warm-start, and budget. On this testbed that step alone reassigned ${\approx}98\%$ of the gap (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}). Cost: one extra arm.
  \item \emph{Hard gate when $t_{\mathrm{MLP}}<s\,t_{\mathrm{sim}}$.} Then filtering buys both simulations and wall time. Below that ratio---our primary grid, where $t_{\mathrm{MLP}}/t_{\mathrm{sim}}\approx 2.2$---it still buys coverage per simulation, so adopt it only if simulator count, not wall clock, is the binding constraint, and expect a small terminal-coverage cost ($-0.83$~pp here) from the skipped tail.
  \item \emph{Soft prompt scalars: do not expect archive gains from restating known quantities.} If the prompt already carries the parent's true fitness, adding a surrogate estimate of the same quantity is redundant by construction; spend the prompt budget on child-level or counterfactual advice instead (\S\ref{sec:h1-impl-threat}). Under latency or API cost, matched genetic ME reached $59.0\%$ coverage in ${\sim}6$~min with zero calls against $60.4\%$ in ${\sim}94$~min with ${\sim}6.5$k calls (\S\ref{sec:cost}).
  \item \emph{Choose the LLM emitter for the artifact, not the metric.} It is justified when editable, human-readable genotypes are the deliverable; on archive metrics here, genetic ME and CMA-ME dominate it.
\end{enumerate}

Do not treat H2's genetic-ME gate as already proven under LLM variation.

\subsection{Hypothesis: stage-dependent integration}
\label{sec:bootstrap-hyp}

Untested scheduling hypothesis (not a claim of this paper): stronger before-generation advice may matter mainly in archive bootstrap; after-generation gating may help across the evaluation trajectory. Consistent with, but not testing, that reading: H2 leads from early/mid budgets on anytime curves (Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition}); terminal H1 mean-coverage TOST on a weak/redundant scalar interface does not speak to early soft value---mixed-stack eval@20k still shows only a small hints lift ($+0.71$~pp; Table~\ref{tab:mixed-2x2}). Follow-ons: first-fill curves, phase-gated filter, LLM-then-genetic schedules.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\subsubsection{H1 and H2 are not an isolated attach-point factorial}
\label{sec:cross-stack}

Comparing H1 to H2 as if only the attach role changed would be incorrect. The confirmatory contrasts fix different factors (Table~\ref{tab:cross-stack}). A shared checkpoint removes model swap, not the remaining design differences. In H1 the surrogate scores a \emph{parent} (or archive state) before a child exists; in H2 it scores an already-generated \emph{child} genotype---different input distributions and prediction tasks. H2's positive axis is mid-budget coverage per real simulation; H1 is reported mainly as a terminal null on frozen \texttt{q1-full} arms without \texttt{archive\_trace.jsonl}. A mixed-emitter $2\times2$ on one LLM stack (Table~\ref{tab:mixed-2x2}; tier \texttt{q1-v3-mixed-2x2}) now supplies eval-indexed readouts on all four cells: soft deltas stay small; hard gating lowers terminal coverage but raises cov@20k eval in 10/10 seeds---the genetic-ME H2 sign on the LLM stack. Primary H2 evidence remains the non-LLM genetic branch; the grid isolates attach factors \emph{within} stack, not across stacks.

\subsubsection{Threats to validity}

\textbf{H1 TOST margin (canonical).}
Because the $\pm 2$~pp TOST band was chosen at reporting time---as a practical-indifference threshold and the same numeric stop used in resource-screening pilots---\emph{not} locked before seeing the matched H1 deltas, the paired-$t$ TOST carries \emph{no formal Type-I error guarantee} as a confirmatory equivalence test. We report it as an exploratory, equivalence-consistent summary of the observed mean $\Delta$cov on primary \texttt{qwen-turbo} (and the parallel \texttt{gpt-4o-mini} row). A pre-specified margin would be required to upgrade that reading. Margin sensitivity without re-locking: Table~\ref{tab:h1-tost-margin}. Elsewhere the manuscript refers to this paragraph rather than restating the hedging.

Separately, the $n{=}10$ distribution-matched shuffled placebo (\S\ref{sec:h1-impl-threat}) addresses channel isolation versus stub constants; it does \emph{not} retrospectively validate the TOST margin, and its late $+1.65$~pp terminal gap vs.\ frozen hints is reported only as a descriptive threat---not as confirmatory equivalence or a soft-channel win.

H1 mean-equivalence is otherwise scoped to primary-\texttt{qwen} paired mean \emph{terminal coverage} only (\S\ref{sec:h1-impl-threat}): not ``hints do not matter,'' not fitness/QD/anytime equivalence, and not a proof that before-generation guidance is useless. QD-score, best fitness, wall, and API calls accompany the TOST (Table~\ref{tab:h1-qd-cost}); eval-indexed soft anytime is in Table~\ref{tab:mixed-2x2}. True parent fitness is already in the JSON; accepted-elite LLM quality proxies are descriptive and near-null (Table~\ref{tab:h1-llm-quality}). Offline child-distribution NMAE and edit-vs-scalar correlations (\S\ref{sec:h1-impl-threat}) bound Path~A mechanism claims but are not confirmatory. Cold-start remains open. Frozen H1 arms still lack per-seed all-evaluated LLM rows; new runs write \texttt{proposal\_log.jsonl} (accepts$+$rejects; protocol \texttt{artifacts/PROPOSAL\_LOG.md}). An interleaved placebo vs.\ hints seed~0 re-run (matched calendar/workers) stopped flat ($-0.92$~pp; protocol \texttt{Q1\_H1\_PLACEBO\_INTERLEAVED.md}); the historical cross-tier $+1.65$~pp placebo gap remains caveated. Regressor-family choice is scoped: MLP vs.\ GP is an offline lineage ablation; boosting was not a confirmatory factor.

H2 saves simulations under a fixed proposal budget with skips---simulator efficiency with ranking contribution beyond rate-matched skip volume (Table~\ref{tab:h2-ranking-controls}; \S\ref{sec:h2-impl-threat}). It is not a wall-clock win on this cheap testbed (\S\ref{sec:h2-breakeven}, \S\ref{sec:cost}). Absolute-$\tau$ oracle replay leaves ${\approx}0.7$~pp same-stream headroom (Table~\ref{tab:h2-oracle-replay}); niche-relative gating remains open. The pyribs coverage contrast searches a continuous relaxation of a mixed discrete/continuous genome; decode ablations still propose in $\mathbb{R}^{21}$ before rounding, and the CMA comparison is a pipeline reference rather than a factorial emitter decomposition (\S\ref{sec:h4}). Matched evaluation budget is not matched dollar cost.

H3 remains not confirmatory on production filter and not superior after gray-zone repair (mean $\Delta$cov $-0.60$~pp; Table~\ref{tab:h-status}). Hold-out gate alignment is appendix~\S\ref{sec:checkpoint-appendix}.

LLM reproducibility is integrity-checked, not bit-exact. Prompt templates live in the repository; composite prompt hashes and (where logged) \texttt{llm\_spec\_hash}, sampling knobs ($T{=}0.2$, \texttt{max\_tokens}{=}350), and a dated run window pin the confirmatory stack (Table~\ref{tab:pins-full}). Frozen primary runs do \emph{not} archive per-call request/response pairs, timestamps, token counts, SDK versions, or retry/timeout traces---hashes are not a substitute for those artifacts. New MAP-Elites LLM runs write \texttt{llm\_call\_log.jsonl} under the run directory (messages, response text, usage tokens when returned, attempts/retries, latency; disable with \texttt{LIFEMANIFOLD\_LLM\_CALL\_LOG=0}). Primary model \texttt{qwen-turbo} is a floating provider alias, so even an identical prompt cannot guarantee the same backend weights. Parallel LLM emit uses four workers, but HTTP completion order does \emph{not} reorder archive updates: \texttt{executor.map} returns slot-ordered responses, emission freezes the archive at iteration start, and evaluate/insert proceed sequentially by \texttt{candidate\_id} (code: \texttt{parallel\_llm\_emit.py} / \texttt{loop.py}). Matched H1's TOST/MDE status is the canonical paragraph above. Other non-rejects are fail-to-reject, not equivalence claims: maze hints$-$stub and LLM$+$filter are small or wrong-direction. H4 is a descriptive continuous-relaxation ceiling (${\sim}13$~pp below CMA-ME; \S\ref{sec:h4}). Maze five-arm Holm $m{=}8$ is complete at $n{=}10$ but mixed (filter AUC rejects; family as a whole does not).

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We audited where a surrogate should attach in an LLM-guided MAP-Elites stack, and found that the first thing to attach is a control. A bundled contrast of the type routinely used to credit LLM emitters showed $+15.09$~pp coverage for surrogate hints; matching target selection alone reassigned ${\approx}{+}14.8$~pp of it to archive policy (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}). What remains is small and bounded: mean terminal-coverage equivalence within $\pm 2$~pp on primary \texttt{qwen-turbo} (and \texttt{gpt-4o-mini}; TOST status \S\ref{sec:limitations}), with DeepSeek mean-TOST failing under high run-to-run variance---not an all-providers-flat claim, and expected under an interface that already supplies the parent's true fitness (\S\ref{sec:h1-impl-threat}).

The hard attach point is conditionally worth its cost, with the condition written out: threshold gating buys coverage per simulation on the non-LLM genetic branch, but repays wall clock only when $t_{\mathrm{MLP}}<s\,t_{\mathrm{sim}}$---roughly a $7\times$ slower simulator than ours (\S\ref{sec:h2-breakeven}). Together with the matched-re-run recipe, the skip anatomy, and the two measurement bugs we corrected in our own pipeline, that inequality is what we expect to transfer; the specific coverage numbers are testbed-bound. This is not a broad LLM-QD win, not a general before-generation null, and not a pure ranking isolation (\S\ref{sec:h2-impl-threat}); maze transfer covers only non-LLM filter AUC. Code/artifacts: tag~\texttt{\SubmissionGitTag} / \texttt{\SubmissionGitShort}. Confounds / $2\times2$: \S\ref{sec:cross-stack}.

\section{Statistical details}
\label{sec:stats-appendix}

Main-text reporting uses paired Wilcoxon tests and within-set Holm (\S\ref{sec:stats}). This appendix records effect-size conventions (including the Kerby $\mathrm{PPS}$ abbreviation) and the paper-wide Holm check.

\paragraph{Multiple-comparison policy.}
Primary decisions use Holm within each pre-specified confirmatory contrast set ($\alpha{=}0.05$). Sets are frozen per family relative to that family's analysis window (bundled stack; maze H5 AUC; per-provider replications; gray-zone NI); matched H1 TOST and the H4 pyribs ceiling sit outside confirmatory Holm sets. Dungeon AUC is the parallel appendix family. As a robustness check, a paper-wide Holm step-down on the pooled confirmatory raw $p$-vector ($m{=}16$; appendix Table~\ref{tab:paper-holm}) leaves every claim-driving reject/non-reject unchanged (\textbf{10/16} reject). Primary outcomes remain Table~\ref{tab:families}.

\paragraph{Paired proportion of favorable pairs ($\mathrm{PPS}$).}
Classic Vargha--Delaney $A_{12}$ is defined for independent samples~\cite{vargha2000new}. On paired seeds we report Kerby's \emph{paired proportion of favorable pairs}~\cite{kerby2014simple} (common-language effect size for matched pairs~\cite{mcgraw1992cl}). The short label $\mathrm{PPS}$ is only a table abbreviation for that established quantity---not a new estimand and not a propensity score:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{PPS} \;=\; \frac{\#\{\Delta_i > 0\} + 0.5\,\#\{\Delta_i = 0\}}{n},
  \label{eq:pps}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_i$ is treatment $-$ control on seed $i$ (for ``less,'' count $\Delta_i < 0$). $\mathrm{PPS}{=}0.5$ is chance under no systematic paired advantage, and (with ties as half) $\mathrm{PPS}$ coincides with the Sign column rate when there are no ties. We use it only as a descriptive companion next to Wilcoxon tests. We do \emph{not} import independent-sample $A_{12}$ magnitude cutoffs (e.g.\ 0.56/0.64/0.71), and $\mathrm{PPS}$ is \emph{not} a confirmatory gate (Holm / protocol $d_z$ decide pass/fail).

\paragraph{H1 TOST vs.\ difference-test MDE.}
Matched-policy H1 takes the paired \emph{mean} $\Delta$cov (terminal coverage) as the primary estimand and uses a paired-$t$ TOST at $|\mathrm{mean}\,\Delta\mathrm{cov}|\le 2$~pp (outside confirmatory Holm; inferential status \S\ref{sec:limitations}). On the $50\times50$ grid, $\pm 2$~pp $=\pm 50$ cells. The substantive indifference rationale is \S\ref{sec:decomposition}; the margin is not derived from SD$_\Delta$. Equivalence is decided by that TOST, not by Wilcoxon. Primary \texttt{qwen-turbo} and \texttt{gpt-4o-mini} accept mean-TOST; DeepSeek mean-TOST fails and must not be rescued by bootstrap-median (seed-influence sensitivity: note to Table~\ref{tab:h1-matched-providers}). Table~\ref{tab:h1-tost-margin} reports the same paired-$t$ mean-TOST at alternate bands $\pm\{1,1.5,2,3\}$~pp (sensitivity only; does not re-lock the claim margin).

Separately, as a sensitivity analysis---not TOST power---we report the approximate minimum detectable effect for a two-sided paired $t$-test of H$_0{:}\,\mathrm{mean}\,\Delta{=}0$ at $n$ seeds, $\alpha{=}0.05$, 80\% power:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{MDE} \;=\; \bigl(t_{1-\alpha/2,\,n-1} + t_{0.80,\,n-1}\bigr)\,\frac{\mathrm{SD}_{\Delta}}{\sqrt{n}},
  \label{eq:mde}
\end{equation}
This is the power to detect a \emph{non-zero} paired mean difference, not the power to conclude equivalence under TOST. At a true effect on the equivalence boundary ($|\Delta|{=}2$~pp), TOST power is necessarily much lower than a difference-test power at that same $\Delta$; we do \emph{not} report post-hoc observed power at $1$/$2$~pp. For H1, $\mathrm{SD}_{\Delta}{=}1.19$~pp is taken from the same matched $n{=}10$ analysis (retrospective; no independent pilot variance was locked for this contrast), yielding $\mathrm{MDE}{\approx}1.2$~pp---a design-sensitivity note only, not a margin justification. Elsewhere we quote only MDE numbers under the same difference-test convention (e.g.\ dungeon hints$-$stub coverage AUC ${\approx}1.2$~pp; maze genetic\_filter coverage AUC ${\approx}2.1$~pp before Holm $m{=}8$). These do not re-open locked Holm families or convert non-rejects into equivalence claims.

QD-score and eval-to-threshold curves are \emph{not} used for confirmatory rejection. Hold-out surrogate CIs and NMAE algebra: appendix~\S\ref{sec:checkpoint-appendix}.

\section{Shared-checkpoint validity (hold-out algebra)}
\label{sec:checkpoint-appendix}
\label{sec:checkpoint}

Main text leads with scale-normalized NMAE and treats $R^2$ as a secondary, variance-sensitive companion. This appendix records why $R^2$ and MAE can move in opposite directions when the compose gate changes, what the hold-out CI means, and what global hold-out does \emph{not} validate.

\textbf{Archive endpoints are never surrogate scores.} Every confirmatory elite is inserted only after \texttt{evaluate\_candidate} (deterministic simulator fitness/BC). Skipped H2 proposals never enter the archive. Terminal coverage and mean elite fitness therefore cannot be an artifact of surrogate error alone; the surrogate affects \emph{which} candidates are simulated (H2) or which scalars appear in the prompt (H1), not the reported archive metrics.

Offline training on the nightly buffer ($N=35{,}114$ rows; schema~2.1, 24-D features; hold-out $n{=}7{,}023$) yields hold-out metrics for checkpoint \texttt{nightly\_v3\_mc\_d005.pkl}. Protocol amendment 2026-07-28 aligns the offline compose gate with runtime YAML ($0.95$): MAE$_{\text{fitness}}=0.029$, NMAE$=\mathrm{MAE}/\mathrm{std}(y_{\mathrm{gate}})=0.112$, $R^2_{\text{fitness}}=0.94$, MAE$_{\text{stability}}=0.040$, passing \texttt{hints\_ok}.

\textbf{Bootstrap unit.} Reported 95\% CIs (NMAE $[0.107,0.117]$; MAE $[0.027,0.030]$; $R^2$ $[0.94,0.95]$) use $B{=}10{,}000$ nonparametric bootstrap \emph{resamples of hold-out rows} treated as exchangeable (artifact: \texttt{holdout\_compose\_gate\_alignment.json}). Rows from the nightly buffer are not independent maps/seeds; the CI is therefore a within-split precision band and may be optimistically narrow under clustering. We do \emph{not} claim a run-clustered standard error.

\textbf{NMAE denominator (cross-gate).} Within a single compose definition, NMAE$=\mathrm{MAE}/\mathrm{std}(y)$ is the natural scale-free error. Across gates, $\mathrm{std}(y)$ itself changes, so gate-specific NMAE is \emph{not} a fixed-denominator contrast. Prefer MAE for absolute error, or NMAE with denominator locked to the runtime target $\mathrm{std}(y_{0.95}){=}0.255$: that fixed-denominator NMAE is $0.027$ at gate~$0.5$ vs.\ $0.112$ at gate~$0.95$ (MAE $0.0069\!\rightarrow\!0.029$). The headline NMAE$0.052\!\rightarrow\!0.112$ uses each gate's own $\mathrm{std}(y)$ and should not be read as a different estimand than the fixed-denominator pair.

At gate~$0.5$, $96.2\%$ of hold-out labels are exactly zero (std$(y){=}0.133$); at gate~$0.95$, only $29.0\%$ are zero and the continuous mass spreads (std$(y){=}0.255$, $\times 1.92$). Because $R^2{=}1{-}\mathrm{SS}_{\mathrm{res}}/\mathrm{SS}_{\mathrm{tot}}$, larger target variance inflates $R^2$ even as absolute MAE grows ($\times 4.12$). Therefore $R^2{=}0.94$ is \emph{not} a model-quality upgrade over the historical $R^2{=}0.76$ headline---it is the correct metric for the runtime target. The misalignment affected only the offline validity metric; live filter logs always used gate~$0.95$.

\textbf{Arm-conditional validation (open).} Global hold-out does \emph{not} certify accuracy on the proposal streams each arm actually visits. We do not report per-arm online NMAE/MAE, descriptor-space error maps, OOD rates, or uncertainty calibration on live candidates. Partial online gate diagnostics exist only for the non-LLM H2 filter branch: skip anatomy (Table~\ref{tab:skip-mech}) and shadow false-skip / missed-elite rates ($7.3\%$ / $2.6\%$; Table~\ref{tab:h2-ranking-controls}). Full arm-conditional surrogate audits remain Future work. Hold-out metrics do not replace simulator endpoints for H1/H2/H3.

\section{MLP vs.\ GP/UCB ablation (offline)}
\label{sec:surrogate-ablation-appendix}

\emph{Not required for the primary H2 claim.} Production H2 uses an MLP ensemble with MC-dropout and a fitness threshold ($\texttt{min\_fit}{=}0.45$, $\texttt{max\_u}{=}1.0$), not SAIL-style GP+UCB. This appendix records the offline MLP-vs-GP/UCB comparison that motivates freezing that family for the attach-role contrast (\S\ref{sec:surrogate-method}). Hold-out NMAE/$R^2$ select the regressor; they do \emph{not} validate live gate decisions (\S\ref{sec:h2-impl-threat}). On the nightly buffer hold-out at runtime-aligned compose gate~$0.95$, the production MLP achieves NMAE$=0.112$ ($R^2{=}0.94$ on that same target) vs.\ NMAE$=0.222$ ($R^2{=}0.89$) for a scikit-learn GP fitness regressor trained on 5{,}000 rows under the \emph{same} label definition, and is ${\sim}37\times$ faster at a 32{,}500-proposal batch. An earlier M1 snapshot at misaligned gate~$0.5$ reported MLP $R^2{=}0.76$ vs.\ GP ${\approx}0.22$; that gap is not reusable as a model-quality argument because ${\approx}96\%$ of gate-$0.5$ labels are zero (\S\ref{sec:checkpoint-appendix}).

Offline acquisition replay: replacing the MLP with the GP under the \emph{same} threshold gate worsens the false-skip proxy ($0.7\%\!\rightarrow\!3.2\%$); a UCB skip rule on GP scores ($\mu{+}\beta\sigma < \texttt{min\_fit}$, $\beta\in\{0.15,0.5,1.0\}$) stays near $99\%$ skip. On logged Q1 filter proposals ($n{=}325{,}000$), replaying UCB on the \emph{same} MLP $\mu/\sigma$ only softens the gate (skip $33.5\%\!\rightarrow\!28.5\%/25.7\%/21.2\%$) and never adds skips. With $\texttt{max\_u}{=}1.0$, uncertainty is not load-bearing for the production threshold gate. GP+UCB is therefore a related SAIL lineage, not a competitive drop-in. Tree boosters (XGBoost / LightGBM) are not part of this ablation; the repository's optional LightGBM path is disclosed in the main text as a non-confirmatory alternative.

\section{Primary evaluator details}
\label{sec:substrate-appendix}

Main-text \S\ref{sec:substrate} sketches genome, simulator, and behaviour axes. This appendix records fitness composition algebra and a computational edit-anatomy case study (LLM vs.\ genetic parent$\rightarrow$child diffs)---not domain claims.

\paragraph{Genome and evaluation.}
Emitters search a 21-D encoding (18 birth/survival rule bits + noise / resource\_regen / predation). Each evaluation runs a fixed $50\times50$, $T_{\mathrm{sim}}{=}200$ toroidal grid; a canonical SHA-256 seed of the genome makes fitness/BC a deterministic function of the genotype. Early extinction yields archive fitness~0.

\paragraph{Behaviour and fitness.}
Archive axes are stability and diversity in $[0,1]$. Authoritative insertion fitness recomposes diversity, survival, oscillation, and topology proxies. All fitness components below are dimensionless scores already scaled to $[0,1]$ before weighting (topology indices TI/TH are likewise clipped into $[0,1]$ before averaging):
\begin{equation}
  E_{\mathrm{ext}} = \mathrm{clip}(1 - \rho_{\mathrm{final}}, 0, 1), \quad
  T_{\mathrm{topo}} = \mathrm{clip}\!\left(\tfrac{\text{TI} + \text{TH}}{2}, 0, 1\right)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:fitness}
  \text{fitness} = \mathrm{clip}\bigl(
    0.45\,D + 0.25\,(1 - E_{\mathrm{ext}}) + 0.20\,A + 0.10\,T_{\mathrm{topo}}
  ,\; 0,\; 1\bigr)
\end{equation}
where $D\in[0,1]$ is clipped diversity, $A\in[0,1]$ is \texttt{oscillation\_score}, and $T_{\mathrm{topo}}\in[0,1]$ is the topology score (distinct from the simulator horizon $T_{\mathrm{sim}}{=}200$ above). The outer $\mathrm{clip}(\cdot,0,1)$ is defensive. The surrogate recomposes the same formula from predicted components (\S\ref{sec:surrogate-method}). QD-score is
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{QD} \;=\; \sum_{c \in \mathrm{filled}} f_c \;=\; |\mathrm{filled}| \cdot \bar f.
  \label{eq:qd}
\end{equation}

\section{Protocol freeze kinds and commit$\leftrightarrow$run timeline}
\label{sec:amendments-appendix}

\emph{Not required to follow the main argument.} Confirmatory families were \emph{prospectively specified in dated internal protocol files} relative to each family's analysis window. That is \emph{not} an externally timestamped pre-registration (no OSF/AsPredicted lock before data). Public \texttt{git} history and filesystem artifact mtimes are integrity aids; they are \emph{mutable} and are not equivalent to an independent registry timestamp. The post-hoc Zenodo deposit (DOI~\texttt{\ZenodoArtifactsDOI}) pins code+artifacts for this revision---an integrity snapshot, not a pre-data preregistration. This appendix only classifies which decisions were freezes, new packages, or reporting-only.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Lock} --- freezes a confirmatory family's endpoints / Holm (or NI) rules in the internal protocol relative to that family's analysis window.
  \item \textbf{Extension} --- adds a new package or repair path; does not rewrite already-frozen families.
  \item \textbf{Reporting} --- records an outcome, descriptive case study, or post-hoc TOST on already-collected paired seeds; not a change of locked family rules.
\end{itemize}

H4 is the explicit exception: reclassified from confirmatory Holm ($m{=}4$) to a descriptive ceiling check without changing the underlying paired deltas. \section{CVT archive sensitivity}
\label{sec:cvt-sensitivity}

\emph{Not required to follow the main argument.} Coverage is a tessellation-dependent construct: the same genotypes map to different niche counts under a $50\times50$ grid vs.\ a CVT. The confirmatory archive geometry was frozen as the $50\times50$ grid before primary-grid readout (protocol v2/v3); CVT is a post-lock \emph{sensitivity} package (\texttt{q1-cvt}), not an alternate confirmatory tessellation and not a re-registration of matched H1 TOST.

\textbf{What transfers (bundled stack only; Table~\ref{tab:cvt}).}
Sign of bundled hints$-$stub coverage: 10/10 on both archives. Terminal ranking of the three bundled arms is preserved in the mid-band sense: stub $\ll$ hints $\approx$ filter (grid ${\approx}45/60/59\%$; CVT ${\approx}37/59/59\%$). Filter evaluation reduction stays in-band (${\sim}31\%$ CVT vs.\ $33.5\%$ grid). Absolute $\Delta$cov is larger on CVT mainly because the stub baseline is lower---do not treat pp gaps as tessellation-invariant.

\textbf{What does not transfer / was not tested.}
Matched-policy H1 TOST (\texttt{stub\_uniform} vs.\ \texttt{hints}) was \emph{not} re-run on CVT, so the exploratory mean-equivalence claim is grid-scoped. We do not compare filled-region geometry (which niches light up) across archives, and we do not claim Holm families or TOST under CVT. Artifact: \texttt{artifacts/Q1\_GRID\_CVT\_ANALYSIS.md}.
