\title{Two Roles for Surrogates in {LLM}-Guided {MAP}-{E}lites: Before- vs.\ After-Generation Surrogates}

\author{Artem Starovoitov\corref{cor1}}
\ead{artem.starovoitov@proton.me}
\ead[url]{https://orcid.org/0009-0005-3170-4865}
\address{Independent Engineer, Moscow, Russia}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author}

\begin{abstract}
In LLM-guided MAP-Elites, a surrogate can attach to the propose$\rightarrow$evaluate loop as a \emph{before-generation surrogate} or an \emph{after-generation surrogate} (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}; synonyms listed once in \S\ref{sec:terminology}). We hold one shared checkpoint fixed and measure the two roles' relative payoff. \textbf{Takeaway:} the after-generation surrogate (Role~2; Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition}) improves coverage per simulation ($\sim$3.7~pp at 15k--20k evaluations; 33.5\% fewer sims at fixed iterations), while the matched-policy before-generation surrogate (RQ1) stays within $\pm 2$~pp (TOST at $n{=}10$; MDE$\approx$1.2~pp). On our cheap evaluator Role~2 is not a terminal-coverage or wall-clock win ($-0.83$~pp; $\sim$20 vs.\ $\sim$7~min). A historical bundled $+15$~pp contrast is confounded (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}). RQ3 (stacking both under LLM) is exploratory: compose-gate divergence blocks confirmatory non-inferiority. After-generation AUC also ports to a second domain. Magnitudes are testbed-specific; caveats in \S\ref{sec:limitations}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Quality-diversity (QD) algorithms maintain archives of solutions that are strong \emph{and} diverse in behaviour space. MAP-Elites and its descendants assign each candidate to a behavioural niche and keep the best elite per cell, enabling illumination of a simulator rather than a single optimum~\cite{mouret2015illuminating}. In practice, every candidate still requires a black-box evaluation---a full simulator roll-out for each proposed world, controller, or design. When that roll-out is costly (CFD, game engines, robotics), evaluation count dominates wall time; surrogate-assisted illumination is motivated for that regime~\cite{gaier2018sail}.

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used as variation operators inside quality-diversity loops: they read structured genotypes or prompts and emit semantically meaningful mutations. An \textbf{LLM-guided MAP-Elites} pipeline therefore couples (i)~an LLM (or hybrid) emitter that proposes candidates, (ii)~a black-box evaluator that buys ground truth, and (iii)~an archive that keeps elites. The LLM does \emph{not} observe simulator outcomes unless the loop explicitly feeds feedback into the prompt; surrogates are a natural way to supply that feedback before or after generation. Surrogate-assisted illumination (SAIL) historically emphasized after-generation gating~\cite{gaier2018sail}; before-generation uses of predictors are common in LLM search but rarely compared head-to-head inside one architecture.

Previous work often bundled before- and after-generation surrogate roles together---and sometimes with archive policy---making it difficult to attribute observed gains.

\textbf{Positioning.} The object of this paper is LLM-guided MAP-Elites design: when should a surrogate attach in the propose$\rightarrow$evaluate loop? The headline contribution is empirical disentanglement of the before-generation surrogate from the after-generation surrogate under one shared checkpoint. LifeManifold is only the controlled evaluator used to measure payoffs (\S\ref{sec:substrate}).

\textbf{Scope of claims.} The before-/after-generation split is an architectural formalization of propose$\rightarrow$evaluate, not a newly invented mechanism. Reported magnitudes are testbed-specific (internally frozen protocol). The positive Role~2 result is evaluation-indexed on our cheap evaluator, not a wall-clock or terminal-coverage claim. RQ3 (stacking both slots under LLM) is exploratory because compose-gate non-inferiority is blocked. Full threats to validity are consolidated in \S\ref{sec:limitations}.

\subsection{Before- and after-generation surrogates}
\label{sec:terminology}

\begin{quote}
\textit{Surrogate information can be injected before generation or after generation; those times are not interchangeable.}
\end{quote}

A MAP-Elites (or any QD) iteration is a short causal chain: select parents $\rightarrow$ \textbf{generate} a candidate $\rightarrow$ decide whether to \textbf{evaluate} it on the simulator $\rightarrow$ update the archive. Surrogate knowledge can intervene at essentially two moments on that chain (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}):

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Before-generation surrogate} --- predictions enter the emitter and try to change \emph{what} is proposed.
  \item \textbf{After-generation surrogate} --- predictions gate the candidate and change \emph{whether} it spends ground truth.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Terminology (canonical names).}
Throughout the paper we use \textbf{before-generation surrogate} and \textbf{after-generation surrogate} for these two roles.
Synonyms appear \emph{only here}: before-generation $\equiv$ prompt hints / prompt guidance / semantic integration; after-generation $\equiv$ acquisition filtering / candidate filtering / evaluation-time surrogate / evaluation integration.
Experiment labels are not alternate role names: \textbf{RQ1} tests the before-generation surrogate; \textbf{Role~2} tests the after-generation surrogate (headline). Prompt placeholders and threshold gates are mechanisms \emph{inside} a role.

Those slots are forced by propose$\rightarrow$evaluate---the after-generation surrogate is SAIL-\emph{kin} as a hard evaluation gate; the before-generation surrogate (RQ1) never skips simulation. What is missing from prior work, and what this paper supplies, is a \textbf{matched within-architecture comparison}: same checkpoint, same archive/budget/emitters, before vs.\ after (with stacking under LLM reported as RQ3). Surrogate-informed parent selection would be a third, earlier slot; we do not implement it. Archive \texttt{target\_selection} remains ordinary MAP-Elites policy.

What an empirical study can test is their \textbf{relative payoff}: does the before-generation surrogate move coverage, and does the after-generation surrogate improve sample efficiency? In our testbed, the after-generation surrogate wins on the evaluation axis (Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition} vs.\ \S\ref{sec:decomposition}): matched-policy RQ1 is flat within $\pm 2$~pp at $n{=}10$ (post-hoc TOST), while Role~2 improves sample efficiency. The memory figure for that Role~2 claim is the matched \texttt{genetic\_me\_uniform} vs.\ \texttt{genetic\_me\_filter} anytime plot (Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition}); we defer it until the Role~2 results section, after the RQ1 control. A design hypothesis follows: use the before-generation surrogate when the need is directional advice, and the after-generation surrogate when the need is evaluation efficiency. Stage-dependent scheduling of the two roles remains a conjecture (\S\ref{sec:bootstrap-hyp}).

\subsection{Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Honest disentanglement of two surrogate roles.} We separate the before-generation surrogate from the after-generation surrogate under one shared checkpoint, matched emitters, archive, and budget (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}; \S\ref{sec:decomposition}--\ref{sec:role2}).
  \item \textbf{The previously observed ``hints'' lift is almost entirely \emph{not} hint content.} A locked bundled stub$\rightarrow$hints contrast looks large ($\approx{+}15$~pp coverage) but confounds policy with surrogate use. At matched \texttt{uniform\_frontier}, target selection alone recovers $\approx{+}14.8$~pp, while live prompt scalars vs.\ stub constants are flat within $\pm 2$~pp (RQ1; $n{=}10$; post-hoc TOST). Enriched / weak-model pilots ($n{\le}3$) stay inside the same band. The historical reading ``surrogate numbers in the prompt work'' does not survive the split.
  \item \textbf{The after-generation surrogate is the positive payoff.} Role~2---threshold gating without an LLM---improves coverage \emph{per simulation} by skipping predicted low-fitness / high-extinction / low-diversity proposals ($\sim$3.7~pp at 15k--20k evaluations; 33.5\% fewer sims; Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition}; mechanism in \S\ref{sec:why-filter}). That is the attach role that carries the surrogate gain. On this cheap evaluator the same gate is not a terminal-coverage or wall-clock win ($-0.83$~pp at fixed iterations; $\sim$20 vs.\ $\sim$7~min); the claim is evaluation-indexed sample efficiency, with expensive-sim usefulness as the motivating regime (\S\ref{sec:cost}).
  \item \textbf{Design implication + reproducible protocol.} If an LLM-guided QD stack already uses a surrogate, prefer the after-generation surrogate (Role~2) over the before-generation surrogate (RQ1). Internally frozen families, open artifacts, and a pyribs continuous-relaxation reference make the split re-checkable (\S\ref{sec:protocol}); stacking both under LLM (RQ3) remains exploratory while compose-gate non-inferiority is blocked.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background and related work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Quality-diversity and MAP-Elites}

Quality-diversity (QD) algorithms seek collections of high-performing yet behaviourally diverse solutions rather than a single optimum~\cite{pugh2016quality,cully2018quality,chatzilygeroudis2021qd}. MAP-Elites maintains an archive partitioned by user-defined behavioural characteristics (BC) and retains the best elite per niche~\cite{mouret2015illuminating}. Heterogeneous emitter sets can improve illumination efficiency relative to a single variation operator~\cite{cully2020multiemitter}; our confirmatory stack mixes random, genetic, and LLM slots in one batch.

Continuous variation operators remain important on fixed-length genomes. Covariance Matrix Adaptation MAP-Elites (CMA-ME) couples CMA-ES adaptation with MAP-Elites archiving and often dominates random or isotropic mutation on continuous control and design tasks~\cite{fontaine2020cmame}. CMA-MAE extends the idea with MAP-annealing and separate result archives, trading coverage for per-niche quality~\cite{fontaine2023cmamae,zhao2024cmamae}. We implement both through \texttt{ribs} v0.11.0~\cite{pyribs2020} with an adapter that evaluates candidates on the same black-box simulator as the LLM stack (\S\ref{sec:method}). Our pyribs baselines use homogeneous CMA-ES emitter pools rather than mixed pyribs ``cocktails,'' so they represent strong but not maximal pyribs configurations.

\subsection{Surrogate-assisted illumination}

Expensive simulators motivate surrogate models that predict outcomes from static design features~\cite{jin2005fitness,jones1998ego}.
Surrogate-Assisted Illumination (SAIL) is the canonical QD answer: fit a Gaussian process over the behaviour--fitness landscape, run MAP-Elites on an acquisition surface (classically UCB-style), and spend real evaluations only on a selected subset~\cite{gaier2018sail,gaier2017sail,gaier2017aiaa}.
Deep Surrogate Assisted MAP-Elites (DSA-ME) replaces the GP with neural predictors trained online in an inner loop and validates candidates in an outer game-engine loop~\cite{grillotti2022dsame}.
Related Bayesian QD lines couple acquisition to descriptor and fitness models~\cite{kent2025boqd}.
Discount Model Search continues the surrogate-QD line without LLMs, replacing histogram discounts with a learned continuous discount model for high-dimensional measure spaces~\cite{tjanaka2026dms}.
Across this family the surrogate is a \emph{search accelerator}: it can change which candidates receive ground truth, and often \emph{is} the surface being illuminated rather than a side channel into an otherwise unchanged MAP-Elites loop.

Reported evaluation savings are strongest in computational design.
On two-dimensional airfoil tasks SAIL needs orders-of-magnitude fewer flow simulations than MAP-Elites while filling behaviour bins~\cite{gaier2018sail}; with the budget CMA-ES requires to optimize one bin, SAIL can match that quality in every bin~\cite{gaier2017sail}.
Three-dimensional aerodynamics and high-dimensional discrete deckbuilding show the same qualitative pattern~\cite{gaier2017aiaa,grillotti2022dsame}.
Evidence for stochastic simulators is thinner: these studies assume expensive but largely deterministic evaluators or batched outer validation, not single-rollout archive updates under seeded simulator noise.
Our confirmatory tier holds the simulator budget fixed (32{,}500 evaluations per seed) and asks, for LLM-guided QD, \emph{where} the same surrogate should attach under full-simulation archive updates---not whether a new SAIL-style illuminator brand wins on expensive CFD.

\textbf{Positioning vs.\ SAIL / DSA-ME.}
Our stack differs from those lineages on four points that matter for the before/after framework.
(i)~\emph{Model:} a multi-task MLP ensemble with MC-dropout over genome features (optional LightGBM path), not a monolithic GP fitness surface; an offline hold-out ablation prefers the production MLP over a sklearn GP on composed fitness ($R^2{=}0.76$ [95\% CI $0.70{,}0.82$] vs.\ ${\approx}0.22$) and rejects GP+UCB as a drop-in acquisition upgrade (\S\ref{sec:acquisition}).
(ii)~\emph{Attach points:} the before-generation surrogate \textbf{never skips} \texttt{evaluate\_candidate}---it only supplies scalars to the LLM prompt---while the after-generation surrogate optionally gates with a production \texttt{threshold\_gate} ($\texttt{min\_fit}{=}0.45$, $\texttt{max\_u}{=}1.0$), not SAIL's GP-UCB illuminator.
(iii)~\emph{Truth vs.\ guidance:} prompt fitness is recomposed from predicted components with the same closed form as the simulator (\S\ref{eq:fitness}), but archive insertion always uses simulated metrics; DSA-ME's online inner/outer split is closer to the after-generation surrogate than to the before-generation surrogate, yet still does not inject surrogate text into an LLM emitter.
(iv)~\emph{Disclosure:} we report an explicit compose-gate mismatch (D1) between offline and online extinction thresholds that blocks confirmatory LLM+filter non-inferiority---honesty that SAIL-style papers rarely need when fitness definitions stay matched.
We therefore treat SAIL/DSA-ME as related surrogate-QD lineages rather than drop-in baselines; pyribs CMA-ME/CMA-MAE (\S\ref{sec:pyribs}) are the quantitative QD baselines for this protocol.

\subsection{LLMs in evolutionary and QD search}

LLMs have been used as variation operators, prompt optimizers, and evolutionary engines~\cite{wu2025ecllm,wang2025llmea,vanstein2024llamea,guo2024evoprompt,liu2024llmeo,lange2024llmes}. Program-search lines pair a frozen LLM with an external evaluator to evolve code or rewards (FunSearch; Eureka)~\cite{romera2024funsearch,ma2024eureka}; Promptbreeder evolves task and mutation prompts self-referentially~\cite{fernando2024promptbreeder}; OpenELM surveys open-ended LLM evolution~\cite{lehman2023openelm}. Closest to our QD setting, LLMatic couples LLM code generation with CVT-MAP-Elites for neural architecture search~\cite{nasir2024llmatic}, and in-context QD generators sample diverse solutions from LLM priors~\cite{lim2024llmqd}. Behaviour-space analyses of LLaMEA further compare dual-prompt mutation strategies on anytime search dynamics~\cite{vanstein2025behaviour}. Concurrent LLM+MAP-Elites applications include D{\'e}j{\`a}Q's learnability-guided curation of RL-with-verifiable-rewards datasets~\cite{ropke2026dejaq} and medical decision-pipeline evolution without a surrogate/gating layer~\cite{sviridov2026medical}---both use LLM variation in a QD loop, but neither treat before- vs.\ after-generation surrogates as an experimental factor. Procedural-content pipelines apply evolutionary search and LLMs to level generators and structured genotypes~\cite{togelius2010sbpcg,earle2022nca}; emitters must respect simulators that validate and repair invalid outputs (as in our dungeon domain, \S\ref{sec:results}). Open-ended CA worlds have likewise been illuminated with QD archives~\cite{faldor2024lenia}.

A complementary line treats the \emph{prompt itself} as the search space: Diverse Prompts applies MAP-Elites over prompt phenotypes for BigBench Lite tasks~\cite{santos2025diverseprompts}, evolutionary sampling explores instruction diversity more broadly~\cite{saletta2024promptspace}, and parameter-efficient neuroevolution steers frozen LLMs by evolving prompt embeddings rather than using the LLM as a genotype emitter~\cite{guo2026qdllm}. Those settings illuminate prompts (or embeddings) for a fixed downstream LLM call, whereas we mutate \texttt{WorldSpec} genomes and inject simulator-derived \emph{surrogate scalars} into the mutation prompt. A different surrogate use keeps the LLM on fitness estimation: LLM-assisted evolutionary algorithms (LAEA) and related LLM-driven SAEAs prompt a model to classify or regress candidate quality~\cite{hao2024laea,xie2025llmsae}. We instead pair a genome-aligned ML regressor (trained on simulator logs) with an LLM \emph{emitter} that edits structured genotypes---the LLM is \emph{not} the surrogate.

\subsection{Seeded simulators and uncertain QD}

Prior LLM+QD systems often evaluate on deterministic or batched offline tasks. Our confirmatory evaluations are \emph{deterministically seeded} from the genotype (canonical SHA-256), so re-evaluating the same candidate reproduces the same fitness and BC even when the underlying simulator rules are stochastic. Elites are therefore not lucky-seed artifacts in the UQD sense.

Uncertain QD (UQD) formalizes the complementary setting where fitness and BC are distributions and archive policies re-sample to limit lucky outliers~\cite{flageat2023uq,flageat2025extractqd,flageat2025tradeoff}. We do \emph{not} claim that confirmatory results stress-test UQD: every inserted elite uses a single seeded pass, and tests use paired experiment seeds with Holm correction on terminal coverage and mean elite fitness (\S\ref{sec:protocol}). We separate \emph{archive truth} (always simulated) from \emph{emitter guidance} (before- and/or after-generation surrogates). Archive target-selection policy is orthogonal to those two surrogate roles. UQD methods remain relevant if future work disables canonical seeding or budgets multiple independent rollouts per niche.

\noindent\textit{Key difference from SAIL:} we do not replace MAP-Elites search with a GP acquisition illuminator; we ask \emph{where} a fixed MLP checkpoint attaches in an LLM-guided propose$\rightarrow$evaluate loop, and we gate with a fitness threshold rather than UCB over a GP surface (\S\ref{sec:acquisition}). \textit{Key difference from LAEA:} the LLM never estimates fitness---the MLP does; the LLM only proposes structured genomes.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

We study an LLM-guided MAP-Elites propose$\rightarrow$evaluate loop with a shared surrogate that can attach as a before-generation surrogate or an after-generation surrogate. Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} is the architectural map; LifeManifold is only the measurement substrate (\S\ref{sec:substrate}). Archive fitness always comes from the simulator---the surrogate never replaces ground truth for insertion.

\subsection{MAP-Elites illuminator loop}

Each run executes \texttt{iterations} $\times$ \texttt{batch\_size} candidate slots in fixed order. Production schedulers use $650 \times 50 = 32{,}500$ simulator calls per seed. For slot~$k$ the loop:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item selects a target niche (behaviour-cell centre) according to \texttt{target\_selection};
  \item calls the slot emitter (\texttt{random}, \texttt{genetic}, or \texttt{llm}) to propose a \texttt{WorldSpec};
  \item optionally applies surrogate acquisition (\S\ref{sec:acquisition});
  \item runs \texttt{evaluate\_candidate} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{run\_world} (always for accepted slots in MVP hint mode);
  \item inserts into the archive if fitness strictly improves the cell incumbent;
  \item appends training data to the surrogate buffer when \texttt{surrogate.enabled}.
\end{enumerate}
LLM slots log \texttt{llm\_fallback} when parsing fails and a genetic repair path is used. Parallel evaluation uses a process pool sized to the batch; LLM emit uses up to four concurrent HTTP workers per iteration. Outputs: \texttt{map\_elites\_archive.jsonl}, optional \texttt{archive\_trace.jsonl}, and \texttt{nightly\_run\_summary.json}.

\textbf{Target selection (not a surrogate role).} \texttt{min\_fitness\_frontier} picks the occupied cell with lowest elite fitness (used by the bundled \texttt{stub} arm). \texttt{uniform\_frontier} samples uniformly among occupied cells (used by \texttt{hints}, filter arms, and matched RQ1/Role~2 controls). These are archive policies over occupied niches; they do \emph{not} use the surrogate to select parents. RQ1 holds this policy fixed (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}). Surrogate-assisted parent/niche selection is out of scope here.

\subsection{Surrogate model}
\label{sec:surrogate-method}

The surrogate maps a canonical \texttt{WorldSpec} to predicted MAP-Elites outcomes without a simulator call (Figure~\ref{fig:surrogate}). Feature schema~2.1 encodes 24 genome-aligned dimensions (discrete rule bits and continuous scalars; \S\ref{sec:substrate}). A multi-head regressor (\textbf{Strategy~A}) predicts seven component targets---stability, diversity, oscillation, two topology proxies, final density, and early-extinction probability---trained from JSONL buffer rows appended after each real evaluation when the surrogate is enabled.

Production checkpoint \texttt{nightly\_v3\_mc\_d005.pkl} is a four-member MLP ensemble with MC-dropout uncertainty ($N=35{,}114$ training rows; hold-out $R^2_{\text{fitness}}=0.76$ [95\% CI $0.70{,}0.82$], MAE$_{\text{fitness}}=0.007$ [$0.005{,}0.008$]; \S\ref{sec:rq2}). Runtime fitness is recomposed via \texttt{compute\_fitness\_from\_prediction} using Eq.~\eqref{eq:fitness} (same weights as the simulator). \texttt{get\_surrogate()} falls back to constant \texttt{StubSurrogate} values if the checkpoint is missing, dimension-mismatched, or fails the \texttt{hints\_ok} gate ($R^2 \ge 0.30$, MAE $< 0.085$).

\textbf{Before-generation surrogate (RQ1).} Before each LLM call, \texttt{SurrogateFacade.predict(parent)} runs on the \emph{parent} elite's spec. Placeholders \texttt{\{surrogate\_mean\}} and \texttt{\{surrogate\_uncertainty\}} in the user prompt receive \texttt{prediction.fitness} and calibrated ensemble spread. When \texttt{surrogate.enabled=false}, fixed YAML stubs (\texttt{stub\_mean}, \texttt{stub\_uncertainty}) are substituted without calling \texttt{predict}. One LLM HTTP request per LLM slot regardless of surrogate mode.

\subsection{LLM emitter}

The \texttt{LlmEmitter} sends the parent \texttt{WorldSpec} JSON and niche centre to a chat-completions API; the model returns a JSON patch applied via \texttt{world\_spec\_from\_llm}. Stack~v2 uses temperature~0.2, \texttt{max\_tokens=350}, and \texttt{llm\_parallel\_workers=4}. Primary LLM runs use Alibaba \texttt{qwen-turbo} (DashScope); multi-provider replications swap provider YAML while keeping prompts and schedulers fixed (\S\ref{sec:results}). Model IDs are window-pinned in run summaries (\texttt{llm\_spec\_hash}); floating endpoint aliases are disclosed per provider.

\textbf{What LLM mutations change (qualitative).} Archive JSONL stores \texttt{parent\_id} and \texttt{generated\_by}, so accepted LLM elites can be compared to their parents. On \texttt{hints} seed~4, of 779 LLM elites with a resolvable parent and nonzero genome diff, typical edits are structured rule and scalar tweaks rather than random bit noise---for example: (i)~add birth rule~6 and replace survival~8 by~7 while nudging \texttt{noise}/\texttt{resource\_regen}/\texttt{predation}; (ii)~shift birth $\{0,1,3\}\!\to\!\{0,1,4\}$ and survival $\{0,1,2\}\!\to\!\{0,1,3\}$ with larger scalar moves, jumping stability $0.09\!\to\!0.37$; (iii)~add birth~7 on a high-fitness parent with a small diversity retreat; (iv)~extend survival with neighbour count~7 and raise \texttt{resource\_regen}. These examples support calling LLM variation \emph{semantic} (rule-table edits + coordinated scalars) without claiming that such edits improve QD once target selection is matched.

\subsection{After-generation surrogate (Role~2)}
\label{sec:acquisition}

Optional \texttt{acquisition.mode=filter} evaluates \texttt{surrogate.predict} \emph{before} \texttt{evaluate\_candidate}. A \texttt{threshold\_gate} skips simulation when predicted fitness falls below \texttt{min\_predicted\_fitness=0.45} (primary grid) subject to uncertainty calibration. Skipped slots are logged to \texttt{surrogate\_archive.jsonl} but do not update the MAP-Elites archive. \texttt{shadow} mode logs counterfactual skips while still evaluating.

\textbf{Why MLP + threshold gate, not SAIL-style GP+UCB.}
We use an MLP ensemble with MC-dropout uncertainty and a fitness threshold gate ($\texttt{min\_fit}{=}0.45$, $\texttt{max\_u}{=}1.0$), rather than a SAIL-style GP+UCB acquisition.
On the nightly buffer hold-out, the production MLP achieves $R^2{=}0.76$ (95\% CI $[0.70, 0.82]$) vs.\ ${\approx}0.22$ for a scikit-learn GP fitness regressor trained on 5{,}000 rows, and is ${\sim}12\times$ faster at a 32{,}500-proposal batch.
Offline acquisition replay shows that replacing the MLP with the GP under the \emph{same} threshold gate worsens the false-skip proxy ($0.7\%\!\rightarrow\!3.2\%$); a UCB skip rule on GP scores ($\mu{+}\beta\sigma < \texttt{min\_fit}$, $\beta\in\{0.15,0.5,1.0\}$) stays near $99\%$ skip and does not improve false-skip.
On logged Q1 filter proposals ($n{=}325{,}000$), replaying UCB on the \emph{same} MLP $\mu/\sigma$ only softens the gate (skip $33.5\%\!\rightarrow\!28.5\%/25.7\%/21.2\%$) and never adds skips---still without claiming an online QD win for UCB.
With $\texttt{max\_u}{=}1.0$, uncertainty is not load-bearing for the production threshold gate.
We therefore treat GP+UCB as a related SAIL lineage, not a competitive drop-in for this stack (artifact: \texttt{artifacts/surrogate/gp\_ucb\_ablation.json}).

The locked compose-gate check for LLM+filter non-inferiority remains \textbf{blocked}: live proposal replay at threshold~0.45 yields divergent skip rate $d=0.528$ (required $\le 0.05$). RQ3 is therefore \textbf{descriptive / exploratory by design} in this version---not a deferred footnote to a confirmatory claim. Role~2 reports matched \texttt{genetic\_me\_uniform} vs.\ \texttt{genetic\_me\_filter} as the primary filter evidence (\S\ref{sec:role2}).

\subsection{pyribs CMA-ME / CMA-MAE baselines}
\label{sec:pyribs}

Gradient QD baselines run through \texttt{run\_pyribs\_baseline.py} (\texttt{ribs==0.11.0}), \emph{not} as native \texttt{EmitterKind} types. The adapter maps the same 21-D genetic genome to \texttt{WorldSpec}, evaluates with the same simulator as the LLM stack, and writes comparable JSONL archives. Each algorithm uses \textbf{five homogeneous} \texttt{EvolutionStrategyEmitter} instances (batch~50 each $\Rightarrow$ ask size~250 per generation), initial step size $\sigma_0=0.2$, and 32{,}500 total evaluations. CMA-ME uses learning rate~1.0 with 2-improvement ranking and filter selection; CMA-MAE uses learning rate~0.01, improvement ranking, and a separate result archive. This is a strong but not maximal pyribs configuration (no mixed ImprovementEmitter / RandomDirectionEmitter cocktail). We treat pyribs comparisons as cross-pipeline references alongside the in-repo heterogeneous MAP-Elites stack---same honesty bar as the continuous-relaxation caveat: encoding and initialization are disclosed below, not hidden advantages.

\textbf{Encoding (continuous relaxation; apples-to-oranges caveat for arXiv v1).}
CMA's \texttt{EvolutionStrategyEmitter} proposes continuous $\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{21}$; \texttt{decode\_genome} then \emph{rints} the 18 rule bits and clips the three scalars before simulation. Genetic / LLM emitters, by contrast, edit discrete rule tables (and named scalars) directly. The comparison is therefore \emph{not} bit-identical variation: CMA searches a continuous relaxation of a mixed discrete/continuous genotype, where covariance adaptation can exploit local structure that bit-flip genetics never sees. We treat the pyribs arms as an \textbf{upper-bound style ceiling for continuous-friendly representations} of this genome---not as a fair discrete-vs-discrete duel. A discrete CMA-style emitter is \emph{implementable} in pyribs via a custom \texttt{EmitterBase}; we run two supplementary controls (\S\ref{sec:ceiling}): \textbf{Bernoulli-decode} CMA-ME (package~C4; continuous proposal, discrete bits at eval) and a \textbf{native bit-flip} \texttt{DiscreteCMAEmitter} (\texttt{q1-v3-pyribs-native-discrete-cma}; discrete genotype in archive). Decode/cold-start ablation shows the hints$\ll$CMA gap survives alternate \emph{continuous} decodes; C4 survives Bernoulli decode vs.\ hints; native discrete search confirms the gap vs.\ hints ($+5.2$~pp) but trails both continuous arms ($-7.8$~pp vs.\ \texttt{rint}), so continuous covariance adaptation—not decode alone—accounts for much of the ceiling. Attack directions on the continuous path cut both ways: (i)~rint may blunt CMA's local model (understate a fully continuous ceiling), or (ii)~the $[0,1]$ relaxation before rounding may overstate CMA relative to bit flips. We keep this encoding because it is the shared genotype interface (\texttt{encode\_world}/\texttt{decode\_genome}) used by genetic ME and the pyribs adapter, so fitness/BC definitions stay matched. Analytic sphere/rastrigin checks validate the pyribs runner without bit-rounding.

\textbf{Warm-start (symmetric).} All confirmatory primary-grid arms---\texttt{vanilla}, genetic ME variants, \texttt{stub}/\texttt{hints}/\texttt{filter}, and pyribs CMA-ME/MAE---load the \emph{same} nightly grid baseline JSONL ($\sim$2{,}614 elites) via \texttt{--load-archive} before the 32{,}500-evaluation budget. Terminal coverage is reported after warm-start merge/collapse. The pyribs contrast is matched on initialization as well as evaluation count; asymmetries arise from emitter pipelines and continuous-relaxation encoding, not from unequal archive seeds.

\subsection{Measurement substrate}
\label{sec:substrate}

Experiments use \emph{LifeManifold} as a controlled black-box evaluator---not as the scientific subject. Candidates are structured genotypes (\texttt{WorldSpec}: birth/survival rule tables plus three continuous scalars) evaluated by a seeded simulator into a $50\times50$ stability--diversity archive. The evaluator is cheap relative to CFD-style SAIL domains, so we treat evaluation-indexed efficiency as the primary after-generation claim and report wall time separately (\S\ref{sec:cost}). Domain knobs are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:domain}; Figure~\ref{fig:elites} shows example elites.

\paragraph{Genome and evaluation.} Emitters search a 21-D encoding (18 rule bits + noise / resource\_regen / predation). Each evaluation runs a fixed $50\times50$, $T{=}200$ toroidal grid; a canonical SHA-256 seed of the genome makes fitness/BC a deterministic function of the genotype (stochastic update rules, deterministic eval). Early extinction (life density reaches zero) yields archive fitness~0.

\paragraph{Behaviour and fitness.} Archive axes are stability and diversity in $[0,1]$. Authoritative insertion fitness recomposes diversity, survival, oscillation, and topology proxies:
\begin{equation}
  E_{\mathrm{ext}} = \mathrm{clip}(1 - \rho_{\mathrm{final}}, 0, 1), \quad
  T = \mathrm{clip}\!\left(\tfrac{\text{TI} + \text{TH}}{2}, 0, 1\right)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:fitness}
  \text{fitness} = \mathrm{clip}\bigl(
    0.45\,D + 0.25\,(1 - E_{\mathrm{ext}}) + 0.20\,\mathrm{clip}(A,0,1) + 0.10\,T
  ,\; 0,\; 1\bigr)
\end{equation}
where $D$ is clipped diversity, $A$ is \texttt{oscillation\_score}, and TI/TH are topology indices. The surrogate recomposes the same formula from predicted components (\S\ref{sec:surrogate-method}).

\paragraph{Archive metrics.} Coverage is the percentage of occupied cells after warm-start merge; mean best fitness averages elites over filled cells; QD-score is
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{QD} \;=\; \sum_{c \in \mathrm{filled}} f_c \;=\; |\mathrm{filled}| \cdot \bar f,
  \label{eq:qd}
\end{equation}
reported descriptively only. Primary archives use a fixed $50\times50$ grid; CVT geometry is appendix sensitivity only~\cite{vassiliades2018cvt}.

\section{Experimental protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

Experiments follow \emph{internally frozen} Q1 protocols v2/v3
(LifeManifold CA; freeze date 2026-07-12) and v4 (dungeon factorial;
freeze date 2026-07-17). We use ``confirmatory'' for families locked in
those documents. This submission does \emph{not} claim an external
OSF/Zenodo pre-registration timestamp (decision locked 2026-07-22: keep
in-repo freeze dates). Descriptive tiers and pilot arms are not pooled
into Holm tests.

\subsection{Design principles}

\textbf{Paired seeds.} All primary CA contrasts use the same integer seeds $0,\ldots,9$ across conditions within a tier. The dungeon factorial uses the same seed indices with independent domain RNG.

\textbf{Primary archive and warm-start.} Confirmatory CA endpoints use the \textbf{50$\times$50 grid}. Every CA arm in the batch driver warm-starts from the same baseline JSONL before the matched evaluation budget (\S\ref{sec:pyribs}).

\textbf{Multiple comparisons (family-wise, not paper-wise).} Each confirmatory family applies Holm step-down on one-sided Wilcoxon tests at $\alpha=0.05$. A family \textbf{passes} only if every test in that family rejects and local effect-size gates are met (paired $d_z \ge 0.5$ on both primary metrics where specified in the frozen protocol). Families are \emph{not} merged into one paper-wide Holm family (appendix Table~\ref{tab:families} lists roughly seven--eight locked families: F-RQ1, F-RQ0, F-RQ-ceiling, F-B4-dungeon, F-RQ1g per provider, \ldots).

\textbf{Why family-wise Type-I control is the intended error budget.} We control the family-wise error rate (FWER) \emph{within} each pre-specified confirmatory family, not a single experiment-wise / paper-wise FWER over every $p$-value in the manuscript. That choice is deliberate and protocol-locked, not a post-hoc split to harvest significance: (i)~each family answers one distinct, pre-declared scientific question (bundled stack smoke test; pyribs continuous-relaxation reference; dungeon AUC transfer; per-provider bundled replication) with its own contrast set and decision rule; (ii)~families were frozen in dated protocol files \emph{before} the corresponding analysis passes, so the partition is not data-driven; (iii)~a paper-wide Holm over unrelated estimands would couple logically independent claims (e.g.\ CA ceiling vs.\ dungeon transfer) into one omnibus gate and over-penalize answers that were never intended as a single joint hypothesis; (iv)~exploratory / pilot / descriptive contrasts (matched RQ1 TOST, $n{=}1$ hint pilots, anytime thresholds, compose-gate-blocked RQ3) are \emph{outside} confirmatory Holm and are labeled as such---they do not spend the confirmatory $\alpha$ budget. The cost of this policy is transparent: the probability of at least one false family-level PASS across the paper is higher than $0.05$ if many independent families are true-null. We accept that trade-off in exchange for interpretable, question-local decisions, and we do \emph{not} claim a paper-wide Type-I guarantee. Reviewers who prefer experiment-wise control may recompute a global Holm over the locked confirmatory $p$-vector in the released statistics JSON; we report family outcomes as the primary protocol claims.

\textbf{Bundled stub vs.\ hints confound (declared).} The locked \texttt{stub} vs.\ \texttt{hints} contrast differs in surrogate use \emph{and} in \texttt{target\_selection} (\S\ref{sec:method}). Scientific RQ1 holds target selection fixed (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}).

\subsection{Endpoints and statistical reporting}
\label{sec:stats}

\textbf{Primary (confirmatory):} terminal \texttt{coverage\_pct} and \texttt{mean\_best\_fitness} on the collapsed archive.

\textbf{Confirmatory vs.\ exploratory tests.} One-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank $p$-values and Holm correction are \emph{inferential} only for the confirmatory families summarized above and listed in appendix Table~\ref{tab:families}. Holm is \emph{family-wise} (FWER within each pre-specified family), not paper-wise: we do not pool all confirmatory $p$-values into one manuscript-level Holm correction, because each family is a distinct locked scientific question rather than a post-hoc slice of a single omnibus test (\S\ref{sec:protocol}, Multiple comparisons). For exploratory / pilot arms (early multi-provider pilots, hint-channel pilots, analytic runner checks, anytime thresholds), we report mean/median paired $\Delta$, sign counts, and bootstrap CIs; any Wilcoxon $p$ shown there is \emph{exploratory} (not a confirmatory claim) and is labeled as such. We never call a $p$-value ``descriptive.'' Matched-policy RQ1 (\texttt{stub\_uniform}) additionally reports a post-hoc TOST equivalence check and a residual-SD minimum detectable effect (MDE) at $n{=}10$ (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}).

\textbf{Paired probability of superiority.} Classic Vargha--Delaney $A_{12}$ is defined for independent samples. On paired seeds we instead report the paired probability of superiority
\begin{equation}
  PS \;=\; \frac{\#\{\Delta_i > 0\} + 0.5\,\#\{\Delta_i = 0\}}{n},
  \label{eq:ps}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_i$ is treatment $-$ control on seed $i$ (for ``less,'' count $\Delta_i < 0$). Tables use the column label $PS$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:ps}), not independent-sample $A_{12}$. $PS=0.5$ is the null; we treat $\sim$0.56/0.64/0.71 as small/medium/large only as rough guides~\cite{vargha2000new}.

\textbf{Other descriptive metrics:} QD-score (Eq.~\eqref{eq:qd}), simulator evaluation count, wall-clock time, LLM fallback rate, anytime curves at matched evaluation budgets where traces exist, paired Cohen's $d_z$, and bootstrap 95\% CIs on median $\Delta$. QD-score and eval-to-threshold curves are \emph{not} used for confirmatory rejection.

\textbf{Scientific RQ2 (surrogate validity):} offline hold-out $R^2$ and MAE on recomposed fitness from checkpoint summaries, with bootstrap 95\% CIs over hold-out rows ($B{=}10{,}000$); supports but does not replace simulator endpoints.

\subsection{Multi-LLM replication}

The primary CA stack uses \texttt{qwen-turbo} on seeds 0--9 (\texttt{q1-full}). We additionally replicate on DeepSeek V4 Pro and OpenAI \texttt{gpt-4o-mini} on seeds 0--9 with identical prompts and schedulers. \textbf{No cross-model pooling.} Each provider is a separate experiment: API decoding is stochastic, tokenizers and context packing differ, and absolute coverage levels are not exchangeable (Table~\ref{tab:g1}). We therefore never average $\Delta$ across LLMs, never run a single Holm family over pooled provider replicates, and never treat ``mean over models'' as a confirmatory endpoint. Holm is applied \emph{within each provider} at $n{=}10$; models are not merged.

\subsection{Dungeon factorial}

The cross-domain dungeon factorial (\texttt{q1-v4-dungeon-rerun}) uses a 16$\times$16 tile dungeon, separate surrogate calibration (\texttt{checkpoint\_benchmark\_v2.pkl}), and five arms at \textbf{5{,}000 proposals} per seed ($n=10$). Quality gates require LLM fallback $\le 5\%$ and filter skip 25--45\%; all passed before seeds 5--9. \textbf{Confirmatory} dungeon endpoints are AUC at matched real evaluations on this 5k matrix. A \textbf{supplementary} CPU pair (\texttt{q1-v4-dungeon-full-cpu}: \texttt{genetic} vs.\ \texttt{genetic\_filter} only, 32{,}500 proposals, $n=10$) checks Role~2 at CA-matched proposal budget; LLM arms at 32{,}500 proposals are deferred to a journal extension (protocol amendment before runs). Protocol v4 §6 notes that coverage-threshold timing metrics were not pre-specified before the first pilot and are descriptive only.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

All confirmatory tests use paired seeds ($n=10$ unless noted), one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on $\Delta = \text{hints} - \text{control}$, and Holm step-down within each confirmatory family ($\alpha=0.05$; \S\ref{sec:stats}). Primary endpoints are archive coverage (\%) and mean best elite fitness on the collapsed 50$\times$50 grid. Effect-size columns report paired $PS$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:ps}). Narrative order is RQ1 (before-generation) $\rightarrow$ Role~2 (after-generation; headline) $\rightarrow$ RQ3 (exploratory interaction).

\subsection{RQ2 --- Surrogate validity}
\label{sec:rq2}

Offline training on the nightly buffer ($N=35{,}114$ rows; schema~2.1, 24-D features; hold-out $n{=}7{,}023$) yields hold-out metrics for checkpoint \texttt{nightly\_v3\_mc\_d005.pkl}: $R^2_{\text{fitness}}=0.76$ (bootstrap 95\% CI $[0.70, 0.82]$), MAE$_{\text{fitness}}=0.007$ ($[0.005, 0.008]$), MAE$_{\text{stability}}=0.040$ ($[0.038, 0.042]$), passing both the \texttt{hints\_ok} and production quality gates. CIs resample hold-out rows ($B{=}10{,}000$), matching the bootstrap reporting used for paired $\Delta$ elsewhere. Scientific RQ2 is therefore supported at the offline gate: the same checkpoint is accurate enough to feed both the before- and after-generation surrogates. Hold-out metrics do not replace simulator endpoints for RQ1/RQ3/Role~2.

\subsection{After-generation surrogate / Role~2 (headline positive)}
\label{sec:role2}

Role~2 is the \textbf{headline empirical result}: matched no-LLM \texttt{genetic\_me\_uniform} vs.\ \texttt{genetic\_me\_filter} (\texttt{uniform\_frontier}, threshold gate, $n=10$; Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition}). We treat \textbf{evaluation-indexed} coverage as the primary Role~2 axis (Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition}). At equal evaluation budgets, filter leads by $+3.65$~pp at 20{,}000 evaluations (10/10 seeds) and reaches 50\%/55\% coverage with 28\%/29\% fewer evaluations. At fixed 650 iterations (matched proposal slots, not matched sims), filter uses 33.5\% fewer simulator calls but finishes $-0.83$~pp below uniform ($58.21\%$ vs.\ $59.04\%$ coverage) and is \emph{slower} in wall time ($\sim$20 vs.\ $\sim$7~min). Together with matched-policy RQ1 flat within $\pm 2$~pp at $n{=}10$ (post-hoc TOST), Role~2 dominates RQ1 on the evaluation axis.

\textbf{Why MLP can cost more than skipped sims.} This is not mysterious invoice noise: acquisition still runs \texttt{predict\_batch} on \emph{every} proposal in the batch (including those later skipped), using a four-member MLP ensemble with MC-dropout uncertainty ($p{=}0.05$) in Python before the cheap simulator roll-out. Simulator evaluations are process-pooled; surrogate inference is a per-candidate Python path whose wall share dominates when $t_{\mathrm{sim}}$ is milliseconds. Skipping 33.5\% of sims therefore removes little wall while adding full-batch MLP work---an engineering trade-off on this domain, not evidence that skip-eval cannot help on expensive simulators.

\textbf{Wall-time break-even (descriptive).} Write wall cost per proposal batch as $(1-s)\,t_{\mathrm{sim}}+t_{\mathrm{MLP}}$ with skip rate $s\approx 0.335$ vs.\ $t_{\mathrm{sim}}$ for full eval. Filter wins on wall only if $t_{\mathrm{MLP}}<s\,t_{\mathrm{sim}}$. Matching the observed wall ratio ($\sim$20 vs.\ $\sim$7~min) implies $t_{\mathrm{MLP}}/t_{\mathrm{sim}}\approx 2.2$ on this evaluator, so the simulator would need to be roughly $\mathbf{7\times}$ slower than today before a 33.5\% skip saves wall clock---an extrapolation to expensive domains, not a measured win on the primary testbed.

\subsection{Why the after-generation surrogate wins (mechanism)}
\label{sec:why-filter}

The Role~2 gain is not a black box. Logged \texttt{surrogate\_archive.jsonl} rows for \texttt{genetic\_me\_filter} ($n{=}10$; $325{,}000$ proposal slots) show \emph{what} the gate discards (Table~\ref{tab:skip-mech}). All skips cite \texttt{below\_fitness\_threshold} at $\tau{=}0.45$ (skip rate $33.5\%$). Skipped proposals have mean predicted fitness $0.21$ vs.\ $0.61$ for evaluated ones; $81\%$ of skips have predicted early-extinction probability $>0.8$ vs.\ $<0.1\%$ of evaluated proposals; skipped predicted diversity is low (mean $0.24$; $70\%$ with diversity $<0.3$) while evaluated proposals sit in high-diversity bands (mean $0.74$). Uncertainty barely separates the two classes (medians both $\approx 0.16$), so the operative signal is predicted fitness / extinction risk, not calibrated uncertainty. Random emitters contribute disproportionately to skips ($49\%$ of random slots skipped vs.\ $23\%$ of genetic). Among evaluated proposals, only $12.7\%$ realize true fitness $<0.45$ (pred--true MAE $0.066$), consistent with a gate that removes likely dead/low-diversity mass and reallocates the fixed evaluation budget toward niches that can still fill.

This is a mechanistic reading of the after-generation surrogate: it hard-constrains simulator spend. The before-generation surrogate, by contrast, only soft-advises an LLM that already mutates under a JSON grammar; RQ1's flat matched contrast and no-large-signal enriched pilots are consistent with advice that is easy to ignore. Threshold sensitivity on $\tau{=}0.45$ is deferred.

\subsection{Continuous-relaxation pyribs reference (CMA-ME / CMA-MAE)}
\label{sec:ceiling}

Pyribs baselines (\texttt{q1-v3-pyribs}, five homogeneous CMA-ES emitters, $n=10$) provide a \textbf{protocol control} under the same warm-start and evaluation budget as other primary-grid arms. Because CMA searches a continuous relaxation with \texttt{rint} decode (\S\ref{sec:pyribs}), this section reports an \textbf{upper-bound-style reference for continuous-friendly representations}---not a validated discrete coverage ceiling against genetic/LLM bit-flip emitters. Mean levels: cma\_me $73.37 \pm 3.65$\%, fitness $0.576 \pm 0.028$; cma\_mae $60.56 \pm 2.16$\%, fitness $0.489 \pm 0.007$; hints $60.41 \pm 0.82$\%, fitness $0.539 \pm 0.008$.

\textbf{Encoding ablation} (\texttt{q1-cma-encoding-ablation}, seeds~0--4, descriptive): alternate rule-bit \emph{decode} and cold-start CMA-ME leave the continuous-relaxation gap intact. These runs vary how continuous proposals map to rule bits; they do \emph{not} test discrete search in the same space as genetic/LLM emitters. \texttt{threshold} ($\geq 0.5$ bits) is \emph{bit-identical} to frozen \texttt{rint} on every seed; \texttt{bernoulli} decode is $-0.46$~pp mean ($72.18\%$); empty-archive \texttt{cold} is $-3.47$~pp ($69.17\%$)---still $\geq +8.8$~pp above hints on every arm mean.

\textbf{Bernoulli-decode CMA-ME (package~C4; \texttt{q1-v3-pyribs-discrete-cma}, $n{=}10$, descriptive).} Same warm-start and 32{,}500-evaluation budget; CMA proposes in $\mathbb{R}^{21}$ but rule bits are Bernoulli-sampled at decode (discrete genotypes at evaluation, continuous covariance adaptation unchanged). Mean coverage $71.25 \pm 2.88$\%, fitness $0.578 \pm 0.020$. Paired vs.\ \texttt{hints}: $+10.84 \pm 2.94$~pp (10/10 seeds); vs.\ \texttt{genetic\_me\_uniform}: $+12.22 \pm 2.56$~pp (10/10); vs.\ \texttt{rint} CMA-ME: $-2.12 \pm 4.93$~pp (3/10).

\textbf{Native discrete-search CMA-ME (\texttt{q1-v3-pyribs-native-discrete-cma}, $n{=}10$, descriptive).} Custom \texttt{DiscreteCMAEmitter}: bit-flip on rule bits + Gaussian on floats, discrete $\{0,1\}$ genotype stored in archive, $\sigma$ adapted by a 1/5 success rule (MVP; not pbCMA-ES). Mean coverage $65.57 \pm 3.40$\%, fitness $0.542 \pm 0.028$. Paired vs.\ \texttt{hints}: $+5.16 \pm 3.67$~pp (10/10); vs.\ \texttt{genetic\_me\_uniform}: $+6.54 \pm 3.76$~pp (10/10); vs.\ Bernoulli: $-5.68 \pm 5.90$~pp (1/10); vs.\ \texttt{rint}: $-7.80 \pm 5.07$~pp (0/10). Bit-identical search therefore \emph{does not} close the hints$\ll$CMA gap to the $\sim$13~pp continuous level: native discrete beats hints on every seed but by a smaller margin, and trails continuous/Bernoulli on all ten. Roughly half the continuous-relaxation advantage appears to come from encoding/covariance adaptation rather than from ``any CMA-ME pipeline wins.''

Paired hints $-$ cma\_me: $\Delta\text{cov} = -12.96 \pm 3.46$~pp (0/10 positive), $\Delta\text{fit} = -0.077 \pm 0.029$ (0/10); CMA-ME dominates coverage on every seed (largest gap at seed~4: $-20.0$~pp). Paired hints $-$ cma\_mae: $\Delta\text{cov} = -0.15 \pm 2.25$~pp (5/10), $\Delta\text{fit} = +0.010 \pm 0.008$ (10/10) --- near parity on coverage, with a small mean-fitness edge for hints.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Synthesis}

These observations suggest that, for LLM-guided QD, \emph{where} a surrogate attaches can matter more than whether a usable predictor exists (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}). Under one shared MLP checkpoint, the after-generation surrogate (Role~2; Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition}) improves coverage per simulation, while the matched-policy before-generation surrogate (RQ1) stays within $\pm 2$~pp at $n{=}10$ (post-hoc TOST). That asymmetry points to a structural distinction: the after-generation surrogate hard-gates evaluation spend (SAIL-\emph{kin}), whereas the before-generation surrogate supplies prompt-side advice that never skips \texttt{evaluate\_candidate}. RQ3 levels further suggest that stacking both under LLM is not a simple sum; confirmatory interaction testing remains blocked by compose-gate divergence (\S\ref{sec:limitations}). Magnitudes are testbed-specific; the before/after framing is intended for LLM-guided QD pipelines more generally.

\subsection{Mechanistic reading}
\label{sec:why-discussion}

Results establish \emph{what} moved (Fig.~\ref{fig:acquisition}); the skip logs and matched contrasts suggest \emph{why}.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Budget reallocation.}
 These observations suggest that the after-generation surrogate mainly reallocates simulator spend rather than inventing stronger proposals. Skip logs (\S\ref{sec:why-filter}) show discarded proposals concentrated at low predicted fitness (mean $0.21$ vs.\ $0.61$ evaluated), high extinction risk ($81\%$ of skips with $p>0.8$), and low diversity, while uncertainty barely separates skip from evaluate. At matched evaluations that discarded mass frees slots for niches that can still fill ($+3.65$~pp @ 20k); at fixed iterations the same gate finishes $-0.83$~pp---a pattern consistent with reallocation under a fixed proposal schedule.
  \item \textbf{Shared checkpoint, asymmetric use.}
 Offline $R^2_{\mathrm{fitness}}{=}0.76$ [95\% CI $0.70{,}0.82$] (RQ2) and low false-accept mass among evaluated proposals ($12.7\%$ with true fitness $<0.45$; MAE $0.066$) suggest the checkpoint is accurate enough to support a hard threshold. The same predictor feeds RQ1 and yields a flat matched contrast, which suggests that \emph{attach role}---gate vs.\ advice---drives the asymmetry more than predictor quality alone.
  \item \textbf{Soft vs.\ hard channel.}
 The before-generation surrogate advises an LLM that already mutates under a JSON grammar; the after-generation surrogate constrains spend. Matched-policy RQ1 at $n{=}10$ stays within $\pm 2$~pp, and enriched/weak-model pilots ($n{\le}3$) stay within $|\Delta\mathrm{cov}|<2$~pp. These observations suggest that prompt-side scalars are easy for the emitter to ignore once archive policy is matched.
  \item \textbf{Warm-start context.}
 Confirmatory arms warm-start from the same $\sim$2{,}614-elite baseline, so Role~2 mid-budget gains arise inside an already seeded archive. A natural reading is that the gate continues to discard dead proposals while many niches remain empty \emph{within} that warm start. Whether before-generation advice would matter more from a true cold start remains an open bootstrap hypothesis (\S\ref{sec:bootstrap-hyp}).
  \item \textbf{Threshold as a design knob.}
 All skips cite \texttt{below\_fitness\_threshold} at one locked $\tau{=}0.45$. These results therefore speak to the after-generation \emph{role} under that gate; a $\tau$ sweep would clarify how sensitive the magnitude is.
  \item \textbf{Prompt numeracy in this stack.}
 The matched RQ1 contrast suggests that, in this stack, before-generation scalars do not move coverage once policy is fixed. Prompt design, grammar constraints, model choice, and mid-band saturation remain plausible modulators.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Working interpretation.} Same checkpoint $\rightarrow$ two attach roles. The after-generation surrogate improves evaluation-indexed coverage by filtering predicted low-fitness / high-extinction / low-diversity proposals and reallocating simulations; the before-generation surrogate leaves the same numbers as soft advice. Threshold sensitivity and LLM process accounts remain open.

\subsection{Boundary conditions}

Several contrasts sharpen what the positive Role~2 reading covers. Matched-policy RQ1 is flat (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}); multi-provider runs reproduce the \emph{bundled} $\Delta$ direction rather than a matched hint-content contrast. Relative to matched-policy genetic ME, the full LLM+\texttt{hints} stack adds only $\approx{+}1.4$~pp at roughly $10\times$ wall time.

Under continuous-relaxation encoding, pyribs CMA-ME leads hints by $\Delta\text{cov} \approx -13$~pp (pyribs-reference family FAIL); Bernoulli-decode CMA-ME leads by $\sim$11~pp and native bit-flip CMA-ME by $\sim$5~pp---both trail \texttt{rint} (0/10 seeds for native). CMA-MAE is near parity on coverage; the sole ceiling-family reject is a small fitness edge vs.\ CMA-MAE ($\Delta\text{fit}{=}+0.010$). Under LLM, stacking the after-generation gate with hints does not improve fixed-iteration coverage (about $-1$~pp), and formal RQ3 stays blocked by compose-gate divergence. On the dungeon domain, genetic MAP-Elites leads all LLM arms by $\sim$19~pp coverage, while after-generation sample-efficiency still ports---suggesting that filter engineering transfers more readily than bundled LLM QD lift.

\subsection{Implications for LLM emitters}
\label{sec:when-llm}

These observations also suggest a separation of objectives. The scientific object is \emph{LLM-guided} QD: pipelines that already use an LLM as a variation operator and must choose a surrogate attach role (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}). Role~2's headline arm drops the LLM (\texttt{genetic\_me\_filter}) so the after-generation surrogate can be isolated; that contrast measures attach-role payoff, while leaving open when structured LLM variation is desirable in its own right. When the objective is sample efficiency among \emph{discrete} emitters at matched policy, genetic$+$after-generation (Role~2) is the attach-role winner (\S\ref{sec:role2}); absolute coverage rankings that invoke CMA-ME inherit the continuous-relaxation caveat (\S\ref{sec:ceiling}). RQ1 and exploratory RQ3 still require an LLM emitter because those contrasts ask about before-generation and stacked attach roles under LLM variation.

On evaluation-indexed coverage, \texttt{genetic\_me\_filter} carries the Role~2 sample-efficiency gain without API cost (\S\ref{sec:role2}, Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition}). Among discrete arms at matched policy, \texttt{genetic\_me\_uniform}/Role~2 and LLM+\texttt{hints} cluster near $\sim$58--60\% at $\sim$6--94~min and \$0--2 API (\S\ref{sec:cost}). Continuous-relaxation CMA-ME reaches $\sim$73\% (\S\ref{sec:ceiling}) and illuminates niches the LLM stack leaves empty (Figure~\ref{fig:heatmaps}); Bernoulli-decode reaches $\sim$71\% and native bit-flip $\sim$66\%---all above hints/genetic ME on mean coverage, but only continuous/Bernoulli preserve a double-digit hints gap. Relative to SAIL, the after-generation surrogate is the evaluation-gating use of a predictor; relative to LAEA, fitness estimation stays with the MLP rather than the LLM.

The LLM emitter's distinctive niche appears to be \emph{structured, human-readable variation}. Genotypes here are named rule tables plus scalars; LLM mutations edit those tables as coherent rule changes (add birth~6, replace survival~8 by~7, coordinated \texttt{resource\_regen}/\texttt{predation} nudges) rather than as anonymous continuous coordinates rounded at decode (\S\ref{sec:method}). Figure~\ref{fig:elites} shows resulting elites as inspectable simulator states; parent$\rightarrow$child diffs for accepted LLM elites support calling that variation semantic. The dungeon factorial reverses the LLM vs.\ genetic ranking on coverage, which suggests that semantic edits need not improve QD metrics once policy is matched. A practical implication follows: when interpretable rule-space exploration is the goal (PCG, design briefs, editable genotypes), keep the LLM as a variation tool and attach the surrogate primarily after generation (Role~2); when archive metrics per evaluation or dollar dominate among \emph{discrete} emitters, prefer genetic$+$after-generation; treat continuous/Bernoulli CMA-ME as upper-bound references; native bit-flip CMA-ME (+5~pp vs.\ hints) does not overturn genetic$+$after-generation as the discrete attach-role winner at matched policy.

\subsection{Hypothesis: stage-dependent integration}
\label{sec:bootstrap-hyp}

The fixed-slot decomposition motivates a scheduling hypothesis that remains untested here:

\begin{quote}
\textit{The before-generation surrogate is most useful during exploration / archive bootstrapping; the after-generation surrogate remains beneficial across the optimization trajectory on the evaluation axis.}
\end{quote}

These observations nevertheless suggest room for \textbf{adaptive integration} (and, separately, adaptive emitter scheduling):

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{After-generation across the trajectory.}
 On Role~2 anytime curves (Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition}), \texttt{genetic\_me\_filter} leads \texttt{genetic\_me\_uniform} from early through mid budgets (about $+1.4$~pp @ 5k evaluations; $+3.0$ @ 10k; peak $\sim$+$3.7$ @ 15k--20k; 10/10 seeds), while skip rates stay high in early/mid/late iteration terciles ($\sim$37\% / 33\% / 31\%). That pattern suggests a mid-trajectory sample-efficiency advantage rather than a cold-start-only effect; by the full 32{,}500-evaluation endpoint the filter is slightly behind on coverage ($-0.8$~pp).
  \item \textbf{Before-generation early.}
 Terminal matched-policy RQ1 is flat within $\pm 2$~pp ($n{=}10$), which is compatible with either little prompt-side value overall or value concentrated in a phase we did not instrument. Soft-vs-hard channel asymmetry (\S\ref{sec:why-discussion}) and the usual MAP-Elites bootstrap/refine split make an early-only reading plausible. Frozen LLM arms lack \texttt{archive\_trace.jsonl}, so an early lift---if present---is not yet localized. Terminal elite histograms show low-fitness mass in filled niches, but as static snapshots they cannot date niche discovery.
  \item \textbf{Emitter scheduling.}
 Terminal mid-band parity (genetic ME $\approx$ LLM+\texttt{hints}) also invites LLM-for-bootstrap / genetics-for-refinement schedules; equally, genetics may simply catch up by iteration~650, or LLM value may be BC-region-specific.
\end{itemize}

Stage-dependent integration is therefore a hypothesis motivated by the observed decomposition. Natural follow-ons include anytime first-fill curves for hints vs.\ stub\_uniform, filter on/off by phase, and LLM for the first $K$ iterations then genetics---moving from ``where to attach the surrogate'' to ``when.''

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Threats to validity.} Primary evidence comes from a cheap evaluator where wall time is dominated by MLP inference, so Role~2's evaluation-indexed gain need not translate into a wall-clock win here. The pyribs ``coverage'' contrast uses continuous-relaxation encoding; decode ablations do not remove that confound because they still search $\mathbb{R}^{21}$ before rounding. RQ3 is exploratory because compose-gate non-inferiority is blocked. LLM endpoints are window-pinned but floating aliases limit bit-exact replay. Holm correction is family-wise within locked protocol families. Hypotheses and analysis rules are internally frozen in dated repository protocol files rather than publicly pre-registered.

\textbf{Testbed scope.} Primary families use the LifeManifold evaluator (\S\ref{sec:substrate}); a dungeon factorial is a second LLM-guided QD domain. Magnitudes (e.g.\ $+3.7$~pp, $33.5\%$ skips) are testbed-specific; the before/after framework targets LLM-guided QD pipelines generally. Break-even for wall-time savings is $\sim$7$\times$ slower simulators (\S\ref{sec:cost}). The dungeon factorial transfers filter AUC but not bundled LLM QD lift. Sphere/rastrigin only validate the pyribs runner.

\textbf{Bundled confirmatory contrast.} The locked stub/hints PASS remains a valid \emph{stack} test; matched-policy RQ1 isolates hint content (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}). Hint-content and weak-model pilots ($n{\le}3$) are exploratory only.

\textbf{Protocol freezing.} Hypotheses and analysis rules are locked in dated repository protocol files (CA: 2026-07-12; dungeon: 2026-07-17). This submission uses that \emph{internally frozen} wording only---no OSF/Zenodo deposit.

\textbf{arXiv v1 scope (preprint vs.\ journal extension).} The preprint reports all frozen CA confirmatory families (v2/v3) unchanged and the dungeon confirmatory AUC family on the \emph{5k five-arm matrix} (\texttt{q1-v4-dungeon-rerun}, $n{=}10$; \textbf{PARTIAL}). Supplementary dungeon evidence adds only the CPU Role~2 pair at CA-matched proposal budget (\texttt{q1-v4-dungeon-full-cpu}; Table~\ref{tab:b4-fullcpu}). Full-budget LLM dungeon arms, a \texttt{random} MAP-Elites baseline @ 32.5k, one dungeon ablation family, and pbCMA-style discrete CMA tuning are deferred to a post--arXiv journal revision under a dated protocol amendment. Bernoulli-decode (package~C4) and native bit-flip \texttt{DiscreteCMAEmitter} supplementary arms ($n{=}10$ each) are reported in \S\ref{sec:ceiling}.

\textbf{Multi-provider and dungeon scope.} Extra LLM providers complete the confirmatory per-provider bundled contrast at $n{=}10$ (Holm \emph{within each provider}---never a pooled cross-LLM family). Absolute coverage under \texttt{gpt-4o-mini} lags stronger models even when $\Delta$ direction is stable. Dungeon timing-to-threshold metrics were not locked before the first feasibility pilot and are exploratory; confirmatory dungeon endpoints are AUC at matched evaluation budget under the v4 protocol on the 5k five-arm matrix.

\textbf{Acquisition ablation scope.} The GP+UCB comparison is offline (hold-out regression + acquisition replay) plus a counterfactual on logged MLP predictions; it does not re-run the confirmatory filter matrix under GP or UCB.

\textbf{Pipelines and instrumentation.} The CMA ceiling contrast compares complete QD pipelines (homogeneous five-emitter CMA pools vs.\ heterogeneous MAP-Elites+LLM), not a factorial decomposition of emitters. A post-hoc decode/cold-start ablation (\texttt{q1-cma-encoding-ablation}, seeds~0--4), Bernoulli-decode CMA-ME (package~C4, $n{=}10$), and native bit-flip CMA-ME ($n{=}10$; \S\ref{sec:ceiling}) show the hints$\ll$CMA gap survives alternate decodes ($+10.8$~pp Bernoulli vs.\ hints; $+5.2$~pp native vs.\ hints; $\geq +8.8$~pp on the weakest encoding-ablation arm) but native discrete trails \texttt{rint} on every seed ($-7.8$~pp mean). Matched evaluation budget is not matched dollar cost (\S\ref{sec:cost}). Floating model aliases and API rate limits constrain scheduling; we window-pin endpoints and log \texttt{llm\_spec\_hash}.

\textbf{LLM-arm reproducibility vs.\ genetic/CMA.} Logging \texttt{llm\_spec\_hash} and window-pinned aliases (\texttt{qwen-turbo}, DeepSeek, \texttt{gpt-4o-mini}) is best-effort provenance, not bit-exact replay: proprietary chat endpoints routinely rename, retrain, or retire models within 1--2 years, so exact reproduction of LLM-emitter arms will likely become impossible in principle---unlike genetic ME and pyribs CMA arms, which remain locally re-runnable from frozen code and seeds.

\textbf{Discrete CMA-ES baseline (tested; encoding vs.\ search decomposed).} pyribs' stock CMA path is continuous ES with \texttt{rint}-decoded rule bits (\S\ref{sec:pyribs}). Package~C4 (Bernoulli decode at eval; $+10.8$~pp vs.\ hints) and a native \texttt{DiscreteCMAEmitter} (bit-flip; $+5.2$~pp vs.\ hints; $n{=}10$ each; \S\ref{sec:ceiling}) decompose the caveat: bit-identical search \emph{does} beat hints on all seeds but by a smaller margin than continuous CMA, and native discrete trails \texttt{rint} ($-7.8$~pp mean). Any ``CMA wins coverage'' reading therefore remains sensitive to continuous covariance adaptation, not a validated bit-flip emitter ranking; pbCMA-style tuning remains an optional journal extension.

\subsection{Future work}

These observations point to explanatory gaps that still matter more than new LLMs or domains (\S\ref{sec:why-discussion}): (1)~\textbf{phase analysis}---extend anytime curves to genetic ME and mid-trajectory Role~2 vs.\ hints; (2)~\textbf{threshold sensitivity} on $\tau\in\{0.3,0.5,0.7,0.9\}$; (3)~\textbf{prompt micro-ablation} (drop uncertainty / predicted fitness / archive stats) to refine the soft-channel reading of RQ1.

Having studied \emph{fixed} attach roles (Table~\ref{tab:role-exp}), a natural next step is \textbf{adaptive integration}: when to activate the before- vs.\ after-generation surrogate, and when to call LLM vs.\ genetic emitters (\S\ref{sec:bootstrap-hyp}). \textbf{Journal extension candidates:} (i)~\textbf{pbCMA / tuned discrete CMA} (beyond MVP bit-flip emitter); (ii)~compose-gate repair followed by confirmatory RQ3 if the gate passes; (iii)~full-budget LLM dungeon factorial, \texttt{random} baseline @ 32.5k, and one dungeon ablation family under a dated protocol amendment; threshold sweep on $\tau$ if cheap. An external OSF/Zenodo protocol timestamp remains optional for a later revision.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Surrogate information can enter the propose$\rightarrow$evaluate loop as a before- or after-generation surrogate (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}). Under one shared checkpoint on our controlled evaluator, the \textbf{after-generation surrogate} (Role~2) is the headline positive result on the evaluation-indexed axis (Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition}): $\sim$3.7~pp mid-budget coverage per simulation and 33.5\% fewer sims at fixed iterations, but $-0.83$~pp terminal coverage and slower wall time ($\sim$20 vs.\ $\sim$7~min) on this cheap evaluator. Matched-policy RQ1 (\texttt{stub\_uniform}, $n{=}10$) is flat within $\pm 2$~pp (post-hoc TOST; MDE$\approx$1.2~pp). RQ3 levels suggest stacking both under LLM is not a simple sum; confirmatory RQ3 remains blocked by compose-gate divergence.

Genetic ME already sits within about $1.4$~pp of the full LLM+scalar stack; continuous-relaxation CMA-ME leads hints by $\sim$13~pp, Bernoulli by $\sim$11~pp, and native bit-flip CMA-ME by $\sim$5~pp (\S\ref{sec:ceiling})---none is a validated winner over genetic$+$after-generation at matched discrete policy. On a second domain, the dungeon AUC family is \textbf{PARTIAL}: after-generation AUC passes Holm, but hints$-$stub and LLM+filter interaction do not---after-generation engineering ports; bundled LLM QD lift does not. We release an internally frozen pipeline so others can reuse the framework on their own LLM-guided QD stacks. \textbf{Caveats:} see \S\ref{sec:limitations}.

\section{CVT archive sensitivity}
\label{sec:cvt-sensitivity}

The scientific object of this paper is the RQ1 / Role~2 contrast on the confirmatory $50\times50$ grid (with RQ3 only as a descriptive LLM-stack check), not archive geometry. Tier \texttt{q1-cvt} ($n{=}10$) only checks that the \emph{bundled} stub/hints/filter directions are not grid-specific (Table~\ref{tab:cvt}): RQ1-s concordance 10/10; RQ3-s evaluation reduction $\sim$31\% on CVT vs.\ 33.5\% on grid. Absolute $\Delta$coverage is larger on CVT mainly because the stub baseline is lower ($\sim$37\% vs.\ $\sim$45\%). These runs are descriptive and outside confirmatory Holm families; they do not alter the RQ1 flat matched contrast or Role~2 eval-axis claims.
