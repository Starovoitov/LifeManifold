\title{Auditing Attribution in Surrogate-Assisted {MAP}-{E}lites: Target Selection, Prompt Scalars, and Evaluation Gating}

\author{Artem Starovoitov\corref{cor1}}
\ead{artem.starovoitov@proton.me}
\ead[url]{https://orcid.org/0009-0005-3170-4865}
\address{Independent Engineer, Moscow, Russia}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author}

\begin{abstract}
An LLM emitter is never swapped into a quality-diversity (QD) loop in isolation---it arrives with a prompt, a parent-selection policy, a warm-start archive, and a budget convention---so a measured gain may not belong to the channel it is attributed to. We audit one such comparison end to end on a controlled structured-genotype MAP-Elites testbed with paired seeds. In the bundled historical comparison, the live-hint arm exceeded the stub arm by $+15.09$~pp terminal coverage ($10/10$ seeds; Holm). A constant-scalar arm using the same \texttt{uniform\_frontier} target-selection policy as the hint arm reproduced $+14.83$~pp of that difference. Under the uniform policy, the primary \texttt{qwen-turbo} live-versus-constant scalar difference was $+0.26$~pp, with a paired 90\% CI of $[-0.43,+0.95]$~pp. Because the practical indifference margin was chosen after observing the data and the API tiers were not interleaved, this is descriptive, provider-specific evidence rather than confirmatory equivalence (DeepSeek mean equivalence at the same band was not established). Completing a descriptive policy$\times$hint $2\times2$ confirms soft residuals near null under both archive policies while target selection alone moves ${\approx}{+}15$~pp on either soft level. On the matched non-LLM genetic stack, threshold filtering reduced simulator calls by $33.5\%$ at a fixed proposal cap; a post-hoc common-support analysis through $20{,}000$ real evaluations showed a $+3.65$~pp coverage difference, while fixed-proposal terminal coverage ($-0.83$~pp) and wall time were worse on this cheap evaluator. Wall break-even is the accounting identity $t_{\mathrm{MLP}}<s\,t_{\mathrm{sim}}$ (here roughly a $7\times$ slower simulator under a constant-$t_{\mathrm{sim}}$ reading), not a portable QD ranking. The reusable contribution is an attribution-audit checklist---matched policy, explicit budget axes, online surrogate audit, distribution-matched placebo, ranking-versus-skip controls, wall-cost decomposition, warm-start/stage disclosure, and a protocol/status ledger---plus that scoped cost identity. The soft and hard case studies are matched within stack on a shared warm-start floor, not a single cross-stack or stage$\times$attach factorial.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Classical surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithms ask where a surrogate should intervene before an expensive evaluation~\cite{jin2005fitness}. Quality-diversity (QD) search with MAP-Elites makes the same budget pressure concrete: each candidate still needs a black-box roll-out before it can enter a behavioural archive~\cite{mouret2015illuminating,gaier2018sail}. LLM-guided MAP-Elites adds a soft channel that classical SAEAs did not have---prompt-side advice---so soft before-generation vs.\ hard after-generation use of one shared checkpoint is a natural model-management contrast. We situate both mechanisms in Jin's evolution-control vocabulary (Table~\ref{tab:jin-map}): H2 is individual-based pre-selection; H1 is a soft non-skipping use of the same model; generation-based control is not tested. We treat that contrast as an audit of attribution and cost under matched controls, not as a stage-universal answer to where a surrogate should attach.

The practical difficulty is attribution. An LLM emitter is never swapped in isolation: it arrives with a prompt, a parent-selection policy, a warm-start archive, and a budget convention, and a gap measured across that bundle can be produced by any of them. We do not audit other authors' designs here, but our own bundled contrast shows how large the mis-attribution can be---a $15$~pp gap that reads as ``hints beat stub'' is recovered by changing target selection alone, with the soft residual near null under both archive policies (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}). We therefore treat the question as an \emph{audit} problem---first attribute a measured gap to its actual source, then price each attach point against evaluation cost---rather than as a search for a new emitter. Related LLM$+$QD work varies emitters or prompts but does not treat before- vs.\ after-generation surrogate attachment as a factor (\S\ref{sec:related}).

We answer three decision questions on one controlled structured-genotype evaluator (\S\ref{sec:substrate}), as two matched case studies that share a checkpoint and warm-start archive but \emph{do not} form a single factorial attach-point experiment (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}; confound table: \S\ref{sec:cross-stack}):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\textbf{RQ1}] Is a bundled LLM ``hints'' coverage gain attributable to parent-level prompt scalars, or to archive policy?
  \item[\textbf{RQ2}] What does an after-generation surrogate gate buy on a matched non-LLM stack (real evaluations vs.\ terminal coverage)?
  \item[\textbf{RQ3}] When does that gate also repay wall clock?
\end{enumerate}
Protocol labels \textbf{H1}--\textbf{H5} (Table~\ref{tab:h-status}) are the audit ledger for those questions---not five equal confirmatory programmes. Maze / CMA-ME / stacking are supporting bounds. Headline numbers: Contributions.

\begin{quote}
\noindent\textbf{Genre.} This is an audit paper, not a new-algorithm paper: we do not propose a better LLM emitter or a coverage SOTA. We show how to attribute a bundled MAP-Elites gain (target selection vs.\ prompt scalars) and how to price evaluation gating under matched within-stack controls on a shared warm-start floor---as a reusable checklist (Table~\ref{tab:audit-checklist}) plus a wall-cost identity instantiated on our testbeds, not as a domain-universal QD ranking, a cold-start theorem, or a cross-stack attach-point factorial.
\end{quote}

\textbf{What a practitioner can decide after reading.} (i)~Before attributing a QD gain to an LLM emitter, re-run the control under the \emph{same} target-selection policy: on this testbed policy alone accounts for the bundled $+15$~pp gap, while the soft residual stays near null under both policies (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}). (ii)~Treat hard gating as a cost-identity question, not a guaranteed win: when $t_{\mathrm{MLP}}<s\,t_{\mathrm{sim}}$ the gate can save wall as well as simulations (\S\ref{sec:h2-breakeven}; re-measure on the target evaluator); on our cheap CA evaluator the descriptive H2 case study saves sims (${\sim}{+}3.7$~pp @ $20$k) but loses wall. (iii)~Use an LLM emitter when editable, human-readable genotypes are the deliverable rather than archive metrics, since on coverage and wall it is dominated here by genetic ME and CMA-ME (\S\ref{sec:when-llm}).

\subsection{Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{An archive-policy confound, quantified.} Our confirmatory bundled \texttt{stub}$\rightarrow$\texttt{hints} contrast yields a large gap in favour of the surrogate prompt ($+15.09 \pm 2.42$~pp coverage, $+0.047 \pm 0.010$ fitness; $10/10$; Holm $m{=}2$). A subsequently completed descriptive policy$\times$hint $2\times2$ (completing \texttt{hints\_minfit}; not a confirmatory factorial) replicates the attribution pattern: soft residuals near null under both policies ($-0.02$ / $+0.26$~pp) while target selection alone moves ${\approx}{+}15$~pp on either soft level (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}). We report the decomposition rather than the gap, and give the matched re-run recipe that produced it.
  \item \textbf{A scoped wall-cost identity for the hard attach point (descriptive H2).} On the matched non-LLM genetic stack, gating changes what gets simulated; its wall-clock value is conditional: with per-proposal cost $(1-s)\,t_{\mathrm{sim}}+t_{\mathrm{MLP}}$, filtering pays only if $t_{\mathrm{MLP}}<s\,t_{\mathrm{sim}}$ (\S\ref{sec:h2-breakeven}). On the primary CA grid $t_{\mathrm{MLP}}/t_{\mathrm{sim}}\approx 2.2$, so under the constant-$t_{\mathrm{sim}}$ identity the simulator must be ${\sim}7\times$ slower before a $33.5\%$ skip repays inference (optimistic if skipped candidates would have been cheaper early extinctions; \S\ref{sec:h2-breakeven}); maze micro-timings give $\delta^{\star}{\approx}2.3$~ms, sanity-checked by an injected-delay run (not a naturally expensive second domain). Eval-indexed magnitudes (${\sim}{+}3.7$~pp @ $20$k real evaluations; $33.5\%$ fewer sims; $-0.83$~pp terminal) are a \emph{descriptive} case study---protocol \S3.8; no Holm family---not a locked confirmatory endpoint. This is a cost identity plus testbed instantiation---not a claim that coverage magnitudes or skip rates transfer across domains.
  \item \textbf{Small soft residual (exploratory H1 estimate).} After matching policy, parent-level surrogate scalars on primary \texttt{qwen-turbo} yield mean $\Delta{=}{+}0.26$~pp (paired 90\% CI $[-0.43,+0.95]$; $n{=}10$)---compatible with a post-hoc $\pm 2$~pp band, not confirmatory equivalence (\texttt{gpt-4o-mini} likewise; DeepSeek mean equivalence not established---Table~\ref{tab:h1-matched-providers}; \S\ref{sec:limitations}), with a mechanistic reading: the prompt already carries the parent's \emph{true} fitness, so the added scalars restate known information on ${\approx}10/50$ LLM slots (\S\ref{sec:h1-impl-threat}).
  \item \textbf{Reusable audit checklist (Table~\ref{tab:audit-checklist}).} Nine checks that make a single-run LLM$+$QD comparison testable: configuration-diff table; matched-policy arm; explicit budget axis (proposal slots / real sims / wall); online surrogate audit; distribution-matched placebo; ranking-versus-skip-volume control; wall-cost decomposition; warm-start/stage disclosure; protocol/status ledger. Instantiations and failure modes are cited in the table (stub constants are \emph{not} a distribution-matched placebo; compose-gate / NMAE-denominator bugs are the measurement failure modes we corrected---Table~\ref{tab:bug-ledger}, \S\ref{sec:checkpoint}, \S\ref{sec:h3-appendix}). Cheap to re-run on other stacks; numeric outcomes are not claimed to transfer.
  \item \textbf{Scope, stated as a bound.} H1 (exploratory estimate) and H2 (descriptive) share one controlled CA testbed under a shared warm-start floor ($971$ niches / $38.84\%$ coverage)---mid/late continuation, not cold-start build-from-empty (\S\ref{sec:warm-start-limit}). A descriptive empty-archive re-run of the mixed-stack $2\times2$ ($n{=}10$) does not close a stage$\times$attach factorial: the soft residual stays noisy-near-null, while the hard mid-budget sign reverses relative to the warm grid. Maze H5 is a main-text \emph{locked} transfer bound (non-LLM filter AUC yes; LLM arms / bundled lift no), with dungeon/Sphere appendix-only. The two case studies are matched \emph{within} stack, not one attach-point $2\times2$; cross-stack factors and unequal inferential status are disclosed in Table~\ref{tab:h-status} / \S\ref{sec:cross-stack} rather than claimed away. Further bounds: LLM$+$filter stacking, CMA-ME (${\sim}13$~pp lead).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background and related work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Quality-diversity and MAP-Elites}

Quality-diversity (QD) algorithms seek collections of high-performing yet behaviourally diverse solutions rather than a single optimum~\cite{pugh2016quality,cully2018quality,chatzilygeroudis2021qd}. MAP-Elites maintains an archive partitioned by user-defined behavioural characteristics (BC) and retains the best elite per niche~\cite{mouret2015illuminating}. Heterogeneous emitter sets can improve illumination efficiency relative to a single variation operator~\cite{cully2020multiemitter}; our confirmatory stack mixes random, genetic, and LLM slots in one batch.

CMA-ME / CMA-MAE are strong continuous QD baselines~\cite{fontaine2020cmame,fontaine2023cmamae,zhao2024cmamae}; we run them via \texttt{ribs} v0.11.0~\cite{pyribs2020} on the same simulator as the LLM stack (\S\ref{sec:pyribs}), with homogeneous CMA-ES pools (strong but not maximal pyribs cocktails).

\subsection{Surrogate-assisted illumination}

Expensive simulators motivate surrogate models that predict outcomes from static design features~\cite{jin2005fitness,jones1998ego}.
Classical surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithms (SAEAs) treat that prediction problem as \emph{model management}: when, and how strongly, to trust the approximate fitness instead of the true evaluator~\cite{jin2005fitness}.
Two recurring evolution-control patterns are \emph{individual-based} control (pre-select or filter candidates before expensive evaluation) and \emph{generation-based} control (evaluate only some generations fully, or alternate approximate and exact generations)~\cite{jin2005fitness}.
Table~\ref{tab:jin-map} places our attach points in that taxonomy once; later sections reuse these names rather than re-arguing the mapping.

Surrogate-Assisted Illumination (SAIL) fits a Gaussian process over the behaviour--fitness landscape, runs MAP-Elites on an acquisition surface, and spends real evaluations on a selected subset~\cite{gaier2018sail,gaier2017sail,gaier2017aiaa}.
Deep Surrogate Assisted MAP-Elites (DSA-ME) replaces the GP with neural predictors trained online and validated in an outer game-engine loop~\cite{grillotti2022dsame}.
Related Bayesian QD and discount-model lines continue that accelerator tradition~\cite{kent2025boqd,tjanaka2026dms}.
Those systems often illuminate a surrogate surface; our design instead keeps a fixed MAP-Elites propose$\rightarrow$evaluate loop and audits two concrete uses of one shared MLP---parent-level prompt scalars vs.\ after-generation threshold gating---under ground-truth archive updates when a slot is evaluated ($32{,}500$ proposal slots per seed; filter arms may skip some sims). Reported SAIL savings on airfoils and deckbuilding motivate the real-evaluation-count axis~\cite{gaier2018sail,grillotti2022dsame}; pyribs CMA-ME/CMA-MAE are our quantitative QD baselines (\S\ref{sec:pyribs}).

\subsection{LLMs in evolutionary and QD search}

LLMs appear as variation operators and evolutionary engines~\cite{wu2025ecllm,wang2025llmea,vanstein2024llamea,guo2024evoprompt,liu2024llmeo,lange2024llmes,fernando2024promptbreeder,lehman2023openelm}, including program search (FunSearch; Eureka)~\cite{romera2024funsearch,ma2024eureka} and QD emitters (LLMatic; in-context QD; LLaMEA behaviour analyses)~\cite{nasir2024llmatic,lim2024llmqd,vanstein2025behaviour}. Concurrent LLM+MAP-Elites applications~\cite{ropke2026dejaq,sviridov2026medical}, PCG pipelines~\cite{togelius2010sbpcg,earle2022nca}, and open-ended CA illumination~\cite{faldor2024lenia} use LLM or QD variation, but not before- vs.\ after-generation surrogate attachment as a factor. Prompt-as-search-space lines~\cite{santos2025diverseprompts,saletta2024promptspace,guo2026qdllm} and LAEA-style LLM fitness models~\cite{hao2024laea,xie2025llmsae} are adjacent; we instead mutate \texttt{WorldSpec} genomes with an LLM emitter and score them with a genome-aligned MLP.

\subsection{Seeded simulators and uncertain QD}

Confirmatory evaluations are deterministically seeded from the genotype (canonical SHA-256 of the canonical \texttt{WorldSpec} payload $\rightarrow$ 32-bit \texttt{world.seed} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{numpy} RNG inside \texttt{run\_world}), so fitness/BC are a function of the candidate genome alone---not of the MAP-Elites run seed and not lucky-seed artifacts in the UQD sense~\cite{flageat2023uq,flageat2025extractqd}. We use one seeded pass per insertion (elites are not re-evaluated for archive labels); re-simulating the same elite with that canonical seed must reproduce the stored fitness/BC. Paired \emph{run} seeds and Holm apply to terminal coverage / mean elite fitness (\S\ref{sec:protocol}). Archive truth is always simulated; surrogate advice is separate. \section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

Everything below implements two within-stack surrogate mechanisms in a MAP-Elites propose$\rightarrow$evaluate loop (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline})---as separate matched case studies on a shared warm-start floor, not one factorial and not a claim about where a surrogate should attach in general (\S\ref{sec:cross-stack}, \S\ref{sec:warm-start-limit}). The LLM is an optional emitter; the evaluator is a controlled measurement substrate (\S\ref{sec:substrate}). Archive insertion always uses simulated fitness.

\subsection{Attachment points and contrast labels}
\label{sec:terminology}

A MAP-Elites iteration is select $\rightarrow$ \textbf{generate} $\rightarrow$ (optionally) gate $\rightarrow$ \textbf{evaluate} $\rightarrow$ archive update. As in Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Before-generation (soft role)} --- predictions enter the emitter and try to change \emph{what} is proposed (never skips simulation). Case study \textbf{H1} instantiates this role as \emph{parent-level surrogate scalars} in the LLM prompt---not before-generation guidance as a class.
  \item \textbf{After-generation (hard role)} --- predictions gate the candidate and change \emph{whether} it spends ground truth (SAIL-\emph{kin} hard gate). Case study \textbf{H2} instantiates this role as absolute-$\tau$ threshold filtering on generated children.
\end{itemize}

Architectural roles stay before- vs.\ after-generation; claim-facing H1 labels use the concrete interface (parent-level scalars). Synonyms appear only here: before-generation $\equiv$ prompt hints / soft intervention; after-generation $\equiv$ acquisition / candidate filtering / hard intervention; attachment point $\equiv$ intervention point / surrogate attachment policy. Table~\ref{tab:h-status} summarizes H1--H5 outcomes in plain language; later prose states the finding rather than repeating a status enum. Prompt placeholders and thresholds are mechanisms \emph{inside} a role. Surrogate-informed parent selection is out of scope; archive \texttt{target\_selection} remains ordinary MAP-Elites policy.

\subsection{MAP-Elites illuminator loop}

Each run executes \texttt{iterations} $\times$ \texttt{batch\_size} \emph{proposal slots} in fixed order. Production schedulers use $650 \times 50 = 32{,}500$ proposal slots per seed. We keep three budgets distinct throughout:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Proposal slots} --- attempted candidates ($32{,}500$ matched across primary-grid arms).
  \item \textbf{Real simulator evaluations} --- slots that call \texttt{run\_world}. Non-filter arms execute all $32{,}500$; filter arms execute fewer (${\sim}21.5$k at $33.5\%$ skip on the genetic H2 stack).
  \item \textbf{Wall / API cost} --- wall-clock time and LLM call counts (Table~\ref{tab:cost}); not interchangeable with either count axis above.
\end{itemize}
H2's interpretation depends on this distinction: terminal levels are at a matched \emph{proposal} budget; evaluation-indexed curves and AUC use \emph{real} evaluations (\S\ref{sec:h2}).

For slot~$k$ the loop:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item selects a target niche (behaviour-cell centre) according to \texttt{target\_selection};
  \item calls the slot emitter (\texttt{random}, \texttt{genetic}, or \texttt{llm}) to propose a \texttt{WorldSpec};
  \item optionally applies surrogate acquisition (\S\ref{sec:acquisition}), which may \emph{skip} the simulator call;
  \item if not skipped, runs \texttt{evaluate\_candidate} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{run\_world} (always for non-filter / MVP hint arms);
  \item inserts into the archive if fitness strictly improves the cell incumbent;
  \item appends training data to the surrogate buffer when \texttt{surrogate.enabled} and a real evaluation occurred.
\end{enumerate}
LLM slots log \texttt{llm\_fallback} when parsing fails and a genetic repair path is used. Parallel evaluation uses a process pool sized to the batch. Outputs: \texttt{map\_elites\_archive.jsonl}, optional \texttt{archive\_trace.jsonl}, and \texttt{nightly\_run\_summary.json}.

\textbf{Target selection.} Both policies select among \emph{frontier} cells only---occupied niches with at least one empty neighbour. \texttt{min\_fitness\_frontier} picks the frontier cell with lowest elite fitness (bundled \texttt{stub}). \texttt{uniform\_frontier} samples uniformly on that frontier set (\texttt{hints}, filter arms, matched H1/H2). These are archive policies, not surrogate roles: they do not use the surrogate to select parents. Scientific H1 and H2 hold this policy fixed (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}, \S\ref{sec:h2}). The historical stub$\rightarrow$hints gap does not. Surrogate-assisted parent selection is out of scope.

\subsection{Surrogate model}
\label{sec:surrogate-method}

The surrogate maps a canonical \texttt{WorldSpec} to predicted MAP-Elites outcomes without a simulator call (Figure~\ref{fig:surrogate}). Emitters edit a 21-parameter genome (18 birth/survival rule bits + three scalars); feature schema~2.1 lifts that to a \textbf{24-D} input by appending three normalized rule-set statistics (birth cardinality, survival cardinality, birth$\cap$survival overlap, each $/9$). Full feature list: appendix~\S\ref{sec:substrate-appendix}. A multi-head regressor (\textbf{Strategy~A}) predicts seven component targets---stability, diversity, oscillation, two topology proxies, final density, and early-extinction probability---trained from JSONL buffer rows appended after each real evaluation when the surrogate is enabled.

Production checkpoint \texttt{nightly\_v3\_mc\_d005.pkl} is a four-member MLP ensemble with MC-dropout uncertainty ($N=35{,}114$ training rows; hold-out at runtime-aligned compose gate~$0.95$: NMAE$=0.112$ [95\% CI $0.107{,}0.117$], MAE$_{\text{fitness}}=0.029$, and $R^2_{\text{fitness}}=0.94$ [95\% CI $0.94{,}0.95$]---the last inflated by target std$\times 1.92$ vs.\ gate~$0.5$, not a model upgrade; appendix~\S\ref{sec:checkpoint-appendix}). Runtime fitness is recomposed via \texttt{compute\_fitness\_from\_prediction} using Eq.~\eqref{eq:fitness} (same weights as the simulator). \texttt{get\_surrogate()} falls back to constant \texttt{StubSurrogate} values if the checkpoint is missing, dimension-mismatched, or fails the \texttt{hints\_ok} gate ($R^2 \ge 0.30$, MAE $< 0.085$).

\textbf{Why this regressor family.} Both case studies pin \emph{one} shared checkpoint so soft vs.\ hard mechanisms are not confounded by swapping models---but they still differ in emitter stack and reporting axis (\S\ref{sec:cross-stack}). An MLP ensemble fits that shared-model contract: multi-head Strategy~A targets, MC-dropout/ensemble uncertainty, and fast batch inference over 32{,}500-proposal schedules. SAIL-style GPs are the natural lineage check, not a required production drop-in: under the same runtime compose labels the MLP leads a scikit-learn GP on hold-out fitness (NMAE $0.112$ vs.\ $0.222$; $R^2{=}0.94$ vs.\ $0.89$ on that same high-variance target) and is ${\sim}37\times$ faster at a full-budget proposal batch, while GP+UCB threshold replays do not beat the production gate (appendix~\S\ref{sec:surrogate-ablation-appendix}). Gradient-boosted trees (e.g.\ XGBoost) are a plausible tabular alternative; the repository retains an optional LightGBM path, but confirmatory H1/H2 pin the MLP checkpoint. We do not claim MLP is uniquely optimal---only that a competent shared regressor was frozen so the soft/hard asymmetry is not confounded with regressor-family shopping.

\textbf{Parent-level surrogate scalars (H1) --- what is predicted and what is inserted.}
For each of the $10/50$ LLM slots per batch, the emitter first selects the archive elite in the target niche (or a random parent if empty). H1 feeds that \emph{parent} \texttt{WorldSpec} to \texttt{SurrogateFacade.predict(parent)}---not a child candidate, and not a counterfactual edit. The user prompt already serializes the parent elite as JSON including its \emph{true} simulated \texttt{fitness}, BC measures, and \texttt{world\_spec}, plus few-shot neighbour elites that also carry true fitness (template: \texttt{prompts/map\_elites\_llm\_emitter\_user.txt}; full text: appendix~\S\ref{sec:h1-prompt-appendix}). The only H1 treatment fields are two additional scalars on one line:
\begin{quote}
\small\texttt{Surrogate predicts fitness} $\approx$ \texttt{\{surrogate\_mean\}, uncertainty = \{surrogate\_uncertainty\}}
\end{quote}
Under \texttt{hints}, those placeholders receive live \texttt{prediction.fitness} and calibrated ensemble uncertainty on the parent genotype. Under \texttt{stub\_uniform}, they are fixed YAML constants (\texttt{stub\_mean}$=0.5$, \texttt{stub\_uncertainty}$=1.0$) with \texttt{surrogate.enabled=false} (no \texttt{predict} call). One LLM HTTP request per LLM slot either way. Because the true parent fitness is already in the JSON block, the live scalars are largely a \emph{noisy re-statement of already-available parent information}, not a directional improvement signal about an unseen child. A null terminal result is therefore unsurprising and does not speak to before-generation guidance in general (\S\ref{sec:h1-impl-threat}).

\textbf{Why this weak actuator (and what it is not).} Matched H1 tests the weakest non-skipping soft channel that still differs from stub constants under a shared checkpoint: parent-level scalars only, on ${\approx}10/50$ LLM slots. It is \emph{not} a test of: (i)~exact archive parent statistics as the sole advice channel (true fitness is already present for both arms); (ii)~a confirmatory shuffled/permutation placebo (a descriptive $n{=}10$ shuffle is \S\ref{sec:h1-impl-threat}); (iii)~evidence that the LLM uses the numbers; (iv)~child-level advice, local counterfactual directions, or candidate rewriting. Stronger before-generation actuators remain out of H1 claim scope (Future work). Surrogate-informed parent selection is likewise out of scope.

\subsection{LLM emitter}

The \texttt{LlmEmitter} sends the parent \texttt{WorldSpec} JSON and niche centre to a chat API; the model returns a JSON patch applied via \texttt{world\_spec\_from\_llm}. Primary LLM runs use \texttt{qwen-turbo}; multi-provider replications swap endpoints while keeping prompts and schedulers fixed (\S\ref{sec:results}). Endpoint pins and decoding hyperparameters sit in appendix Table~\ref{tab:pins-full} / repository YAMLs---reproducibility detail, not a scientific factor.

Accepted LLM elites typically differ from parents by compact named-field rule/scalar edits rather than bit noise (edit-anatomy case study, appendix~\S\ref{sec:edit-anatomy}).

\subsection{After-generation surrogate (H2)}
\label{sec:acquisition}

Optional \texttt{acquisition.mode=filter} evaluates \texttt{surrogate.predict} \emph{before} \texttt{evaluate\_candidate}. A \texttt{threshold\_gate} skips simulation when predicted fitness falls below \texttt{min\_predicted\_fitness=0.45} (primary grid) subject to uncertainty calibration. Skipped slots are logged to \texttt{surrogate\_archive.jsonl} but do not update the MAP-Elites archive. \texttt{shadow} mode logs counterfactual skips while still evaluating. Production after-generation uses that same MLP checkpoint with a fitness threshold---not SAIL-style GP+UCB (\S\ref{sec:surrogate-method}; appendix~\S\ref{sec:surrogate-ablation-appendix}). Primary H2 evidence is the matched genetic filter contrast (\S\ref{sec:h2}); LLM$+$filter stacking is not confirmatory on production filter and not superior after gray-zone repair (Table~\ref{tab:h-status}; \S\ref{sec:h3}).

\subsection{pyribs CMA-ME / CMA-MAE baselines}
\label{sec:pyribs}

Gradient QD baselines run through \texttt{run\_pyribs\_baseline.py} (\texttt{ribs==0.11.0}), not as native \texttt{EmitterKind} types. The adapter maps the same 21-D genetic genome to \texttt{WorldSpec}, evaluates with the same simulator, and writes comparable JSONL archives. Each algorithm uses five homogeneous \texttt{EvolutionStrategyEmitter} instances (batch~50 $\Rightarrow$ ask size~250), $\sigma_0=0.2$, and $32{,}500$ asks (asks $=$ sims; no filter skips). CMA-ME uses learning rate~1.0 with 2-improvement ranking; CMA-MAE uses learning rate~0.01 with a separate result archive. This is a strong but not maximal pyribs configuration (no mixed ImprovementEmitter cocktail). Encoding asymmetries are disclosed below.

\textbf{Encoding (continuous relaxation).}
CMA proposes continuous $\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{21}$; \texttt{decode\_genome} then \emph{rints} the 18 rule bits and clips the three scalars. Genetic / LLM emitters edit discrete rule tables directly. The comparison is therefore not bit-identical: CMA searches a continuous relaxation where covariance adaptation can exploit structure that bit-flip genetics never sees. We treat pyribs arms as an upper-bound-style ceiling for continuous-friendly representations. Decode / discrete-search controls (Bernoulli, native bit-flip, pbCMA) are appendix~\S\ref{sec:encoding-appendix}.

\textbf{Warm-start (symmetric).} All confirmatory primary-grid arms---\texttt{vanilla}, genetic ME variants, \texttt{stub}/\texttt{hints}/\texttt{filter}, and pyribs CMA-ME/MAE---load the \emph{same} nightly grid baseline JSONL via \texttt{--load-archive} before the $32{,}500$-proposal-slot budget (\texttt{artifacts/map\_elites\_nightly/baseline/map\_elites\_archive.jsonl}; identical file for every seed and arm). The file holds $2{,}614$ JSONL records; \texttt{load\_and\_collapse\_jsonl} keeps the best fitness per niche (strict $>\;$; first record wins ties), yielding $971$ occupied cells on the $50\times50$ grid---initial coverage $38.84\%$ and QD-score $\sum f_i\approx417.9$ (mean elite fitness $\approx0.430$). Relative to terminal hints ${\approx}58$--$60\%$ (vanilla ${\approx}42\%$), this is a large shared floor, not a cold start; terminal coverage is reported after warm-start merge. Logged pyribs \texttt{warm\_start\_elites}${}=2{,}614$ counts raw lines before collapse, not occupied niches. The pyribs contrast is matched on initialization and on proposal/ask count ($32{,}500$ asks with no skips); asymmetries arise from emitter pipelines and continuous-relaxation encoding, not from unequal archive seeds.

\subsection{Measurement substrate}
\label{sec:substrate}

Primary contrasts use a controlled black-box evaluator---not a domain contribution. Each candidate is a structured \texttt{WorldSpec}: birth/survival neighbour-count rule tables (18 bits) plus three continuous scalars (\texttt{noise}, \texttt{resource\_regen}, \texttt{predation}). The simulator runs a seeded $50\times50$ toroidal cellular-automaton world for $T_{\mathrm{sim}}{=}200$ steps (early extinction $\Rightarrow$ fitness~0). Behaviour characteristics are stability and diversity in $[0,1]$, so the primary archive is a $50\times50$ niche grid over that plane; insertion fitness is a fixed weighted recompose of diversity, survival, oscillation, and topology proxies (Eq.~\eqref{eq:fitness} in appendix~\S\ref{sec:substrate-appendix}; weights protocol-locked, not tuned---\S\ref{sec:limitations}). Genetic emitters flip bits / nudge scalars; LLM emitters edit the same named fields as JSON. The evaluator is cheap relative to CFD-style SAIL domains, so H2 claims evaluation-indexed efficiency and reports wall time separately (\S\ref{sec:cost}). Structured named fields make both soft before-generation and hard after-generation interventions testable---the reason for a synthetic CA primary testbed rather than a pure $\mathbb{R}^d$ literature toy as the sole domain. Table~\ref{tab:domain} lists the knobs; example elites and fitness algebra remain in the appendix. Coverage is occupied-cell percentage after warm-start merge; mean elite fitness averages elites over occupied bins (logged column \texttt{mean\_best\_fitness} is a historical name, not archive-max); QD-score (Eq.~\eqref{eq:qd}) is descriptive only. CVT geometry is appendix sensitivity~\cite{vassiliades2018cvt}. Complementary literature Sphere H2 transfer (threshold gate only; no LLM) is appendix~\S\ref{sec:sphere-h2-appendix}.

\section{Experimental protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

Confirmatory contrast sets were prospectively specified \emph{per family} in dated internal protocol files relative to that family's analysis window (locked Holm families: bundled stack / F-RQ0; multi-provider F-RQ1g; maze H5; H3-gray; appendix dungeon; historically F-RQ4)---not as an externally timestamped OSF/AsPredicted pre-registration. Matched H1 is an exploratory effect estimate outside those Holm sets; matched genetic H2 (\texttt{genetic\_me\_uniform} vs.\ \texttt{genetic\_me\_filter}) is a \emph{descriptive} protocol \S3.8 case study with \emph{no} Holm family---cov@$20$k / AUC@$20$k are reporting cuts, not locked endpoints (Table~\ref{tab:h-status}, Table~\ref{tab:test-map}). H4 (pyribs ceiling) was listed as confirmatory Holm $m{=}4$ in the v3 freeze; the manuscript reports it as a \emph{descriptive ceiling check} by \emph{methodological category correction} (continuous-relaxation encoding made ``hints $>$ CMA'' an unfair confirmatory hypothesis), not by demoting an inconvenient reject---paired deltas and the locked F-RQ4 FAIL readout are unchanged (appendix~\S\ref{sec:amendments-appendix}). Later rows add packages, repair paths, or reporting notes; they do not silently rewrite already-frozen claim-driving Holm families (Table~\ref{tab:timeline}). Descriptive and pilot arms are not pooled into confirmatory Holm tests.

\subsection{Design principles}

\textbf{Paired seeds.} All primary-grid contrasts use the same integer seeds $0,\ldots,9$ across conditions within a tier. The maze factorial (and appendix dungeon) use the same seed indices with independent domain RNG. Pairing is the analysis unit for \emph{run-level} stochasticity (emitter RNG, warm-start collapse, proposal schedule)---not a full control of external LLM sampling: confirmatory LLM arms use $T{=}0.2$ without a provider seed, were not time-interleaved across soft levels, and frozen runs lack per-call I/O logs (floating \texttt{qwen-turbo} alias; \S\ref{sec:limitations}).

\textbf{Primary archive and warm-start.} Confirmatory primary-grid endpoints use the \textbf{50$\times$50 grid} (frozen before readout). CVT is appendix sensitivity only (\S\ref{sec:cvt-sensitivity})---coverage depends on tessellation, so CVT does not re-lock Holm/TOST. Every primary-grid arm in the batch driver warm-starts from the same baseline JSONL ($2{,}614$ records $\rightarrow$ $971$ niches / $38.84\%$ coverage after collapse; \S\ref{sec:pyribs}) before the matched $32{,}500$-proposal-slot budget.

\textbf{Multiple comparisons.} Each confirmatory contrast set uses Holm step-down on one-sided Wilcoxon tests at $\alpha=0.05$ (within set). A set passes only if every test in that set rejects (and local effect-size gates where the frozen protocol requires them). Matched H1 TOST sits outside primary-grid confirmatory Holm sets; H3-gray is the separate NI family ($m{=}3$; Table~\ref{tab:h-status}). We do \emph{not} pool families into a single paper-wide Holm vector: multiplicity is controlled within each pre-specified set (appendix~\S\ref{sec:stats-appendix}).

\textbf{Power and non-rejection.} Matched H1 reports a retrospective sensitivity MDE for a two-sided paired-$t$ \emph{difference} test (H$_0{:}\,\mathrm{mean}\,\Delta{=}0$), not TOST power (Table~\ref{tab:decomposition}; appendix~\S\ref{sec:stats-appendix}). Elsewhere, a Holm non-reject is failure to reject, not equivalence. At $n{=}10$, exact one-sided Wilcoxon $p$-values have a discrete floor $9.8\times10^{-4}$ (Table~\ref{tab:test-map} note)---reject/non-reject still depends on Holm width and effect direction, but $p$ alone cannot grade effect size. Outcomes for H3--H4 (bounds / descriptive ceiling) are Table~\ref{tab:h-status}.

\textbf{Bundled stub vs.\ hints.} The locked \texttt{stub} vs.\ \texttt{hints} contrast differs in surrogate use and in \texttt{target\_selection} (\S\ref{sec:method}). Scientific H1 holds target selection fixed (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}).

\textbf{Confirmatory default.} Paired seeds ($n{=}10$ unless noted), one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, Holm within each pre-specified contrast set (\S\ref{sec:protocol}).

\textbf{Paired effect-size companion ($\mathrm{PPS}$; selective).} Kerby's \emph{paired proportion of favorable pairs}~\cite{kerby2014simple} (ties as half; Eq.~\eqref{eq:pps}; not Vargha--Delaney $A_{12}$~\cite{vargha2000new}) coincides with the Sign rate when there are no ties. At $n{=}10$, unanimous contrasts therefore yield $\mathrm{PPS}\in\{0,1\}$ and add no information beyond Sign---we omit $\mathrm{PPS}$ in those tables. We report $\mathrm{PPS}$ only where it is non-extreme or where no Sign column is shown (e.g.\ H4 near-parity; H3-gray NI). Descriptive only; Holm / protocol $d_z$ decide confirmatory outcomes.

Matched-policy H1 reports the paired mean $\Delta$cov and its CI, with an exploratory paired-$t$ TOST vs.\ a post-hoc $\pm 2$~pp band as a consistency check only (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}; status \S\ref{sec:limitations}). Pilot arms report mean $\Delta$, sign counts, and bootstrap CIs. Surrogate hold-out NMAE/MAE/$R^2$ are validity checks, not endpoints; cross-gate algebra and bootstrap unit: \S\ref{sec:checkpoint}.

\subsection{RQ1 --- The apparent LLM gain is mostly a policy effect}

\subsubsection{RQ3 --- When filtering pays on wall}
\label{sec:h2-breakeven}
\label{sec:why-filter}

Under a fixed proposal stream, wall per proposal is $(1-s)\,t_{\mathrm{sim}}+t_{\mathrm{MLP}}$. Filter wins on wall only if
\[
t_{\mathrm{MLP}}<s\,t_{\mathrm{sim}}
\]
---an accounting identity (re-measure $t,s$ on the target evaluator), not a portable QD ranking. Here $s{\approx}0.335$ and $t_{\mathrm{MLP}}/t_{\mathrm{sim}}\approx 2.2$, so under constant-$t_{\mathrm{sim}}$ the simulator must be roughly ${\sim}7\times$ slower before a $33.5\%$ skip saves wall (optimistic if skips are cheap early extinctions; Table~\ref{tab:skip-mech}). On the LLM stack the API cost is paid before the skip, so gating does not cut chat completions (\S\ref{sec:cost}). Maze injected-delay sanity ($\delta{=}10$~ms; filter faster $9/10$): descriptive only; Sphere: appendix~\S\ref{sec:sphere-h2-appendix}.

\subsection{H3 --- LLM$+$filter stacking (boundary; appendix detail)}
\label{sec:h3}

Stacking the after-generation gate on the LLM$+$hint arm is \emph{not} a simple sum of H1$+$H2 (Table~\ref{tab:h-status}). Production \texttt{filter} is not confirmatory because the locked compose-gate skip-agreement fails ($d{=}0.528$): hard fitness recompose zeros a candidate when predicted extinction probability exceeds a gate, and offline diagnosis shows live filtering used extinction gate~$0.95$ while an earlier hold-out path used~$0.5$, so skip/accept decisions disagree on a large gray band of proposals ($p_{\mathrm{ext}}\in[0.5,0.95)$; appendix~\S\ref{sec:h3-appendix}). Those production levels are therefore descriptive only (about $-1$~pp vs.\ hints). A gray-zone repair clears non-inferiority margins at mean $\Delta$cov $-0.60$~pp but does not establish superiority (Holm $m{=}3$). Full levels, $\tau\times$emitter diagnosis, and NI table: appendix~\S\ref{sec:h3-appendix}. Primary after-generation evidence remains H2 (\S\ref{sec:h5} for maze).

\subsection{H4 --- Continuous-relaxation pyribs ceiling (descriptive)}
\label{sec:h4}

Pyribs baselines (five homogeneous CMA-ES emitters, $n=10$) provide a continuous-relaxation \emph{ceiling check} under the same warm-start and $32{,}500$-ask budget as other primary-grid arms (no skips; asks $=$ sims)---parallel in role to H3 production stacking: a bound, not a confirmatory Holm family. CMA searches $\mathbb{R}^{21}$ with \texttt{rint} decode (\S\ref{sec:pyribs}), so the comparison is an upper-bound-style reference for continuous-friendly representations, not a fair discrete-genotype contest. Mean levels: cma\_me $73.37 \pm 3.65$\%, mean elite fitness $0.576 \pm 0.028$; cma\_mae $60.56 \pm 2.16$\%, $0.489 \pm 0.007$; hints $60.41 \pm 0.82$\%, $0.499 \pm 0.005$ (same frozen \texttt{q1-full/hints} arm and occupied-bin fitness endpoint as Table~\ref{tab:rq1}).

Decode / discrete-search controls (Bernoulli, native bit-flip, pbCMA) are appendix~\S\ref{sec:encoding-appendix}---not required to read H4 as a continuous-relaxation ceiling.

\label{sec:h5-maze}

\textbf{Scope.} Does the after-generation surrogate \emph{port} beyond the primary evaluator? Main-text H5 is the maze factorial ($n{=}10$; 32{,}500 proposals/arm; 900-cell grid; $\tau{=}0.685$ in a $\sim$25--45\% skip band). \section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Synthesis}

Canonical magnitudes and outcome board: Contributions and Table~\ref{tab:h-status}.

The load-bearing finding is attributional, not algorithmic (RQ1). The confirmatory bundled analysis establishes that a large coverage gap exists ($+15.09$~pp; Holm); the descriptive policy$\times$hint $2\times2$ assigns it to archive policy with soft residuals near null (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}). The effect is real; attributing it to parent-level hint scalars was not. Carry Table~\ref{tab:audit-checklist} to another stack: match policy, warm-start, and budget axes before crediting an emitter.

Given that correction, the two attach points behave differently and for legible reasons. Within the non-LLM H2 stack, hard after-generation control moves evaluation-indexed coverage (Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition}) by removing proposals with low predicted fitness and high extinction risk (Table~\ref{tab:skip-mech}), and its wall-clock value is governed by an explicit inequality rather than by the coverage number alone (\S\ref{sec:h2-breakeven}). Within the LLM H1 stack, the residual soft effect after policy matching is a small estimate on primary \texttt{qwen} (mean $+0.26$~pp; 90\% CI $[-0.43,+0.95]$, compatible with a post-hoc $\pm 2$~pp band)---not confirmatory equivalence and not ``hints do not matter'' (\S\ref{sec:h1-impl-threat}, \S\ref{sec:limitations})---and the prompt already contains the parent's true fitness, so a small residual is what the interface predicts. Stacking / CMA / maze are bounds. Not interchangeable attach-role cells (\S\ref{sec:cross-stack}). Scope: \S\ref{sec:limitations}.

\subsection{Mechanistic reading}
\label{sec:why-discussion}

Skip logs explain the H2 eval-axis gain (\S\ref{sec:why-filter}; Table~\ref{tab:skip-mech}): discarded proposals have low predicted fitness / high extinction / low diversity; uncertainty does not separate classes. Warm-start ($971$ niches / $38.84\%$ coverage) means mid-budget H2 gains arise in an already seeded archive. A descriptive empty-archive mixed $2\times2$ ($n{=}10$) does \emph{not} turn parent-level scalars into a large cold-start residual; the stage-sensitive piece is the hard gate, whose @20k sign reverses from empty (\S\ref{sec:bootstrap-hyp}, \S\ref{sec:warm-start-limit}). Threshold $\tau$ trades mid-budget fill against terminal coverage (Table~\ref{tab:h2-tau-sweep}). In Jin's terms (Table~\ref{tab:jin-map}): individual-based gating reallocates sims; soft advice as tested does not; generation-based control untested. Whether the LLM \emph{used} the H1 scalars is a separate question (\S\ref{sec:h1-impl-threat}).

\subsection{Scope of the H1 soft-residual estimate}
\label{sec:h1-impl-threat}

\textbf{Admissible conclusion.} On primary \texttt{qwen-turbo} under matched \texttt{uniform\_frontier}, the soft residual is an effect estimate: mean $\Delta{=}{+}0.26$~pp with paired 90\% CI $[-0.43,+0.95]$ (Table~\ref{tab:decomposition}; status \S\ref{sec:limitations}). That CI is compatible with a post-hoc $\pm 2$~pp practical band; the paired-$t$ TOST is an exploratory consistency check only, not confirmatory equivalence. The same CI-compatible reading holds on \texttt{gpt-4o-mini}; on DeepSeek mean equivalence at $\pm 2$~pp was not established (Table~\ref{tab:h1-matched-providers}). Do not pool providers into a common null. Scope: mean terminal coverage only---not ``hints do not matter,'' not fitness/QD/anytime/cost claims, and not a model-independent before-generation null.

\textbf{What ``matched policy'' isolates (and what it does not).}
Matched H1 holds fixed: emitter mix ($20$ random / $20$ genetic / $10$ LLM), \texttt{uniform\_frontier} parent/target selection, system$+$user prompt \emph{templates}, JSON parse / genetic repair / coercion paths, warm-start archive, and the $32{,}500$-proposal-slot budget with no after-generation skips (hence $32{,}500$ sims). Logged transport is identical on primary \texttt{qwen-turbo}: $6{,}500$ LLM emit attempts/seed and $0$ fallbacks on both arms (Table~\ref{tab:h1-qd-cost}; appendix~\S\ref{sec:h1-prompt-appendix}). The intended treatment is only the filled surrogate line (live parent MLP scalars vs.\ YAML constants $0.5$/$1.0$). That is \emph{not} a full semantic isolation of the hint channel: (i)~stub constants are not a shuffled / mismatched / length-matched placebo drawn from the live scalar distribution, so context length and number formatting are only approximately matched; (ii)~arms were run as separate batch tiers, not time-interleaved; (iii)~there is no manipulation check that emitted candidates \emph{follow} the surrogate line---accepted-elite proxies (Table~\ref{tab:h1-llm-quality}) are near-null and descriptive, and rejected genotypes were never logged. A terminal null therefore cannot separate ``informative hints do not help search'' from ``the model does not use the hint channel.''

\paragraph{Shuffled placebo ($n{=}10$; descriptive).}
Tier \texttt{q1-h1-placebo-pilot} permutes intact live MLP $(\mathrm{fitness},\mathrm{uncertainty})$ pairs across the ${\approx}10$ LLM slots after \texttt{prepare\_emit}, preserving the batch scalar multiset while breaking parent alignment (protocol \texttt{artifacts/Q1\_H1\_PLACEBO\_LOCK.md}). Offline vs.\ frozen mixed-stack \texttt{hints}/\texttt{stub\_uniform}: terminal coverage $62.17\pm1.70\%$ (placebo) vs.\ $60.52\pm0.72\%$ / $60.21\pm0.81\%$; paired placebo$-$hints $+1.65\pm1.73$~pp (8/10; Wilcoxon $p{\approx}0.020$), but cov@20k only $+0.42$~pp---a late-terminal gap, not a mid-budget lift. An exploratory $\pm 2$~pp TOST of placebo vs.\ hints does not meet its criterion (90\% CI $[+0.64,+2.65]$). This does \emph{not} reopen matched H1 (effect-estimate reading unchanged) and is \emph{not} a confirmatory soft-channel win: the direction (shuffle above aligned live) is the wrong sign for ``correct numbers help,'' and the package is cross-tier (placebo later, $2$ LLM workers vs.\ frozen hints at $4$). A matched interleaved engineering seed (\texttt{q1-h1-placebo-interleaved}; protocol \texttt{Q1\_H1\_PLACEBO\_INTERLEAVED.md}; both arms, $2$ workers) was collected for seed~0 only: placebo$-$hints $-0.92$~pp terminal / $-1.44$~pp @20k, with interleaved hints matching frozen mixed-2x2 seed~0 ($+0.04$~pp). That single seed cannot support inference about non-reproduction of the historical $+1.65$~pp late gap; no sequential stop is claimed. The cross-tier placebo package therefore remains a caveated descriptive threat. The narrower audit lesson (prefer distribution-matched placebos over YAML $0.5$/$1.0$ alone) remains. Artifacts: \texttt{q1-h1-placebo-pilot/ANALYSIS.md}; \texttt{q1-h1-placebo-interleaved/ANALYSIS.md}.

\textbf{Why a near-null is still expected under the confirmatory interface.} The confirmatory user prompt already includes the parent elite's \emph{true} simulated fitness inside \texttt{current\_elite\_json}, plus neighbour elites with true fitness (appendix~\S\ref{sec:h1-prompt-appendix}). Live H1 only appends a surrogate estimate of fitness/uncertainty on that same parent genotype---a noisy re-statement of information the archive already holds---not a predicted child improvement or a rewrite instruction. Advice touches only ${\approx}10/50$ batch slots. Matched hints vs.\ stub remaining near-null is therefore unsurprising; the historical shuffled-placebo gap suggested that \emph{which} live scalars sit in which slot might move the late archive, but only one interleaved engineering seed was collected---so that threat stays caveated and no non-reproduction inference is drawn. Terminal coverage after a large warm-start ($971$ niches / $38.84\%$ of the grid) can hide early differences; frozen confirmatory H1 lacks cold-start curves, but the mixed-stack $2\times2$ supplies eval-indexed readouts (cov@20k hints$-$stub $+0.71$~pp; Table~\ref{tab:mixed-2x2}). Resource-screening pilots ($n{\le}3$) and Path~A child rewrite are demoted to appendix~\S\ref{sec:h1-pilots-appendix}---neither enters the H1 claim.

\textbf{What package~A adds (and still does not).}
Accepted-elite LLM quality proxies are now reported for \texttt{hints} vs.\ \texttt{stub\_uniform} (Table~\ref{tab:h1-llm-quality}): insert rate ${\approx}12\%$ vs.\ $11\%$, cell-improve rate ${\approx}5.6\%$ vs.\ $5.1\%$, realized child$-$parent $\Delta$fit ${\approx}{+}0.01$ both arms, compact edits (Hamming ${\approx}1.6$--$1.9$). These are descriptive, not Holm. Rejected LLM genotypes were never logged per-seed on frozen arms, so all-evaluated fitness and fail anatomy remain unavailable there; new runs append \texttt{proposal\_log.jsonl} (accepts$+$rejects; \texttt{artifacts/PROPOSAL\_LOG.md}). Offline MLP predict on a subsample of \emph{accepted} children systematically under-shoots true fitness (selection bias toward high realized fitness; not a logged emit-time score). A descriptive Path~A child-rewrite package ($n{=}10$; second LLM pass when predicted child fitness $<$ parent true fitness) likewise failed to move coverage beyond single-pass hints at ${\sim}{+}35\%$ wall---numbers and offline diagnostics in appendix~\S\ref{sec:h1-pilots-appendix}. Still open: cold-start H1; rewrite-stub control if Path~A is revisited (\S\ref{sec:limitations}, Future work). Interleaved placebo engineering seed~0: above ($n{=}1$; no inference).

\paragraph{Contribution framing.}
H1 is a within-stack case study of one weak, possibly redundant \emph{parent-level scalar} interface under one checkpoint---not a general before-generation impossibility result, and not an isolated soft-vs-hard attach-point theorem. Mixed-stack $2\times2$ anytime is complete (Table~\ref{tab:mixed-2x2}).

\subsection{Implications for LLM emitters}
\label{sec:when-llm}

The LLM is one emitter in the H1 stack, not the product under test, and not the stack on which H2 was isolated (Table~\ref{tab:jin-map}; \S\ref{sec:cross-stack}). On coverage/wall here it is mostly not the metrics winner (CMA-ME; genetic ME; Figure~\ref{fig:ladder}). Niche: structured, human-readable parent$\rightarrow$child edits quantified in the edit-anatomy appendix (\S\ref{sec:edit-anatomy})---not the outcome-morphology panels of Figure~\ref{fig:elites}.

\textbf{Decision rule (as tested here).} Let $t_{\mathrm{sim}}$ be evaluator cost, $t_{\mathrm{MLP}}$ surrogate inference cost per proposal, and $s$ the achieved skip rate.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Attribution first.} Before adopting any emitter on the strength of a reported gap, re-run the control under matched parent/target selection, warm-start, and budget. On this testbed the completed policy$\times$hint $2\times2$ assigns the bundled $+15$~pp gap to archive policy with soft residuals near null under both policies (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}). Cost: one extra arm (or the missing cell of an incomplete factorial).
  \item \emph{Hard gate when $t_{\mathrm{MLP}}<s\,t_{\mathrm{sim}}$ (accounting identity under fixed proposals and roughly constant $t_{\mathrm{sim}}$; re-measure $t$ and $s$).} Then filtering can buy both simulations and wall. On our CA stack the unconditional ${\sim}7\times$ slowdown estimate is optimistic if skipped candidates would have been cheaper early extinctions (\S\ref{sec:h2-breakeven}). Below that ratio it still buys coverage per simulation---adopt only if simulator count, not wall, binds---and expect a small terminal-coverage cost ($-0.83$~pp here). On an LLM mixed stack the gate does not cut API calls.
  \item \emph{Soft prompt scalars: do not expect archive gains from restating known quantities.} If the prompt already carries the parent's true fitness, adding a surrogate estimate of the same quantity is redundant by construction; spend the prompt budget on child-level or counterfactual advice instead (\S\ref{sec:h1-impl-threat}). Under latency or API cost, matched genetic ME reached $59.0\%$ coverage in ${\sim}6$~min with zero calls against $60.4\%$ in ${\sim}94$~min with ${\sim}6.5$k calls (\S\ref{sec:cost}).
  \item \emph{Choose the LLM emitter for the artifact, not the metric.} It is justified when editable, human-readable genotypes are the deliverable; on archive metrics here, genetic ME and CMA-ME dominate it.
\end{enumerate}

Do not treat H2's genetic-ME gate as already proven under LLM variation.

\subsection{Hypothesis: stage-dependent integration}
\label{sec:bootstrap-hyp}

Scheduling hypothesis (not a claim of this paper, and not a confirmatory stage$\times$attach factorial): stronger before-generation advice may matter mainly in \emph{cold} archive bootstrap; after-generation gating may help across mid/late evaluation trajectories. Primary-grid H1/H2 do \emph{not} test that table---they start from a $38.84\%$ warm-start floor (\S\ref{sec:warm-start-limit}). A descriptive empty-archive re-run of the mixed-stack $2\times2$ ($n{=}10$; same emitters, $\tau$, checkpoint) bounds the parent-scalar half: cold hints$-$stub is $+1.48\pm3.57$~pp terminal (7/10; seed~0's $+9.88$~pp smoke did not replicate) and $+1.07\pm1.71$~pp @20k---still a small, noisy residual, not a bootstrap win. The hard half is the stage-sensitive piece: warm mixed-stack filter \emph{raises} cov@20k ($+3.38$ / $+2.82$~pp; 10/10), while the same gate from empty \emph{lowers} it ($-1.72$ / $-2.01$~pp; 3/10 and 2/10) and costs more terminal coverage ($-5.7$ / $-6.2$~pp; 0/10), with skip rate falling from ${\sim}34\%$ to ${\sim}24\%$ because empty-bin force-eval is more common. Remaining follow-ons: first-fill curves, phase-gated filter, LLM-then-genetic schedules, genetic-stack cold H2.

\subsection{Limitations}

\subsubsection{Warm-start is a major boundary, not a footnote}
\label{sec:warm-start-limit}

Every confirmatory primary-grid arm loads the same nightly baseline before the matched $32{,}500$-proposal budget: $2{,}614$ JSONL records collapse to $971$ occupied niches ($38.84\%$ coverage; QD-score ${\approx}417.9$) against terminal hints ${\approx}58$--$60\%$ (\S\ref{sec:pyribs}). The experiment therefore studies how methods \emph{continue} an already substantially filled archive---not how they build a QD archive from empty. Exploration / empty-cell discovery / parent selection under cold start can differ sharply; surrogate gating and soft prompt advice are especially stage-sensitive. A descriptive empty-archive re-run of the mixed-stack $2\times2$ (tier \texttt{q1-v3-mixed-2x2-cold-smoke}; $n{=}10$; not Holm/TOST; Table~\ref{tab:cold-2x2}) does not upgrade warm H1 or Table~\ref{tab:mixed-2x2}. Cold terminals sit ${\sim}5$--$10$~pp below the warm band (hints $55.70\pm2.18\%$ vs.\ $60.52\pm0.72\%$). The parent-scalar residual stays small and noisy ($+1.48\pm3.57$~pp terminal; 7/10)---seed~0's $+9.88$~pp smoke was an outlier. The hard gate is the stage-sensitive result: from empty it loses both terminal coverage ($-5.7$/$-6.2$~pp; 0/10) and cov@20k (sign flip vs.\ the warm mixed grid), with skip falling from ${\sim}34\%$ to ${\sim}24\%$. Authors' own open item (cold-start H1; \S\ref{sec:bootstrap-hyp}) is therefore \emph{bounded}, not closed: the near-null parent-scalar residual survives empty start on this stack; the mid-budget filter gain does \emph{not}. Neither licenses a general answer to ``where should the surrogate attach?'' across cold / mid / late stages or across genetic vs.\ LLM stacks. Maze / dungeon / Sphere supporting tiers inherit their own archive conventions and do not close this bound for the primary CA case studies.

\subsubsection{Threats to validity}

\textbf{H1 TOST margin (canonical).}
Because the $\pm 2$~pp band was chosen at reporting time as a practical-indifference threshold (appendix~\S\ref{sec:stats-appendix})---\emph{not} locked before seeing the matched H1 deltas---the paired-$t$ TOST carries \emph{no formal Type-I error guarantee} and must \emph{not} be read as confirmatory equivalence. The substantive case for why $<2$~pp is operationally small is in that appendix; reuse of the same number in earlier resource screens does not register a SESOI. We report the primary-\texttt{qwen} effect estimate (mean $\Delta{=}{+}0.26$~pp; paired 90\% CI $[-0.43,+0.95]$; likewise \texttt{gpt-4o-mini}) and note that this CI is compatible with that post-hoc band; the TOST is only an exploratory consistency check (``criterion met / not met,'' not accept/reject equivalence). A prospectively locked margin (and preferably a fresh matched re-run) would be required to upgrade to an equivalence claim; this manuscript does not make that upgrade. Margin sensitivity ($\delta_{\min}$ and alternate fixed bands): Table~\ref{tab:h1-tost-margin}. Elsewhere the manuscript refers to this paragraph rather than restating the hedging.

Separately, the $n{=}10$ distribution-matched shuffled placebo (\S\ref{sec:h1-impl-threat}) addresses channel isolation versus stub constants; it does \emph{not} retrospectively validate the $\pm 2$~pp band, and its late $+1.65$~pp terminal gap vs.\ frozen hints is reported only as a descriptive threat---not as a soft-channel win.

H1's soft-residual estimate is otherwise scoped to primary-\texttt{qwen} paired mean \emph{terminal coverage} only (\S\ref{sec:h1-impl-threat}): not ``hints do not matter,'' not fitness/QD/anytime claims, and not a proof that before-generation guidance is useless. QD-score, best fitness, wall, and API calls accompany the TOST (Table~\ref{tab:h1-qd-cost}); eval-indexed soft anytime is in Table~\ref{tab:mixed-2x2}. True parent fitness is already in the JSON; accepted-elite LLM quality proxies are descriptive and near-null (Table~\ref{tab:h1-llm-quality}). Offline child-distribution NMAE and edit-vs-scalar correlations (appendix~\S\ref{sec:h1-pilots-appendix}) bound Path~A mechanism claims but are not confirmatory. Warm-start remains a major bound; a descriptive empty-archive $2\times2$ bounds but does not close stage$\times$attach (\S\ref{sec:warm-start-limit}). Frozen H1 arms still lack per-seed all-evaluated LLM rows; new runs write \texttt{proposal\_log.jsonl} (accepts$+$rejects; protocol \texttt{artifacts/PROPOSAL\_LOG.md}). An interleaved placebo vs.\ hints engineering seed~0 was collected (matched calendar/workers; $-0.92$~pp; protocol \texttt{Q1\_H1\_PLACEBO\_INTERLEAVED.md})---one seed, no inference; the historical cross-tier $+1.65$~pp placebo gap remains caveated. Regressor-family choice is scoped: MLP vs.\ GP is an offline lineage ablation; boosting was not a confirmatory factor.

H2 saves simulations under a fixed \emph{proposal-slot} budget with skips---simulator efficiency with ranking contribution beyond rate-matched skip volume (Table~\ref{tab:h2-ranking-controls}; \S\ref{sec:h2-impl-threat}). It is not a wall-clock win on this cheap testbed (\S\ref{sec:h2-breakeven}, \S\ref{sec:cost}). Absolute-$\tau$ oracle replay leaves ${\approx}0.7$~pp same-stream headroom (Table~\ref{tab:h2-oracle-replay}); niche-relative gating remains open. The pyribs coverage contrast searches a continuous relaxation of a mixed discrete/continuous genome; decode ablations still propose in $\mathbb{R}^{21}$ before rounding, and the CMA comparison is a pipeline reference rather than a factorial emitter decomposition (\S\ref{sec:h4}). Matched proposal slots are not matched real evaluations (under filter) and not matched dollar cost.

\textbf{API dollars vs.\ wall break-even (canonical).}
The hard-attach break-even $t_{\mathrm{MLP}}<s\,t_{\mathrm{sim}}$ is a \emph{wall / simulator} cost identity (\S\ref{sec:h2-breakeven}); it does not require token invoices. Separately, Table~\ref{tab:cost}'s $\sim\$1$--$2$ API column is only an order-of-magnitude sketch from published \texttt{qwen-turbo} rates $\times$ $6{,}500$ calls/seed. Frozen primary LLM runs did \emph{not} log per-call prompt/completion token counts (or request/response bodies), so those dollar figures cannot support fine-grained cost accounting, provider billing audits, or confirmatory \$ endpoints. New runs can emit usage tokens via \texttt{llm\_call\_log.jsonl}; frozen arms are not backfilled. Do not read the break-even rule as validated dollar-cost rigor.

\textbf{Fitness compose weights (canonical).}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fitness} uses fixed weights $(0.45,0.25,0.20,0.10)$ on diversity / survival / oscillation / topology---protocol-locked illuminator algebra shared by the simulator and the surrogate recompose, \emph{not} tuned to the H1/H2 contrasts and not justified by a preference study or ablation. The compose-gate measurement bugs corrected in this paper concern the extinction \emph{threshold} ($0.5$ vs.\ $0.95$), not these coefficients. We report no weight-sensitivity sweep (online or offline); coverage/fitness magnitudes and skip anatomy could shift under reweighting. Treat the weights as a fixed substrate choice (\S\ref{sec:substrate-appendix}); offline recompose sensitivity is Future work.

H3 remains not confirmatory on production filter and not superior after gray-zone repair (mean $\Delta$cov $-0.60$~pp; Table~\ref{tab:h-status}). Hold-out gate alignment is appendix~\S\ref{sec:checkpoint-appendix}.

LLM reproducibility is integrity-checked, not bit-exact. Paired run seeds do \emph{not} seed the provider API or pin backend weights: sampling uses $T{=}0.2$ without a documented provider RNG seed, soft arms were run as separate batch tiers (not nested/interleaved LLM calls), and frozen primary runs lack per-call I/O. Prompt templates live in the repository; composite prompt hashes and (where logged) \texttt{llm\_spec\_hash}, sampling knobs ($T{=}0.2$, \texttt{max\_tokens}{=}350), and a dated run window pin the confirmatory stack (Table~\ref{tab:pins-full}). Frozen primary runs do \emph{not} archive per-call request/response pairs, timestamps, token counts, SDK versions, or retry/timeout traces---hashes are not a substitute for those artifacts (API-\$ limitation: paragraph above). New MAP-Elites LLM runs write \texttt{llm\_call\_log.jsonl} under the run directory (messages, response text, usage tokens when returned, attempts/retries, latency; disable with \texttt{LIFEMANIFOLD\_LLM\_CALL\_LOG=0}). Primary model \texttt{qwen-turbo} is a floating provider alias, so even an identical prompt cannot guarantee the same backend weights. Parallel LLM emit uses four workers, but HTTP completion order does \emph{not} reorder archive updates: \texttt{executor.map} returns slot-ordered responses, emission freezes the archive at iteration start, and evaluate/insert proceed sequentially by \texttt{candidate\_id} (code: \texttt{parallel\_llm\_emit.py} / \texttt{loop.py}). Genetic ME and pyribs CMA remain locally re-runnable. Holm is within locked contrast sets only (no pooled paper-wide vector; appendix~\S\ref{sec:stats-appendix}). Endpoint$\rightarrow$test choices are Table~\ref{tab:test-map} (Wilcoxon direction vs.\ paired-$t$ TOST band vs.\ NI); at $n{=}10$ the exact one-sided Wilcoxon $p$-floor is $9.8\times10^{-4}$. Matched H1's TOST/MDE status is the canonical paragraph above. Other non-rejects are fail-to-reject, not equivalence claims: maze hints$-$stub and LLM$+$filter are small or wrong-direction. H4 is a descriptive continuous-relaxation ceiling (${\sim}13$~pp below CMA-ME; category correction for unfair encoding, with dual-report that locked F-RQ4 remains FAIL; \S\ref{sec:h4}). Maze five-arm Holm $m{=}8$ is complete at $n{=}10$ but mixed (filter AUC rejects; family as a whole does not).

\subsubsection{Generalizability}

H1 (exploratory) and H2 (descriptive) share one controlled structured-genotype CA evaluator (\S\ref{sec:substrate}) chosen so that both soft prompt-side and hard after-generation interventions are definable---not a multi-domain QD ranking study. Both claim-facing case studies are \emph{warm-started} mid/late illuminations ($38.84\%$ initial coverage); a descriptive empty-archive mixed $2\times2$ bounds stage-sensitivity without closing a factorial (\S\ref{sec:warm-start-limit}). Maze is the main-text discrete PCG transfer domain with a \emph{locked} AUC family; it supports non-LLM filter AUC but not bundled LLM lift or H3-style stacking, and H2 magnitudes ($+3.7$~pp @$20$k, $33.5\%$ skips) remain testbed-specific descriptive cuts. The wall break-even $t_{\mathrm{MLP}}<s\,t_{\mathrm{sim}}$ is a cost identity instantiated on CA wall ratios and maze micro-timings (\S\ref{sec:h2-breakeven}); it is not validated on a naturally expensive second domain. We do not claim a universal ranking on expensive continuous genomes or CFD-style SAIL domains, nor a stage-universal attach-point theorem. A literature Sphere H2 check and a dungeon factorial are appendix~\S\ref{sec:sphere-h2-appendix} / \S\ref{sec:dungeon-appendix} (not required for the H1/H2 case studies or locked maze H5). Maze genetic arms hit a 900-cell ceiling; secondary-domain $\tau$ was set by shadow replay, not by repeating the primary online sweep (Table~\ref{tab:h2-tau-sweep}). Multi-provider rows replicate the bundled $\Delta$ direction at $n{=}10$, with almost $2\times$ variation in $\Delta$cov and a weaker \texttt{gpt-4o-mini} hints arm (\S\ref{sec:multi-llm}; matched soft residual remains \texttt{qwen-turbo}).

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We audited attribution in a surrogate-assisted MAP-Elites setting---target selection, prompt scalars, and evaluation gating as separate within-stack contrasts on a shared warm-start floor---and found that the first control to check is the right one. The confirmatory bundled contrast of the type routinely used to credit LLM emitters showed $+15.09$~pp coverage for surrogate hints; the subsequently completed descriptive policy$\times$hint $2\times2$ replicates the attribution pattern---archive policy ${\approx}{+}15$~pp on either soft level; soft residuals near null under both---without a preregistered factorial ANOVA (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}). What remains after matching policy is a small effect estimate: mean $\Delta{=}{+}0.26$~pp with paired 90\% CI $[-0.43,+0.95]$ on primary \texttt{qwen-turbo} (compatible with a post-hoc $\pm 2$~pp band; exploratory TOST only---\S\ref{sec:limitations}), with \texttt{gpt-4o-mini} likewise and DeepSeek mean equivalence not established under high run-to-run variance---not an all-providers null, and expected under an interface that already supplies the parent's true fitness (\S\ref{sec:h1-impl-threat}).

The hard attach point is priced with an explicit cost identity (descriptive H2 case study; no Holm family): threshold gating buys mid-budget coverage per simulation on the non-LLM genetic branch, but repays wall clock only when $t_{\mathrm{MLP}}<s\,t_{\mathrm{sim}}$---roughly a $7\times$ slower simulator than ours (\S\ref{sec:h2-breakeven}). That inequality is an accounting identity under fixed proposals; the ${\sim}7\times$ / $\delta^{\star}$ numbers and the ${\sim}{+}3.7$~pp @$20$k cut are CA$+$maze instantiations (injected-delay sanity only; unconditional $t_{\mathrm{sim}}$ can overstate skip savings), not a portable QD ranking or a locked confirmatory endpoint. What we expect to reuse across stacks is Table~\ref{tab:audit-checklist}---not domain-specific coverage deltas. This is not a broad LLM-QD win, not a general before-generation null, and not a pure ranking isolation (\S\ref{sec:h2-impl-threat}); maze transfer covers only non-LLM filter AUC. Code/artifacts: tag~\texttt{\SubmissionGitTag} / \texttt{\SubmissionGitShort}. Confounds / $2\times2$: \S\ref{sec:cross-stack}.

\section{Statistical details}
\label{sec:stats-appendix}

Main-text reporting follows the endpoint$\rightarrow$test map in Table~\ref{tab:test-map} (\S\ref{sec:stats}): confirmatory direction contrasts use paired Wilcoxon$+$Holm; matched H1 uses exploratory paired-$t$ TOST; H3-gray uses protocol NI. This appendix records selective Kerby $\mathrm{PPS}$ reporting.

\paragraph{Multiple-comparison policy.}
Primary decisions use Holm within each pre-specified confirmatory contrast set ($\alpha{=}0.05$). Sets are frozen per family relative to that family's analysis window (bundled stack / floor; maze H5 AUC; per-provider replications; gray-zone NI; appendix dungeon AUC); matched H1 TOST sits outside confirmatory Holm sets. H4 is dual-reported: locked F-RQ4 FAIL retained, with a descriptive continuous-relaxation reading (\S\ref{sec:h4}). We do \emph{not} pool these families into one paper-wide Holm vector---families differ in estimand (terminal vs.\ AUC vs.\ NI) and were locked on separate analysis windows; within-set correction is the protocol default. Primary outcomes remain Table~\ref{tab:families}.

\paragraph{Paired proportion of favorable pairs ($\mathrm{PPS}$; selective).}
Classic Vargha--Delaney $A_{12}$ is defined for independent samples~\cite{vargha2000new}. On paired seeds Kerby's \emph{paired proportion of favorable pairs}~\cite{kerby2014simple} (common-language effect size for matched pairs~\cite{mcgraw1992cl}) is
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{PPS} \;=\; \frac{\#\{\Delta_i > 0\} + 0.5\,\#\{\Delta_i = 0\}}{n},
  \label{eq:pps}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_i$ is treatment $-$ control on seed $i$ (for ``less,'' count $\Delta_i < 0$). $\mathrm{PPS}{=}0.5$ is chance under no systematic paired advantage, and (with ties as half) $\mathrm{PPS}$ coincides with the Sign rate when there are no ties. At $n{=}10$, unanimous contrasts therefore give $\mathrm{PPS}\in\{0,1\}$; main-text tables omit that redundant column and keep Sign. We report $\mathrm{PPS}$ only when it is non-extreme or when no Sign column is shown (H4 MAE coverage; H3-gray NI). Not a propensity score, not an $A_{12}$ cutoff import, and not a confirmatory gate (Holm / protocol $d_z$ decide pass/fail).

\paragraph{H1 matched residual (audit detail for RQ1).}
Main text \S\ref{sec:decomposition} reports the decision numbers only. This subsection keeps the statistical apparatus that audits the soft residual.

\textbf{Primary estimand.} On \texttt{qwen-turbo} ($n{=}10$), hints $-$ \texttt{stub\_uniform} mean $\Delta$cov $=+0.26$~pp (SD~$1.19$; 90\% CI $[-0.43,+0.95]$; bootstrap 95\% CI $[-0.43,+0.98]$). Table~\ref{tab:decomposition} lists companions. An exploratory paired-$t$ TOST vs.\ a post-hoc $\pm 2$~pp band meets its criterion ($p_{\mathrm{TOST}}{=}6.4\times10^{-4}$) but does \emph{not} upgrade the estimate to confirmatory SESOI equivalence (\S\ref{sec:limitations}). \textbf{Why $\pm 2$~pp (substantive).} On a $2500$-cell archive, $\pm 2$~pp $=\pm 50$ cells (${\approx}2\%$ of the grid; ${\approx}3.3\%$ of the observed ${\sim}60\%$ coverage level). \paragraph{DeepSeek seed influence (appendix sensitivity only).}
Table~\ref{tab:h1-matched-providers} keeps the full-$n{=}10$ readout that mean equivalence was \textbf{not established} as the claim-facing DeepSeek row. For completeness: seed~8 $\Delta{=}{+}10.24$~pp (hints $69.12\%$ vs.\ stub\_uniform $58.88\%$) is the sole high-leverage paired delta under an intercept-only Cook's distance on $\{\Delta\mathrm{cov}_i\}$ ($D_8{\approx}0.85$; rule-of-thumb $4/n{=}0.40$; all other seeds $D_i{\le}0.13$). It is not an LLM-fallback artifact ($0/6500$ fallbacks on hints; $4/6500$ on stub\_uniform). Offline leave-one-out dropping seed~8 alone yields mean $\Delta{\approx}0$ and a mean-TOST criterion met (90\% CI $[-0.90,0.90]$); no other single-seed drop flips not-met$\rightarrow$met. This sensitivity explains why DeepSeek variance is high; it does \emph{not} amend the provider claim, does not license pooling with qwen/\texttt{gpt-4o-mini}, and is not a main-text rescue of a band reading.

Separately, as a sensitivity analysis---not TOST power---we report the approximate minimum detectable effect for a two-sided paired $t$-test of H$_0{:}\,\mathrm{mean}\,\Delta{=}0$ at $n$ seeds, $\alpha{=}0.05$, 80\% power:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{MDE} \;=\; \bigl(t_{1-\alpha/2,\,n-1} + t_{0.80,\,n-1}\bigr)\,\frac{\mathrm{SD}_{\Delta}}{\sqrt{n}},
  \label{eq:mde}
\end{equation}
This is the power to detect a \emph{non-zero} paired mean difference, not power for a confirmatory TOST at $\pm 2$~pp. At a true effect on the reporting-band edge ($|\Delta|{=}2$~pp), TOST power is necessarily much lower than a difference-test power at that same $\Delta$; we do \emph{not} report post-hoc observed power at $1$/$2$~pp. For H1, $\mathrm{SD}_{\Delta}{=}1.19$~pp is taken from the same matched $n{=}10$ analysis (retrospective; no independent pilot variance was locked for this contrast), yielding $\mathrm{MDE}{\approx}1.2$~pp---a design-sensitivity note only, not a margin justification. Elsewhere we quote only MDE numbers under the same difference-test convention (e.g.\ dungeon hints$-$stub coverage AUC ${\approx}1.2$~pp; maze genetic\_filter coverage AUC ${\approx}2.1$~pp before Holm $m{=}8$). These do not re-open locked Holm families or convert non-rejects into confirmatory band claims.

\section{Shared-checkpoint validity (hold-out algebra)}
\label{sec:checkpoint-appendix}
\label{sec:checkpoint}

Main text leads with scale-normalized NMAE and treats $R^2$ as a secondary, variance-sensitive companion. This appendix records why $R^2$ and MAE can move in opposite directions when the compose gate changes, what the hold-out CI means, and what global hold-out does \emph{not} validate.

\textbf{Archive endpoints are never surrogate scores.} Every confirmatory elite is inserted only after \texttt{evaluate\_candidate} (deterministic simulator fitness/BC). Skipped H2 proposals never enter the archive. Terminal coverage and mean elite fitness therefore cannot be an artifact of surrogate error alone; the surrogate affects \emph{which} candidates are simulated (H2) or which scalars appear in the prompt (H1), not the reported archive metrics.

Offline training on the nightly buffer ($N=35{,}114$ rows; schema~2.1, 24-D features; hold-out $n{=}7{,}023$) yields hold-out metrics for checkpoint \texttt{nightly\_v3\_mc\_d005.pkl}. Protocol amendment 2026-07-28 aligns the offline compose gate with runtime YAML ($0.95$): MAE$_{\text{fitness}}=0.029$, NMAE$=\mathrm{MAE}/\mathrm{std}(y_{\mathrm{gate}})=0.112$, $R^2_{\text{fitness}}=0.94$, MAE$_{\text{stability}}=0.040$, passing \texttt{hints\_ok}.

\textbf{Bootstrap unit.} Reported 95\% CIs (NMAE $[0.107,0.117]$; MAE $[0.027,0.030]$; $R^2$ $[0.94,0.95]$) use $B{=}10{,}000$ nonparametric bootstrap \emph{resamples of hold-out rows} treated as exchangeable (artifact: \texttt{holdout\_compose\_gate\_alignment.json}). Rows from the nightly buffer are not independent maps/seeds; the CI is therefore a within-split precision band and may be optimistically narrow under clustering. We do \emph{not} claim a run-clustered standard error.

\textbf{NMAE denominator (cross-gate).} Within a single compose definition, NMAE$=\mathrm{MAE}/\mathrm{std}(y)$ is the natural scale-free error. Across gates, $\mathrm{std}(y)$ itself changes, so gate-specific NMAE is \emph{not} a fixed-denominator contrast. Prefer MAE for absolute error, or NMAE with denominator locked to the runtime target $\mathrm{std}(y_{0.95}){=}0.255$: that fixed-denominator NMAE is $0.027$ at gate~$0.5$ vs.\ $0.112$ at gate~$0.95$ (MAE $0.0069\!\rightarrow\!0.029$). The headline NMAE$0.052\!\rightarrow\!0.112$ uses each gate's own $\mathrm{std}(y)$ and should not be read as a different estimand than the fixed-denominator pair.

At gate~$0.5$, $96.2\%$ of hold-out labels are exactly zero (std$(y){=}0.133$); at gate~$0.95$, only $29.0\%$ are zero and the continuous mass spreads (std$(y){=}0.255$, $\times 1.92$). Because $R^2{=}1{-}\mathrm{SS}_{\mathrm{res}}/\mathrm{SS}_{\mathrm{tot}}$, larger target variance inflates $R^2$ even as absolute MAE grows ($\times 4.12$). Therefore $R^2{=}0.94$ is \emph{not} a model-quality upgrade over the historical $R^2{=}0.76$ headline---it is the correct metric for the runtime target. The misalignment affected only the offline validity metric; live filter logs always used gate~$0.95$.

\textbf{Arm-conditional validation.} Global hold-out does \emph{not} certify accuracy on every arm's proposal stream. For the non-LLM H2 gate we now report shadow-stream online ranking/calibration (AUROC~$0.906$, precision/recall @$\tau$, emitter-wise NMAE; \S\ref{sec:h2-impl-threat}) in addition to skip anatomy and false-skip / missed-elite rates. Still open: descriptor-space error maps, uncertainty calibration, and per-arm online NMAE on LLM mixed / rewrite streams (child-distribution NMAE ${\approx}0.63$/$0.39$ in appendix~\S\ref{sec:h1-pilots-appendix} is a soft-channel diagnostic, not the H2 genetic gate). Hold-out metrics do not replace simulator endpoints for H1/H2/H3.

\textbf{Consistency after measurement fixes.} The compose-gate and NMAE-denominator bugs (Table~\ref{tab:bug-ledger}) changed offline validity reporting and H3 confirmatory eligibility, not archive insertion fitness. Claim-facing terminal coverage / mean elite fitness / QD on frozen primary tiers (\texttt{q1-full}, matched H1, genetic H2) remain those of the original simulator summaries; no re-aggregation of archive endpoints was required after the 2026-07-28 hold-out alignment.

\section{MLP vs.\ GP/UCB ablation (offline)}
\label{sec:surrogate-ablation-appendix}

\emph{Not required for the descriptive H2 case study.} Production H2 uses an MLP ensemble with MC-dropout and a fitness threshold ($\texttt{min\_fit}{=}0.45$, $\texttt{max\_u}{=}1.0$), not SAIL-style GP+UCB. This appendix records the offline MLP-vs-GP/UCB comparison that motivates freezing that family for the attach-role contrast (\S\ref{sec:surrogate-method}). Hold-out NMAE/$R^2$ select the regressor; they do \emph{not} validate live gate decisions (\S\ref{sec:h2-impl-threat}). On the nightly buffer hold-out at runtime-aligned compose gate~$0.95$, the production MLP achieves NMAE$=0.112$ ($R^2{=}0.94$ on that same target) vs.\ NMAE$=0.222$ ($R^2{=}0.89$) for a scikit-learn GP fitness regressor trained on 5{,}000 rows under the \emph{same} label definition, and is ${\sim}37\times$ faster at a 32{,}500-proposal batch. An earlier M1 snapshot at misaligned gate~$0.5$ reported MLP $R^2{=}0.76$ vs.\ GP ${\approx}0.22$; that gap is not reusable as a model-quality argument because ${\approx}96\%$ of gate-$0.5$ labels are zero (\S\ref{sec:checkpoint-appendix}).

Offline acquisition replay: replacing the MLP with the GP under the \emph{same} threshold gate worsens the false-skip proxy ($0.7\%\!\rightarrow\!3.2\%$); a UCB skip rule on GP scores ($\mu{+}\beta\sigma < \texttt{min\_fit}$, $\beta\in\{0.15,0.5,1.0\}$) stays near $99\%$ skip. On logged Q1 filter proposals ($n{=}325{,}000$), replaying UCB on the \emph{same} MLP $\mu/\sigma$ only softens the gate (skip $33.5\%\!\rightarrow\!28.5\%/25.7\%/21.2\%$) and never adds skips. With $\texttt{max\_u}{=}1.0$, uncertainty is not load-bearing for the production threshold gate. GP+UCB is therefore a related SAIL lineage, not a competitive drop-in. Tree boosters (XGBoost / LightGBM) are not part of this ablation; the repository's optional LightGBM path is disclosed in the main text as a non-confirmatory alternative.

\section{CMA encoding / discrete-search controls}
\label{sec:encoding-appendix}

\emph{Not required to read main-text H4 as a continuous-relaxation ceiling.} Main-text \S\ref{sec:h4} reports \texttt{rint}-decode CMA-ME/MAE vs.\ hints. This appendix records decode and discrete-search controls that bound how much of that gap is encoding vs.\ search.

\textbf{Encoding ablation} (seeds~0--4, descriptive): alternate rule-bit \emph{decode} and cold-start CMA-ME leave the continuous-relaxation gap intact. These runs vary how continuous proposals map to rule bits; they do \emph{not} test discrete search in the same space as genetic/LLM emitters. \texttt{threshold} ($\geq 0.5$ bits) is \emph{bit-identical} to frozen \texttt{rint} on every seed; \texttt{bernoulli} decode is $-0.46$~pp mean ($72.18\%$); empty-archive \texttt{cold} is $-3.47$~pp ($69.17\%$)---still $\geq +8.8$~pp above hints on every arm mean.

\textbf{Bernoulli-decode CMA-ME} ($n{=}10$, descriptive). Same warm-start and $32{,}500$-ask budget (asks $=$ sims); CMA proposes in $\mathbb{R}^{21}$ but rule bits are Bernoulli-sampled at decode (discrete genotypes at evaluation, continuous covariance adaptation unchanged). Mean coverage $71.25 \pm 2.88$\%, fitness $0.578 \pm 0.020$. Paired vs.\ \texttt{hints}: $+10.84 \pm 2.94$~pp (10/10 seeds); vs.\ \texttt{genetic\_me\_uniform}: $+12.22 \pm 2.56$~pp (10/10); vs.\ \texttt{rint} CMA-ME: $-2.12 \pm 4.93$~pp (3/10).

\textbf{Native discrete-search CMA-ME} ($n{=}10$, descriptive). Custom bit-flip emitter on rule bits + Gaussian on floats, discrete $\{0,1\}$ genotype stored in archive, $\sigma$ adapted by a 1/5 success rule (MVP). \section{Primary evaluator details}
\label{sec:substrate-appendix}

Main-text \S\ref{sec:substrate} sketches genome, simulator, and behaviour axes. This appendix records fitness composition algebra and a computational edit-anatomy case study (LLM vs.\ genetic parent$\rightarrow$child diffs)---not domain claims.

\paragraph{Genome and evaluation.}
Emitters search a 21-parameter genome: nine birth bits $b_0,\ldots,b_8$, nine survival bits $s_0,\ldots,s_8$, and continuous scalars $(\texttt{noise},\texttt{resource\_regen},\texttt{predation})$. CMA-ME/MAE propose in the same $\mathbb{R}^{21}$ before \texttt{rint}/clip decode (\S\ref{sec:pyribs}). The surrogate does \emph{not} ingest raw 21-D alone: schema~2.1 builds a 24-D feature vector
\begin{equation}
  x \;=\; \bigl(b_0,\ldots,b_8,\; s_0,\ldots,s_8,\; \texttt{noise},\; \texttt{resource\_regen},\; \texttt{predation},\;
  |B|/9,\; |S|/9,\; |B\cap S|/9\bigr),
  \label{eq:features-24}
\end{equation}
where $B{=}\{i:b_i{=}1\}$ and $S{=}\{i:s_i{=}1\}$ (implementation: \texttt{encode\_world\_spec\_features\_v21}). Legacy schema~2.0 was the 21-D prefix only. Each evaluation runs a fixed $50\times50$, $T_{\mathrm{sim}}{=}200$ toroidal grid. Before \texttt{run\_world}, \texttt{apply\_canonical\_seed} sets \texttt{world.seed} from SHA-256 of the canonical genome JSON (independent of the MAP-Elites run seed $0$--$9$), so fitness/BC are a deterministic function of the genotype; early extinction yields archive fitness~0. Terminal elites keep the fitness/BC from that single insertion pass---they are not re-scored at end of run. Illustration panels that re-call \texttt{run\_world} on archived genomes (Figure~\ref{fig:elites}) are for visualisation; under the same canonical seed they must match the stored labels.

\paragraph{Behaviour and fitness.}
Archive axes are stability and diversity in $[0,1]$. Authoritative insertion fitness recomposes diversity, survival, oscillation, and topology proxies. All fitness components below are dimensionless scores already scaled to $[0,1]$ before weighting (topology indices TI/TH are likewise clipped into $[0,1]$ before averaging):
\begin{equation}
  E_{\mathrm{ext}} = \mathrm{clip}(1 - \rho_{\mathrm{final}}, 0, 1), \quad
  T_{\mathrm{topo}} = \mathrm{clip}\!\left(\tfrac{\text{TI} + \text{TH}}{2}, 0, 1\right)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:fitness}
  \text{fitness} = \mathrm{clip}\bigl(
    0.45\,D + 0.25\,(1 - E_{\mathrm{ext}}) + 0.20\,A + 0.10\,T_{\mathrm{topo}}
  ,\; 0,\; 1\bigr)
\end{equation}
where $D\in[0,1]$ is clipped diversity, $A\in[0,1]$ is \texttt{oscillation\_score}, and $T_{\mathrm{topo}}\in[0,1]$ is the topology score (distinct from the simulator horizon $T_{\mathrm{sim}}{=}200$ above). The outer $\mathrm{clip}(\cdot,0,1)$ is defensive. Weights $(0.45,0.25,0.20,0.10)$ are fixed protocol constants shared by live evaluation and surrogate recompose---not fit to the H1/H2 data, not preference-elicited, and not swept for sensitivity in this manuscript (status \S\ref{sec:limitations}). The surrogate recomposes the same formula from predicted components (\S\ref{sec:surrogate-method}). QD-score is
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{QD} \;=\; \sum_{c \in \mathrm{filled}} f_c \;=\; |\mathrm{filled}| \cdot \bar f.
  \label{eq:qd}
\end{equation}
