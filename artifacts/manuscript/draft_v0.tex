\title{Matching Target Selection Reverses a Bundled {LLM}-Emitter Advantage in Warm-Started Surrogate-Assisted {MAP}-{E}lites}

\author{Artem Starovoitov\corref{cor1}}
\ead{artem.starovoitov@proton.me}
\ead[url]{https://orcid.org/0009-0005-3170-4865}
\address{Independent Engineer, Moscow, Russia}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author}

\begin{abstract}
An LLM emitter enters a quality-diversity (QD) loop with a prompt, a target-selection policy, a warm-start archive, and a budget convention, so a measured gain may not belong to the claimed channel. Matching those factors is ordinary EC hygiene; we do not introduce a new matching method. We measure one bundled comparison on structured-genotype MAP-Elites. Locked primary-grid arms continue a shared floor of $971$ niches / $38.84\%$ coverage: mid/late continuation from one JSONL, not cold-start illumination. A bundled hints-versus-stub gap of about $15$ percentage points (pp) of terminal coverage collapses to about $0.3$~pp when target selection is matched: the tested parent-level scalar augmentation contributed little additional terminal coverage under that match. The policy magnitude is consistent with this fitness--descriptor geometry; bundling it with the LLM arm made the $15$~pp liable to be misattributed to the prompt channel. A calendar-blocked $2\times2$ on five independently built floors of the same occupancy, with a dated model ID and call logs, replicates that pattern (policy ${\approx}{+}15$~pp; leftover $+0.29$~pp; distribution-matched shuffle leftover $+0.28$~pp). An empty-archive maze $2\times2$ on a second evaluator repeats the inversion (policy ${\approx}{+}39$~pp at $5$k; leftover $+0.02$/$+0.23$~pp); those magnitudes are not the CA continuation pp. Not a new emitter and not a mediation estimate. A genetic threshold gate illustrates evaluation efficiency, not a terminal or wall win on this cheap cellular-automaton evaluator. Hard gating sign-flips from empty on the mixed stack (descriptive); warm-start gating should not be generalized to cold-start illumination.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A measured quality-diversity (QD) gain on an LLM emitter is not automatically a gain from the channel it is attributed to. The emitter arrives with a prompt, a target-selection policy, a warm-start archive, and a budget convention, and any of those can produce the gap. On a controlled structured-genotype MAP-Elites testbed the attribution error is large: a $15$~pp coverage difference that reads as ``hints beat stub'' collapses when the control uses the same target-selection policy, with the parent-level prompt-scalar residual near null under both archive policies (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}). The policy magnitude is consistent with this fitness--descriptor geometry ($w_D{=}0.45$; \S\ref{sec:fd-coupling}); the result is that bundling it with the LLM arm made $+15$~pp liable to be misattributed to the prompt channel. That leftover is the expected residual of a redundant parent-level interface. The locked Holm contrast is a pipeline-level difference on one floor file. A later calendar-blocked $2\times2$ on five independent floors replicates the collapse; it is still not a mediation of the Holm gap.

An LLM emitter is a \emph{composite intervention} coupled to prompts, target selection, representation, floating model state, and evaluation budget. Fair comparison, matched budgets, disclosed initialization, and configuration hygiene are ordinary experimental concerns in EC~\cite{eiben2011parameter,bartzbeielstein2006experimental}. Because those factors travel with the operator, a pipeline-level swap \emph{can} co-vary them with the claimed channel. Table~\ref{tab:audit-checklist} (\S\ref{sec:terminology}) records the hygiene used here (\S\ref{sec:related}).

MAP-Elites still spends a black-box roll-out per evaluated candidate~\cite{mouret2015illuminating,gaier2018sail}, and classical SAEAs ask how to spend that budget~\cite{jin2005fitness}. Those taxonomies situate the two mechanisms we also run (Table~\ref{tab:jin-map}). We measure where the bundled gap belongs under matched controls.

On one controlled evaluator (\S\ref{sec:substrate}) we answer one decision question:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\textbf{RQ1}] Does matching target selection collapse a bundled LLM ``hints'' coverage gain, or do parent-level prompt scalars retain it?
\end{enumerate}
That answer is a mid/late continuation on a $971$-niche / $38.84\%$ floor. Locked Holm / matched-H1 rows use one JSONL; the calendar-blocked $2\times2$ uses five independently built floors of the same occupancy (\S\ref{sec:rq1-cf}, \S\ref{sec:warm-start-limit}). Protocol labels \textbf{H1}--\textbf{H5} are the audit ledger (Table~\ref{tab:h-status}).

Previous QD and LLM-emitter comparisons motivate matched experimental controls, but the practical size of the resulting attribution error is unclear.

\textbf{What a practitioner can decide after reading.} Match target selection before crediting an LLM emitter (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}). The tested parent-level scalar augmentation contributed little additional terminal coverage under matched target selection (\S\ref{sec:h1-impl-threat}). Gating is a cost-accounting check (\S\ref{sec:h2}). Magnitudes: Contributions.

\subsection{Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A bundled comparison reverses attribution when target selection is unmatched (primary).} On the shared $38.84\%$ warm-start floor, bundled \texttt{stub}$\rightarrow$\texttt{hints} yields $+15.09 \pm 2.42$~pp coverage ($+0.047 \pm 0.010$ fitness; $10/10$; Holm $m{=}2$). Completing \texttt{hints\_minfit} gives a historical policy$\times$hint $2\times2$ (three tiers): soft residuals near null under both policies ($-0.02$ / $+0.26$~pp) while matching target selection removes ${\approx}{+}15$~pp on either soft level (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}). A calendar-blocked $2\times2$ on five independently built $971$-niche floors, dated \texttt{gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18}, and call logs replicates the same pattern: policy $+14.53$ / $+14.82$~pp of \emph{new} coverage above the floor (10/10 blocks; archive-clustered 95\% CI $[+11.8,+18.0]$ / $[+12.2,+18.1]$); live vs.\ stub collapsed archives match at minfit in all 10 blocks; the uniform leftover is $+0.29$~pp (archive-clustered 95\% CI $[-0.20,+0.75]$); a distribution-matched shuffle leftover is $+0.28$~pp (archive-clustered 95\% CI $[+0.02,+0.54]$; Table~\ref{tab:rq1-cf}). An API-free genetic policy swap moves $+13.1$~pp. That bound does not identify the historical Holm $+15.09$~pp, and we do not claim that target selection \emph{caused} it; the admissible reading is that target selection accounts for the overwhelming majority of the \emph{observed} bundled gap. The \emph{magnitude} of that policy movement is this fitness--descriptor geometry ($w_D{=}0.45$; appendix Table~\ref{tab:fd-coupling}); the attribution (policy vs.\ scalars) is the within-geometry finding. The same stack under two controls reaches opposite conclusions ($+15.09$~pp Holm PASS vs.\ $+0.26$~pp residual): that inversion is the contribution, not a checklist. Comparison-hygiene checks used here: Table~\ref{tab:audit-checklist} (Methods; not a numbered contribution). Two measurement inconsistencies were identified during the study; neither affected archive insertion fitness or the primary H1/H2 outcomes (appendix Table~\ref{tab:bug-ledger}). We report that matched-control collapse rather than a mediation claim. This is the load-bearing result.
  \item \textbf{Expected leftover after matching (H1 residual, not a standalone finding).} After matching policy, those scalars on primary \texttt{qwen-turbo} yield mean $\Delta{=}{+}0.26$~pp (paired 95\% CI $[-0.59,+1.11]$; $n{=}10$). The calendar-blocked leftover at uniform is $+0.29$~pp (archive-clustered 95\% CI $[-0.20,+0.75]$); at minfit the two arms wrote identical collapsed terminal archives in all 10 blocks (same occupied bins, elite fitness, and stored genotypes). Those intervals are substantially narrower than the policy effect (${\approx}15$~pp); the study was not a prospective equivalence test. The same leftover pattern holds on \texttt{gpt-4o-mini}; on DeepSeek the paired 95\% CI $[-1.49,+3.53]$ is not near zero---not provider-universal (Table~\ref{tab:h1-matched-providers}; \S\ref{sec:limitations}). We do not claim that prompt scalars do not help LLM emitters: this is the residual of a redundant parent-level interface (true $f$ already in the prompt on ${\approx}10/50$ LLM slots), not a general soft-channel null (\S\ref{sec:h1-impl-threat}). The experiment estimates the incremental effect of providing parent-level scalar information through this prompt interface, not the effect of a surrogate-derived scalar when the downstream LLM demonstrably conditions on it.
  \item \textbf{Budget-axis illustration (robustness; not a co-equal claim).} On the matched non-LLM genetic stack, from a shared warm-start floor of $971$ niches / $38.84\%$ initial coverage, a threshold gate skipped $33.5\%$ of proposals, raising evaluation-indexed coverage $+3.65$~pp at $20{,}000$ real evaluations, lost $-0.83$~pp terminal coverage at equal slots, and lost paired wall $+13.6$~min. Surrogate utility therefore depends on which budget is claimed and on evaluator cost (\S\ref{sec:h2}). Fontaine Sphere reproduces the evaluation-indexed pattern ($+7.88$~pp; appendix Table~\ref{tab:sphere-h2}).
  \item \textbf{Scope, stated as a bound.} The attribution result is a warm-started mid/late continuation ($971$ niches / $38.84\%$ coverage) on one structured-genotype CA evaluator, not cold-start build-from-empty (\S\ref{sec:warm-start-limit}). Hard gating sign-flips from empty on the mixed stack (appendix Table~\ref{tab:cold-2x2}); genetic-stack H2 is untested from empty. Maze H5 FAIL ($2/8$): only non-LLM filter AUC (appendix Table~\ref{tab:b5-holm}). An empty-archive maze policy$\times$hint $2\times2$ at $5$k repeats the CA inversion (policy $+39.10$/$+39.31$~pp; leftover $+0.02$/$+0.23$~pp; Table~\ref{tab:rq1-maze})---same qualitative pattern, not portable pp, not a Holm family. CMA ceiling: appendix~\S\ref{sec:encoding-appendix}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background and related work}
\label{sec:related}

Quality-diversity (QD) algorithms seek diverse high-performing collections rather than a single optimum~\cite{pugh2016quality,cully2018quality,chatzilygeroudis2021qd}. MAP-Elites retains the best elite per behavioural niche~\cite{mouret2015illuminating}; heterogeneous emitters can improve illumination~\cite{cully2020multiemitter}. CMA-ME / CMA-MAE are strong continuous QD baselines~\cite{fontaine2020cmame,fontaine2023cmamae,zhao2024cmamae,pyribs2020}. We run them on the same simulator (\S\ref{sec:pyribs}): production pyribs is a representation-mismatched \texttt{rint} pipeline; native discrete CMA-ME / pbCMA are the matched-representation controls (\S\ref{sec:h4}).

Classical SAEAs treat prediction as \emph{model management}---when to trust approximate fitness~\cite{jin2005fitness,jones1998ego}. Global predictive accuracy of that model is not sufficient evidence that the surrogate is useful as an evolution-control mechanism: a good hold-out regressor need not be a useful acquisition policy. Table~\ref{tab:jin-map} places the two surrogate roles we also run in that taxonomy once: pipeline topology of \emph{this} stack, not the paper's gap. SAIL / DSA-ME illuminate a surrogate acquisition surface and spend real evaluations on a subset~\cite{gaier2018sail,gaier2017sail,grillotti2022dsame,kent2025boqd,tjanaka2026dms}. We keep a fixed MAP-Elites propose$\rightarrow$evaluate loop with ground-truth archive updates. An after-generation threshold gate is a budget-axis illustration, not a second primary claim.

That mapping is not the gap: prior work did not need a before- vs.\ after-generation factorial.

LLMs appear as variation operators and QD emitters~\cite{wu2025ecllm,lehman2023openelm,nasir2024llmatic,lim2024llmqd,vanstein2025behaviour,romera2024funsearch,ma2024eureka}, including concurrent LLM$+$MAP-Elites applications~\cite{ropke2026dejaq,sviridov2026medical}. We do not claim that prior LLM-QD studies generally suffer from a target-selection (or warm-start, or budget) confound, and we do not audit other authors' configs. A complete-pipeline comparison---a new emitter, prompt, or hybrid stack against a baseline---\emph{can} co-vary those factors with the claimed channel; that is a possibility under ordinary EC experimental hygiene~\cite{eiben2011parameter,bartzbeielstein2006experimental}, not a field-wide finding. We demonstrate that the attribution error can be large in one controlled stack: on our own bundled contrast a $+15.09$~pp ``hints'' reading inverts to a $+0.26$~pp residual once target selection is matched, and a calendar-blocked $2\times2$ on independent floors replicates that inversion (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}; Tables~\ref{tab:rq1-design},~\ref{tab:rq1-cf}). An empty-archive maze $2\times2$ repeats the inversion on a second evaluator without portable pp (Table~\ref{tab:rq1-maze}). LAEA-style LLM fitness models~\cite{hao2024laea,xie2025llmsae} are adjacent; we mutate \texttt{WorldSpec} genomes with an LLM emitter and score them with a genome-aligned MLP. Archive evaluations are genotype-canonical seeded (one pass per insertion), not UQD lucky-seed artifacts~\cite{flageat2023uq,flageat2025extractqd}. Line-by-line mechanism map vs.\ SAIL / LAEA / LLMatic: appendix Table~\ref{tab:positioning}.

\subsection{Roles, policies, and contrast labels}
\label{sec:terminology}

A MAP-Elites iteration is select $\rightarrow$ \textbf{generate} $\rightarrow$ (optionally) gate $\rightarrow$ \textbf{evaluate} $\rightarrow$ archive update. As in Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Claim-facing H1 intervention.} Redundant parent-level surrogate scalars: the MLP writes $\hat f$ and $\hat u$ into a prompt that already contains the parent's true fitness, on ${\approx}10/50$ LLM slots. Every proposal is still simulated. This is not a test of a general before-generation channel (no child-level prediction, no rewrite-from-prediction).
  \item \textbf{H2 illustration (architectural after-generation).} The MLP scores an already-generated candidate and may skip simulation (absolute-$\tau$ threshold on the genetic stack). Budget-axis check, not a co-equal RQ.
\end{itemize}

Jin's before-/after-generation labels stay in Table~\ref{tab:jin-map} as pipeline topology only. Do not read them as the paper's hypothesis names, and do not treat ``soft intervention'' or ``attachment point'' as synonyms of the H1 result. \textbf{Evidence roles.} Main-text claims use three roles (Table~\ref{tab:h-status}): \emph{Primary} $=$ the RQ1 attribution package (bundled gap as motivation, matched-policy collapse, residual after matching); \emph{Robustness} $=$ bounds that qualify that claim (H2 budget axis including Sphere, maze H5, maze empty policy$\times$hint $2\times2$, mixed/empty $2\times2$, CMA as a pipeline reference); \emph{Supplementary} $=$ pilots, Path~A, placebo detail, dungeon, CVT, H3 production diagnosis, and protocol dual-report. Role is not a Holm status. Locked families and Lock/Extension/Reporting chronology: appendix~\S\ref{sec:amendments-appendix}. Later prose states the finding rather than repeating a status enum. Prompt placeholders and thresholds are mechanisms inside H1/H2 as instantiated. Surrogate-informed parent selection is out of scope; archive \texttt{target\_selection} remains ordinary MAP-Elites policy.

\subsection{MAP-Elites illuminator loop}

Each run executes \texttt{iterations} $\times$ \texttt{batch\_size} \emph{proposal slots} in fixed order. Production schedulers use $650 \times 50 = 32{,}500$ proposal slots per seed. We keep three budgets distinct throughout:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Proposal slots} --- attempted candidates ($32{,}500$ matched across primary-grid arms).
  \item \textbf{Real simulator evaluations} --- slots that call \texttt{run\_world}. Non-filter arms execute all $32{,}500$; filter arms execute fewer (${\sim}21.5$k at $33.5\%$ skip on the genetic H2 stack).
  \item \textbf{Wall / LLM call counts} --- wall-clock time and emit attempts (Table~\ref{tab:cost}); not interchangeable with either count axis above, and not converted to API dollars.
\end{itemize}

Wall repayment is Eq.~\eqref{eq:h2-breakeven}---it prices only the third axis, it is not a substitute for naming which budget is claimed. That distinction is the budget-axis check in Table~\ref{tab:audit-checklist}, not a second research question.

For slot~$k$ the loop:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item selects a target niche (behaviour-cell centre) according to \texttt{target\_selection};
  \item calls the slot emitter (\texttt{random}, \texttt{genetic}, or \texttt{llm}) to propose a \texttt{WorldSpec};
  \item optionally applies surrogate acquisition (\S\ref{sec:acquisition}), which may \emph{skip} the simulator call;
  \item if not skipped, runs \texttt{evaluate\_candidate} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{run\_world} (always for non-filter / MVP hint arms);
  \item inserts into the archive if fitness strictly improves the cell incumbent;
  \item appends training data to the surrogate buffer when \texttt{surrogate.enabled} and a real evaluation occurred.
\end{enumerate}
LLM slots log \texttt{llm\_fallback} when parsing fails and a genetic repair path is used. Parallel evaluation uses a process pool sized to the batch. Outputs: \texttt{map\_elites\_archive.jsonl}, optional \texttt{archive\_trace.jsonl}, and \texttt{nightly\_run\_summary.json}.

\textbf{Target selection.} Both policies select among \emph{frontier} cells only---occupied niches with at least one empty neighbour. \texttt{min\_fitness\_frontier} picks the frontier cell with the \emph{lowest} elite fitness (bundled \texttt{stub}; not the highest-fitness frontier cell). \texttt{uniform\_frontier} samples uniformly on that frontier set (\texttt{hints}, filter arms, matched H1/H2). Because insertion fitness puts weight $w_D{=}0.45$ on diversity $D$, which is also a BC axis (Eq.~\eqref{eq:fitness}), lowest-$f$ frontier parents are systematically low-$D$ / extinction-adjacent (\S\ref{sec:fd-coupling}). Random, genetic, and LLM MAP-Elites slots consume the same \texttt{select\_target\_cell} once per proposal ($50/50$ in a batch); parent-level H1 scalars fill only the ${\approx}10$ LLM slots. pyribs CMA-ME/MAE do not use this archive policy (ask/tell emitters; Table~\ref{tab:conditions}). These are archive policies, not surrogate roles: they do not use the surrogate to select parents. Scientific H1 holds this policy fixed (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}); the H2 illustration does likewise (\S\ref{sec:h2}). The historical stub$\rightarrow$hints gap does not. Surrogate-assisted parent selection is out of scope. Exact policies: appendix Algorithm~\ref{alg:target-selection}. Floor snapshot (one of $303$ frontier cells): appendix Figure~\ref{fig:floor-frontier}.

\textbf{Terms.} \emph{Target selection} is the archive policy \texttt{target\_selection} that chooses a frontier niche for the next proposal. The \emph{parent} is the incumbent elite in that niche (or a random elite if empty); there is no separate parent-selection operator. \emph{Archive policy} in the RQ1 wording means this target-selection choice, not a third mechanism.

\subsection{Surrogate model}
\label{sec:surrogate-method}

The surrogate maps a canonical \texttt{WorldSpec} to predicted MAP-Elites outcomes without a simulator call (Figure~\ref{fig:surrogate}). Emitters edit a 21-parameter genome; schema~2.1 lifts that to a 24-D input (appendix~\S\ref{sec:substrate-appendix}). Production checkpoint \texttt{nightly\_v3\_mc\_d005.pkl} is a four-member MLP ensemble ($N=35{,}114$). The runtime checkpoint passed a hold-out validity screen (NMAE$=0.112$); we additionally validate the gate on online proposal streams because global hold-out accuracy does not establish arm-conditional ranking quality (algebra: appendix~\S\ref{sec:checkpoint-appendix}; online ranking: appendix~\S\ref{sec:h2-impl-threat}). Runtime fitness uses Eq.~\eqref{eq:fitness}. One shared checkpoint pins the MLP so model shopping is not confounded with the audited treatments; GP/UCB lineage check: appendix~\S\ref{sec:surrogate-ablation-appendix}.

\textbf{Parent-level surrogate scalars (H1).}
On ${\approx}10/50$ LLM slots, H1 calls \texttt{predict(parent)}---not a child---and writes two scalars onto a prompt that already contains the parent's true fitness (appendix~\S\ref{sec:h1-prompt-appendix}):
\begin{quote}
\small\texttt{Surrogate predicts fitness} $\approx$ \texttt{\{surrogate\_mean\}, uncertainty = \{surrogate\_uncertainty\}}
\end{quote}
\texttt{hints} fills live MLP $\hat f$/$\hat u$; \texttt{stub\_uniform} uses YAML $0.5$/$1.0$ (\texttt{surrogate.enabled=false}). The live scalars restate known parent information, not a child-improvement signal (\S\ref{sec:h1-impl-threat}). Calendar-blocked shuffle: \S\ref{sec:rq1-cf}. Historical placebo / Path~A: appendix~\S\ref{sec:h1-pilots-appendix}.

\subsection{LLM emitter}

The \texttt{LlmEmitter} sends the parent \texttt{WorldSpec} JSON and niche centre to a chat API; the model returns a JSON patch applied via \texttt{world\_spec\_from\_llm}. Primary LLM runs use \texttt{qwen-turbo}; multi-provider replications swap endpoints while keeping prompts and schedulers fixed (\S\ref{sec:results}). Endpoint pins and decoding hyperparameters sit in appendix Table~\ref{tab:pins-full} / repository YAMLs---reproducibility detail, not a scientific factor.

Accepted LLM elites typically differ from parents by compact named-field rule/scalar edits rather than bit noise (edit-anatomy case study, appendix~\S\ref{sec:edit-anatomy}).

\subsection{After-generation surrogate (H2 illustration)}
\label{sec:acquisition}

Optional \texttt{acquisition.mode=filter} evaluates \texttt{surrogate.predict} \emph{before} \texttt{evaluate\_candidate}. A \texttt{threshold\_gate} skips simulation when predicted fitness falls below \texttt{min\_predicted\_fitness=0.45} (primary grid; inherited from the v2 combat/shadow skip-band $25$--$45\%$, not tuned on the matched genetic H2 readout) subject to uncertainty calibration. Skipped slots are logged to \texttt{surrogate\_archive.jsonl} but do not update the MAP-Elites archive. \texttt{shadow} mode logs counterfactual skips while still evaluating. Production after-generation uses that same MLP checkpoint with an absolute fitness threshold---not SAIL-style illumination of an acquisition surface, and not a live GP+UCB model-management baseline (\S\ref{sec:surrogate-method}; appendix~\S\ref{sec:surrogate-ablation-appendix}; \S\ref{sec:limitations}). Offline GP+UCB threshold replay is not a substitute for that missing arm. Primary gating evidence is the matched genetic filter contrast (\S\ref{sec:h2}), reported as a budget-axis illustration; LLM$+$filter stacking is supplementary (H3; Table~\ref{tab:h-status}; \S\ref{sec:h3}).

\subsection{Measurement substrate}
\label{sec:substrate}

Primary contrasts use a controlled black-box evaluator---not a domain contribution. Each candidate is a structured \texttt{WorldSpec}: birth/survival neighbour-count rule tables (18 bits) plus three continuous scalars (\texttt{noise}, \texttt{resource\_regen}, \texttt{predation}). The simulator runs a seeded $50\times50$ toroidal cellular-automaton world for $T_{\mathrm{sim}}{=}200$ steps (early extinction $\Rightarrow$ fitness~0). Behaviour characteristics are stability and diversity in $[0,1]$, so the primary archive is a $50\times50$ niche grid over that plane; insertion fitness is a fixed weighted recompose of diversity, survival, oscillation, and topology proxies (Eq.~\eqref{eq:fitness} in appendix~\S\ref{sec:substrate-appendix}; weights protocol-locked, not tuned). Diversity $D$ is both a BC axis and the largest fitness weight ($w_D{=}0.45$), so $f$ and $D$ are coupled by construction (\S\ref{sec:fd-coupling}). Genetic emitters flip bits / nudge scalars; LLM emitters edit the same named fields as JSON. The evaluator is cheap relative to CFD-style SAIL domains, so H2 claims evaluation-indexed efficiency and reports wall time separately (\S\ref{sec:cost}). Structured named fields make parent-level prompt scalars and after-generation gating testable---the reason for a synthetic CA primary testbed rather than a pure $\mathbb{R}^d$ literature toy as the sole domain. Table~\ref{tab:domain} lists the knobs; example elites and fitness algebra remain in the appendix. Coverage is occupied-cell percentage of the $2500$-cell tessellation after warm-start merge (not of a proven achievable set; \S\ref{sec:coverage-support}); mean elite fitness averages elites over occupied bins (logged column \texttt{mean\_best\_fitness} is a historical name, not archive-max; the denominator moves as new niches fill); QD-score (Eq.~\eqref{eq:qd}) is the descriptive coverage$\times$quality companion. CVT geometry is appendix sensitivity~\cite{vassiliades2018cvt}. Complementary literature Sphere H2 transfer (threshold gate only; no LLM) is appendix~\S\ref{sec:sphere-h2-appendix}.

\subsection{Design principles}

Primary-grid contrasts share seeds $0$--$9$ within a tier (maze/dungeon: same indices, independent domain RNG). Pairing controls run-level stochasticity, not external LLM sampling ($T{=}0.2$, no provider seed, non-interleaved frozen arms; \S\ref{sec:llm-id}). Endpoints use the frozen $50\times50$ grid; locked Holm / matched-H1 / H2 / H4 arms warm-start from the same JSONL ($2{,}614$ records $\rightarrow$ $971$ niches / $38.84\%$; \S\ref{sec:pyribs}) before $32{,}500$ proposal slots. Those $n{=}10$ seeds are continuation RNG on that file, not independent warm-start archives. The calendar-blocked RQ1 $2\times2$ uses five independently generated floors truncated at the same occupancy (\S\ref{sec:rq1-cf}). A maze empty-archive policy$\times$hint $2\times2$ at $5$k is a second-evaluator bound, not a Holm family (\S\ref{sec:rq1-maze-design}). CVT is appendix sensitivity only. Holm is within-family only (no paper-wide vector); a set passes only if every test rejects. Matched H1 sits outside those sets. The calendar-blocked factorial is not a Holm family. A Holm non-reject is fail-to-reject, not an in-band claim; at $n{=}10$ exact one-sided Wilcoxon $p$ has floor $2^{-10}{=}9.77\times10^{-4}$ (Table~\ref{tab:test-map} note). The locked \texttt{stub} vs.\ \texttt{hints} contrast also differs in \texttt{target\_selection}; scientific H1 holds that policy fixed (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}).

\subsection{Endpoints and statistical reporting}
\label{sec:stats}

\textbf{Primary endpoints:} terminal coverage (\%) $=$ occupied niches over the $2500$-cell $50\times50$ tessellation of $[0,1]^2$ (not a proven achievable set; \S\ref{sec:coverage-support}), and mean elite fitness on occupied bins (logged column \texttt{mean\_best\_fitness}---a historical name, not archive-max). Occupied-bin mean changes the denominator as new, often low-fitness, niches fill; QD-score (Eq.~\eqref{eq:qd}) is the coverage$\times$quality companion $\sum_c f_c$. On the primary CA grid that companion is not a Holm endpoint; maze H5 includes QD-score AUC at the matched-eval horizon. Quality on the \emph{intersection} of occupied cells across arms is not reported. Endpoint$\rightarrow$test map: appendix Table~\ref{tab:test-map} (Role follows Table~\ref{tab:h-status}). Mixing is by estimand, not by shopping for significance.

\textbf{Notation.} Unless a caption names a confidence interval, $\pm$ is the sample SD of the $n{=}10$ seed-level values (or of the paired per-seed $\Delta$ when the cell is a contrast). Claim-facing leftover intervals are Student-$t$ 95\% CIs on the paired mean; Table~\ref{tab:rq1-cf} uses archive-clustered percentile bootstrap $95\%$ CIs (outer unit $=$ floor archive, $k{=}5$). Figure~\ref{fig:ladder} is unpaired Student-$t$ 95\% CI on each arm mean, not paired $\Delta$. Exploratory TOST 90\% CIs remain appendix-only.

\textbf{Default test for locked Holm families.} Paired seeds ($n{=}10$ unless noted), one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, Holm within each pre-specified contrast set. Kerby $\mathrm{PPS}$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:pps}) is a selective companion---omitted when unanimous (coincides with Sign). Matched-policy H1 reports the paired mean $\Delta$cov and its 95\% CI. Post-hoc $\pm 2$~pp / TOST: appendix~\S\ref{sec:stats-appendix}. Surrogate hold-out NMAE/MAE/$R^2$ are validity checks on global predictive accuracy, not endpoints and not evidence of evolution-control utility~\cite{jin2005fitness} (appendix~\S\ref{sec:h2-impl-threat}).

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

The load-bearing result is RQ1 attribution on a shared warm-start floor of $971$ niches / $38.84\%$ coverage. This section is one Results block, not three programmes. Read it in three groups (roles: Table~\ref{tab:h-status}):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Attribution (primary).} Bundled $+15$~pp ``hints'' gap collapses when \texttt{target\_selection} is matched (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}; Tables~\ref{tab:estimands}--\ref{tab:rq1-cf}). A maze empty $2\times2$ is a second-evaluator bound on that inversion, not a second primary RQ (Table~\ref{tab:rq1-maze}).

\end{enumerate}
Tests: appendix Table~\ref{tab:test-map}. Primary endpoints: tessellation coverage and occupied-bin mean elite fitness. Shared-checkpoint hold-out is a validity screen, not a utility claim (\S\ref{sec:surrogate-method}; appendix~\S\ref{sec:checkpoint-appendix}).

\subsection{RQ1 --- Matching policy collapses the bundled gap}
\label{sec:decomposition}
\label{sec:policy-x-hint}

The bundled contrast produced a $+15.09$~pp terminal-coverage difference favouring live hints over historical \texttt{stub} ($10/10$; Holm $m{=}2$). After matching \texttt{target\_selection}, that difference collapses: on primary \texttt{qwen-turbo}, hints $-$ \texttt{stub\_uniform} is $+0.26$~pp (paired 95\% CI $[-0.59,+1.11]$). Completing the historical policy$\times$hint grid (Table~\ref{tab:policy-x-hint}) shows the same pattern under both archive policies---soft leftovers near null ($-0.02$ / $+0.26$~pp) while matching policy removes ${\approx}{+}15$~pp on either soft level. The four cells come from three tiers, so this is an observed matched-control collapse, not a causal mediation of the Holm gap. Write $Y(\mathrm{soft},\mathrm{policy})$ for the mean of a cell in Table~\ref{tab:policy-x-hint}: bundled $\Delta_{\mathrm{bundle}}$ is the \emph{off-diagonal} $Y(\texttt{hints},\texttt{uniform})-Y(\texttt{stub},\texttt{minfit})$, not a same-policy soft contrast. Table~\ref{tab:estimands} lists the observed contrasts, including a descriptive difference-in-differences $\Delta_{\mathrm{int}}$ that asks whether the scalar treatment depends on target selection; that term is not interpreted as mediation of the historical bundled contrast. On cell means $\Delta_{\mathrm{bundle}}=\Delta_{\mathrm{policy}\mid\mathrm{stub}}+\Delta_{\mathrm{soft}\mid\mathrm{uniform}}$ is arithmetic, not a mediation identification. Policy is also a larger \emph{dose}: \texttt{target\_selection} runs on every proposal slot ($50/50$), whereas parent-level scalars fill only ${\approx}10/50$ LLM slots. Do not read the near-null soft leftover as a per-slot potency ranking. An API-free genetic policy swap still moves $+13.1$~pp (\S\ref{sec:genetic-policy}). That $+13.1$~pp does not identify the historical Holm $+15.09$~pp, and we do not claim that target selection caused that Holm improvement; matching policy collapses the observed gap, with policy-cell contrasts ${\approx}15$~pp, so target selection accounts for the overwhelming majority of the observed bundled gap. The coverage \emph{magnitude} of that policy movement is this fitness--descriptor geometry ($w_D{=}0.45$); the attribution (policy vs.\ scalars) holds \emph{within} it. The matched H1 leftover compares two arms under the same \texttt{uniform\_frontier} policy, so it is less exposed to that coupling than the bundled $+15$~pp gap (\S\ref{sec:fd-coupling}). The same inversion on independently built floors, with a dated model ID and call logs, is \S\ref{sec:rq1-cf}.

\subsection{Independent-floor calendar-blocked $2\times2$}
\label{sec:rq1-cf}

Table~\ref{tab:rq1-cf} is the contemporaneous identification of that inversion---five independently built $971$-niche floors, two continuation blocks each, dated \texttt{gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18}, call logs complete on $50/50$ arms---not a comparison of two historical pipeline tiers. Primary endpoint: new coverage above the floor. Policy $\Delta$ is $+14.53$~pp under stub and $+14.82$~pp under live (10/10 blocks; archive-clustered 95\% CI $[+11.8,+18.0]$ / $[+12.2,+18.1]$). Live vs.\ stub collapsed terminal archives match at minfit in all 10 blocks: same occupied bins, elite fitness, and stored genotypes---equal coverage is the consequence, not the definition (LLM proposals may simply not have changed that archive under a ${\approx}1.6$-target visit process). At uniform the leftover is $+0.29$~pp (archive-clustered 95\% CI $[-0.20,+0.75]$; 5/10). The distribution-matched placebo leftover (\texttt{live\_uniform}$-$\texttt{shuffled\_uniform}) is $+0.28$~pp (archive-clustered 95\% CI $[+0.02,+0.54]$; 6/10): at most a small incremental alignment effect, far below the target-selection effect---not a second ${\sim}15$~pp channel. The outer unit is five floor archives ($k{=}5$); that shuffle interval is a clustered bootstrap, not a confirmatory alignment test. Absolute terminal coverage sits ${\sim}2$~pp below the one-JSONL historical cells because the floors differ; the \emph{contrasts} match. Mechanism logs (LLM / genetic / random share \texttt{select\_target\_cell}): minfit uses ${\approx}1.6$ unique targets per run (normalized entropy $0.16$; consecutive repeat $1.00$); uniform uses ${\approx}670$ (entropy $0.97$). Stub vs.\ live at a fixed policy do not change that distribution. Shuffle mismatches parent $(f,u)$ on $89\%$ of LLM slots (L1 $0.21$) while leaving target entropy unchanged---the placebo is a real alignment break, not a second live arm. This factorial is not a Holm family and does not identify the historical Holm $\Delta$.

\subsection{Maze empty-archive bound}
\label{sec:rq1-maze}
A second-evaluator bound on the same inversion, not a new primary RQ. Maze H5 \texttt{llm\_stub}/\texttt{llm\_hints} cannot show a policy collapse: they share \texttt{uniform\_frontier} at $32.5$k, where genetic already saturates the 900-cell grid (${\approx}99.6\%$). Genetic Phase~A first established headroom at $5$k (minfit $16.79\pm3.29\%$ vs.\ frozen uniform $56.98\pm4.47\%$; $\Delta{=}{-}40.19\pm4.13$~pp, $10/10$). The LLM $2\times2$ authorized by that GO is empty-archive, $5$k slots, dated \texttt{gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18}, call logs on $40/40$ (Table~\ref{tab:rq1-maze}). Policy $\Delta$ is $+39.10\pm3.38$~pp under stub and $+39.31\pm3.97$~pp under live (both $10/10$). Leftover is $+0.02\pm0.14$~pp at minfit ($5/2/3$) and $+0.23\pm3.42$~pp at uniform ($5/4/1$). The off-diagonal bundle equals $\Delta_{\mathrm{policy}\mid\mathrm{stub}}+\Delta_{\mathrm{soft}\mid\mathrm{uniform}}$ seed-wise. Archives are not bit-identical at minfit ($0/10$), unlike the CA calendar-blocked factorial; coverage still matches to $0.02$~pp. The leftover SD at uniform ($3.42$~pp) is an order of magnitude larger than the CA leftover: read $+0.23$ as no second ${\sim}39$~pp channel, not as a tight scalar-null identification. Magnitudes are maze empty-start @ $5$k, not the CA continuation ${\approx}15$~pp. LLM terminal levels sit below genetic at both policies at this cut (uniform $44.2$ vs.\ $57.0$; minfit $5.1$ vs.\ $16.8$)---not an LLM-vs-genetic ranking. Not Holm, not H5, and not an identification of CA Holm $+15.09$.

Table~\ref{tab:rq1-design} maps those designs onto one reading: the confounded bundled contrast and the matched-policy re-run reverse the conclusion. This is a scoped attribution result---not ``hints do not matter,'' not a prospective equivalence claim, not provider-universal transfer, and not a mediation theorem.

\textbf{Identification under a floating alias (ranked bounds).}
\label{sec:llm-id}
The frozen $+0.26$~pp leftover pairs \texttt{q1-full/hints} (window-pinned \texttt{qwen-turbo}, 2026-07-02\ldots07-11) against a later \texttt{stub\_uniform} tier: not time-interleaved, not weight-pinned, no provider seed, no call log. That pairing does \emph{not} identify a bit-reproducible soft-channel null. The attribution claim---matching policy collapses the bundled $+15$~pp gap---does not rest on that pairing alone. Ranked bounds: (i)~an API-free genetic policy swap moves $+13.1$~pp (\S\ref{sec:genetic-policy}), so coverage of that order does not require DashScope weights; (ii)~re-running \emph{both} soft cells in one mixed-stack tier leaves a same-calendar leftover $+0.31\pm0.29$~pp (Table~\ref{tab:mixed-2x2}); (iii)~matched leftover $+0.12$~pp on \texttt{gpt-4o-mini} (response snapshot \texttt{gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18}; Table~\ref{tab:h1-matched-providers}); (iv)~the calendar-blocked $2\times2$ on that dated ID, with call logs, on five independent floors (Table~\ref{tab:rq1-cf}); (v)~a maze empty-archive $2\times2$ on the same dated ID with call logs (Table~\ref{tab:rq1-maze})---same qualitative inversion, maze pp, not CA occupancy. \section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Synthesis}

Canonical magnitudes and outcome board: Contributions and Table~\ref{tab:h-status}.

The load-bearing finding is attributional, not algorithmic (RQ1). The bundled Holm test is of a pipeline-level difference ($+15.09$~pp)---not a valid test of the causal contribution of the prompt scalars. Matching \texttt{target\_selection} collapses that observed gap; the historical policy$\times$hint $2\times2$ shows the same pattern under both policies, with soft leftovers near null (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}). A calendar-blocked $2\times2$ on five independent floors of the same occupancy, dated \texttt{gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18}, and call logs replicates that inversion on new coverage (policy $+14.53$/$+14.82$~pp; leftover $+0.29$~pp at uniform; matching collapsed archives at minfit; Table~\ref{tab:rq1-cf}). That factorial is not a Holm family and does not identify the historical Holm $\Delta$. An empty-archive maze $2\times2$ on a second evaluator repeats the inversion at $5$k (policy $+39.10$/$+39.31$~pp, $10/10$; leftover $+0.02$/$+0.23$~pp; Table~\ref{tab:rq1-maze})---same qualitative pattern, not portable pp, not occupancy-matched to the CA floor, and not a reread of Holm H5. Attributing the Holm gap to parent-level hint scalars is the claim that fails. An API-free genetic policy swap still moves $+13.1$~pp (\S\ref{sec:genetic-policy}). That bound does not identify the Holm $+15.09$; we do not claim that target selection caused the historical contrast. The $+15$~pp / $+13.1$~pp \emph{magnitudes} are this fitness--descriptor geometry; the attribution (policy vs.\ scalars) is the within-geometry finding (\S\ref{sec:fd-coupling}). The reusable lesson is to match target selection before crediting prompt scalars or an LLM emitter (Table~\ref{tab:rq1-design}). Comparison-hygiene checks used here sit in Methods (Table~\ref{tab:audit-checklist}); they are not a portable methodology contribution.

That correction is the paper: the surprise is the bundled attribution error, not the near-null leftover. We do not claim that prompt scalars do not help LLM emitters. A matched genetic threshold gate is a budget-axis illustration, not a terminal or wall win on this cheap evaluator (Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition}; \S\ref{sec:h2}). CMA / maze H5 Holm are appendix bounds. Do not read H1 and genetic H2 as interchangeable attach-role cells (\S\ref{sec:cross-stack}). Scope: \S\ref{sec:limitations}.

\subsection{Scope of the H1 soft-residual estimate}
\label{sec:h1-impl-threat}

\textbf{Admissible conclusion.} On primary \texttt{qwen-turbo} under matched \texttt{uniform\_frontier}, the leftover is $+0.26$~pp (paired 95\% CI $[-0.59,+1.11]$; $n{=}10$; \S\ref{sec:decomposition}). The calendar-blocked leftover at uniform is $+0.29$~pp (archive-clustered 95\% CI $[-0.20,+0.75]$); at minfit live and stub wrote matching collapsed terminal archives in all 10 blocks (same occupied bins, elite fitness, stored genotypes; Table~\ref{tab:rq1-cf}). Those intervals are an order of magnitude below the policy $\Delta$ (${\approx}15$~pp); they are exploratory effect estimates, not a prospective equivalence test. On DeepSeek the paired 95\% CI $[-1.49,+3.53]$ is not near zero---not provider-universal (Table~\ref{tab:h1-matched-providers}). DeepSeek seed~8 influence (Cook $D_8{\approx}0.85$): appendix~\S\ref{sec:stats-appendix}. Do not pool providers into a common null. Scope: mean terminal coverage only---not ``hints do not matter,'' not fitness/QD/anytime/cost claims, and not a model-independent before-generation null.

\textbf{What ``matched policy'' isolates (and what it does not).}
Matched H1 holds fixed: emitter mix ($20$ random / $20$ genetic / $10$ LLM), \texttt{uniform\_frontier} target selection, system$+$user prompt \emph{templates}, JSON parse / genetic repair / coercion paths, warm-start archive, and the $32{,}500$-proposal-slot budget with no after-generation skips (hence $32{,}500$ sims). Logged transport is identical on primary \texttt{qwen-turbo}: $6{,}500$ LLM emit attempts/seed and $0$ fallbacks on both arms (Table~\ref{tab:h1-qd-cost}; appendix~\S\ref{sec:h1-prompt-appendix}). The intended treatment is only the filled surrogate line (live parent MLP scalars vs.\ YAML constants $0.5$/$1.0$). Frozen H1 is still not a full semantic isolation of the hint channel: those stub constants are not a distribution-matched placebo; the historical arms were separate batch tiers; there is no manipulation check that emitted candidates follow the surrogate line. The calendar-blocked factorial adds a contemporaneous distribution-matched shuffle on dated \texttt{gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18} (mismatch on $89\%$ of LLM slots; leftover $+0.28$~pp, at most a small incremental alignment effect; Table~\ref{tab:rq1-cf}). The experiment estimates the incremental effect of providing parent-level scalar information through this prompt interface, not the effect of a surrogate-derived scalar when the downstream LLM demonstrably conditions on it. The $+15$~pp collapse is not a causal mediation of the Holm gap. Historical wrong-sign placebo, interleaved seed~0, accepted-elite quality proxies, and Path~A rewrite: appendix~\S\ref{sec:h1-pilots-appendix}.

\textbf{Why a near-null is still expected under the locked H1 interface.} The locked user prompt already includes the parent elite's \emph{true} simulated fitness inside \texttt{current\_elite\_json}, plus neighbour elites with true fitness (appendix~\S\ref{sec:h1-prompt-appendix}). Live H1 only appends a surrogate estimate of fitness/uncertainty on that same parent genotype---a noisy re-statement of information the archive already holds---not a predicted child improvement or a rewrite instruction. Advice touches only ${\approx}10/50$ batch slots. Matched hints vs.\ stub remaining near-null is therefore unsurprising.

\paragraph{Contribution framing.}
H1 is a within-stack case study of one redundant \emph{parent-level scalar} interface under one checkpoint---not a general before-generation impossibility result.

\subsection{Implications for LLM emitters}
\label{sec:when-llm}

The LLM is one emitter in the H1 stack, not the product under test (\S\ref{sec:cross-stack}). On coverage/wall here it is mostly not the metrics winner (Figure~\ref{fig:ladder}).

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Attribution first.} Before crediting an emitter from a reported gap, re-run the control under matched target selection, warm-start, and budget. On this testbed matching policy removes the observed $+15$~pp bundled gap (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}; Table~\ref{tab:rq1-cf})---descriptive collapse, not mediation.
  \item \emph{Choose the LLM emitter for the artifact, not the metric.} Editable genotypes can justify the cost; coverage here does not. Hints ($60.4\%$) sit slightly above genetic ME ($59.0\%$ at ${\sim}6$~min) and below discrete CMA-ME / pbCMA / \texttt{rint} (appendix Table~\ref{tab:discrete-cma}). Hard gating is a cost-accounting check on the warm-start floor (\S\ref{sec:h2}; appendix~\S\ref{sec:h2-breakeven}), not a co-equal result, and does not cut API calls on the mixed stack.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\subsubsection{Warm-start is a major boundary, not a footnote}
\label{sec:warm-start-limit}
\label{sec:bootstrap-hyp}

Locked Holm / H1 / H2 seeds continue one nightly baseline JSONL: $2{,}614$ records collapse to $971$ niches / $38.84\%$ coverage. That $n{=}10$ is continuation RNG on one floor file, not archive-to-archive variance. The calendar-blocked $2\times2$ closes the independent-floor bound at the same occupancy (Table~\ref{tab:rq1-cf}). The experiment studies how methods \emph{continue} an already filled archive. An empty-archive mixed $2\times2$ (appendix Table~\ref{tab:cold-2x2}) does not re-run the policy$\times$hint grid from empty: parent-scalar leftover stays small and noisy ($+1.48\pm3.57$~pp); the hard gate sign-flips @20k (warm $+$ vs.\ empty $-$). Genetic H2 $+3.65$~pp @20k remains a result on the $38.84\%$ floor and is untested from empty on the genetic stack. Maze empty $2\times2$ at $5$k (Table~\ref{tab:rq1-maze}) is a second-evaluator empty-start bound, not occupancy-matched to the CA floor.

\subsubsection{Coverage is of the tessellation, not a proven achievable set}
\label{sec:coverage-support}

Primary-grid coverage divides occupied niches by the $2500$ cells of the $50\times50$ $[0,1]^2$ tessellation. Gray heatmap cells are empty, not certified infeasible. We do not have an infeasible-cell oracle---the 21-D genotype is not enumerable. CMA-ME occupancy ($73.37\pm3.65\%$ mean; $80.5\%$ on the illustrative seed-4 panel) is an empirical lower bound on reachability: large low-diversity holes that remain gray under hints are filled by a different emitter, so they are not uniformly unreachable. Residual empty cells after CMA-ME may mix unexplored and possibly unreachable regions. Maze genetic arms hit a known 900-cell ceiling (appendix Table~\ref{tab:b5}); the primary CA grid has no analogous proven-achievable mask. We do not re-express coverage as a fraction of CMA-ME support.

\subsubsection{H1 and the gating illustration are not an isolated attach-point factorial}
\label{sec:cross-stack}

Comparing H1 to H2 as if only the attach role changed would be incorrect---and this paper does not make that comparison as its claim. The contrasts fix different factors (appendix Table~\ref{tab:cross-stack}). The within-stack mixed $2\times2$ isolates those factors on one LLM mix as a bound (\S\ref{sec:mixed-2x2}; appendix Table~\ref{tab:mixed-2x2}); primary gating evidence remains the non-LLM genetic illustration.

\subsubsection{Threats to validity}

Claim-facing H1 is the paired mean $\Delta$cov and 95\% CI (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}); matching policy collapses the observed $+15$~pp gap in these rows, not a mediation (\S\ref{sec:llm-id}). Policy magnitudes are this $f$--$D$ geometry (\S\ref{sec:fd-coupling}); historical placebo / Path~A / TOST stay appendix. Calendar-blocked shuffle leftover: Table~\ref{tab:rq1-cf}. Wall break-even~\eqref{eq:h2-breakeven} is a simulator-cost check, not API invoices (Table~\ref{tab:cost}). H2 ranking replay: appendix~\S\ref{sec:h2-controls-appendix}.

\label{sec:fd-coupling}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fitness} puts $w_D{=}0.45$ on diversity $D$, also a BC axis, so $f$ and $D$ are coupled by construction ($\rho(f,D){=}0.986$ on the warm-start floor; appendix Table~\ref{tab:fd-coupling}, Figure~\ref{fig:floor-frontier}). \texttt{min\_fitness\_frontier} therefore selects low-$D$ parents. Attribution (policy vs.\ scalars) holds \emph{within} that geometry. Visit entropy / consecutive repeat are measured on the calendar-blocked factorial (\S\ref{sec:rq1-cf}) and remain unmeasured on frozen H1.

LLM runs are integrity-checked, not bit-reproducible: $T{=}0.2$, no provider seed, non-interleaved frozen arms, no call log on primary H1 (pins: Table~\ref{tab:pins-full}; new runs can write \texttt{llm\_call\_log.jsonl}, which does not backfill frozen arms). The calendar-blocked factorial joins every LLM call to its proposal on dated \texttt{gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18}. HTTP completion order does not reorder archive updates (\texttt{executor.map} is slot-ordered). The genetic $+13.1$~pp bound does not require repeating the floating \texttt{qwen-turbo} alias. Holm is within-family only.

\subsubsection{Generalizability}
\label{sec:generalizability}

The load-bearing attribution result sits on one structured-genotype CA evaluator (\S\ref{sec:substrate}), as a warm-started continuation ($38.84\%$). Maze empty $2\times2$ is qualitative transfer without portable pp (Table~\ref{tab:rq1-maze}); maze H5 / Sphere / dungeon are appendix bounds. Policy magnitudes are this $f$--$D$ geometry (\S\ref{sec:fd-coupling}), not a transferable ranking. We do not claim a stage-universal attach-point theorem or a CFD-style SAIL ranking.

\subsubsection{No live SAIL / acquisition-management baseline}
\label{sec:no-sail}

H2 instantiates an absolute fitness-threshold gate on a shared MLP, not SAIL-style illumination of an acquisition surface~\cite{gaier2018sail}. Offline GP+UCB threshold replay (appendix~\S\ref{sec:surrogate-ablation-appendix}) is a related-lineage check on logged proposals, not a live SAIL arm, and does not beat the production gate. This paper therefore does not rank threshold gating against acquisition-based surrogate management; that comparison remains future work.

\subsubsection{Scope of this paper}

Per-family internal-protocol freezes (not whole-paper external prereg): appendix~\S\ref{sec:amendments-appendix}. Main spine: RQ1 attribution. H2 is a budget-axis illustration; H4 / maze H5 Holm are appendix bounds; maze empty $2\times2$ is the second-evaluator attribution bound (Table~\ref{tab:rq1-maze}). Open items: Future work.

\subsection{Future work}

Completed packages (calendar-blocked RQ1 $2\times2$ plus shuffle, maze empty policy$\times$hint $2\times2$, shuffled placebo pilot, Path~A, historical policy$\times$hint, mixed/empty $2\times2$, ranking and true-fitness-threshold replay, $\tau$ sweep) are cited where they appear; they are not open items.

Remaining upgrades---none is a condition of the policy-collapse claim: rewrite-stub if Path~A is revisited; all-evaluated LLM quality on new \texttt{proposal\_log} tiers (frozen arms are not backfilled); niche-relative incumbent gating and per-arm online surrogate maps; archive-phase LLM/genetic scheduling (\S\ref{sec:bootstrap-hyp}); (i)~a naturally expensive LLM$+$QD evaluator for wall above break-even~\cite{grillotti2022dsame}; (ii)~full-budget LLM dungeon/maze $\tau$; (iii)~optional expert preference on edit pairs (\S\ref{sec:edit-anatomy}); (iv)~a live fitness-weight swap (\S\ref{sec:fd-coupling}); (v)~a pinned open-weight follow-on as a further upgrade of leftover identification (\S\ref{sec:llm-id}); (vi)~a live SAIL-style acquisition MAP-Elites arm, distinct from offline GP$+$UCB replay (\S\ref{sec:no-sail}).

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We measured where a bundled LLM ``hints'' coverage gain belongs---parent-level prompt scalars or target selection---and found that the first control to check is the right one. Locked primary-grid arms continue a shared floor of $971$ niches / $38.84\%$ coverage from one JSONL: a mid/late continuation of an already substantially populated archive, not cold-start illumination from empty. The bundled contrast of the type routinely used to credit LLM emitters showed $+15.09$~pp coverage for surrogate hints. Matching target selection collapses that observed gap; a later historical policy$\times$hint $2\times2$ shows the same pattern---policy ${\approx}{+}15$~pp on either soft level; leftovers near null under both---and an API-free genetic policy swap moves $+13.1$~pp (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}, \S\ref{sec:genetic-policy}). A calendar-blocked $2\times2$ on five independently built floors of that occupancy, with dated \texttt{gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18} and call logs, replicates the inversion on new coverage (policy $+14.53$/$+14.82$~pp; leftover $+0.29$~pp at uniform; shuffle leftover $+0.28$~pp; Table~\ref{tab:rq1-cf}). That is a matched-control collapse, not a mediation of the Holm $\Delta$, and not a Holm family. An empty-archive maze $2\times2$ on a second evaluator repeats the inversion at $5$k (policy $+39.10$/$+39.31$~pp; leftover $+0.02$/$+0.23$~pp; Table~\ref{tab:rq1-maze})---not occupancy-matched to the CA floor, not a Holm family, and not a reread of H5. An empty-archive mixed $2\times2$ does not re-run this policy$\times$hint grid from empty. Those policy magnitudes are this fitness--descriptor geometry ($w_D{=}0.45$; \S\ref{sec:fd-coupling}); the attribution (policy vs.\ scalars) holds within it. What remains after matching policy is the small leftover in \S\ref{sec:decomposition}---not an all-providers null, and expected under an interface that already supplies the parent's true fitness on ${\approx}10/50$ slots (\S\ref{sec:h1-impl-threat}).

A matched genetic threshold gate is reported only as a budget-axis illustration (robustness; Table~\ref{tab:h-status}): on the $38.84\%$ warm-start floor it buys mid-budget coverage per simulation, but does not repay wall on this cheap evaluator (\S\ref{sec:h2}). Hard gating is stage-dependent on the mixed LLM stack (sign-flip from empty; Table~\ref{tab:cold-2x2}); conclusions from the warm-start gating illustration should not be generalized to cold-start illumination. That bound does not isolate stage on the genetic stack, and is stack-specific under a fixed proposal stream (constant-$t_{\mathrm{sim}}$ special case; $\mathbb{E}[T_{\mathrm{sim}}\mid\mathrm{skip}]$ unmeasured)---not a co-equal claim or a portable QD ranking. What we expect to reuse across stacks is the lesson to match target selection before crediting prompt scalars (Table~\ref{tab:rq1-design})---not domain-specific coverage deltas, and not Table~\ref{tab:audit-checklist} as a method. This is not a broad LLM-QD win, not a general before-generation null, and not a pure ranking isolation (appendix~\S\ref{sec:h2-impl-threat}); maze transfer covers only non-LLM filter AUC, with the empty $5$k $2\times2$ a separate attribution bound. \paragraph{Multiple-comparison policy.}
Primary decisions use Holm within each pre-specified confirmatory contrast set ($\alpha{=}0.05$). Sets are frozen per family relative to that family's analysis window (bundled stack / floor; maze H5 AUC; per-provider replications; gray-zone NI; appendix dungeon AUC); matched H1 effect estimate sits outside confirmatory Holm sets. H4 is dual-reported: locked F-RQ4 FAIL retained, with a descriptive representation-mismatched end-to-end reading (\S\ref{sec:h4}). Primary outcomes remain Table~\ref{tab:h-status}.

\paragraph{Paired proportion of favorable pairs ($\mathrm{PPS}$; selective).}
Classic Vargha--Delaney $A_{12}$ is defined for independent samples~\cite{vargha2000new}. On paired seeds Kerby's \emph{paired proportion of favorable pairs}~\cite{kerby2014simple} (common-language effect size for matched pairs~\cite{mcgraw1992cl}) is
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{PPS} \;=\; \frac{\#\{\Delta_i > 0\} + 0.5\,\#\{\Delta_i = 0\}}{n},
  \label{eq:pps}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_i$ is treatment $-$ control on seed $i$ (for ``less,'' count $\Delta_i < 0$). $\mathrm{PPS}{=}0.5$ is the expected value under a paired null of no systematic sign (ties as half), and coincides with the Sign rate when there are no ties. At $n{=}10$, unanimous contrasts therefore give $\mathrm{PPS}\in\{0,1\}$; main-text tables omit that redundant column and keep Sign. We report $\mathrm{PPS}$ only when it is non-extreme or when no Sign column is shown (H4 MAE coverage; H3-gray NI). Not a propensity score, not an $A_{12}$ cutoff import, and not a confirmatory gate (Holm / protocol $d_z$ decide the family outcome).

\paragraph{H1 matched residual (audit detail for RQ1).}

That diagnostic is \emph{not} a proxy for floating-alias backend drift on primary \texttt{qwen-turbo} (\S\ref{sec:llm-id}).

Separately, as a sensitivity analysis---not TOST power---we report the approximate minimum detectable effect for a two-sided paired $t$-test of H$_0{:}\,\mathrm{mean}\,\Delta{=}0$ at $n$ seeds, $\alpha{=}0.05$, 80\% power:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{MDE} \;=\; \bigl(t_{1-\alpha/2,\,n-1} + t_{0.80,\,n-1}\bigr)\,\frac{\mathrm{SD}_{\Delta}}{\sqrt{n}},
  \label{eq:mde}
\end{equation}
This is the power to detect a \emph{non-zero} paired mean difference, not power for a confirmatory TOST at $\pm 2$~pp. At a true effect on the reporting-band edge ($|\Delta|{=}2$~pp), TOST power is necessarily much lower than a difference-test power at that same $\Delta$; we do \emph{not} report post-hoc observed power at $1$/$2$~pp. For H1, $\mathrm{SD}_{\Delta}{=}1.19$~pp is taken from the same matched $n{=}10$ analysis (retrospective; no independent pilot variance was locked for this contrast), yielding $\mathrm{MDE}{\approx}1.2$~pp---a design-sensitivity note only, not a margin justification. Elsewhere we quote only MDE numbers under the same difference-test convention (e.g.\ dungeon hints$-$stub coverage AUC ${\approx}1.2$~pp; maze genetic\_filter coverage AUC ${\approx}2.3$~pp and maze hints$-$stub coverage AUC ${\approx}2.6$~pp before Holm $m{=}8$; Table~\ref{tab:b5-holm}). These do not re-open locked Holm families or convert non-rejects into confirmatory band claims.

\section{Shared-checkpoint validity (hold-out algebra)}
\label{sec:checkpoint-appendix}
\label{sec:checkpoint}

Main text leads with scale-normalized NMAE and treats $R^2$ as a secondary, variance-sensitive companion. This appendix records why $R^2$ and MAE can move in opposite directions when the compose gate changes, what the hold-out CI means, and what global hold-out does \emph{not} validate.

\textbf{Archive endpoints are never surrogate scores.} Every confirmatory elite is inserted only after \texttt{evaluate\_candidate} (deterministic simulator fitness/BC). Skipped H2 proposals never enter the archive. Terminal coverage and mean elite fitness therefore cannot be an artifact of surrogate error alone; the surrogate affects \emph{which} candidates are simulated (H2) or which scalars appear in the prompt (H1), not the reported archive metrics.

Offline training on the nightly buffer ($N=35{,}114$ rows; schema~2.1, 24-D features; hold-out $n{=}7{,}023$) yields hold-out metrics for checkpoint \texttt{nightly\_v3\_mc\_d005.pkl}. Protocol amendment 2026-07-28 aligns the offline compose gate with runtime YAML ($0.95$): MAE$_{\text{fitness}}=0.029$, NMAE$=\mathrm{MAE}/\mathrm{std}(y_{\mathrm{gate}})=0.112$, $R^2_{\text{fitness}}=0.94$, MAE$_{\text{stability}}=0.040$, passing \texttt{hints\_ok}. The split unit is an i.i.d.\ $20\%$ row shuffle (\texttt{holdout\_split}, \texttt{random\_state}$=42$), not a temporal or leave-runs-out split: $35{,}087$ unique canonical genotype hashes, $27$ extra exact-duplicate rows, $9$ hashes in both train and hold-out, and all $971$ collapsed warm-start elites in the buffer (covariate overlap; \S\ref{sec:warm-start-limit}).

\textbf{Bootstrap unit.} Reported 95\% CIs (NMAE $[0.107,0.117]$; MAE $[0.027,0.030]$; $R^2$ $[0.94,0.95]$) use $B{=}10{,}000$ nonparametric bootstrap \emph{resamples of hold-out rows} treated as exchangeable (artifact: \texttt{holdout\_compose\_gate\_alignment.json}). Rows from the nightly buffer are not independent maps/seeds; the CI is therefore a within-split precision band and may be optimistically narrow under clustering. We do \emph{not} claim a run-clustered standard error.

\textbf{NMAE denominator (cross-gate).} Within a single compose definition, NMAE$=\mathrm{MAE}/\mathrm{std}(y)$ is the natural scale-free error. Across gates, $\mathrm{std}(y)$ itself changes, so gate-specific NMAE is \emph{not} a fixed-denominator contrast. Prefer MAE for absolute error, or NMAE with denominator locked to the runtime target $\mathrm{std}(y_{0.95}){=}0.255$: that fixed-denominator NMAE is $0.027$ at gate~$0.5$ vs.\ $0.112$ at gate~$0.95$ (MAE $0.0069\!\rightarrow\!0.029$). The headline NMAE$0.052\!\rightarrow\!0.112$ uses each gate's own $\mathrm{std}(y)$ and should not be read as a different estimand than the fixed-denominator pair.

At gate~$0.5$, $96.2\%$ of hold-out labels are exactly zero (std$(y){=}0.133$); at gate~$0.95$, only $29.0\%$ are zero and the continuous mass spreads (std$(y){=}0.255$, $\times 1.92$). Because $R^2{=}1{-}\mathrm{SS}_{\mathrm{res}}/\mathrm{SS}_{\mathrm{tot}}$, larger target variance inflates $R^2$ even as absolute MAE grows ($\times 4.12$). Therefore $R^2{=}0.94$ is \emph{not} a model-quality upgrade over the historical $R^2{=}0.76$ headline---it is the correct metric for the runtime target. The misalignment affected only the offline validity metric; live filter logs always used gate~$0.95$.

\textbf{Arm-conditional validation.} Global hold-out does \emph{not} certify accuracy on every arm's proposal stream. For the non-LLM H2 gate we now report shadow-stream online ranking/calibration (AUROC~$0.906$, precision/recall @$\tau$, emitter-wise NMAE; appendix~\S\ref{sec:h2-impl-threat}) in addition to skip anatomy and false-skip / missed-elite rates. Still open: descriptor-space error maps, uncertainty calibration, and per-arm online NMAE on LLM mixed / rewrite streams (child-distribution NMAE ${\approx}0.63$/$0.39$ in appendix~\S\ref{sec:h1-pilots-appendix} is a soft-channel diagnostic, not the H2 genetic gate). Hold-out metrics do not replace simulator endpoints for H1/H2/H3, and global accuracy is not evolution-control utility (appendix~\S\ref{sec:h2-impl-threat}).

\textbf{Consistency after measurement fixes.} The compose-gate and NMAE-denominator bugs (Table~\ref{tab:bug-ledger}) changed offline validity reporting and H3 confirmatory eligibility, not archive insertion fitness. Claim-facing terminal coverage / mean elite fitness / QD on frozen primary tiers (\texttt{q1-full}, matched H1, genetic H2) remain those of the original simulator summaries; no re-aggregation of archive endpoints was required after the 2026-07-28 hold-out alignment.

\section{MLP vs.\ GP/UCB ablation (offline)}
\label{sec:surrogate-ablation-appendix}

\emph{Not required for the descriptive H2 case study.} Production H2 uses an MLP ensemble with MC-dropout and a fitness threshold ($\texttt{min\_fit}{=}0.45$, $\texttt{max\_u}{=}1.0$), not SAIL-style GP+UCB. This appendix records the offline MLP-vs-GP/UCB comparison that motivates freezing that family for the attach-role contrast (\S\ref{sec:surrogate-method}). Hold-out NMAE/$R^2$ select the regressor; they do \emph{not} validate live gate decisions (appendix~\S\ref{sec:h2-impl-threat}). On the nightly buffer hold-out at runtime-aligned compose gate~$0.95$, the production MLP achieves NMAE$=0.112$ ($R^2{=}0.94$ on that same target) vs.\ NMAE$=0.222$ ($R^2{=}0.89$) for a scikit-learn GP fitness regressor trained on 5{,}000 rows under the \emph{same} label definition, and is ${\sim}37\times$ faster at a 32{,}500-proposal batch. An earlier M1 snapshot at misaligned gate~$0.5$ reported MLP $R^2{=}0.76$ vs.\ GP ${\approx}0.22$; that gap is not reusable as a model-quality argument because ${\approx}96\%$ of gate-$0.5$ labels are zero (\S\ref{sec:checkpoint-appendix}).

Offline acquisition replay: replacing the MLP with the GP under the \emph{same} threshold gate worsens the false-skip proxy ($0.7\%\!\rightarrow\!3.2\%$); a UCB skip rule on GP scores ($\mu{+}\beta\sigma < \texttt{min\_fit}$, $\beta\in\{0.15,0.5,1.0\}$) stays near $99\%$ skip. On logged Q1 filter proposals ($n{=}325{,}000$), replaying UCB on the \emph{same} MLP $\mu/\sigma$ only softens the gate (skip $33.5\%\!\rightarrow\!28.5\%/25.7\%/21.2\%$) and never adds skips. With $\texttt{max\_u}{=}1.0$, uncertainty is not load-bearing for the production threshold gate. GP+UCB is therefore a related SAIL lineage, not a competitive drop-in. Tree boosters (XGBoost / LightGBM) are not part of this ablation; the repository's optional LightGBM path is disclosed in the main text as a non-confirmatory alternative.

\section{Primary evaluator details}
\label{sec:substrate-appendix}

Main-text \S\ref{sec:substrate} sketches genome, simulator, and behaviour axes. This appendix records fitness composition algebra and a computational edit-anatomy case study (LLM vs.\ genetic parent$\rightarrow$child diffs)---not domain claims.

\paragraph{Genome and evaluation.}
Emitters search a 21-parameter genome: nine birth bits $b_0,\ldots,b_8$, nine survival bits $s_0,\ldots,s_8$, and continuous scalars $(\texttt{noise},\texttt{resource\_regen},\texttt{predation})$. CMA-ME/MAE propose in the same $\mathbb{R}^{21}$ before \texttt{rint}/clip decode (\S\ref{sec:pyribs}). The surrogate does \emph{not} ingest raw 21-D alone: schema~2.1 builds a 24-D feature vector
\begin{equation}
  x \;=\; \bigl(b_0,\ldots,b_8,\; s_0,\ldots,s_8,\; \texttt{noise},\; \texttt{resource\_regen},\; \texttt{predation},\;
  |B|/9,\; |S|/9,\; |B\cap S|/9\bigr),
  \label{eq:features-24}
\end{equation}
where $B{=}\{i:b_i{=}1\}$ and $S{=}\{i:s_i{=}1\}$ (implementation: \texttt{encode\_world\_spec\_features\_v21}). Legacy schema~2.0 was the 21-D prefix only. Each evaluation runs a fixed $50\times50$, $T_{\mathrm{sim}}{=}200$ toroidal grid. Before \texttt{run\_world}, \texttt{apply\_canonical\_seed} sets \texttt{world.seed} from SHA-256 of the canonical genome JSON (independent of the MAP-Elites run seed $0$--$9$), so fitness/BC are a deterministic function of the genotype; early extinction yields archive fitness~0. Terminal elites keep the fitness/BC from that single insertion pass---they are not re-scored at end of run. Illustration panels that re-call \texttt{run\_world} on archived genomes (Figure~\ref{fig:elites}) are for visualisation; under the same canonical seed they must match the stored labels.

\paragraph{Behaviour and fitness.}
Archive axes are stability and diversity in $[0,1]$. Authoritative insertion fitness recomposes diversity, survival, oscillation, and topology proxies. All fitness components below are dimensionless scores already scaled to $[0,1]$ before weighting (topology indices TI/TH are likewise clipped into $[0,1]$ before averaging):
\begin{equation}
  E_{\mathrm{ext}} = \mathrm{clip}(1 - \rho_{\mathrm{final}}, 0, 1), \quad
  T_{\mathrm{topo}} = \mathrm{clip}\!\left(\tfrac{\text{TI} + \text{TH}}{2}, 0, 1\right)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:fitness}
  \text{fitness} = \mathrm{clip}\bigl(
    0.45\,D + 0.25\,(1 - E_{\mathrm{ext}}) + 0.20\,A + 0.10\,T_{\mathrm{topo}}
  ,\; 0,\; 1\bigr)
\end{equation}
where $D\in[0,1]$ is clipped diversity, $A\in[0,1]$ is \texttt{oscillation\_score}, and $T_{\mathrm{topo}}\in[0,1]$ is the topology score (distinct from the simulator horizon $T_{\mathrm{sim}}{=}200$ above). The outer $\mathrm{clip}(\cdot,0,1)$ is defensive. Weights $(0.45,0.25,0.20,0.10)$ are fixed protocol constants shared by live evaluation and surrogate recompose---not fit to the H1/H2 data, not preference-elicited, and not swept for sensitivity in this manuscript. Because $D$ is also a BC axis, $f$ and $D$ are coupled by construction (status \S\ref{sec:fd-coupling}; Table~\ref{tab:fd-coupling}). The surrogate recomposes the same formula from predicted components (\S\ref{sec:surrogate-method}). QD-score is
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{QD} \;=\; \sum_{c \in \mathrm{filled}} f_c \;=\; |\mathrm{filled}| \cdot \bar f.
  \label{eq:qd}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Wall-clock accounting condition}
\label{sec:h2-breakeven}
\label{sec:why-filter}

Fix a proposal stream and a skip indicator with rate $s$. Let $T_{\mathrm{sim}}$ be the simulator time of a candidate \emph{if} it were evaluated, $t_{\mathrm{pred}}$ the online per-proposal inference cost, and $\Delta t_{\mathrm{gate}}$ extra gate / serialization / batching / archive overhead relative to the unfiltered loop. Unfiltered wall per proposal is $\mathbb{E}[T_{\mathrm{sim}}]$; filtered wall is $t_{\mathrm{pred}}+\Delta t_{\mathrm{gate}}+(1-s)\,\mathbb{E}[T_{\mathrm{sim}}\mid\mathrm{keep}]$. Filter wins iff
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:h2-breakeven}
t_{\mathrm{pred}}+\Delta t_{\mathrm{gate}}
<
s\,\mathbb{E}[T_{\mathrm{sim}}\mid \mathrm{skip}],
\end{equation}
by the law of total expectation. Figure~\ref{fig:budget-axes} is the conceptual three-axis claim; this identity prices only the wall axis on this stack. Online archives diverge, so the right-hand side is counterfactual without shadow-timing skipped candidates. Training of the frozen checkpoint is amortized offline and is not in~\eqref{eq:h2-breakeven}.

If $T_{\mathrm{sim}}$ is constant and residual overheads are absorbed into $t_{\mathrm{MLP}}:=t_{\mathrm{pred}}+\Delta t_{\mathrm{gate}}$,~\eqref{eq:h2-breakeven} collapses to the special case $t_{\mathrm{MLP}}<s\,t_{\mathrm{sim}}$. Here $s{\approx}0.335$ and $t_{\mathrm{MLP}}/t_{\mathrm{sim}}\approx 2.2$, so under that special case the simulator must be roughly ${\sim}7\times$ slower before a $33.5\%$ skip saves wall. That ${\sim}7\times$ is a stack/hardware-specific constant-$t_{\mathrm{sim}}$ illustration, not a portable identity and not an interval estimate. Frozen H2 logs do not time $T_{\mathrm{sim}}$ per candidate, so $\mathbb{E}[T_{\mathrm{sim}}\mid\mathrm{skip}]$ and $\mathbb{E}[T_{\mathrm{sim}}\mid\mathrm{keep}]$ are unmeasured on that CA stream; skip mass is high predicted early-extinction (Table~\ref{tab:skip-mech}), which would make the constant-$t$ figure optimistic. On the LLM stack chat completions are issued before the skip, so gating does not cut emit attempts (\S\ref{sec:cost}). Maze injected-delay is a descriptive \emph{regime-transition} check of the constant-$t$ special case \emph{on maze}, not a measurement of CA $\mathbb{E}[T_{\mathrm{sim}}\mid\mathrm{skip}]$ and not a naturally expensive second domain: sleep $\delta{=}10$~ms (above that domain's ${\sim}3$~ms algebraic threshold) is applied only inside \texttt{evaluate\_maze} on simulated (non-skipped) proposals. Under that inflated $t_{\mathrm{sim}}$ the same gate is faster on wall for $9/10$ seeds. Sphere (Table~\ref{tab:sphere-h2}) is an evaluation-indexed substrate check, not a wall-break-even measurement.

\subsection{H2 supporting controls}
\label{sec:h2-impl-threat}

Global predictive accuracy is not sufficient evidence that a surrogate is useful as an evolution-control mechanism~\cite{jin2005fitness}. The hold-out screen (\S\ref{sec:surrogate-method}) is that accuracy check. Online ranking of the live gate stream is separate: shadow AUROC~$0.906$, precision/recall @$0.927$/$0.801$ at $\tau{=}0.45$. Archive \emph{utility} is the gating contrast itself: evaluation-indexed coverage $+3.65$~pp (\S\ref{sec:h2}); rate-matched \texttt{random\_skip} loses to live filter; \texttt{filter\_eval\_matched} exceeds uniform when real sims are matched, so the fixed-proposal $-0.83$~pp terminal is budget truncation; same-stream absolute-$\tau$ true-fitness-threshold replay leaves $+0.73$~pp headroom (Tables~\ref{tab:skip-mech},~\ref{tab:h2-tau-sweep},~\ref{tab:h2-ranking-controls}). Skip mass is low predicted fitness / high extinction; $\tau$ trades mid-budget fill against terminal coverage. Niche-relative incumbent gating remains open.

\section{Protocol freeze kinds and commit$\leftrightarrow$run timeline}

\emph{Not required to follow the main argument.} Confirmatory families were \emph{prospectively specified in dated internal protocol files} relative to each family's analysis window. That is \emph{not} an externally timestamped pre-registration (no OSF/AsPredicted lock before data). Public \texttt{git} history and filesystem artifact mtimes are integrity aids; they are \emph{mutable} and are not equivalent to an independent registry timestamp. The post-hoc Zenodo deposit (DOI~\texttt{\ZenodoArtifactsDOI}) pins code+artifacts for this revision---an integrity snapshot, not a pre-data preregistration. This appendix only classifies which decisions were freezes, new packages, or reporting-only.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Lock} --- freezes a confirmatory family's endpoints / Holm (or NI) rules in the internal protocol relative to that family's analysis window.
  \item \textbf{Extension} --- adds a new package or repair path; does not rewrite already-frozen families.
  \item \textbf{Reporting} --- records an outcome, descriptive case study, or post-hoc TOST on already-collected paired seeds; not a change of locked family rules.
\end{itemize}

\section{Archive heatmap panel (supplemental)}
\label{sec:heatmap-panel}

Figure~\ref{fig:heatmaps} is an \emph{illustrative} view of where the paired coverage gap lives on one outcome-selected seed, not a proof of a general spatial pattern: CMA-ME illuminates broad regions of behaviour space that hints leave empty---especially low-diversity bands and scattered interior holes---rather than only polishing already-filled high-fitness niches. Fitness colour is higher on average in the CMA panel, consistent with the fitness delta, but the dominant visual story is \emph{coverage}. Those gray bands are empty cells of the tessellation, not a certified infeasible mask: the same seed's CMA-ME panel occupies them, so raw coverage over $2500$ cells does not hide a uniformly unreachable region (\S\ref{sec:coverage-support}). Across all ten paired seeds the same geometry appears as occupancy probability and $\Delta P$ (Figure~\ref{fig:occupancy-n10}): CMA-ME occupies a broader $(S,D)$ support; gray there means never occupied in those ten archives. The gap is also visible along the evaluation axis (Figure~\ref{fig:anytime-ladder}): on seed~4, CMA-ME reaches $\sim$63\% coverage by 10{,}000 evaluations while hints are still $\sim$47\%. Aggregate evidence for the ceiling remains the $n{=}10$ paired $\Delta$cov, not the heatmap geometry.
