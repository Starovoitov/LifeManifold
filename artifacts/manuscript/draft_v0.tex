\title{Matching Target Selection Reverses a Bundled {LLM}-Emitter Advantage in Warm-Started Surrogate-Assisted {MAP}-{E}lites}

\author{Artem Starovoitov\corref{cor1}}
\ead{artem.starovoitov@proton.me}
\ead[url]{https://orcid.org/0009-0005-3170-4865}
\address{Independent Engineer, Moscow, Russia}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author}

\begin{abstract}
In quality-diversity (QD) MAP-Elites, an improvement from a language-model emitter is often attributed to the prompt itself. But the emitter also brings an archive policy, a warm-start, and a budget---each of which can change coverage. Here we show that this error can be substantial.

On a structured-genotype cellular automaton we continue a shared archive of $971$ niches ($38.84\%$ coverage). Live parent-level surrogate scalars appear to beat a constant-scalar stub by about $15$ percentage points (pp). After both arms use the same target-selection policy, only $+0.26$~pp remains. The $15$~pp had been credited to the prompt; they came from unmatched policy. Five independently built floors of the same occupancy repeat the split (policy ${\approx}{+}15$~pp; leftover $+0.29$~pp; shuffle leftover $+0.28$~pp). An empty-archive maze shows the same pattern (policy ${\approx}{+}39$~pp at $5$k; leftover $+0.02$/$+0.23$~pp). The archive was already partly filled.

The cellular automaton is cheap to simulate. On it, a genetic threshold gate skips evaluations and raises coverage at a matched evaluation count, but it does not raise terminal coverage or shorten wall-clock time.
\end{abstract}

\begin{highlights}
\item Matching policy turns a +15.09 pp gap into +0.26 pp.
\item On five floors, policy is ~15 pp; the tested scalars add little.
\item Gating saves evaluations and costs wall time.
\item Unmatched and matched controls point to opposite causes.
\end{highlights}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Large language models are now used as variation operators and emitters in quality-diversity (QD) search. MAP-Elites keeps the best elite in each behavioural niche~\cite{mouret2015illuminating}; an LLM can propose the next genome from a parent and a prompt~\cite{lehman2023openelm,nasir2024llmatic,lim2024llmqd}. When coverage rises after that swap, researchers often credit the language model: the prompt, the scalars it receives, the edits it writes. The emitter also changes the archive policy, the warm-start, the budget, and the representation. Any of those can change coverage too. Matching those factors is ordinary experimental practice in evolutionary computation~\cite{eiben2011parameter,bartzbeielstein2006experimental}. With an LLM they are easy to miss, because several of them sit in one configuration file.

This paper looks at one such case in a MAP-Elites setup we control. The historical comparison swapped a whole pipeline: \texttt{hints} writes live parent-level surrogate scalars into the prompt, while \texttt{stub} fills constant placeholders ($0.5$/$1.0$). The two arms also used different \texttt{target\_selection} rules. On a structured-genotype cellular automaton, continuing a shared warm-start of $971$ niches ($38.84\%$ coverage), live hints appear to beat the stub by $+15.09$ percentage points (pp) of terminal coverage. Once the archive policy is the same, almost all of that gap is gone. Under \texttt{uniform\_frontier} the leftover is $+0.26$~pp (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}). We expected the scalars to matter. They did not.

The policy gap is large because insertion fitness puts weight $w_D{=}0.45$ on diversity $D$, and $D$ is also a behaviour axis, so the lowest-fitness frontier cells are the lowest-diversity ones (\S\ref{sec:fd-coupling}). Picking the worst frontier cell and sampling the frontier uniformly therefore search different parts of the archive. The same two policies, with no LLM at all, move coverage by $+13.1$~pp.

The main numbers come from one cellular-automaton evaluator, continued from a partly filled archive. Five independently built floors of the same occupancy repeat the split (policy ${\approx}{+}15$~pp; leftover $+0.29$~pp). A maze with a different genotype and fitness shows the same pattern from an empty archive (policy ${\approx}{+}39$~pp at $5$k slots; leftover $+0.02$/$+0.23$~pp; Table~\ref{tab:rq1-maze}). Sphere numbers are in the appendix.

MAP-Elites still calls the simulator on every evaluated candidate~\cite{gaier2018sail}. How to spend that budget is a classical surrogate-assisted question~\cite{jin2005fitness}. On the same cheap cellular automaton we therefore also run a threshold gate on the matched genetic emitter (\S\ref{sec:h2}).

\textbf{RQ1.} Does matching target selection remove the bundled hints coverage gain, or do the parent-level prompt scalars keep it?

If an LLM emitter is dropped in as a whole pipeline, the first thing to hold fixed is target selection. That is what went wrong here.

\subsection{Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Where the $+15$~pp went.} A $+15.09$~pp \texttt{stub}-to-\texttt{hints} coverage gap on the shared $38.84\%$ floor largely disappears when target selection is matched: the policy contrast remains ${\approx}{+}15$~pp, while the matched parent-level scalar residual is ${\le}0.3$~pp. A calendar-blocked $2\times2$ on five independent $971$-niche floors repeats this split (Table~\ref{tab:rq1-cf}). The bundled gain belongs to target selection, not to the tested parent-level prompt scalars.
  \item \textbf{What remains after matching.} On the primary evaluator, hints $-$ \texttt{stub\_uniform} is $+0.26$~pp (paired 95\% CI $[-0.59,+1.11]$; \S\ref{sec:decomposition}). The calendar-blocked leftover at uniform is $+0.29$~pp (95\% CI $[-0.20,+0.75]$).
  \item \textbf{Gating and the budget axis.} A threshold gate on the matched non-LLM genetic emitter skips $33.5\%$ of proposals and improves evaluation-indexed coverage by $+3.65$~pp at $20{,}000$ real evaluations, but reduces terminal coverage by $0.83$~pp at equal slots and increases paired wall time by $13.6$~min (\S\ref{sec:h2}). Whether the surrogate helps depends on which budget one counts, and on how expensive the simulator is. The same evaluation-indexed pattern appears on the Fontaine Sphere.
  \item \textbf{Scope.} The main result is a warm-started continuation on one structured-genotype cellular-automaton evaluator. Maze empty $2\times2$: Table~\ref{tab:rq1-maze}. Empty-start and transfer checks stay in the appendix.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background and related work}
\label{sec:related}

Quality-diversity (QD) algorithms seek diverse, high-performing collections rather than a single optimum~\cite{pugh2016quality,cully2018quality,chatzilygeroudis2021qd}. MAP-Elites retains the best elite in each behavioural niche~\cite{mouret2015illuminating}, while heterogeneous emitters can improve illumination~\cite{cully2020multiemitter}. CMA-ME / CMA-MAE are strong continuous-QD baselines~\cite{fontaine2020cmame,fontaine2023cmamae,zhao2024cmamae,pyribs2020}. We run them on the same simulator, alongside a representation-mismatched \texttt{rint} pipeline and matched discrete CMA-ME / pbCMA (\S\ref{sec:h4}).

Classical SAEAs treat prediction as a component of model management~\cite{jin2005fitness,jones1998ego}. Hold-out accuracy of a regressor still has to earn its keep as an evolution-control mechanism. SAIL / DSA-ME use surrogate models to define acquisition surfaces and allocate real evaluations to selected candidates~\cite{gaier2018sail,gaier2017sail,grillotti2022dsame,kent2025boqd,tjanaka2026dms}. We keep a fixed MAP-Elites propose$\rightarrow$evaluate loop with ground-truth archive updates.

LLMs have been used as variation operators and QD emitters~\cite{wu2025ecllm,lehman2023openelm,nasir2024llmatic,lim2024llmqd,vanstein2025behaviour,romera2024funsearch,ma2024eureka}, including in LLM$+$MAP-Elites systems~\cite{ropke2026dejaq,sviridov2026medical}. Within one controlled stack, a complete-pipeline comparison can attribute a large bundled gain to the wrong channel when those factors go unmatched: the $+15.09$~pp coverage gap falls to $+0.26$~pp once target selection is matched, and a calendar-blocked $2\times2$ replicates this inversion (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}). An empty-archive maze $2\times2$ shows the same pattern. LLM-based fitness models in LAEA-style systems~\cite{hao2024laea,xie2025llmsae} use a learned fitness signal; here we mutate \texttt{WorldSpec} genomes with an LLM emitter and score them with a genome-aligned MLP. Archive evaluations are genotype-canonical and seeded: one evaluation per insertion~\cite{flageat2023uq,flageat2025extractqd}.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

The experiments below test this attribution within a MAP-Elites propose$\rightarrow$evaluate loop. We match the stack components and use warm-start floors with the same occupancy. The LLM is an optional emitter; the evaluator is the measurement environment (\S\ref{sec:substrate}). Archive insertion always uses simulated fitness.

\subsection{Roles, policies, and contrast labels}
\label{sec:terminology}

A MAP-Elites iteration consists of select $\rightarrow$ generate $\rightarrow$ (optionally) gate $\rightarrow$ evaluate $\rightarrow$ archive update:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{H1: parent-level surrogate scalars.} The MLP writes $\hat f$ and $\hat u$ into a prompt that already contains the parent's true fitness, on approximately $10$ of $50$ LLM slots. Every proposal is still simulated.
  \item \textbf{H2: after-generation gate.} The MLP scores an already-generated candidate and may skip its simulation using an absolute-$\tau$ threshold on the genetic stack.
\end{itemize}

Jin's before-/after-generation labels~\cite{jin2005fitness} describe pipeline topology.

Main-text claims use three evidence roles (Table~\ref{tab:h-status}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Primary:} the RQ1 attribution package;
  \item \emph{Robustness:} experiments that qualify the primary claim, including the five-floor $2\times2$, H2, Sphere H2, maze H5, the empty-maze $2\times2$, mixed/empty $2\times2$, and CMA;
  \item \emph{Supplementary:} pilots, Path~A, placebo detail, dungeon, CVT, H3, protocol dual-report, Sphere leftover $2\times2$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{MAP-Elites illuminator loop}

Each run executes $650 \times 50 = 32{,}500$ \emph{proposal slots} in a fixed order. Three budgets stay distinct: proposal slots; real simulator evaluations (filter arms ${\approx}21.5$k at $33.5\%$ skip); and wall-clock time / LLM call counts. H2 terminal coverage is compared at matched slots; evaluation-indexed curves use real evaluations (\S\ref{sec:h2}).

Each slot: select a frontier niche (\texttt{target\_selection}) $\rightarrow$ emit a \texttt{WorldSpec} $\rightarrow$ optionally skip $\rightarrow$ otherwise \texttt{run\_world} $\rightarrow$ insert on strict fitness improvement. LLM slots log \texttt{llm\_fallback} on parse failure.

\textbf{Target selection.} Both policies use \emph{frontier} cells only---occupied niches with an empty neighbour. \texttt{min\_fitness\_frontier} picks the lowest-fitness frontier cell (bundled \texttt{stub}), whereas \texttt{uniform\_frontier} samples uniformly from that set (\texttt{hints}, filter, matched H1/H2). The insertion fitness weights diversity $D$ by $w_D{=}0.45$; $D$ is also a behaviour-characterization axis. As a result, the lowest-fitness parents are systematically low-$D$ (\S\ref{sec:fd-coupling}).

Every MAP-Elites slot uses the same \texttt{select\_target\_cell} (batch of $50$). Parent-level H1 scalars are added on only the ${\approx}10$ LLM slots in that batch. The H1 comparison holds the target-selection policy fixed (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}); the original stub$\rightarrow$hints gap does not.

\textbf{Terminology.} Here, \texttt{target\_selection} denotes archive target selection, and the parent is the incumbent elite in the selected niche. ``Archive policy'' in RQ1 is this choice.

\subsection{Surrogate model}
\label{sec:surrogate-method}

The surrogate maps a canonical \texttt{WorldSpec} to predicted MAP-Elites outcomes without a simulator call (appendix Figure~\ref{fig:surrogate}). Schema~2.1 lifts the 21-parameter genome to a 24-dimensional representation. The production checkpoint \texttt{nightly\_v3\_mc\_d005.pkl} is a four-member MLP ensemble on the nightly buffer ($N{=}35{,}114$ rows; hold-out NMAE${=}0.112$). Global hold-out accuracy does not establish arm-conditional ranking. All arms use the same MLP checkpoint.

\textbf{Parent-level surrogate scalars (H1).}
On the ${\approx}10$ LLM slots in each batch of $50$, H1 calls \texttt{predict(parent)} and writes two scalars into a prompt that already contains the parent's true fitness:
\begin{quote}
\small\texttt{Surrogate predicts fitness} $\approx$ \texttt{\{surrogate\_mean\}, uncertainty = \{surrogate\_uncertainty\}}
\end{quote}
\texttt{hints} fills these fields with live MLP $\hat f$/$\hat u$; \texttt{stub\_uniform} uses the YAML constants $0.5$/$1.0$. The predicted fitness therefore restates information already present in the prompt (\S\ref{sec:h1-impl-threat}).

\subsection{LLM emitter}

The \texttt{LlmEmitter} sends the parent \texttt{WorldSpec} JSON and niche centre to a chat API and applies a JSON patch. Primary runs use \texttt{qwen-turbo}; multi-provider replications swap endpoints at fixed prompts (\S\ref{sec:results}).

\subsection{After-generation surrogate (H2)}
\label{sec:acquisition}
\label{sec:mixed-2x2-design}

Optional \texttt{acquisition.mode=filter} skips simulation when predicted fitness falls below \texttt{min\_predicted\_fitness=0.45}. Skipped slots do not update the archive. The gate uses an absolute threshold on the shared MLP. Primary gating evidence is the matched genetic filter contrast (\S\ref{sec:h2}).

\subsection{pyribs CMA-ME / CMA-MAE baselines}
\label{sec:pyribs}

Gradient QD baselines run through \texttt{run\_pyribs\_baseline.py} (\texttt{ribs==0.11.0}): five \texttt{EvolutionStrategyEmitter}s, $32{,}500$ asks, each corresponding to one simulator evaluation. The same simulator is used. CMA proposes $\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{21}$; \texttt{decode\_genome} rounds the 18 rule bits to integers (\texttt{rint})---a representation-mismatched pipeline.

Discrete CMA / pbCMA live in the appendix. All primary-grid arms, including pyribs, load the same JSONL ($2{,}614$ records $\rightarrow$ $971$ niches, $38.84\%$ coverage).

\subsection{Measurement substrate}
\label{sec:substrate}

Primary contrasts use a controlled black-box evaluator. Each candidate is a structured \texttt{WorldSpec}: 18 rule bits plus three scalars. The simulator runs a seeded $50\times50$ toroidal CA for $T_{\mathrm{sim}}{=}200$ steps (early extinction $\Rightarrow$ fitness~0). The behaviour descriptor uses stability and diversity, both in $[0,1]$, on a $50\times50$ grid. The insertion fitness combines diversity, survival, oscillation, and topology; because diversity enters with $w_D{=}0.45$, fitness $f$ and diversity $D$ are coupled (\S\ref{sec:fd-coupling}).

Genetic emitters flip bits; LLM emitters edit the same named fields as JSON. The evaluator is inexpensive, so H2 reports evaluation-indexed efficiency and wall-clock time separately (\S\ref{sec:h2}). Coverage is the occupied-cell percentage of the $2500$-cell tessellation; mean elite fitness averages elite fitness over occupied bins. The logged \texttt{mean\_best\_fitness} is the occupied-bin mean.

Implementation and analysis scripts in the accompanying repository were drafted with assistance from Cursor (Composer). The author reviewed and edited that code and is responsible for its correctness.

\section{Experimental protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

Holm membership and the H2 reporting rule are in \S\ref{sec:stats}.

\subsection{Design principles}

Primary-grid contrasts share seeds $0$--$9$ within a tier. Pairing therefore controls run-level stochasticity ($T{=}0.2$ and no provider seed). Locked Holm, H1, H2, and H4 arms continue one JSONL warm-start ($971$ niches / $38.84\%$) for $32{,}500$ slots; the $n{=}10$ seeds are continuation RNGs on that floor. The calendar-blocked $2\times2$ instead uses five independently generated floors of the same occupancy. The maze empty $2\times2$ at $5$k uses a different evaluator. The locked \texttt{stub} vs.\ \texttt{hints} contrast also differs in \texttt{target\_selection}; the H1 comparison holds that policy fixed (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}).

\subsection{Experimental conditions}

Locked primary-grid arms share a $50\times50$ GridArchive, the same JSONL, and $32{,}500$ slots. Mixed LLM arms use a $20$ random ${+}20$ genetic ${+}10$ LLM batch. Matched H1 uses \texttt{stub\_uniform}; \texttt{hints\_minfit} completes the historical $2\times2$. Calendar-blocked cells add \texttt{shuffled\_uniform} on independent floors. Maze empty cells are the four \texttt{llm\_*} minfit/uniform arms at $5$k. Bundled stub$\rightarrow$hints is the off-diagonal $Y(\texttt{hints},\texttt{uniform})-Y(\texttt{stub},\texttt{minfit})$ (Table~\ref{tab:estimands}).

\subsection{Contemporaneous RQ1 factorial}
\label{sec:rq1-cf-design}

The design is specified in \path{artifacts/Q1_RQ1_CONTEMPORANEOUS_FACTORIAL.md}. Five independent no-LLM floors are truncated at $971$ occupied cells / $38.84\%$ coverage, with two continuation RNG replicates per floor ($10$ blocks). Within each block, five arms run in a deterministic randomized order: \texttt{stub\_minfit}, \texttt{live\_minfit}, \texttt{stub\_uniform}, \texttt{live\_uniform}, and \texttt{shuffled\_uniform}. The shuffled arm is a distribution-matched placebo beside the $2\times2$. All LLM calls use dated \texttt{gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18} and are joined to their proposals. The endpoint is terminal new coverage above the floor. Uncertainty is an archive-clustered percentile bootstrap $95\%$ CI (floor unit).

\subsection{Maze empty RQ1 $2\times2$}
\label{sec:rq1-maze-design}

The design is specified in \path{artifacts/Q1_RQ1_SECOND_DOMAIN.md}. Maze uses a $900$-cell archive, a different genotype, and $f{=}0.55$ length $+0.30$ branch $+0.15$ reach ($\rho(f,\mathrm{path}){\approx}0.89$ on frozen genetic seed~0). Phase~A established headroom at $5$k (paired $\Delta{=}{-}40.19\pm4.13$~pp). Phase~B is the $\{\texttt{stub},\texttt{live}\}\times\{\texttt{minfit},\texttt{uniform}\}$ factorial from an empty archive at $5{,}000$ slots, using dated \texttt{gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18}, with call logs and $n{=}10$. Locked H5 \texttt{llm\_*} arms share \texttt{uniform\_frontier} at $32.5$k, where genetic search already saturates the grid; that family cannot replay a policy collapse (appendix Table~\ref{tab:b5-holm}).

\subsection{Endpoints and statistical reporting}
\label{sec:stats}

The primary endpoints are terminal coverage (occupied niches as a percentage of the $2500$-cell tessellation) and occupied-bin mean elite fitness. QD-score is the coverage$\times$quality companion. The endpoint-to-test map is appendix Table~\ref{tab:test-map}.

Confirmatory decisions use a one-sided Wilcoxon test with Holm correction within each locked family (bundled stack, maze H5 AUC, multi-provider bundled replications, gray-zone NI, dungeon AUC). A non-reject is fail-to-reject. Families are not pooled into one paper-wide vector. The matched leftover, the calendar-blocked $2\times2$, the maze empty $2\times2$, the Sphere leftover $2\times2$, and H2 (including Sphere H2) are paired mean $\Delta$ with a $95\%$ CI; they are not Holm decisions. H2 is a budget-axis illustration on the genetic stack, not a co-equal research question. The resampling unit is the continuation seed on one floor, or the floor archive when $k{=}5$ independent floors. Exploratory companions (post-hoc $\pm 2$~pp TOST, $\mathrm{PPS}$, MDE) are in \ref{sec:stats-appendix}.

Unless a caption names a CI, $\pm$ is the sample SD of $n{=}10$ seed-level values (or of the paired $\Delta$).

\subsection{RQ1 --- Matching policy collapses the bundled gap}
\label{sec:decomposition}
\label{sec:policy-x-hint}

The bundled contrast produced a $+15.09$~pp terminal-coverage difference favouring live hints over the historical \texttt{stub} ($10/10$; Holm $m{=}2$). After matching \texttt{target\_selection}, that difference collapses: on the primary Qwen-Turbo arm, \texttt{hints} $-$ \texttt{stub\_uniform} is $+0.26$~pp (paired 95\% CI $[-0.59,+1.11]$). Completing the historical policy$\times$hint grid (Table~\ref{tab:policy-x-hint}) gives the same pattern under both archive policies: the soft leftovers are near zero ($-0.02$ / $+0.26$~pp), while changing policy accounts for approximately $15$~pp at either soft level.

The four cell means aggregate observations from three tiers: an observed matched-control collapse.

Let $Y(\mathrm{soft},\mathrm{policy})$ denote the mean of a cell in Table~\ref{tab:policy-x-hint}. The bundled contrast is the off-diagonal difference $Y(\texttt{hints},\texttt{uniform})-Y(\texttt{stub},\texttt{minfit})$. The same table reports the derived cell-mean contrasts, including a difference-in-differences $\Delta_{\mathrm{int}}$ that measures whether the scalar contrast varies with target selection. On cell means, $\Delta_{\mathrm{bundle}}=\Delta_{\mathrm{policy}\mid\mathrm{stub}}+\Delta_{\mathrm{soft}\mid\mathrm{uniform}}$ is an arithmetic identity.

Target selection is applied at every proposal slot ($50/50$), whereas parent-level scalars fill only ${\approx}10/50$ LLM slots. An API-free genetic policy swap still changes coverage by $+13.1$~pp (\S\ref{sec:genetic-policy}).

Thus matching policy leaves a leftover of $+0.26$~pp (Table~\ref{tab:policy-x-hint}).

\subsection{Independent-floor calendar-blocked $2\times2$}
\label{sec:rq1-cf}

Table~\ref{tab:rq1-cf} provides a contemporaneous replication of the inversion. It uses five independently built $971$-niche floors, with two continuation blocks per floor, and the dated \texttt{gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18} model. Call logs are complete for all $50/50$ arms. The primary endpoint is new coverage above the floor.

The policy effect is $+14.53$~pp under stub and $+14.82$~pp under live ($10/10$ blocks; archive-clustered 95\% CIs $[+11.8,+18.0]$ and $[+12.2,+18.1]$, respectively). At minfit, the terminal archives of live and stub match in all $10$ blocks: they have the same occupied bins, elite fitness values, and stored genotypes. Equal coverage is therefore a consequence of archive equality, not its definition. Under uniform selection, the leftover is $+0.29$~pp (archive-clustered 95\% CI $[-0.20,+0.75]$; $5/10$). The distribution-matched placebo, \texttt{live\_uniform}$-$\texttt{shuffled\_uniform}, is $+0.28$~pp (archive-clustered 95\% CI $[+0.02,+0.54]$; $6/10$). The observed placebo effect is small relative to target selection. The outer unit is the five floor archives ($k{=}5$).

Absolute terminal coverage is ${\sim}2$~pp below the one-JSONL historical cells because the floors differ; the contrasts are consistent. Mechanism logs show that LLM, genetic, and random proposals share the same \texttt{select\_target\_cell} implementation. Under minfit, runs visit ${\approx}1.6$ unique targets (normalized entropy $0.16$; consecutive-repeat rate $1.00$), whereas uniform selection visits ${\approx}670$ targets (entropy $0.97$). Stub and live produce the same target distribution when the policy is fixed. The shuffle mismatches the parent $(f,u)$ on $89\%$ of LLM slots ($\mathrm{L1}{=}0.21$) while leaving target entropy unchanged.

\subsection{Maze empty-archive $2\times2$}

The maze experiment starts from an empty archive. Genetic Phase~A first established headroom at $5$k: minfit reached $16.79 \pm 3.29$\% coverage versus $56.98 \pm 4.47$\% for frozen uniform (paired $\Delta{=}{-}40.19 \pm 4.13$~pp; $10/10$). \subsection{Identification under a non-pinned model}
\label{sec:llm-id}
\label{sec:genetic-policy}

The $+0.26$~pp frozen leftover pairs a window-pinned Qwen-Turbo \texttt{hints} arm with a later-tier \texttt{stub\_uniform} arm. The runs are not time-interleaved, the model state is not pinned across the comparison, and no call log is available.

Further controls: an API-free genetic policy swap gives $+13.1$~pp; the same-calendar leftover is $+0.31\pm0.29$~pp (appendix Table~\ref{tab:mixed-2x2}); the matched leftover is $+0.12$~pp on \texttt{gpt-4o-mini}. The calendar-blocked and empty-maze factorials add pinned model IDs and complete call logs.

Additional inferential analyses are reported in \ref{sec:stats-appendix}.

On cell means,
\begin{equation*}
  \Delta_{\mathrm{bundle}}
  =\Delta_{\mathrm{policy}\mid\mathrm{s}}
  +\Delta_{\mathrm{soft}\mid\mathrm{u}}
  =14.83+0.26=15.09~\mathrm{pp}.
\end{equation*}
The difference-in-differences $\Delta_{\mathrm{int}}$ measures whether the scalar contrast changes with target selection (paired $+0.28\pm1.21$~pp; bootstrap 95\% CI $[-0.42,+1.0]$; Table~\ref{tab:policy-x-hint}). In the calendar-blocked design it equals the uniform leftover, because the minfit leftover is exactly zero.

The same policy movement appears without any API calls: \texttt{genetic\_me} with $20$R+$30$G under \texttt{min\_fitness\_frontier} reaches $45.93 \pm 1.50$\%, close to the historical \texttt{stub}, whereas \texttt{genetic\_me\_uniform} reaches $59.04 \pm 0.76$\% ($+13.1$~pp). This is consistent with the $f$--$D$ geometry discussed in \S\ref{sec:fd-coupling}.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Synthesis}

The load-bearing finding is attributional (RQ1). Matching \texttt{target\_selection} collapses the bundled $+15.09$~pp gap; a calendar-blocked $2\times2$ on independent floors replicates it (policy $+14.53$/$+14.82$~pp new coverage; leftover $+0.29$~pp at uniform; Table~\ref{tab:rq1-cf}). The empty-archive maze $2\times2$ shows the same inversion. Genetic $+13.1$~pp is the same policy movement without an LLM.

The bundled gap looks impressive---fifteen percentage points, unanimous across seeds. But it evaporates the moment we hold the archive policy fixed. The scalars were never doing the work we thought they were.

H2 asks about evaluation budget (Figure~\ref{fig:acquisition}). CMA / maze H5 Holm are in the appendix.

\subsection{Fitness--descriptor coupling is the first critique of the policy magnitude}
\label{sec:fd-coupling}

Two separable claims. (a)~The size of the CA policy $\Delta$ (${\approx}15$~pp) is consistent with this $f$--$D$ coupling. (b)~The small matched leftover is the residual of providing parent-level scalars through a prompt that already contains true $f$ (\S\ref{sec:h1-impl-threat}).

Eq.~\eqref{eq:fitness} puts $w_D{=}0.45$ on diversity $D$, which is also a BC axis, so $f$ and $D$ are coupled ($\rho(f,D){=}0.986$ on the warm-start floor; $r{=}0.977$). \texttt{min\_fitness\_frontier} therefore selects low-$D$ parents (warm-start minfit cell: $D{=}0.0078$, $f{=}0$ vs.\ frontier-mean $D{=}0.459$ among $303$ frontier cells). Calendar-blocked visit logs make the mechanism explicit: minfit uses ${\approx}1.6$ unique targets per run (normalized entropy $0.16$; consecutive repeat $1.00$); uniform uses ${\approx}670$ (entropy $0.97$). Genetic $+13.1$~pp is that movement without an LLM.

$w_D{=}0.45$ on a BC axis is the protocol fitness. We did not run a live $w_D$ swap. Maze uses $f=0.55\,\mathrm{length}+0.30\,\mathrm{branch}+0.15\,\mathrm{reach}$, BC $=(\mathrm{path},\mathrm{branch})$, Spearman $\rho(f,\mathrm{path}){\approx}0.89$ and $\rho(f,\mathrm{branch}){\approx}0.42$ on frozen genetic seed~0. The empty $2\times2$ inverts (policy $+39.10$/$+39.31$~pp; leftover $+0.02$/$+0.23$; Table~\ref{tab:rq1-maze}).

The system prompt also states the insertion-fitness formula. Soft leftovers stay small under \emph{both} policies (historical $-0.02$ / $+0.26$~pp; calendar-blocked $+0.29$~pp at uniform; matching collapsed archives at minfit), so neither the provided scalars nor that known-weight channel accounts for the bundled $+15$~pp. The historical comparison bundled target selection with the claimed prompt channel; here the error is large \emph{because} $f$ and $D$ are coupled.

\subsection{Scope of the H1 leftover}
\label{sec:h1-impl-threat}

\textbf{Leftover.} On primary \texttt{qwen-turbo} under matched \texttt{uniform\_frontier}, the leftover is $+0.26$~pp (paired 95\% CI $[-0.59,+1.11]$; $n{=}10$; \S\ref{sec:decomposition}). The calendar-blocked leftover at uniform is $+0.29$~pp (archive-clustered 95\% CI $[-0.20,+0.75]$); at minfit live and stub wrote matching collapsed terminal archives in all 10 blocks. Those intervals are an order of magnitude below the policy $\Delta$ (${\approx}15$~pp). On DeepSeek the paired 95\% CI $[-1.49,+3.53]$ is wide.

\textbf{What matched policy isolates.} Matched H1 holds emitter mix, \texttt{uniform\_frontier}, prompt templates, repair/coercion, warm-start, and $32{,}500$ slots/sims fixed; the intended treatment is the filled surrogate line (live parent MLP vs.\ YAML $0.5$/$1.0$). Calendar-blocked shuffle on dated \texttt{gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18} is a contemporaneous distribution-matched placebo (mismatch on $89\%$ of LLM slots; leftover $+0.28$~pp; Table~\ref{tab:rq1-cf}).

\textbf{Why the leftover is small.} The locked user prompt already includes the parent elite's \emph{true} simulated fitness inside \texttt{current\_elite\_json}, plus neighbour elites with true fitness; the system prompt also states Eq.~\eqref{eq:fitness}, including $w_D{=}0.45$. Live H1 appends a surrogate estimate of fitness/uncertainty on that same parent genotype. Advice touches only ${\approx}10/50$ batch slots.

\subsection{Implications for LLM emitters}
\label{sec:when-llm}

The LLM is one emitter in the H1 stack (\S\ref{sec:cross-stack}). It is rarely the metrics winner on coverage or wall time.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Attribution first.} Before crediting an emitter from a reported gap, re-run the control under matched target selection, warm-start, and budget. Matching policy removes the $+15$~pp bundled gap (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}).
  \item \emph{Choose the LLM emitter for the artifact.} Editable genotypes can justify the cost; coverage here does not. Hints ($60.4\%$) sit slightly above genetic ME ($59.0\%$ at ${\sim}6$~min) and below discrete CMA-ME / pbCMA / \texttt{rint} (appendix Table~\ref{tab:discrete-cma}). Hard gating is a wall-clock check on the warm-start floor.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The primary claim is the attribution inversion on this warm-started CA. The rest of the boundary, stated once: this paper is not a factorial of where a surrogate should attach; not a causal mediation of the locked Holm gap; not a prospective equivalence test of the leftover; not a ranking of illumination methods, LLM prompts, or SAIL-style acquisition; not a literature-domain replica of the $+15$~pp (the maze is our second evaluator; Sphere leftover is appendix-only); not a live GP+UCB comparator; not a config audit of other LLM-QD papers; and not a general before-generation impossibility result.

\subsubsection{Warm-start is a major boundary, not a footnote}
\label{sec:warm-start-limit}
\label{sec:bootstrap-hyp}

Locked Holm / H1 / H2 seeds continue one fixed baseline JSONL: $2{,}614$ records collapse to $971$ niches / $38.84\%$ coverage. That $n{=}10$ is continuation RNG on one floor file. The calendar-blocked $2\times2$ provides an independent-floor replication at the same occupancy (Table~\ref{tab:rq1-cf}). The experiment studies how methods \emph{continue} an already filled archive. An empty-archive mixed $2\times2$ leaves parent-scalar leftover small and noisy ($+1.48\pm3.57$~pp); the hard gate sign-flips @20k (warm $+$ vs.\ empty $-$). Genetic H2 $+3.65$~pp @20k remains a result on the $38.84\%$ floor.

\subsubsection{Coverage is of the tessellation, not a proven achievable set}
\label{sec:coverage-support}

Primary-grid coverage divides occupied niches by the $2500$ cells of the $50\times50$ $[0,1]^2$ tessellation. Gray heatmap cells are empty. We do not have an infeasible-cell oracle---the 21-D genotype is not enumerable. CMA-ME occupancy ($73.37\pm3.65\%$ mean; $80.5\%$ on the illustrative seed-4 panel) is an empirical lower bound on the number of niches demonstrated reachable under the tested emitters: large low-diversity holes that remain gray under hints are filled by a different emitter. Residual empty cells after CMA-ME may mix unexplored and possibly unreachable regions. Maze genetic arms hit a known 900-cell ceiling.

\subsubsection{H1 and H2 do not form an isolated attach-point comparison}
\label{sec:cross-stack}

Comparing H1 to H2 as if only the attach role changed would be incorrect. The contrasts fix different factors (appendix Table~\ref{tab:cross-stack}). The mixed $2\times2$ isolates those factors on one LLM mix. Primary gating evidence is the genetic filter.

\subsubsection{Threats to validity}

Claim-facing H1 is the paired mean $\Delta$cov and 95\% CI (\S\ref{sec:decomposition}). Historical placebo / Path~A / TOST stay appendix. Calendar-blocked shuffle leftover is in Results. Wall break-even is a simulator-cost check.

LLM runs are integrity-checked: $T{=}0.2$, no provider seed, and non-interleaved frozen arms. The primary H1 runs lack per-call logs; the calendar-blocked replication records them on dated \texttt{gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18}. Archive updates are slot-ordered, so HTTP completion order does not alter the experimental order.

\subsubsection{Generalizability}
\label{sec:generalizability}

The load-bearing attribution result sits on one structured-genotype CA evaluator, as a warm-started continuation ($38.84\%$). Maze empty $2\times2$: Table~\ref{tab:rq1-maze}. Maze H5 / Sphere / dungeon are in the appendix.

\subsubsection{No live SAIL / acquisition-management baseline}
\label{sec:no-sail}

H2 instantiates an absolute fitness-threshold gate on a shared MLP. Ranking that gate against SAIL-style acquisition remains future work~\cite{gaier2018sail}. Offline GP+UCB threshold replay is a related-lineage check on logged proposals.

\subsubsection{Scope of the evidence}

The protocol freezes were specified per analysis family (Data availability). The main evidence concerns RQ1 attribution.

\subsection{Future work}

Several natural extensions remain: a live fitness--descriptor weight swap (\S\ref{sec:fd-coupling}); a rewrite-stub control if Path~A is revisited; all-evaluated LLM quality on new \texttt{proposal\_log} tiers; a naturally expensive evaluator for wall-clock above break-even; a pinned open-weight residual; and a live SAIL-style acquisition arm.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied a bundled LLM ``hints'' improvement in MAP-Elites and asked whether its apparent coverage gain was attributable to parent-level prompt scalars or to target selection. The first control to check is target selection. On the primary warm-started CA grid, the historical stub$\rightarrow$hints contrast was $+15.09$~pp, but matching target selection reduced the remaining soft contrast to $+0.26$~pp. A contemporaneous calendar-blocked $2\times2$ on five independently generated floors replicated the same separation: target selection contributed $+14.53$/$+14.82$~pp, whereas the remaining live-vs-stub difference was $+0.29$~pp at uniform selection (Table~\ref{tab:rq1-cf}).

The policy effect was also observed without LLM calls ($+13.1$~pp in the matched genetic comparison) and on an empty-archive maze. The bundled LLM improvement went with target-selection policy, not with the parent-level scalars.

If an LLM emitter is dropped in as a whole pipeline, the first thing to hold fixed is target selection. Code and experimental artifacts are released at the pinned repository revision \texttt{\SubmissionGitShort}.

\subsubsection*{Diagnostic companions}

\paragraph{Paired proportion of favorable pairs ($\mathrm{PPS}$; selective).}
Classic Vargha--Delaney $A_{12}$ is defined for independent samples~\cite{vargha2000new}. On paired seeds Kerby's \emph{paired proportion of favorable pairs}~\cite{kerby2014simple} (common-language effect size for matched pairs~\cite{mcgraw1992cl}) is
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{PPS} \;=\; \frac{\#\{\Delta_i > 0\} + 0.5\,\#\{\Delta_i = 0\}}{n},
  \label{eq:pps}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_i$ is treatment $-$ control on seed $i$ (for ``less,'' count $\Delta_i < 0$). $\mathrm{PPS}{=}0.5$ is the expected value under a paired null of no systematic sign (ties as half), and coincides with the Sign rate when there are no ties. At $n{=}10$, unanimous contrasts therefore give $\mathrm{PPS}\in\{0,1\}$; main-text tables omit that redundant column and keep Sign. We report $\mathrm{PPS}$ only as a robustness descriptor when it is not $0$ or $1$, or when no Sign column is shown (H4 MAE coverage; H3-gray NI). It is not a propensity score, not an $A_{12}$ cutoff, and not a family decision rule.

\textbf{Why $\pm 2$~pp (provenance).} The $\pm 2$~pp band first appeared as an internal extend-or-stop gate on $n{\le}3$ resource screens (Table~\ref{tab:hint-pilots}; \S\ref{sec:h1-impl-threat}). Later reuse of that number as a TOST margin is post-hoc and is not a locked equivalence claim. The claim-facing comparison is the leftover $95\%$ CI against the policy $\Delta$ of ${\approx}15$~pp. On a $2500$-cell archive, $\pm 2$~pp is $\pm 50$ cells; that conversion is descriptive.

\paragraph{MDE (difference-test companion).}
Separately, as a sensitivity analysis---not TOST power---we report the approximate minimum detectable effect for a two-sided paired $t$-test of H$_0{:}\,\mathrm{mean}\,\Delta{=}0$ at $n$ seeds, $\alpha{=}0.05$, 80\% power:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{MDE} \;=\; \bigl(t_{1-\alpha/2,\,n-1} + t_{0.80,\,n-1}\bigr)\,\frac{\mathrm{SD}_{\Delta}}{\sqrt{n}},
  \label{eq:mde}
\end{equation}
This is the power to detect a \emph{non-zero} paired mean difference, not power for a confirmatory TOST at $\pm 2$~pp. At a true effect on the reporting-band edge ($|\Delta|{=}2$~pp), TOST power is necessarily much lower than a difference-test power at that same $\Delta$; we do \emph{not} report post-hoc observed power at $1$/$2$~pp. For H1, $\mathrm{SD}_{\Delta}{=}1.19$~pp is taken from the same matched $n{=}10$ analysis (retrospective; no independent pilot variance was locked for this contrast), yielding $\mathrm{MDE}{\approx}1.2$~pp---a design-sensitivity note only, not a margin justification. Elsewhere we quote only MDE numbers under the same difference-test convention (e.g.\ dungeon hints$-$stub coverage AUC ${\approx}1.2$~pp; maze genetic\_filter coverage AUC ${\approx}2.3$~pp and maze hints$-$stub coverage AUC ${\approx}2.6$~pp before Holm $m{=}8$; Table~\ref{tab:b5-holm}). These do not convert non-rejects into confirmatory band claims.

\section{Shared-checkpoint validation diagnostics}
\label{sec:checkpoint-appendix}
\label{sec:checkpoint}

Main text leads with scale-normalized NMAE and treats $R^{2}$ as a secondary, variance-sensitive companion. This appendix records the validity screen, the compose-gate mismatch, the NMAE denominator, split dependence, and what global hold-out does \emph{not} validate.

Archive endpoints are never surrogate scores.
Every confirmatory elite is inserted only after \texttt{evaluate\_candidate} (deterministic simulator fitness/BC). Skipped H2 proposals never enter the archive. Terminal coverage and mean elite fitness therefore cannot be an artifact of surrogate error alone; the surrogate affects \emph{which} candidates are simulated (H2) or which scalars appear in the prompt (H1), not the reported archive metrics.

Offline training on the nightly buffer ($N=35{,}114$ rows; schema~2.1, 24-D features; hold-out $n{=}7{,}023$) yields hold-out metrics for checkpoint \texttt{nightly\_v3\_mc\_d005.pkl}. Protocol amendment 2026-07-28 aligns the offline compose gate with runtime YAML ($0.95$). On that runtime target the hold-out reads
\[
  \mathrm{MAE}_{\mathrm{fitness}}=0.029,\quad
  \mathrm{NMAE}_{\mathrm{fitness}}=0.112,\quad
  R^{2}_{\mathrm{fitness}}=0.94,\quad
  \mathrm{MAE}_{\mathrm{stability}}=0.040,
\]
passing \texttt{hints\_ok}. Gate-specific $\mathrm{NMAE}_{\mathrm{fitness}}=\mathrm{MAE}_{\mathrm{fitness}}/\mathrm{std}(y_{\mathrm{gate}})$.

The split is a random $20\%$ row-level hold-out (\texttt{holdout\_split}, \texttt{random\_state}$=42$), not a temporal or leave-runs-out split. The nightly buffer has $35{,}087$ unique canonical genotype hashes and $27$ extra exact-duplicate rows ($35{,}087+27=35{,}114$). Because rows from the nightly buffer are not independent maps/seeds, this split does not provide run-level independence. The row-level split also contains limited genotype-hash overlap: $9$ canonical genotype hashes occur in both train and hold-out. Thus the hold-out is a row-level interpolation check, not a strict unseen-genotype generalization test. All $971$ collapsed warm-start elites are in the buffer (covariate overlap; \S\ref{sec:warm-start-limit}).

\textbf{Bootstrap unit.} Reported 95\% CIs (NMAE $[0.107,0.117]$; MAE $[0.027,0.030]$; $R^{2}$ $[0.94,0.95]$) use $B{=}10{,}000$ nonparametric bootstrap \emph{resamples of hold-out rows} treated as exchangeable (artifact: \texttt{holdout\_compose\_gate\_alignment.json}). The CI is therefore a within-split precision band and may be too narrow if row-level dependence is present. We do \emph{not} claim a run-clustered standard error.

\textbf{NMAE denominator (cross-gate).} Within a single compose definition, $\mathrm{NMAE}=\mathrm{MAE}/\mathrm{std}(y)$ is the natural scale-free error. Across gates, $\mathrm{std}(y)$ itself changes, so gate-specific NMAE is \emph{not} a fixed-denominator contrast. Prefer MAE for absolute error, or NMAE with denominator locked to the runtime target $\mathrm{std}(y_{0.95}){=}0.255$: that fixed-denominator NMAE is $0.027$ at gate~$0.5$ vs.\ $0.112$ at gate~$0.95$ (MAE $0.0069\!\rightarrow\!0.029$). The headline gate-specific NMAE changes from $0.052$ to $0.112$ because each gate uses its own $\mathrm{std}(y)$; that pair is a different estimand from the fixed-denominator contrast.

At gate~$0.5$, $96.2\%$ of hold-out labels are exactly zero ($\mathrm{std}(y){=}0.133$); at gate~$0.95$, only $29.0\%$ are zero and the continuous mass spreads ($\mathrm{std}(y){=}0.255$, $\times 1.92$). Because $R^{2}{=}1{-}\mathrm{SS}_{\mathrm{res}}/\mathrm{SS}_{\mathrm{tot}}$, larger target variance inflates $R^{2}$ even as absolute MAE grows ($\times 4.12$). Therefore $R^{2}{=}0.94$ should not be interpreted as a model-quality improvement over the historical $R^{2}{=}0.76$: the target distribution changed. We report it as a secondary variance-sensitive metric for the runtime target. The misalignment affected only the offline validity metric; live filter logs always used gate~$0.95$.

\textbf{Arm-conditional validation.} Global hold-out does \emph{not} certify accuracy on every arm's proposal stream. For the non-LLM H2 gate we now report shadow-stream online ranking/calibration (AUROC~$0.906$, precision/recall @$\tau$, emitter-wise NMAE; appendix~\S\ref{sec:h2-impl-threat}) in addition to skip anatomy and false-skip / missed-elite rates. Descriptor-space error maps, uncertainty calibration, and per-arm online NMAE on LLM mixed / rewrite streams are not reported here (child-distribution NMAE ${\approx}0.63$/$0.39$ in \ref{sec:h1-pilots-appendix} is a soft-channel diagnostic, not the H2 genetic gate). Hold-out metrics do not replace simulator endpoints for H1/H2/H3, and global accuracy is not evolution-control utility (appendix~\S\ref{sec:h2-impl-threat}).

\textbf{Consistency after measurement fixes.} The compose-gate and NMAE-denominator bugs (Table~\ref{tab:bug-ledger}) changed offline validity reporting and H3 confirmatory eligibility, not archive insertion fitness. Claim-facing terminal coverage, mean elite fitness, and QD-score on frozen primary tiers (\texttt{q1-full}, matched H1, genetic H2) remain those of the original simulator summaries; no re-aggregation of archive endpoints was required after the 2026-07-28 hold-out alignment.

\section{MLP vs.\ GP/UCB ablation (offline)}
\label{sec:surrogate-ablation-appendix}

This analysis is not required for the descriptive H2 case study. Production H2 uses a four-member MLP ensemble with MC-dropout and an absolute fitness threshold ($\texttt{min\_fit}{=}0.45$, $\texttt{max\_u}{=}1.0$), not a SAIL-style GP+UCB acquisition policy. This appendix records an offline MLP-versus-GP/UCB comparison that motivates freezing this surrogate family for the attach-role contrast (\S\ref{sec:surrogate-method}). Hold-out NMAE/$R^{2}$ are used for offline surrogate comparison; they do not validate live gate decisions (\ref{sec:h2-impl-threat}).

On the nightly-buffer hold-out at the runtime-aligned compose gate of $0.95$, the production MLP achieves $\mathrm{NMAE}{=}0.112$ and $R^{2}{=}0.94$, versus $\mathrm{NMAE}{=}0.222$ and $R^{2}{=}0.89$ for a scikit-learn GP fitness regressor trained on $5{,}000$ rows under the same label definition. The MLP is approximately $37\times$ faster for inference on a $32{,}500$-proposal batch. This comparison supports the choice of MLP as the production surrogate on offline predictive accuracy and batch-inference cost, but does not establish superior evolutionary control. It is an offline baseline comparison rather than an architecture-only ablation, because the GP uses a smaller training subset. An earlier M1 snapshot at the misaligned gate~$0.5$ reported MLP $R^{2}{=}0.76$ versus GP ${\approx}0.22$; because ${\approx}96\%$ of gate-$0.5$ labels are zero, that comparison is not used as a model-quality claim (\ref{sec:checkpoint-appendix}).

Offline acquisition replay further illustrates the distinction between prediction quality and gate behavior. Replacing the MLP with the GP under the same threshold gate increases the false-skip proxy from $0.7\%$ to $3.2\%$. A GP UCB skip rule using $\mu{+}\beta\sigma < \texttt{min\_fit}$, with $\beta\in\{0.15,0.5,1.0\}$, remains near $99\%$ skip, indicating that the GP/UCB scores are poorly aligned with the runtime threshold at these $\beta$ values. On logged Q1 filter proposals ($n{=}325{,}000$), applying the same UCB rule to the MLP's $\mu/\sigma$ only softens the gate, reducing the skip rate from $33.5\%$ to $28.5\%$, $25.7\%$, and $21.2\%$, respectively, without adding skips. At $\texttt{max\_u}{=}1.0$, uncertainty therefore does not materially affect the production threshold gate in this replay.

These results do not constitute a live SAIL comparison or establish GP+UCB as an inferior evolutionary-control algorithm. GP+UCB is treated here as a related SAIL-lineage reference rather than a competitive drop-in baseline. Tree boosters (XGBoost / LightGBM) are outside the scope of this surrogate-family diagnostic.

\paragraph{Genome and evaluation.}
Emitters search a 21-parameter genome: nine birth bits $b_0,\ldots,b_8$, nine survival bits $s_0,\ldots,s_8$, and continuous scalars $(\texttt{noise},\texttt{resource\_regen},\texttt{predation})$. CMA-ME/MAE propose in the same $\mathbb{R}^{21}$ before \texttt{rint}/clip decode (\S\ref{sec:pyribs}). The surrogate does \emph{not} ingest raw 21-D alone: schema~2.1 builds a 24-D feature vector
\begin{multline}
  x \;=\; \bigl(b_0,\ldots,b_8,\; s_0,\ldots,s_8,\;
    \texttt{noise},\; \texttt{resource\_regen},\; \texttt{predation}, \\
    |B|/9,\; |S|/9,\; |B\cap S|/9\bigr),
  \label{eq:features-24}
\end{multline}
where $B{=}\{i:b_i{=}1\}$ and $S{=}\{i:s_i{=}1\}$ (implementation: \texttt{encode\_world\_spec\_features\_v21}). Legacy schema~2.0 was the 21-D prefix only. Each evaluation runs a fixed $50\times50$, $T_{\mathrm{sim}}{=}200$ toroidal grid. Before \texttt{run\_world}, \texttt{apply\_canonical\_seed} sets \texttt{world.seed} from SHA-256 of the canonical genome JSON (independent of the MAP-Elites run seed $0$--$9$), so fitness/BC are a deterministic function of the genotype; early extinction yields archive fitness~0. Terminal elites keep the fitness/BC from that single insertion pass---they are not re-scored at end of run. Illustration panels that re-call \texttt{run\_world} on archived genomes (Figure~\ref{fig:elites}) are for visualisation; under the same canonical seed they must match the stored labels.

\paragraph{Behaviour and fitness.}
Archive axes are stability and diversity in $[0,1]$. Authoritative insertion fitness recomposes diversity, survival, oscillation, and topology proxies. All fitness components below are dimensionless scores already scaled to $[0,1]$ before weighting (topology indices TI/TH are likewise clipped into $[0,1]$ before averaging):
\begin{equation}
  E_{\mathrm{ext}} = \mathrm{clip}(1 - \rho_{\mathrm{final}}, 0, 1), \quad
  T_{\mathrm{topo}} = \mathrm{clip}\!\left(\tfrac{\text{TI} + \text{TH}}{2}, 0, 1\right)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:fitness}
  \text{fitness} = \mathrm{clip}\bigl(
    0.45\,D + 0.25\,(1 - E_{\mathrm{ext}}) + 0.20\,A + 0.10\,T_{\mathrm{topo}}
  ,\; 0,\; 1\bigr)
\end{equation}
Thus, for non-clipped values, $1-E_{\mathrm{ext}}=\rho_{\mathrm{final}}$.
Here $D\in[0,1]$ is clipped diversity, $A\in[0,1]$ is \texttt{oscillation\_score}, and $T_{\mathrm{topo}}\in[0,1]$ is the topology score (distinct from the simulator horizon $T_{\mathrm{sim}}{=}200$ above). The outer $\mathrm{clip}(\cdot,0,1)$ is defensive. Weights $(0.45,0.25,0.20,0.10)$ are fixed protocol constants shared by live evaluation and surrogate recompose---not fit to the H1/H2 data, not preference-elicited, and not swept for sensitivity in this manuscript. Because $D$ is also a BC axis, $f$ and $D$ are coupled by construction (status \S\ref{sec:fd-coupling}; Table~\ref{tab:fd-coupling}). The surrogate recomposes the same formula from predicted components (\S\ref{sec:surrogate-method}). QD-score is
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{QD} \;=\; \sum_{c \in \mathrm{filled}} f_c \;=\; |\mathrm{filled}| \cdot \bar f.
  \label{eq:qd}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection*{Path A child rewrite}
Tier \texttt{q1-h1-child-rewrite-pilot} adds a conditional second LLM pass after \texttt{predict(draft)} when predicted child fitness is below parent true fitness (protocol \texttt{artifacts/Q1\_H1\_CHILD\_REWRITE.md}). The $90\%$ CI of the paired coverage difference lies within a $\pm 2$~pp band ($[-0.50,+1.20]$), but this exploratory post-hoc check is not an equivalence test. This is not a clean test that stronger before-generation actuators fail. There is no rewrite-stub arm. Approximately $80\%$ of drafts were unchanged, so the second pass often had no effect. Child-level NMAE is also substantially worse than parent hold-out validity, indicating substantially lower predictive validity on the child distribution. Matched H1 is unchanged. \subsubsection*{Historical shuffled placebo}
Tier \texttt{q1-h1-placebo-pilot} permutes intact live MLP $(\mathrm{fitness},\mathrm{uncertainty})$ pairs across the ${\approx}10$ LLM slots after \texttt{prepare\_emit}, preserving the batch scalar multiset while breaking parent alignment (protocol \texttt{artifacts/Q1\_H1\_PLACEBO\_LOCK.md}). Offline vs.\ frozen mixed-stack \texttt{hints}/\texttt{stub\_uniform}: terminal coverage $62.17\pm1.70\%$ (placebo) vs.\ $60.52\pm0.72\%$ / $60.21\pm0.81\%$; paired placebo$-$hints $+1.65\pm1.73$~pp (8/10; Wilcoxon $p{\approx}0.020$), but cov@20k only $+0.42$~pp---a late-terminal gap, not a mid-budget lift. The $90\%$ CI of the paired coverage difference extends beyond the $\pm 2$~pp band ($[+0.64,+2.65]$); this exploratory check therefore does not support practical equivalence. This package does not reopen matched H1 and is not a confirmatory soft-channel result: the direction is opposite to that expected if correctly aligned scalars were beneficial, and the comparison is cross-tier (placebo later, $2$ LLM workers vs.\ frozen hints at $4$). A matched interleaved engineering seed (\texttt{q1-h1-placebo-interleaved}; protocol \texttt{Q1\_H1\_PLACEBO\_INTERLEAVED.md}; both arms, $2$ workers) was collected for seed~0 only: placebo$-$hints $-0.92$~pp terminal / $-1.44$~pp @20k, with interleaved hints matching frozen mixed-2x2 seed~0 ($+0.04$~pp). That single seed cannot support inference about non-reproduction of the historical $+1.65$~pp late gap. The narrower lesson is that a distribution-matched placebo is a better alignment control than YAML $0.5$/$1.0$ constants alone. Artifacts: \texttt{q1-h1-placebo-pilot/ANALYSIS.md}; \texttt{q1-h1-placebo-interleaved/ANALYSIS.md}.

The contemporaneous factorial below supersedes these historical pilots for the alignment question.

\subsubsection*{Calendar-blocked shuffle}
The contemporaneous factorial's \texttt{shuffled\_uniform} arm uses the same permutation on dated \texttt{gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18}, inside calendar-blocked blocks, with call logs (protocol \path{artifacts/Q1_RQ1_CONTEMPORANEOUS_FACTORIAL.md}). \subsection{Wall-clock accounting condition}
\label{sec:h2-breakeven}
\label{sec:why-filter}

Fix a proposal stream and a skip indicator with rate $s$. Let $T_{\mathrm{sim}}$ be the simulator time of a candidate \emph{if} it were evaluated, $t_{\mathrm{pred}}$ the online per-proposal inference cost, and $\Delta t_{\mathrm{gate}}$ extra gate / serialization / batching / archive overhead relative to the unfiltered loop. Unfiltered wall per proposal is $\mathbb{E}[T_{\mathrm{sim}}]$; filtered wall is $t_{\mathrm{pred}}+\Delta t_{\mathrm{gate}}+(1-s)\,\mathbb{E}[T_{\mathrm{sim}}\mid\mathrm{keep}]$. Filter wins iff
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:h2-breakeven}
t_{\mathrm{pred}}+\Delta t_{\mathrm{gate}}
<
s\,\mathbb{E}[T_{\mathrm{sim}}\mid \mathrm{skip}],
\end{equation}
by the law of total expectation. Figure~\ref{fig:budget-axes} is the conceptual three-axis claim; this identity prices only the wall axis on this stack. Online archives diverge, so the right-hand side is counterfactual without shadow-timing skipped candidates. Training of the frozen checkpoint is amortized offline and is not in~\eqref{eq:h2-breakeven}.

If $T_{\mathrm{sim}}$ is constant and residual overheads are absorbed into $t_{\mathrm{MLP}}:=t_{\mathrm{pred}}+\Delta t_{\mathrm{gate}}$,~\eqref{eq:h2-breakeven} collapses to the special case $t_{\mathrm{MLP}}<s\,t_{\mathrm{sim}}$. Here $s{\approx}0.335$ and $t_{\mathrm{MLP}}/t_{\mathrm{sim}}\approx 2.2$, so under that special case the simulator must be roughly $7\times$ more expensive than the online MLP overhead for filtering to break even. That factor is a stack/hardware-specific constant-$t_{\mathrm{sim}}$ illustration, not a portable identity and not an interval estimate. Frozen H2 logs do not time $T_{\mathrm{sim}}$ per candidate, so $\mathbb{E}[T_{\mathrm{sim}}\mid\mathrm{skip}]$ and $\mathbb{E}[T_{\mathrm{sim}}\mid\mathrm{keep}]$ are unmeasured on that CA stream; skip mass is high predicted early-extinction (Table~\ref{tab:skip-mech}), which would make the constant-$t$ figure optimistic. On the LLM stack chat completions are issued before the skip, so gating does not cut emit attempts (\S\ref{sec:cost}). Maze injected-delay is a descriptive \emph{regime-transition} check of the constant-$t$ special case \emph{on maze}, not a measurement of CA $\mathbb{E}[T_{\mathrm{sim}}\mid\mathrm{skip}]$ and not a naturally expensive second domain: sleep $\delta{=}10$~ms (above that domain's ${\sim}3$~ms algebraic threshold) is applied only inside \texttt{evaluate\_maze} on simulated (non-skipped) proposals. Under that inflated $t_{\mathrm{sim}}$ the same gate is faster on wall for $9/10$ seeds. Sphere (Table~\ref{tab:sphere-h2}) is an evaluation-indexed substrate check, not a wall-break-even measurement.

\subsection{H2 supporting controls}
\label{sec:h2-impl-threat}

Predictive accuracy alone does not establish utility as an evolution-control mechanism~\cite{jin2005fitness}. The hold-out screen (\S\ref{sec:surrogate-method}) therefore serves as an accuracy check, while the live ranking and gating controls below assess decision utility. The controls separate three questions: whether the surrogate ranks proposals usefully, whether skipping those proposals preserves search progress, and whether the apparent terminal disadvantage is only a consequence of the reduced evaluation budget. Shadow AUROC is $0.906$ (precision/recall $0.927$/$0.801$ at $\tau{=}0.45$). At a matched real-evaluation budget, \texttt{filter\_eval\_matched} exceeds uniform by $+3.65$~pp (\S\ref{sec:h2}); rate-matched \texttt{random\_skip} loses to live filter; that matched-eval excess means the fixed-proposal $-0.83$~pp terminal is budget truncation; same-stream true-fitness replay exceeds the live replay by $+0.73$~pp, quantifying residual ranking headroom (Tables~\ref{tab:skip-mech},~\ref{tab:h2-tau-sweep},~\ref{tab:h2-ranking-controls},~\ref{tab:h2-oracle-replay}). Skip mass is low predicted fitness / high extinction; $\tau$ trades mid-budget fill against terminal coverage.

\section{CVT archive sensitivity}
\label{sec:cvt-sensitivity}

\emph{Not required to follow the main argument.} Coverage is a tessellation-dependent construct: the same genotypes map to different niche counts under a $50\times50$ grid vs.\ a CVT. The confirmatory archive geometry was frozen as the $50\times50$ grid before primary-grid readout (protocol v2/v3); CVT is a post-lock \emph{sensitivity} package (\texttt{q1-cvt}), not an alternate confirmatory tessellation and not a re-registration of matched H1 TOST.

\textbf{What transfers (bundled stack only; Table~\ref{tab:cvt}).}
Sign of bundled hints$-$stub coverage: 10/10 on both archives. Terminal ranking of the three bundled arms is preserved in the mid-band sense: stub $\ll$ hints $\approx$ filter (grid ${\approx}45/60/59\%$; CVT ${\approx}37/59/59\%$). Filter evaluation reduction stays in-band (${\sim}31\%$ CVT vs.\ $33.5\%$ grid). Absolute $\Delta$cov is larger on CVT mainly because the stub baseline is lower---do not treat pp gaps as tessellation-invariant.

\textbf{What does not transfer / was not tested.}
Matched-policy H1 (\texttt{stub\_uniform} vs.\ \texttt{hints}) was \emph{not} re-run on CVT, so the soft-residual estimate / exploratory TOST summary is grid-scoped. We do not compare filled-region geometry (which niches light up) across archives. Artifact: \texttt{artifacts/Q1\_GRID\_CVT\_ANALYSIS.md}.

\section{Archive heatmap panel (supplemental)}
\label{sec:heatmap-panel}

Figure~\ref{fig:heatmaps} is an \emph{illustrative} view of where the paired coverage gap lives on one outcome-selected seed, not a proof of a general spatial pattern: CMA-ME illuminates broad regions of behaviour space that hints leave empty---especially low-diversity bands and scattered interior holes---rather than only polishing already-filled high-fitness niches. Fitness colour is higher on average in the CMA panel, consistent with the fitness delta, but the dominant visual story is \emph{coverage}. Those gray bands are empty cells of the tessellation, not a certified infeasible mask: the same seed's CMA-ME panel occupies them, so raw coverage over $2500$ cells does not hide a uniformly unreachable region (\S\ref{sec:coverage-support}). Across all ten paired seeds the same geometry appears as occupancy probability and $\Delta P$ (Figure~\ref{fig:occupancy-n10}): CMA-ME occupies a broader $(S,D)$ support; gray there means never occupied in those ten archives. Aggregate evidence remains the $n{=}10$ paired $\Delta$cov, not the heatmap geometry.
