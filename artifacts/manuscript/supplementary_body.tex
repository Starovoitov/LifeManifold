\section{MLP vs.\ GP/UCB ablation (offline)}
\label{sec:surrogate-ablation-appendix}

This analysis is not required for the descriptive H2 case study. Production H2 uses a four-member MLP ensemble with MC-dropout and an absolute fitness threshold ($\texttt{min\_fit}{=}0.45$, $\texttt{max\_u}{=}1.0$), not a SAIL-style GP+UCB acquisition policy. This section records an offline MLP-versus-GP/UCB comparison that motivates freezing this surrogate family for the attach-role contrast (\S\ref{sec:surrogate-method}). Hold-out NMAE/$R^{2}$ are used for offline surrogate comparison; they do not validate live gate decisions (\ref{sec:h2-impl-threat}).

On the nightly-buffer hold-out at the runtime-aligned compose gate of $0.95$, the production MLP achieves $\mathrm{NMAE}{=}0.112$ and $R^{2}{=}0.94$, versus $\mathrm{NMAE}{=}0.222$ and $R^{2}{=}0.89$ for a scikit-learn GP fitness regressor trained on $5{,}000$ rows under the same label definition. The MLP is approximately $37\times$ faster for inference on a $32{,}500$-proposal batch. This comparison supports the choice of MLP as the production surrogate on offline predictive accuracy and batch-inference cost, but does not establish superior evolutionary control. It is an offline baseline comparison rather than an architecture-only ablation, because the GP uses a smaller training subset. An earlier M1 snapshot at the misaligned gate~$0.5$ reported MLP $R^{2}{=}0.76$ versus GP ${\approx}0.22$; because ${\approx}96\%$ of gate-$0.5$ labels are zero, that comparison is not used as a model-quality claim (\ref{sec:checkpoint-appendix}).

Offline acquisition replay further illustrates the distinction between prediction quality and gate behavior. Replacing the MLP with the GP under the same threshold gate increases the false-skip proxy from $0.7\%$ to $3.2\%$. A GP UCB skip rule using $\mu{+}\beta\sigma < \texttt{min\_fit}$, with $\beta\in\{0.15,0.5,1.0\}$, remains near $99\%$ skip, indicating that the GP/UCB scores are poorly aligned with the runtime threshold at these $\beta$ values. On logged Q1 filter proposals ($n{=}325{,}000$), applying the same UCB rule to the MLP's $\mu/\sigma$ only softens the gate, reducing the skip rate from $33.5\%$ to $28.5\%$, $25.7\%$, and $21.2\%$, respectively, without adding skips. At $\texttt{max\_u}{=}1.0$, uncertainty therefore does not materially affect the production threshold gate in this replay.

These results do not constitute a live SAIL comparison or establish GP+UCB as an inferior evolutionary-control algorithm. GP+UCB is treated here as a related SAIL-lineage reference rather than a competitive drop-in baseline. Tree boosters (XGBoost / LightGBM) are outside the scope of this surrogate-family diagnostic.

\subsubsection*{Path A child rewrite}
Tier \texttt{q1-h1-child-rewrite-pilot} adds a conditional second LLM pass after \texttt{predict(draft)} when predicted child fitness is below parent true fitness (protocol \texttt{artifacts/Q1\_H1\_CHILD\_REWRITE.md}). The $90\%$ CI of the paired coverage difference lies within a $\pm 2$~pp band ($[-0.50,+1.20]$), but this exploratory post-hoc check is not an equivalence test. Tier \texttt{q1-h1-placebo-pilot} permutes intact live MLP $(\mathrm{fitness},\mathrm{uncertainty})$ pairs across the ${\approx}10$ LLM slots after \texttt{prepare\_emit}, preserving the batch scalar multiset while breaking parent alignment (protocol \texttt{artifacts/Q1\_H1\_PLACEBO\_LOCK.md}). Offline vs.\ frozen mixed-stack \texttt{hints}/\texttt{stub\_uniform}: terminal coverage $62.17\pm1.70\%$ (placebo) vs.\ $60.52\pm0.72\%$ / $60.21\pm0.81\%$; paired placebo$-$hints $+1.65\pm1.73$~pp (8/10; Wilcoxon $p{\approx}0.020$), but cov@20k only $+0.42$~pp---a late-terminal gap, not a mid-budget lift. The $90\%$ CI of the paired coverage difference extends beyond the $\pm 2$~pp band ($[+0.64,+2.65]$); this exploratory check therefore does not support practical equivalence. This package does not reopen matched H1 and is not a confirmatory soft-channel result: the direction is opposite to that expected if correctly aligned scalars were beneficial, and the comparison is cross-tier (placebo later, $2$ LLM workers vs.\ frozen hints at $4$). A matched interleaved engineering seed (\texttt{q1-h1-placebo-interleaved}; protocol \texttt{Q1\_H1\_PLACEBO\_INTERLEAVED.md}; both arms, $2$ workers) was collected for seed~0 only: placebo$-$hints $-0.92$~pp terminal / $-1.44$~pp @20k, with interleaved hints matching frozen mixed-2x2 seed~0 ($+0.04$~pp). That single seed cannot support inference about non-reproduction of the historical $+1.65$~pp late gap. The narrower lesson is that a distribution-matched placebo is a better alignment control than YAML $0.5$/$1.0$ constants alone. Artifacts: \texttt{q1-h1-placebo-pilot/ANALYSIS.md}; \texttt{q1-h1-placebo-interleaved/ANALYSIS.md}.

The contemporaneous factorial below supersedes these historical pilots for the alignment question.

\subsubsection*{Calendar-blocked shuffle}
The contemporaneous factorial's \texttt{shuffled\_uniform} arm uses the same permutation on dated \texttt{gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18}, inside calendar-blocked blocks, with call logs (protocol \path{artifacts/Q1_RQ1_CONTEMPORANEOUS_FACTORIAL.md}). Alignment leftover \texttt{live\_uniform}$-$\texttt{shuffled\_uniform} is $+0.28$~pp of new coverage (archive-clustered 95\% CI $[+0.02,+0.54]$; $6/10$; outer unit $k{=}5$ floors; Table~\ref{tab:rq1-cf}): consistent with only a small incremental alignment effect, far below the target-selection effect---not a second ${\sim}15$~pp channel and not a confirmatory alignment test. Prompt $(f,u)$ mismatches the source parent on $89\%$ of LLM slots (mean L1 $0.21$); a signed mean of prompt$-$source fitness is uninformative because the permutation preserves the multiset. This contrast does not license an equivalence claim. The historical placebo above remains a bound on that earlier package.

\subsubsection*{Additional diagnostics}
Accepted-elite LLM quality proxies for \texttt{hints} vs.\ \texttt{stub\_uniform} (Table~\ref{tab:h1-llm-quality}): insert rate ${\approx}12\%$ vs.\ $11\%$, cell-improve rate ${\approx}5.6\%$ vs.\ $5.1\%$, realized child$-$parent $\Delta$fit ${\approx}{+}0.01$ both arms, compact edits (Hamming ${\approx}1.6$--$1.9$). These are descriptive. Rejected LLM genotypes were never logged per-seed on frozen arms; later runs append \texttt{proposal\_log.jsonl} (accepts$+$rejects; \texttt{artifacts/PROPOSAL\_LOG.md}). Offline MLP predict on a subsample of \emph{accepted} children systematically under-shoots true fitness. Path~A therefore provides provenance and diagnostic evidence, but does not alter the matched-H1 estimate.

\section{Archive heatmap panel (supplemental)}
\label{sec:heatmap-panel}

Figure~\ref{fig:heatmaps} is an \emph{illustrative} view of where the paired coverage gap lives on one outcome-selected seed, not a proof of a general spatial pattern: CMA-ME illuminates broad regions of behaviour space that hints leave empty---especially low-diversity bands and scattered interior holes---rather than only polishing already-filled high-fitness niches. Fitness colour is higher on average in the CMA panel, consistent with the fitness delta, but the dominant visual story is \emph{coverage}. Those gray bands are empty cells of the tessellation, not a certified infeasible mask: the same seed's CMA-ME panel occupies them, so raw coverage over $2500$ cells does not hide a uniformly unreachable region (\S\ref{sec:coverage-support}). Across all ten paired seeds the same geometry appears as occupancy probability and $\Delta P$ (Figure~\ref{fig:occupancy-n10}): CMA-ME occupies a broader $(S,D)$ support; gray there means never occupied in those ten archives. The gap is also visible along the evaluation axis (Figure~\ref{fig:anytime-ladder}): on seed~4, CMA-ME reaches $\sim$63\% coverage by 10{,}000 evaluations while hints are still $\sim$47\%. Aggregate evidence remains the $n{=}10$ paired $\Delta$cov, not the heatmap geometry.
